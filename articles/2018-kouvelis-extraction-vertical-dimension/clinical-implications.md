# Clinical implications — Kouvelis 2018

Kouvelis G, Dritsas K, Doulis I, Kloukos D, Gkantidis N. Effect of orthodontic treatment with 4 premolar extractions compared with nonextraction treatment on the vertical dimension of the face: a systematic review. Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop 2018;154(2):175-87.

**The review in brief:**

- **Question:** does treatment with four premolar extractions change the skeletal vertical dimension compared with nonextraction treatment?
- **Evidence:** 14 retrospective cohort studies, 857 patients (430 extraction, 427 nonextraction; derived from Table I). Mostly adolescents with Class I or Class II malocclusion, group mean ages 10.8-18.5 years.
- **Outcomes:** changes in mandibular plane angles (SN-GoGn, FMA and others) and face heights (N-Me, ANS-Me) on lateral cephalograms (Table III).
- **Quality:** risk of bias moderate in 5 studies, serious in 5, critical in 3, and one study split (Table II). No meta-analysis.
- **Result:** 10 studies found no difference. The 4 significant results split two each way (Table III).

**Attribution on this page:** "the paper says" = printed in Kouvelis 2018. "Derived" = computed from the printed tables (`checks.py`). "The literature says" = another source checked in the research pass, marked VERIFIED or UNVERIFIED. "Proffit" = the resident's Proffit study pages or the 7th-edition text searched in the research pass (VERIFIED there). "My read" = interpretation.

## What this changes or confirms at the chair

1. **Do not extract premolars to close the mandibular plane or to shorten a long face.**
   - The paper says: 10 of 14 studies found no extraction-nonextraction difference in any vertical measure. Apart from Paquette's nonextraction group (−2.0°), no group's mean mandibular-plane change exceeded 1.2° (Discussion, pp 181 and 184; Table III). Its conclusion: extracting to reduce or control the vertical is not justified (p 185).
   - Derived:
     - Pooling the 12 mandibular-plane comparisons that have SDs gives an extraction-minus-nonextraction difference of −0.32° (95% CI −0.68 to 0.04).
     - In the 6 hyperdivergent subgroups alone it is −0.60° (−1.14 to −0.06).
     - Either way, the most an extraction could plausibly buy is about 1°.
   - The literature says the newer studies agree:
     - Rüedi 2024: 45 vs 31 hyperdivergent patients, SN-ML difference 0.07° (VERIFIED).
     - Ferrillo, Pandis & Fleming 2026: MMPA effect −0.40° (−1.75 to 0.96) after adjusting for baseline (VERIFIED).
     - Kim 2005: neither first- nor second-premolar extraction reduced the vertical in hyperdivergent patients (VERIFIED).
   - Proffit: from childhood to adulthood the mandibular core rotates forward 10-15°, and the mandibular plane angle shows only about 2-4° of it because of surface remodelling (Ch 4, study pages and 7th ed text). A treatment effect under 1° is lost in normal growth variation (my read).
   - Caveat: all 14 studies were retrospective, and the extraction groups usually started with a steeper mandibular plane (10 of 13 comparisons, derived).

2. **Extraction does not make the face longer either.**
   - The paper says: no extraction group's mean mandibular-plane change exceeded +0.5° (Sivakumar FMA, not significant; Table III). The two results in the opposite direction (the abstract's "opposite findings") were:
     - Sivakumar: N-Me +2.3 vs +0.9 mm (P = 0.033) with extraction, but FMA did not differ (+0.5 vs +0.1°). Risk of bias critical.
     - Paquette: the nonextraction FMA fell 2.0° (vs +0.3° with extraction, P < 0.010) (Table III). Risk of bias serious.
   - My read: a hyperdivergent patient who needs extraction for crowding or protrusion should not be refused it for fear of lengthening the face, provided anchorage and vertical mechanics are controlled.
   - Caveat: no study had an extraction group of only hypodivergent patients (p 185). The belief that extraction deepens the bite in low-angle patients is not tested here. Luppanapornlarp's nonextraction group was hypodivergent (FMA 18.9°) and the study found no difference, at critical risk of bias (Tables I-III).

3. **Vertical control comes from mechanics, not from the number of teeth.**
   - The paper says:
     - Gkantidis treated hyperdivergent Class II/1 patients (mean age 11.8 y extraction, 11.0 y nonextraction) with "non-extrusive" mechanics in the extraction group: Nance and Goshgarian arches, no Class II elastics, no low-pull headgear or bite plates.
     - The nonextraction group had "extrusive" mechanics: low-pull headgear in all, anterior bite planes, and Class II or posterior crossbite elastics when needed (Table I).
     - The groups still did not differ: SN-GoGn −0.1 vs +0.1°, ANS-Me +4.0 vs +3.3 mm (Table III).
     - The authors' possible explanation, taken from Gkantidis: during space closure the posterior teeth move mesially parallel to the occlusal plane, not along the maxillary and mandibular planes as the wedge theory assumes (p 184).
   - Proffit:
     - 1 mm of posterior elongation opens the bite about 2 mm anteriorly (Ch 5).
     - Distalising molars more than 2 mm with headgear extrudes them. That is acceptable only with good ramus growth; otherwise the mandible rotates down and back (Ch 16).
     - Class II elastics extrude the lower molars (Ch 16, 7th ed text p 575).
     - Holding or depressing the posterior teeth lets the mandible rotate up and forward (Ch 7, 7th ed text p 242).
   - Hub: in `2026-khaleel-deep-bite-turbos-rcos-elastics` the Mn-Mx angle changed +0.69° to +1.84° within the deep-bite mechanics arms (growth not controlled). That is the same order as the largest extraction-nonextraction differences here (1.7° and 2.3°).
   - My read: Gkantidis' patients were growing, so ramus growth probably absorbed the extrusive mechanics, as Proffit's Ch 16 caveat predicts. In an adult the same mechanics may not be absorbed.

4. **Proffit's "severe vertical discrepancy" exception is not what this review tested.**
   - Proffit Ch 7: with under 4 mm of crowding, extraction is rarely indicated except for severe incisor protrusion or a severe vertical discrepancy (study pages; 7th ed text p 221).
   - The paper says: only skeletal outcomes were measured (p 176), with no overbite and no open-bite samples. The steepest group mean at baseline was SN-GoGn 40.0° (Porto, Table I).
   - The literature says:
     - Aras 2002: in skeletal open bites extending to the posterior teeth, second-premolar or first-molar extraction (n = 17) gave closing rotation; in anterior-only open bites, first-premolar extraction (n = 15) left the vertical dysplasia unchanged (VERIFIED).
     - Garlington & Logan 1990: enucleating lower 5s in 23 patients with SN-MP above 38° reduced lower face height against historical norms (VERIFIED; weak control).
   - My read: in an open bite with protrusion, extraction may close the bite dentally by retracting and relatively extruding the incisors, which this review did not measure. Use extraction there for the incisors, not to rotate the mandible. Whether this is Proffit's reasoning is UNVERIFIED.

5. **To shorten a long face, intrude or hold the posterior teeth.**
   - Proffit:
     - Long face / open bite: hold the maxilla vertically and inhibit posterior eruption in both arches. For severe cases the only successful methods are posterior intrusion on skeletal anchorage and segmental maxillary osteotomy (Ch 14).
     - Moving the maxilla up so the mandible rotates up and forward is the most stable orthognathic procedure (Ch 20 in the 6th ed, Ch 22 in the 7th).
   - The paper says: no study reports skeletal anchorage for vertical control. Hayasaki states "no skeletal anchorage", and 7 studies give no treatment details (Table I).
   - Past exam (2023, Ch 14): a 15-year-old with an open bite and a hyperdivergent mandible, molars to be intruded → mini-screws.

6. **If an extraction effect exists, it is under about 1°, and baseline differences could explain it.**
   - Derived:
     - The extraction groups had the steeper mandibular plane at baseline in 10 of 13 comparisons, by up to 4.6° (Basciftci Class II) and 5.8° (Luppanapornlarp) (Table I). A steep start can drift back on its own.
     - Extraction treatment ran longer in 7 of the 8 studies that report duration by group. Longer treatment means more growth, which biases the linear measures against extraction.
   - The paper says: in Porto the same patients gave a significant SN-GoGn difference (P = 0.020) but no FMA difference (P = 0.240) (Table III).
   - My read: the answer can change with the reference plane, S-N or Frankfort. One angle on one ceph is not enough to judge a vertical effect.

7. **Growth swamps treatment in the linear measures.**
   - The paper says: within groups, N-Me rose +0.9 to +7.0 mm and ANS-Me +0.6 to +4.0 mm (Table III), in treatments lasting 1.5-3.7 years where duration was reported (Table I).
   - The largest between-group difference, Kumari's 4.0 mm in N-Me, has three problems:
     - It is reported without SDs, and sex and treatment time are unknown.
     - The risk-of-bias table notes that only posttreatment values were compared (Table II).
     - Kumari & Fida's own abstract describes a cross-sectional comparison and reports no significant vertical difference (VERIFIED abstract).
   - Proffit: vertical growth lasts longest; increases in face height decline to adult levels only in the early 20s in boys and somewhat earlier in girls (Ch 4).
   - My read: a face height that "increased" during treatment in a growing child is mostly growth. Compare with the growth you would expect, not with zero.

8. **In a high-angle extraction case the real issue is anchorage, not the wedge.**
   - The paper says: most studies were downgraded because the mechanics used to close the spaces were not reported (p 178).
   - The literature says: anchorage loss was 3.01-3.47 mm across facial types, with a trivial effect on the mandibular plane angle (Dzingle 2026, VERIFIED).
   - Past exam (2019, Ch 8): high vertical pattern, first-premolar extractions, maximum anchorage → bone screw.

## Practical protocol notes

1. **Make two separate decisions:**
   - Extraction or not: decide on crowding, incisor position and lip profile (Proffit Ch 7: under 4 mm rarely extract, 5-9 mm either, 10 mm or more almost always).
   - Vertical control: decide on mechanics.
   - Write them as two lines in the treatment plan so the vertical does not become a hidden reason to extract.
2. **High-angle mechanics checklist** (Proffit Ch 14 and Ch 16, VERIFIED in the study pages):
   - Avoid cervical headgear and long-term Class II elastics where possible; both extrude molars.
   - Limit posterior eruption: high-pull headgear ("possibly" intrusive; true intrusion rarely occurs) or posterior bite blocks.
   - Use skeletal anchorage for maximum anchorage and for molar intrusion.
   - Gkantidis' "non-extrusive" extraction protocol used Nance and Goshgarian arches, no Class II elastics and no low-pull headgear (Table I).
3. **Measure the vertical with more than one reference:**
   - Record SN-GoGn, FMA and a face-height ratio (LAFH/TFH).
   - Porto (SN-GoGn P = 0.020, FMA P = 0.240) and Kumari (N-Me significant, FMA not) show how one measure can disagree with another (Table III).
   - Superimpose serial cephs on stable structures rather than relying on a single angle (my read; see the hub's superimposition papers below).
4. **Expect growth:** in the studies of younger patients, N-Me rose 4.5-7.0 mm in both groups (Hayasaki, Kocadereli, Luppanapornlarp [age not given], Paquette, Staggers; Table III), over 1.6-2.9 years where duration was reported (Table I). An increase in face height during treatment is not by itself a treatment failure.
5. **Audit your own cases fairly:** in this literature the extraction groups started steeper (10 of 13 comparisons, derived). Compare outcomes only between patients with similar starting angles.
6. **Consent wording:** extraction is chosen for crowding or protrusion; it will not change face height by itself. Face height depends on growth and on how the back teeth are controlled vertically.

## What the paper does NOT tell you

- **How far the molars moved, or whether they extruded.** Neither was extracted from the studies. The "parallel to the occlusal plane" argument comes from one study (Gkantidis, p 184), not from data across studies.
- **The dental vertical:** overbite, open-bite closure and incisor extrusion were not outcomes.
- **Patients at the extremes:** skeletal open bite, severe hyperdivergence beyond a group mean of SN-GoGn 40.0°, and hypodivergent patients treated with extraction.
- **Growing vs nongrowing patients separately:** no study reported results separately (p 178).
- **Class III and other extraction patterns** (second premolars, molars): not covered.
- **Long-term vertical change after retention.**
- **Which mechanics were used:** treatment details are missing for 7 of 14 studies (Table I).
- **Internal vs surface rotation:** every outcome is a plane angle or a linear height against S-N, FH or the palatal plane; no study used structural or implant superimposition. The hub's `1989-nielsen-maxillary-superimposition-three-methods`, `1994-doppel-maxillary-superimposition-implants` and `2018-nguyen-3d-mandibular-regional-superimposition` cover the methods that could separate them.
- **Equivalence:** single studies could detect only differences of about 1.6-2.6° (derived). The pooled interval is narrower, but it is exploratory and not from the review.
- **Untreated growth:** every control group was treated; there was no untreated comparison.
- **A formal certainty rating:** "moderate to low quality" is the authors' description, with no GRADE assessment.
- **Soft-tissue vertical:** excluded; 2 soft-tissue-only studies were dropped at full text (Figure).

## One sentence for a patient or a junior

- **To a junior:** "Fourteen retrospective studies and 857 patients say four-premolar extraction does not close the mandibular plane: 10 found nothing and the other 4 split two each way by 1.7-2.3° or 1.4-4.0 mm, so extract for crowding and protrusion and control the vertical with mechanics or mini-screws."
- **To a parent:** "We remove teeth to make room and bring the front teeth back; it will not make a long face shorter or a normal face longer, because face height depends on growth and on how we hold the back teeth."
