# Exam questions: Kouvelis 2018, four-premolar extraction and the vertical dimension of the face

Source: Kouvelis G, Dritsas K, Doulis I, Kloukos D, Gkantidis N. Effect of orthodontic treatment with 4 premolar extractions compared with nonextraction treatment on the vertical dimension of the face: a systematic review. Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop 2018;154(2):175-87. doi:10.1016/j.ajodo.2018.03.007. 251 Hellenic Air Force Hospital (Athens), University of Bern, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki.

Design: a systematic review (protocol not registered) of 14 retrospective studies. Together they include 857 patients (derived from Table I: 430 extraction, 427 nonextraction). Risk of bias was assessed with ROBINS-I, and the synthesis was narrative, with no meta-analysis.

This file starts with past exam questions linked to the paper. Then come twelve questions: Q1 to Q7 are single-best-answer MCQs, Q8 to Q11 short answer, and Q12 a case-based oral defence. Tags: R = recall, A = application, N = analysis (4 R, 3 A, 5 N).

### Past exam 1 (Proffit Ch 8 · 2019). Anchorage in a high-angle extraction case

A Class I skeletal patient with a Class III molar relationship and a high vertical pattern. You extract first premolars and want maximum anchorage. What do you use?

- **Keyed answer:** a bone screw (miniscrew).
- **What Kouvelis adds:**
  - Four-premolar extraction had no consistent effect on the skeletal vertical dimension (Conclusions, p 185).
  - The idea that forward molar movement closes the mandible, the wedge effect, "has not been proven" (Discussion, p 184).
  - So there is no vertical reason to let the molars come forward. Anchorage is chosen for the anteroposterior plan, and a bone screw also leaves the option of intruding the molars.
- **Agreement with the paper:** consistent. No flag.
- **Same key, other years:** Class III with lower premolar extraction and maximum anchorage, keyed miniscrew (Ch 8 · 2023).

### Past exam 2 (Proffit Ch 7 · 2020 and 2024; also Q74 in the recall file "Promotion 2021"). Relative intrusion against relative extrusion

What is the discriminating factor between relative intrusion and relative extrusion?

- **Keyed answer:** mandibular rotation. The recall file lists "elongation of the mandibular ramus" as the other option.
- **What Kouvelis adds:**
  - The wedge hypothesis is a claim about mandibular rotation: forward movement of the posterior teeth is said to rotate the mandible counterclockwise (Introduction, p 175).
  - The review's angular outcomes (SN-GoGn, FMA or FH-MP, SN-GoMe, PP-GoGn or PP-GoMe, MP-HP) measure that rotation. Apart from one nonextraction group (Paquette, FMA −2.0°), no mean angular change in any group exceeded 1.2° (Discussion, pp 181 and 184).
  - Proffit's mechanism is posterior eruption against ramus growth (Ch 7; Ch 16, VERIFIED in the notes), and extraction is not part of it.
- **Agreement with the paper:** consistent. No flag.

### Past exam 3 (Proffit Ch 14 · 2023). The hyperdivergent open bite

A 15-year-old with poor oral hygiene, an anterior open bite and a hyperdivergent mandible. The plan is to intrude the molars. How?

- **Keyed answer:** mini-screws (TADs).
- **What Kouvelis adds:**
  - The review concludes that an extraction protocol to reduce or control the vertical is not justified (Conclusions, p 185).
  - No included sample had an open bite, and the highest mean baseline was SN-GoGn 40.0° (Porto, Table I).
  - For severely affected long-face children, Proffit names only posterior intrusion on skeletal anchorage or a segmental maxillary osteotomy (Ch 14, VERIFIED in the notes).
  - About 1 mm of posterior intrusion closes about 2 mm of anterior open bite, so a 4-mm open bite often closes in about as many months (Proffit 7th ed Ch 20, VERIFIED in the notes).
  - Topic 18 makes the same contrast: Kusnoto and Schneider report lower molars intruded 3 to 5 mm on a skeletal anchorage system, while the extraction-based wedge principle is listed among approaches that "have not been scientifically supported" (Semin Orthod 2000;6:33-42, p 40; read this run, VERIFIED).
- **Agreement with the paper:** consistent. Note that the paper does not study open bite. No flag.

### Other recalls on the same theme

**Keyed in the Proffit study-page bank**
- **Ch 7 · 2019, 2020, 2022 and 2024:** in a borderline or moderate-crowding case, decide on extraction by the chin and lip (nose, chin and lip) relationship.
  - This is linked mainly to Stephens 2005, paper 5 of this topic.
  - It is also the decision rule Kouvelis leaves standing. Extract for crowding, protrusion and profile, not for face height (p 185).
- **Ch 16 · 2024:** Class II elastics give a negative result in a vertical (high-angle) grower.
  - Gkantidis 2011 gave its nonextraction arm Class II elastics, low-pull headgear in every patient and bite planes. In growing 11-year-olds there was no measurable skeletal difference: SN-GoGn −0.1 vs +0.1°, P .616 (Tables I and III).
  - The textbook risk is real; growth of the ramus can absorb it.
- **Ch 14 · 2022:** a Class II patient with increased vertical growth is keyed to headgear attached to a maxillary splint.
- **Ch 14 · 2023:** a Class II patient with an open bite is keyed to headgear with an anterior splint and a functional appliance.
  - Both keys control eruption. Neither uses extraction, which fits this review.
- **Ch 14 · 2022 and 2023:** high-pull headgear for vertical control is keyed at 250 g per side. The 2019 recall keyed 500 g for a similar stem, and the study pages note that the years differ.
  - The review lists high-pull headgear alongside extraction as strategies "considered intrusive" (Discussion, p 181).

**Recall files in `promotion/` (no key unless stated)**
- **Recall file "Promotion 2022", headgear set:** "Class II correction using high-pull headgear results in..." The file marks restriction of maxillary horizontal growth as the answer, not "significant vertical changes in LAFH and SN-MP".
  - The same message as this review: the "intrusive" strategies change the skeletal vertical less than clinicians believe.
- **Recall file "Promotion 2021", p 16, Q1:** an open-bite tendency with root-treated upper first molars, a root-treated lower right second premolar with an apical lesion, impacted lower third molars and supernumeraries. Which extractions? Options: upper first molars; upper first molars plus lower premolars and third molars; upper and lower first premolars. **No key is recorded.**
  - My answer: the compromised teeth, option 2.
  - Kouvelis gives no vertical reason to take sound first premolars instead (p 185). Molar-extraction studies were outside its scope (p 184).
  - Aras 2002 found a closing rotation with second-premolar or first-molar extraction in open bites extending posteriorly (literature, VERIFIED abstract).
- **Recall file "Promotion 2021", Q24:** an anterior open-bite case from the article "Skeletal anchorage system for open-bite correction". Options: surgery; upper molar intrusion on skeletal anchorage; lower molar intrusion on skeletal anchorage. **No key is recorded.**
  - A margin note on the same page of the file says the molar intrusion came with minimal lower-incisor extrusion and counterclockwise rotation of the occlusal plane.
  - That counterclockwise closure is what the wedge theory promises from extraction. Here it comes from intrusion.
- **Recall file "Promotion 2021", Q32:** a Class II case could be treated with upper and lower first premolars, or with upper first and lower second premolars. What is the difference? Options: more distal incisor movement with first premolars; no significant difference. **No key is recorded.**
  - Kouvelis does not separate these patterns; only Bravo used lower second premolars (p 178).
  - On the vertical, Kim 2005 and Al-Nimri 2006 found no difference between first- and second-premolar extraction (literature, VERIFIED abstracts).
  - The incisor question is about anchorage, not this review.
- **Recall file "Promotion 2024", Q65:** a 4 mm open bite; how much posterior intrusion gives a normal overbite? Options 2, 3, 4 or 5 mm. **No key is recorded.**
  - Proffit's 2:1 lever applies: 1 mm of posterior elongation opens about 2 mm anteriorly (Ch 5); 1 mm of intrusion closes about 2 mm (7th ed Ch 20) (both VERIFIED in the notes).
  - That gives about 2 mm to close 4 mm, or about 3 mm if "normal overbite" means +2 mm (derived). Check the stem if it reappears.
- **Recall file "Promotion 2021", Q47:** an open bite with increased SN-MP and PP-MP and decreased PFH/AFH and UFH/LFH. Most likely cause? **No key is recorded.**
  - These are the skeletal vertical measures the included studies used, which points to a skeletal open bite (my answer, not a recorded key).
- **Recall file "Promotion 2022":** a hyperdivergent patient treated with a lip bumper, a bonded expander, lip seal and headgear (the recalled wording). What is the treatment effect? No options are recorded.
  - The stem is from Sankey et al 2000 (topic 17, paper 2). Its abstract reports "true" forward mandibular rotation 2.7 times greater than in controls, with relative intrusion of the upper molars (AJODO 2000;118:317-27; read this run, VERIFIED).
  - Sankey also notes that extraction gives dentoalveolar compensation, but that molar eruption during space closure could cancel the gain in face height (p 318).
- **Topic link:** Q32 in the recall file "Promotion 2024" (a 35-year-old unhappy with his profile 10 years after four premolar extractions) sits beside Stephens 2005, paper 5 of this topic. See `2005-stephens-long-term-profile-extraction-nonextraction`.

## Reference card

**Question and eligibility (Methods, p 176)**
- Population: patients of any age treated with full fixed appliances in both arches.
- Intervention: four premolar extractions, one per quadrant.
- Comparison: fixed-appliance treatment without extraction.
- Outcome: skeletal vertical change measured quantitatively on radiographs, before and after treatment.
- Any design with a treated comparison group was eligible.
- Excluded: animal and in-vitro studies, orthognathic surgery, case reports, and studies with fewer than 10 patients.

**Search and selection (Methods, p 176; flow chart, p 177; Appendix, p 187)**
- Databases: MEDLINE (Ovid and PubMed, 1946 to 2 August 2017), EMBASE (Ovid), the Cochrane Oral Health Group's Trials Register and CENTRAL.
- Grey literature: ClinicalTrials.gov, the National Research Register and ProQuest dissertations. Reference lists were hand-searched. No language or date restriction.
- Selection and data extraction were done independently and in duplicate by two authors; selection was not blinded to the studies' authors or results. Disagreements went to discussion with the other authors.
- Measures planned: mean differences with SDs. A P printed as 0.000 was to be imputed as 0.0004. Authors of the primary studies were e-mailed for missing data.
- Heterogeneity: clinical, methodological and statistical. Differences in sex distribution between studies were ignored, "since a differential effect due to sex was not expected" (p 177).
- Flow chart:
  - 927 database records, then 231 after duplicates were removed.
  - Plus 29 from other sources; the same box also says "19 studies identified after hand searching".
  - 260 screened; 226 excluded (183 at title, 43 at abstract); 34 full texts.
  - 20 full texts excluded: 4 had no vertical outcome, 2 soft tissue only, 3 not retrievable, 4 had no angular or linear measures, 6 had no treated nonextraction control, 1 had no posttreatment outcome.
  - 14 included in the qualitative synthesis; 0 in a meta-analysis.
- The MEDLINE strategy is 11 lines with hits from 13 to 22 826. No line combines them (p 187).

**Included studies (Results, p 178; Table I, pp 179-181)**
- All 14 were retrospective, published 1992 to 2016.
- Extraction pattern:
  - four first premolars in 11 studies;
  - upper first premolars with lower first or second premolars in Bravo;
  - not specified in Basciftci and Katsaros.
- Vertical-pattern subgroups:
  - Normodivergent: 4 studies (Hayasaki, Sivakumar, Paquette, Bravo).
  - Hyperdivergent: 6 studies. Class I: Kocadereli, Kumari, Basciftci. Class II: Gkantidis, Porto, Basciftci, Katsaros.
  - All patterns: Kirschneck.
  - Normodivergent extraction against hypodivergent nonextraction: Luppanapornlap.
  - Pattern not defined: Staggers, Hosseinzadeh-Nik.
- Mainly nongrowing samples: Kumari, Sivakumar, Hosseinzadeh-Nik (p 178).

**Derived numbers (`checks.py` and counts from the tables)**
- 857 patients, 430 extraction and 427 nonextraction. Group sizes range from 14 (Hayasaki Class II extraction) to 45 (Staggers nonextraction).
- An exploratory fixed-effect pool of the 12 mandibular-plane comparisons with SDs gives Ex − Nonex −0.32° (95 % CI −0.68 to 0.04), I² = 0 %.
  - It mixes SN-GoGn, FMA, SN-GoMe and MP-HP.
  - Kumari (−0.8°), Luppanapornlap (−0.9°) and Paquette (+2.3°) could not be weighted because they give no SDs.
- Minimum detectable difference at 80 % power with an SD of 2.5°: 2.56° (15 per group), 2.21° (20), 1.81° (30), 1.57° (40).
- Baseline angle higher in the extraction group in 10 of the 13 comparisons that give both values.
- Extraction treatment longer in 7 of the 8 studies that report duration by group (9 of 10 comparisons); the exception is Sivakumar, 1.5 vs 2.9 y.
- Magnification correction "NA" in 8 of 14 studies (machine factor 4, ruler 2). This affects linear measures only.
- Outcome assessor not blinded in 13 of 14 studies.
- Recomputing the printed P values from the means and SDs (t test):
  - Most reproduce closely.
  - A few differ by up to about 0.15, probably because the primary studies used other tests (Gkantidis SN-GoGn: printed .616, recomputed ≈ .73).
  - Only Sivakumar FMA is far off: printed .985, recomputed ≈ .41.
- Female proportion: Paquette 61 % vs 37 %; Sivakumar 84 % vs 62 %.

**Context to keep beside these numbers**
- Primary studies, checked against their own abstracts (literature, VERIFIED):
  - Gkantidis 2011: the two groups came from two orthodontic offices with contrasting protocols.
  - Sivakumar 2008: mesial movement of the posterior teeth came with enough extrusion to increase the vertical dimension; the mandibular plane angle was unchanged.
  - Kumari and Fida 2010: no significant difference in the vertical changes, which increased in both groups. The abstract calls the design a cross-sectional comparative study and lists molar-height measures that the review did not extract.
- Other studies (literature, VERIFIED abstracts):
  - Kim 2005: 27 first- vs 27 second-premolar extraction cases, hyperdivergent. No decrease in facial vertical dimension with either; the wedge hypothesis was called invalid.
  - Al-Nimri 2006: lower first vs lower second premolars, 26 and 26, Class II division 1. The mandibular plane angle was unchanged in both groups.
  - Aras 2002, skeletal open bite: second-premolar or first-molar extraction (n = 17) gave a closing rotation when the open bite extended posteriorly; first-premolar extraction (n = 15) gave no change.
  - Garlington and Logan 1990: 23 patients with SN-MP above 38°, lower second premolars enucleated; LAFH fell against historical norms (weak control).
  - Chua 1993: 174 patients; nonextraction increased ANS-Me significantly and extraction did not. Not in the review; why is unknown.
  - Staggers 1990: 22 second-molar vs 22 first-premolar extractions, no nonextraction group.

### Q8 (R). Methods of the review

Describe (a) the review question and eligibility criteria, (b) the search, (c) study selection, data extraction and risk-of-bias assessment, (d) the synthesis, and (e) the flow of studies.

**Model answer:**

(a) **Question and eligibility (p 176)**
- The aim was to assess four-premolar extraction against nonextraction treatment for its effect on the skeletal vertical dimension of the face.
- Any design with a treated comparison group was eligible.
- Population: any age, with full fixed appliances in both arches.
- Intervention: four premolar extractions, one per quadrant. Comparison: fixed appliances without extraction.
- Outcome: skeletal vertical change measured on radiographs before and after treatment.
- Excluded: animal and in-vitro studies, orthognathic surgery, case reports, and fewer than 10 patients.

(b) **Search (p 176; Appendix, p 187)**
- MEDLINE (Ovid and PubMed, 1946 to 2 August 2017), EMBASE, the Cochrane Oral Health Group's Trials Register and CENTRAL.
- Grey literature: ClinicalTrials.gov, the National Research Register and ProQuest. Reference lists hand-searched. No language or date limits.
- Only the MEDLINE strategy is printed. The protocol was not registered.

(c) **Selection, extraction and risk of bias (pp 176-178)**
- Two authors worked independently and in duplicate at each step; selection was unblinded to the studies' authors and results. Disagreements were settled by discussion with the other authors; risk-of-bias disagreements went to the last author.
- Missing data were requested from the study authors by e-mail.
- Risk of bias: ROBINS-I across seven domains. No study could be low on confounding. Studies lacking information in a domain were judged between serious and critical.

(d) **Synthesis (pp 176-178)**
- Mean differences with SDs were planned, and a P printed as 0.000 was to be imputed as 0.0004.
- Clinical heterogeneity was judged high: growth stage, skeletal pattern, extraction pattern, controls and outcome measures varied. So there was no quantitative synthesis.
- Results were described by vertical-pattern subgroup: normodivergent, hyperdivergent Class I and Class II, and other.

(e) **Flow (p 177)**
- 927 database records, 231 after duplicates were removed, plus 29 from other sources, so 260 screened.
- 226 excluded at title or abstract; 34 full texts; 20 excluded with reasons.
- 14 retrospective studies included, 857 patients (derived), and none meta-analysed.

1. The PICO with "treated nonextraction control" and "skeletal vertical outcome on radiographs".

### Q10 (N). Appraising the conclusion and its certainty

The authors conclude that four-premolar extraction has "no specific effect" on the skeletal vertical dimension, "regardless of age, sex, malocclusion type, or vertical growth pattern", and describe the quality of evidence as moderate to low. Appraise these statements.

**Model answer:**

**What supports the conclusion**
- A broad search: four databases plus grey literature, no language limit.
- Duplicate selection and extraction, and an appropriate risk-of-bias tool for non-randomised studies (ROBINS-I).
- Consistency: 10 of 14 studies found no difference. The four significant results were small (1.4 to 4.0 mm, 1.7 to 2.3°), split two and two in direction, and in each the second measure disagreed (Table III).
- An exploratory pool of the 12 mandibular-plane comparisons with SDs gives −0.32° (95 % CI −0.68 to 0.04), I² = 0 % (derived). Even the limit favouring extraction is under 1°.
- Later work agrees (literature, VERIFIED abstracts): Rüedi 2024 (0.07°, −0.90 to 1.01); Ferrillo 2026 (MMPA −0.40°, −1.75 to 0.96); Alrubaie 2026, prospective (no LAFH difference).

**What limits it**
1. *Design and confounding.*
   - All 14 studies are retrospective, with confounding by indication. The extraction group started with the higher angle in 10 of 13 comparisons (derived).
   - Regression to the mean would favour extraction; continued adverse growth would work against it.
   - Extraction treatment lasted longer in 7 of 8 studies reporting duration by group (derived), which inflates linear growth increments in the extraction groups.
2. *Mechanics unknown.*
   - "Deviations from intended interventions" is "No information" in 8 of 14 studies (Table II). The authors call missing space-closure mechanics the main problem (p 178).
3. *Measurement.*
   - 2D cephalograms. Assessors not blinded in 13 of 14 studies. Magnification correction "NA" in 8 of 14, which affects linear measures only.
   - The angles measure total rotation, which hides most core rotation (Proffit Ch 4, VERIFIED in the notes).
   - No molar measurement was extracted, so the mechanism is not tested.
4. *Synthesis and power.*
   - Vote counting on P values, with no study-level mean differences or CIs, although the Methods planned them (p 176).
   - Single studies of 14 to 45 per group can detect only about 1.6 to 2.6° (derived). A single "no difference" is not equivalence.
5. *Reporting checks.*
   - Kumari's 4 mm result carries a Table II note that "only posttreatment values" were compared, and Kumari and Fida's own abstract reports no significant vertical difference (literature, VERIFIED).
   - Sivakumar's printed FMA P of .985 recomputes to about .41 (derived).
   - The ROBINS-I ratings were applied unevenly: Paquette's sex imbalance (61 % vs 37 % female) is rated moderate on confounding, Sivakumar's (84 % vs 62 %) critical.
6. *Overreach in "regardless of...".*
   - Sex differences between studies were set aside in the Methods (p 177), and there were no subgroup analyses.
   - Class III appears only inside Kirschneck's mixed sample. No extraction group was hypodivergent.
   - No open-bite sample; the highest mean baseline was 40.0°; few adults (three mainly nongrowing samples).
7. *Certainty wording.*
   - "Moderate to low" has no stated method. Under GRADE, non-randomised evidence with serious-to-critical risk of bias would be low to very low (appraisal judgement, derived).
   - Critical-risk studies still count in the "considerable agreement".
8. *Disclosure point.* The senior author is first author of an included study (Gkantidis 2011), which supplies the mechanistic explanation. Disagreements arose only for serious and critical studies, and that study was rated moderate (p 178). State this neutrally.

**Level of evidence:** a systematic review of retrospective cohorts. Oxford CEBM 2009 level 2a with a minus for heterogeneity; level 3 on the 2011 table; GRADE-style certainty low to very low (appraisal mapping, derived).

**What survives:** in adolescents with mild-to-moderate high or average angles, four-premolar extraction has no clinically meaningful effect on the skeletal vertical dimension; the best estimate is within about ±1°. Extraction should not be chosen to control the vertical. The conclusion does not extend to severe open bite, adults or other extraction patterns.

**Marking points (6):**
1. Strengths, including consistency with numbers.
2. Confounding by indication with a direction.
3. Mechanics and measurement limits.
4. Vote counting and power, or the pooled CI.
5. Overreach of "regardless of".

### Q11 (N). The wedge effect, the evidence and Proffit

Explain the wedge-effect hypothesis, appraise what this review and the related literature say about it, and reconcile the review with Proffit's position on vertical control and on extraction for a "severe vertical discrepancy".

**Model answer:**

**The hypothesis (pp 175 and 184)**
- When extraction spaces close, the posterior teeth move forward. Because the jaws converge posteriorly, the molars move into the wider part of the wedge, and this is said to reduce the vertical dimension by counterclockwise (forward) rotation of the mandible.
- It is used to argue for extraction in long-face and open-bite patients (refs 3 to 7). The review cites ref 25, Fields, Proffit et al 1984, for the concept; whether that paper describes it is UNVERIFIED.
- In the year's reading list, Kusnoto and Schneider (topic 18) describe two extraction routes to the "wedge principle": anchor molars moved forward into the wider part of the wedge, or first or second molars extracted to lower posterior height (pp 39-40, VERIFIED).

**What the review shows**
- No study clearly showed the expected smaller increase in the vertical with extraction (p 181).
- Mean angular changes were at most 1.2° in every group except Paquette's nonextraction −2.0°, which runs the other way (pp 181 and 184).
- The authors' explanation, from Gkantidis 2011: molars move parallel to the occlusal plane, not along the maxillary and mandibular planes. The face is not "a simple articulator", and neuromuscular balance matters more than the number of teeth (p 184).

**Why the review cannot test the mechanism directly**
- No molar position or eruption was extracted; all outcomes are skeletal (Table I). Where a primary study did look, the molars extruded as they came forward (Sivakumar abstract, literature, VERIFIED).
- Mandibular-plane angles measure total rotation. Proffit (Ch 4, VERIFIED in the notes): the core rotates forward up to 10 to 15°, but the mandibular plane angle moves only 2 to 4° because of surface remodelling. A small difference in true rotation could be hidden.
- No included study used implant or structural superimposition. This links to topic 1 in the hub: Nielsen 1989, Doppel 1994 and Nguyen 2018 (registry).

**Related literature (VERIFIED abstracts or full text)**
- Against the wedge:
  - Kim 2005 (first vs second premolars, hyperdivergent): no decrease in facial vertical dimension with either; the hypothesis was called invalid.
  - Al-Nimri 2006: mandibular plane unchanged with lower first or second premolar extraction.
  - Dzingle 2026: about 3 to 3.5 mm of anchorage loss in all facial types.
  - Kusnoto and Schneider 2000 (topic 18): the extraction-based wedge principle is among the approaches that "have not been scientifically supported" (p 40).
  - Sankey 2000 (topic 17): molar eruption during space closure can cancel the face-height gain of extraction (p 318).
- For a narrow indication: Aras 2002 found a closing rotation with second-premolar or first-molar extraction in open bites extending posteriorly, but none with first premolars.
- Weakly controlled: Garlington and Logan 1990, where LAFH fell against historical norms after lower second premolars were enucleated in high-angle patients.

**Proffit's position**
- The term "wedge effect" is not in the textbook (searched this run).
- Proffit's vertical mechanism is posterior eruption against ramus growth:
  - 1 mm of posterior elongation opens the bite about 2 mm anteriorly (Ch 5, VERIFIED in the notes).
  - Open bite is treated by depressing the posterior teeth or stopping their eruption, so the mandible rotates up and forward (Ch 7, 7th-edition text seen this run).
  - Vertical control uses high-pull headgear, splints or bite blocks, and, in severe cases, posterior intrusion on skeletal anchorage or a segmental osteotomy (Ch 14, VERIFIED in the notes).
  - Class II elastics and headgear distalisation extrude molars (Ch 16, VERIFIED in the notes).
- That fits the review: the vertical depends on molar height, not on tooth number or anteroposterior molar position.
- Partial conflict: Proffit's Ch 7 guideline lists a severe vertical discrepancy as a reason to extract even with less than 4 mm of crowding (VERIFIED in the notes).
  - Reconcile it this way: the review covers mild-to-moderate high angles without open bite and measured skeletal outcomes only. Proffit's exception concerns severe vertical problems, where extraction may help close an open bite dentally through incisor retraction (my interpretation, UNVERIFIED), or where molar or second-premolar patterns apply (Aras 2002).
  - Vaden and Pearson (topic 18) make the same kind of point: with excessive lower face height, extractions may be necessary to upright the lower incisors (p 127, VERIFIED).
  - In an exam answer: extraction does not reduce face height by itself; control the vertical with the posterior teeth; keep extraction for crowding, protrusion and the severe open-bite exception.

**Marking points (6):**
1. The hypothesis stated with the mechanism.
2. The review's numbers (≤ 1.2°; the Paquette exception) and the Gkantidis explanation.
3. The measurement limit: no molar data, and total versus core rotation.
4. At least two outside studies with direction.
5. Proffit's eruption-based mechanism with chapter.
6. The "severe vertical discrepancy" reconciliation.

## Part 3. Case-based oral defence

### Q12 (A). "Can taking out four teeth make her face shorter?"

**Case.** A 12-year-old girl, before her pubertal growth spurt peak, has a Class II division 1 malocclusion with a 7 mm overjet and proclined upper incisors. SN-GoGn is 41°. Her lips are 4 mm apart at rest, her overbite is 1 mm, and she has 5 mm of mandibular and 3 mm of maxillary crowding. Her mother has read that "extracting four teeth closes a long face" and asks you to do it for that reason.

**Examiner question 1. What does Kouvelis 2018 tell you that is relevant?**
Model answer:
- A systematic review of 14 retrospective studies, 857 patients (derived), comparing four-premolar extraction with nonextraction on skeletal vertical measures.
- Ten studies found no difference. Four found small differences, two for extraction and two against, none confirmed by a second measure (Table III).
- In hyperdivergent Class II division 1 patients like her (Table III):
  - Gkantidis: SN-GoGn −0.1 vs +0.1°, P .616.
  - Porto: −0.9 vs +0.8°, P .020, but FMA not different.
  - Basciftci: +0.2 vs +0.8°, P .425.
  - Katsaros: SN-GoMe −0.8 vs −0.3°, P .431.
- The authors conclude that an extraction protocol to reduce or control the vertical is not justified (p 185).

**Examiner question 2. What can the review not tell you?**
Model answer:
- Severe open bites, adults and molar extraction patterns are not covered, and second-premolar extraction is not reported separately (only Bravo used lower second premolars). The highest mean baseline was 40.0° and no sample had an open bite (Table I; pp 178 and 184).
- It cannot say why there is no effect, because molar movement was not extracted.
- Its outcomes are total rotation, which hides core rotation (Proffit Ch 4, VERIFIED in the notes).
- It cannot predict an individual patient. All studies were retrospective, mechanics were often unreported, and certainty is low to very low (derived judgement).

**Examiner question 3. If not extraction, what controls her vertical?**
Model answer:
- The vertical position of her posterior teeth against the growth of her ramus. 1 mm of posterior elongation opens the bite about 2 mm anteriorly (Proffit Ch 5, VERIFIED in the notes).
- Proffit's vertical-control options (Ch 14, VERIFIED in the notes): high-pull headgear to the molars, or better to a maxillary splint, or a functional appliance with bite blocks. If she is severe, posterior intrusion on skeletal anchorage.
- The past recall keys match: headgear attached to a maxillary splint for a Class II patient with increased vertical growth (Ch 14, 2022).
- Avoid extrusive mechanics: Class II elastics and cervical or low-pull headgear (Ch 16, VERIFIED in the notes; past recall Ch 16, 2024: Class II elastics give a negative result in a vertical grower).

**Examiner question 4. Would you extract for her, and why?**
Model answer:
- Possibly, but for the right reasons.
- She has 5 mm of lower crowding, in Proffit's 5 to 9 mm borderline zone, where the decision rests on the hard and soft tissues and the final incisor position (Ch 7, VERIFIED in the notes). The past recalls key the chin and lip relationship as the deciding factor (Ch 7, 2019 to 2024).
- The 7 mm overjet, the proclined incisors and 4 mm of lip incompetence favour creating space to retract the incisors. Lips follow incisor retraction only partly, until lip competence is reached (Ch 7, VERIFIED in the notes).
- So the indication is crowding, protrusion and lip competence. Not face height.
- The retraction plan also depends on how much of the Class II can be corrected with her growth.

**Examiner question 5. If you extract, how do you avoid making her vertical worse?**
Model answer:
- Choose anchorage for the anteroposterior plan, not to "wedge" the bite closed. If maximum anchorage is needed, use a bone screw (past recall Ch 8, 2019, keyed bone screw). Screws can also intrude the molars if needed.
- Close space without extrusive mechanics: avoid Class II elastics and low-pull headgear; consider high-pull headgear.
- Monitor the overbite and the mandibular plane during space closure. Molar eruption during space closure can cancel any gain (Sankey 2000, topic 17, VERIFIED).
- Gkantidis's non-extrusive extraction protocol, Nance and Goshgarian arches without Class II elastics, is a reasonable template (Table I). Even so, it produced no measurable vertical advantage (Table III).

**Examiner question 6. The mother asks: "Won't the jaw close when the back teeth move forward?"**
Model answer:
- That is the wedge-effect idea.
- In the review, no group changed its mandibular-plane angle by more than 1.2°, except one nonextraction group whose angle closed by 2° (pp 181 and 184).
- The likely reason is that the molars slide forward along the occlusal plane, keeping their contact, rather than along the jaw planes into the wider part of the wedge (Gkantidis, cited p 184). Face height follows how tall the back teeth are, not how far forward they sit (Proffit Ch 5).
- Studies comparing first- and second-premolar extraction found no decrease in vertical dimension either (Kim 2005; Al-Nimri 2006; literature, VERIFIED abstracts).

**Examiner question 7. How strong is this evidence?**
Model answer:
- Fourteen retrospective cohorts, at moderate to critical risk of bias, synthesised by vote counting.
- Single studies could only detect about 1.6 to 2.6° (derived). The exploratory pool is −0.32° (−0.68 to 0.04), I² = 0 % (derived).
- GRADE-style certainty is low to very low. Its consistency is what makes it believable.
- Later studies agree:
  - Rüedi 2024: hyperdivergent children with baseline SN-ML 40.2°, close to her own angle; difference 0.07°, 95 % CI −0.90 to 1.01.
  - Ferrillo 2026: MMPA −0.40°, −1.75 to 0.96.
  - Alrubaie 2026: prospective CBCT, no LAFH difference (literature, VERIFIED abstracts).

**Examiner question 8. Tell the mother in one or two sentences.**
Model answer: "We may need to remove four teeth to make room and bring her front teeth back so her lips can close, but removing teeth will not make her face shorter. Her face height depends on her growth and on how we control her back teeth, and we plan for that separately."

**Paper inconsistencies an examiner may raise.** Most are visible in the paper itself; items 7, 11 and 18 also draw on the primary studies' abstracts. None changes the main finding. Raise them as "typo, or a different measure?".
1. Paquette's between-group FMA difference is 1.7° in the Results (p 178) and 2.3° in Table III (+0.3 vs −2.0°). The Discussion's "2°" (p 181) is the within-group nonextraction change.
2. The Results say three studies included "mainly nongrowing" participants and that "most studies included growing patients" (p 178). The Discussion says "only nongrowing" and "most included both growing and nongrowing" (p 185).
3. "Regardless of age, sex, malocclusion type, or vertical growth pattern" (p 185) goes beyond the data. Sex differences between studies were set aside (p 177), there were no subgroup analyses, Class III was not reported separately, no extraction group was hypodivergent, and no sample had an open bite.
4. "No study clearly confirmed the expected effect" (p 181), yet Porto (P = .020) and Kumari (P < .05) are significant in the expected direction. The authors discount them because FMA did not agree.
5. Quality of evidence is called "moderate to low" without a stated certainty method (no GRADE).
6. The flow chart's other-sources box gives n = 29 and also "19 studies identified after hand searching". 231 + 29 = 260 screened (derived). The Results text mentions only the 927 database records.
7. Kumari: Table II notes "only posttreatment values compared", while Table III reports T1 − T0 changes with P < .05. Kumari and Fida's own abstract reports no significant vertical difference and calls the design cross-sectional (literature, VERIFIED).
8. Sivakumar FMA: +0.5 ± 1.7 vs +0.1 ± 2.0° with 31 and 29 patients gives P ≈ .41, not the printed .985 (derived).
9. Kirschneck is "all patterns" in Table I and the Results (p 178), but "hyperdivergent patients" in the Table III effect column.
10. ROBINS-I was applied unevenly. Sivakumar is critical on confounding for sex and treatment-time differences, while Paquette, with a comparable sex imbalance, is moderate. Staggers is critical overall with no domain rated critical, through the "no information" rule (p 178).
11. Ref 19 (Staggers 1994, an included first-premolar study) is cited on p 184 as a molar-extraction study. Staggers 1990 compared second-molar with first-premolar extraction (literature, VERIFIED abstract; the slip is an inference).
12. The Methods planned mean differences, SDs and a P = 0.0004 imputation (p 176). No mean difference is computed; Table III gives group mean changes (with SDs where available) and P values only.
13. The table footnotes expand "Class II, 1" as "Subdivision 1" where division 1 is meant. Table III also prints "4.5′" for Gkantidis's ANS-Me SD and "PP-GoME".
14. "Luppanapornlap" in the text and tables; "Luppanapornlarp" in ref 16.
15. The female-only list on p 185 (refs 13, 22 and 24) leaves out Hosseinzadeh-Nik, whose groups were 18 and 18 girls (Table I).
16. Porto lists N-S-Gn as an outcome in Table I, but Table III does not report it.
17. Hosseinzadeh-Nik's two groups have identical ages, 16.4 ± 0.4 y (Table I). This was not checked against the original paper (UNVERIFIED).
18. The p 181 sentence that refs 17 and 18 claimed an extraction effect their data did not support sits beside Sivakumar's own conclusion that extracting only to reduce the mandibular plane angle might not be justified, which agrees with this review (literature, VERIFIED abstract).
