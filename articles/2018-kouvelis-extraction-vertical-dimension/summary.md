# Four-premolar extraction and the vertical dimension of the face: a systematic review (Kouvelis et al 2018)

Kouvelis G, Dritsas K, Doulis I, Kloukos D, Gkantidis N. Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop 2018;154(2):175-87. doi:10.1016/j.ajodo.2018.03.007.

## Bottom line

Across 14 retrospective studies and 857 patients, four-premolar extraction showed no specific effect on the skeletal vertical dimension compared with nonextraction treatment. Ten studies found no difference and the four significant results split two each way, so extracting to reduce or control the vertical is not evidence-based (abstract; p 185).

## What they did

- Systematic review, protocol not registered: MEDLINE (to 2 August 2017), EMBASE, the Cochrane Oral Health Group register, CENTRAL and grey literature, no language limit (p 176).
- Eligible: fixed appliances with 4 premolar extractions vs a treated nonextraction control, skeletal vertical outcomes on radiographs; studies under 10 patients excluded (p 176).
- 927 database records + 29 from other sources → 34 full texts → 14 studies, all retrospective; none pooled (Figure).
- ROBINS-I risk of bias: 5 moderate, 5 serious, 3 critical, 1 split serious/critical (Table II).

## What they found

- No difference in 10 of 14 studies (Table III). The 4 significant results came from 2 of the 4 normodivergent and 2 of the 6 hyperdivergent studies (p 178).
- In the "wedge" direction: Porto SN-GoGn −0.9 vs +0.8° (P = 0.020; FMA P = 0.240); Kumari N-Me +1.5 vs +5.5 mm (P < 0.05, no SDs) (Table III).
- In the opposite direction: Sivakumar N-Me +2.3 vs +0.9 mm (P = 0.033); Paquette FMA +0.3 vs −2.0° (P < 0.010) (Table III).
- No group's mean angular change exceeded 1.2°, except Paquette's nonextraction −2.0° (pp 181, 184).
- Gkantidis gave the extraction group non-extrusive and the nonextraction group extrusive mechanics, and still found SN-GoGn −0.1 vs +0.1° (Tables I and III).
- Pooling the 12 mandibular-plane comparisons with SDs gives Ex − Nonex −0.32° (95% CI −0.68 to 0.04) (derived; the review pooled nothing).

## Why it matters

Extraction to control the vertical is a long-held strategy for long-face patients (pp 175, 181). The review concludes that an extraction protocol to reduce or control the vertical is not justified (p 185). The extraction decision then rests on crowding, incisor position and profile (Proffit Ch 7), and vertical control on limiting posterior eruption (Proffit Ch 14) (both VERIFIED, study pages). It fits the hub's `2005-stephens-long-term-profile-extraction-nonextraction`, where extraction and nonextraction profiles also aged alike after treatment.

## One-line caveat

The evidence is retrospective and confounded by indication (the extraction groups started steeper in 10 of 13 comparisons, derived); molar movement and extrusion are not reported in the review; open-bite, severe high-angle, hypodivergent-extraction and adult patients are barely represented.
