## Bottom line

- TMD is the main cause of non-dental pain in the orofacial region. Its cause is poorly understood and multifactorial, and occlusion is often blamed (abstract).
- TMD signs, especially joint sounds, are common in children and adolescents and rise between 15 and 45 years. Treatment lasts about 2 years, so signs can appear during or after it and the orthodontist gets blamed, sometimes in court (abstract).
- The review asks whether claims that orthodontics causes or cures TMD rest on good evidence (abstract). Its own verdict is UNVERIFIED (full text not read).
- The author's later position: no orthodontic approach has been proven to cause, cure or prevent TMD (2025 Sheldon Friel lecture, PMC12573241, VERIFIED).

## What they did

- A narrative critical review of malocclusion, orthodontic treatment and TMD (abstract). Search strategy and grading not stated in the abstract (UNVERIFIED).

## What the evidence says

| Question | Answer | Source and status |
|---|---|---|
| Is occlusion a major cause? | No. Open bite, unilateral crossbite, overjet >6-7 mm, ≥5-6 missing posterior teeth and RCP-ICP slides >2 mm raised risk, but most variation was not occlusal. Open bite in osteoarthrosis was a result, not a cause | Pullinger 1993, cited (ref 60); PubMed abstract VERIFIED |
| Do occlusal interferences cause TMD? | Healthy women adapted (masseter activity fell, no TMD in 8 days). Women with a TMD history got more signs over 2 weeks with a true interference | Michelotti 2005 and Le Bell 2002, cited (refs 30-31); abstracts VERIFIED |
| Does orthodontics cause TMD? | No. 20-year cohorts found no link; a meta-analysis of 31 studies found no rise in prevalence | Macfarlane 2009 (ref 92), Egermark 2003 (ref 61), Kim 2002 (ref 89); abstracts VERIFIED |
| Does it prevent TMD? | No evidence | Macfarlane 2009 conclusion; VERIFIED |
| Does it cure TMD? | Not shown. Short-term gains fit placebo and non-specific effects | 2018 interview (PMC6018446), VERIFIED |
| Does TMD cause malocclusion? | Yes, rarely: condylar hyperplasia, osteochondroma, rheumatoid arthritis | 2018 interview naming this section, VERIFIED |

## Think about

**Does orthodontic treatment cause, prevent or cure TMD?** None of the three, on current evidence.
- Cause: no. Macfarlane 2009 found no link with new or persistent TMD; the only predictors at 30-31 years were female sex (OR 3.0) and TMD in adolescence (OR 4.5). Egermark 2003 found no difference between treated and untreated people at 20 years (VERIFIED).
- Prevent: no. Macfarlane 2009 concludes orthodontics neither causes nor prevents TMD (VERIFIED).
- Cure: not shown. TMD pain responds to many treatments, placebo included, so improvement during treatment does not prove an occlusal cause (2018 interview, VERIFIED).
- Why the question keeps arising: onset falls in the treatment years by timing alone (abstract).

**What should be done if TMD signs appear during treatment?** The author's protocol (2018 interview, VERIFIED; 2010 wording UNVERIFIED):
1. Evaluate and make a differential diagnosis.
2. Pause active treatment (elastics, activations) to remove aggravating factors.
3. Give conservative care: medication, counselling, behavioural therapy, home exercises, physical therapy, and a splint if needed.
4. Once pain-free, continue as planned or modify the plan.
- Refer to a TMD specialist if there is no response (2025 flowchart, VERIFIED).
- Before treatment: screen (3Q/TMD), record every finding, and do not start while facial pain persists (Fernández-González 2015 citing this review, VERIFIED attribution).

## Likely course and quiz questions

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| Age band with increased TMD prevalence? | 15 to 45 years (abstract) |
| Is TMD in an orthodontic patient caused by the braces? | Usually a timing coincidence; no causal link shown |
| Which occlusal features carry some risk? | Pullinger's five: open bite, unilateral crossbite, overjet >6-7 mm, ≥5-6 missing posterior teeth, slide >2 mm |
| Who adapts worse to an occlusal change? | People with a TMD history or high parafunction |
| Pain at the first visit? | Treat TMD first; start orthodontics once pain is controlled |
| Pain mid-treatment? | Diagnose, pause, conservative care, resume or modify |

## One-line caveat

A narrative review whose evidence is mainly observational, with mixed diagnostic criteria before DC/TMD; it shows no detectable harm or benefit rather than proof of none (interpretation).
