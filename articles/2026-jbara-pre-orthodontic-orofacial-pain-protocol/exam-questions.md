# Jbara 2026 · Exam questions

Source: Jbara J, Baghdadi ZD. Proposed protocol for orofacial pain assessment prior to orthodontic treatment: an expert-informed framework. Adolescents 2026;6(1):3. doi:10.3390/adolescents6010003. Expert-informed proposal; no patient data.

> **Access: full text read** (MDPI, open access). Every answer cites the paper's section. DC/TMD figures were checked this run on PMC4478082 (Schiffman 2014).

Format: Q1 to Q8 are single-best-answer MCQs, Q9 to Q11 short answer, and Q12 a case-based oral defence. Tags: R = recall, A = application, N = analysis.

## Part 1. Multiple-choice questions

### Q1 (R). The risk tiers

In the Jbara and Baghdadi pre-orthodontic TMD score, which total places a patient in the high-risk tier?

A. ≥4 points
B. ≥5 points
C. ≥7 points
D. ≥10 points
E. ≥12 points

**Answer:** C
**Rationale:** Low is 0–3, moderate 4–6, high ≥7 (Section 4.2).

### Q2 (A). The moderate-risk patient

A 15-year-old scores 5 on the framework. There is no bruxism and no psychosocial concern. What does the framework recommend?

A. Start orthodontics with no special follow-up.
B. Start orthodontics, review every 6 months, and refer to an orofacial pain specialist.
C. Defer orthodontics until the TMD is fully managed.
D. Order MRI of both joints before any decision.
E. Equilibrate the occlusion before bonding.

**Answer:** B
**Rationale:**
- Moderate risk (4–6) means proceed with 6-monthly follow-up (Section 4.2).
- Moderate- and high-risk patients should be referred to an OFP specialist (Section 5.2.1).
- C is the high-risk pathway (≥7). D and E are not part of the protocol.

### Q3 (R). The opening cut-off

Which maximal interincisal opening does the framework use to flag disc displacement without reduction?

A. < 30 mm
B. < 35 mm
C. < 38 mm
D. < 40 mm
E. < 45 mm

**Answer:** C
**Rationale:**
- The clinician records maximal interincisal opening, flag < 38 mm (Section 4.1); the same cut-off is used for trismus (Section 6).
- D is the DC/TMD rule: assisted opening, including vertical incisal overlap, < 40 mm for DDwoR with limited opening (Schiffman 2014, VERIFIED on PMC4478082). The paper cites DC/TMD for its 38 mm value.

### Q4 (R). How the tool was built

How was the questionnaire developed?

A. A three-round Delphi with a convened international panel.
B. A systematic review with meta-analysis of diagnostic accuracy.
C. Synthesis of DC/TMD, a targeted narrative review and clinical expertise, without a formal consensus process.
D. A prospective cohort of orthodontic patients followed through treatment.
E. A randomised trial of screened against unscreened patients.

**Answer:** C
**Rationale:** The authors used DC/TMD, a non-systematic review of PubMed and Google Scholar (English only) and clinical expertise. They state no Delphi, no consensus thresholds and no panel were used (Sections 3.1, 3.2).

### Q5 (A). Scoring the DJD module

A 19-year-old has crepitus, condylar flattening on CBCT, preauricular pain on chewing rated 6/10, and a new posterior open bite. Using the DJD module, what is the score and the action?

A. 3: proceed with a 6-month OFP review
B. 4: proceed without concern
C. 5: manage the DJD before orthodontics
D. 5: proceed with 6-monthly monitoring
E. 6: high risk on the total score

**Answer:** C
**Rationale:**
- Crepitus 1 + imaging change 1 + pain 5–10/10 = 2 + occlusal change 1 = 5, the module maximum (Section 4.4.3).
- 3–5 means manage the DJD first (Section 4.4.3).
- E is impossible: the module range is 0–5.
- Note: the module bands overlap (0–2, 2–3, 3–5), but a score of 5 is unambiguous.

### Q6 (N). Bruxism

A 16-year-old scores 5 (moderate). She reports daily clenching, has marked tooth wear and is under heavy exam stress. How does the framework treat these findings?

A. Add 2 points for bruxism, which makes her high risk.
B. Ignore them, because only scored items count.
C. They are not scored, but she may be managed as functionally higher risk: closer review, earlier OFP referral, or TMD care before starting.
D. Bruxism is an absolute contraindication to orthodontics.
E. Add Axis II points from a separate psychosocial scale.

**Answer:** C
**Rationale:**
- Bruxism and Axis II stress are contextual modifiers, not scored items (Sections 5.2.2, 5.2.3).
- A moderate-risk patient with either may be handled as higher risk (Sections 5.2.2, 5.2.3).
- Modifiers are documented but not yet quantified (Section 5.2.3), so A and E are wrong.

### Q7 (N). The main weakness

What is the main limitation the authors acknowledge?

A. Small sample size with wide confidence intervals.
B. High drop-out during follow-up.
C. No sensitivity, specificity, accuracy or reproducibility data, and no pilot testing.
D. Industry funding.
E. The tool was tested only in adults over 45.

**Answer:** C
**Rationale:**
- Section 7 states that none of these data exist yet and that the questionnaire has not been piloted.
- A and B do not apply: there was no sample at all (Data Availability Statement).
- D: no external funding. E: the target group is adolescents and young adults (Section 3.1).

### Q8 (A). The arthralgia module

A 17-year-old has a TMJ that is tender to palpation. There is no history of trauma. According to the arthralgia/capsulitis module, what is the score and the action?

A. 0: proceed without concern
B. 1: treat the arthralgia before orthodontics, then follow up during treatment
C. 1: proceed, review in 12 months
D. 2: defer orthodontics for one year
E. 2: refer for TMJ arthroscopy

**Answer:** B
**Rationale:**
- Palpation tenderness 1, trauma 0, range 0–2 (Section 4.4.3).
- Any score of 1–2 means address the arthralgia before orthodontics, with follow-up during treatment (Section 4.4.3).
- Point of discussion: one point here means treat first, yet a total of 0–3 is low risk (Section 4.2). The paper does not explain how the module and the total combine.

## Part 2. Short answer

### Q9 (R). Three pathways

Name the three pathways and the total score for each.

**Model answer:**
- 0–3, low risk: proceed with orthodontics without concern.
- 4–6, moderate risk: proceed, with follow-up every 6 months (and OFP referral, Section 5.2.1).
- ≥7, high risk: manage the TMD before orthodontics.
(Sections 3.4, 4.2)

### Q10 (R). Pterygoid trismus after a MAD

How does the framework score lateral pterygoid trismus after a mandibular advancement device for sleep apnoea, and what follows?

**Model answer:**
- MAD use for OSA = 1 point; no posterior tooth contact in centric occlusion = 1 point (Section 4.4.2).
- 0 = proceed. 1–2 = treat the trismus first and monitor throughout orthodontics (Section 4.4.2).
- The authors count such patients as high risk (Section 4.4.1).

### Q11 (N). Validation

List the steps the authors propose to validate the framework.

**Model answer:**
1. Pilot testing for feasibility, clarity and inter-examiner reliability.
2. Reliability on repeated administration.
3. Criterion validity against a full DC/TMD examination and imaging.
4. Prospective multi-centre studies of predictive validity: does baseline risk predict TMD onset or flare during treatment?
(Sections 5.5, 8.1, 8.2)

## Part 3. Case-based oral defence

### Q12 (N). The referred adolescent

A 16-year-old with a Class II division 1 malocclusion is referred for fixed appliances. She wore a mandibular advancement device for sleep apnoea for a year. Her back teeth no longer meet on closing. She also has temple headaches on chewing and mild masseter tenderness. Maximal interincisal opening is 36 mm.

Prompts: How would you assess her with the Jbara framework? What would you do? What are the limits of the score you used?

**Model answer:**
- **Flags.** MAD use 1 + no posterior contact 1 = 2 on the trismus module: treat first, monitor throughout (Section 4.4.2). Opening of 36 mm is below the 38 mm flag (Sections 4.1, 6). Temple headache and masseter tenderness are scored items (Section 4.1).
- **Decision.** Do not start orthodontics yet. The trismus module and the authors' MAD case both point to treating first (Sections 4.4.1, 4.4.2).
- **Referral.** OFP specialist for diagnosis and conservative care (Section 5.2.1). Physical therapist for trismus and myofascial pain (Section 5.2.1). Her physician for the sleep-related breathing disorder (Section 5.2.1).
- **Document.** Record the baseline findings and counsel her and her parents before consent (Sections 1, 5.4).
- **Limits.** The score is not validated: no accuracy or reliability data (Section 7). Most item weights are not printed, so her total cannot be computed exactly (derived). The 38 mm flag differs from the DC/TMD 40 mm assisted-opening rule (Section 4.1; Schiffman 2014, VERIFIED). Final diagnosis needs a DC/TMD examination, not this screen (Section 5.2.1).
- **Reassess.** Rescreen once symptoms settle, then plan orthodontics (Section 4.3, Case 3 logic).
