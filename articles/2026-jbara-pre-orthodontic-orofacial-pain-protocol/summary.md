# Jbara 2026 · Proposed protocol for orofacial pain assessment prior to orthodontic treatment: an expert-informed framework

Jbara J, Baghdadi ZD. Proposed protocol for orofacial pain assessment prior to orthodontic treatment: an expert-informed framework. Adolescents 2026;6(1):3. doi:10.3390/adolescents6010003

> **Access: full text read** (open access, MDPI). Course slot: 15:00, "TMD & orthodontic treatment: pre-orthodontic screening".

## Bottom line

- A proposal, not a study. Two clinicians suggest a pre-orthodontic TMD questionnaire with weighted points and three risk tiers (Abstract; Sections 3, 4).
- Total score: **0–3 low, 4–6 moderate, ≥7 high** (Section 4.2).
- Low: start orthodontics. Moderate: start, review every 6 months, refer to an orofacial pain (OFP) specialist. High: treat the TMD first (Sections 4.2, 5.2.1).
- Opening flag: maximal interincisal opening **< 38 mm** (Section 4.1). DC/TMD uses < 40 mm assisted opening (Schiffman 2014, VERIFIED on PMC4478082).
- Not validated. No patients, no pilot, no sensitivity, specificity or reliability data (Section 7).
- The authors call the legal and consistency benefits theoretical, not shown (Sections 5.4, 7).

## What they did

- Built from three inputs: DC/TMD criteria, a targeted literature review, and clinical expertise (Section 3.1).
- Review: PubMed/MEDLINE and Google Scholar, English only, deliberately not systematic (Section 3.2).
- No Delphi, no consensus thresholds, no expert panel (Section 3.1).
- Steps: choose diagnostic categories, write the questions, give each a weighted score (Section 3.2).
- Aimed at adolescents and young adults, when orthodontics starts and pain-related TMD peaks (Sections 2, 3.1).
- Said to take under 10 min at the first visit (Section 1).

## What goes into the score

| Category | Screening item (paraphrased) | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Disc displacement with reduction | Clicking or popping | 4.1 |
| Disc displacement without reduction | Limited opening; clinician measures interincisal opening, flag < 38 mm | 4.1 |
| Degenerative joint disease | Crepitus or grinding on movement | 4.1 |
| DD with intermittent locking | Listed; no sample question | 3.3 |
| Myalgia and myofascial pain (grouped) | Muscle pain on function, or referred pain on pressure | 4.1 |
| Arthralgia/capsulitis | TMJ tenderness worse with function or after trauma | 4.1 |
| Headache attributed to TMD | Temple headache brought on by jaw use | 4.1 |
| Trismus | Opening restricted with pain or muscle guarding | 4.1 |

- Myalgia and myofascial pain are grouped because separating them needs OFP training (Section 4.1).
- Points are printed for three modules only (MAD trismus, DJD, arthralgia). The weights of the other items are not given, so the case scores below cannot be recomputed (Sections 4.1–4.4, derived).

## The three tiers

| Tier | Total | Action | Section |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 0–3 | Proceed without concern | 4.2 |
| Moderate | 4–6 | Proceed; follow-up every 6 months; refer to OFP specialist | 4.2, 5.2.1 |
| High | ≥7 | Manage TMD before orthodontics; refer to OFP specialist | 4.2, 5.2.1 |

## Not scored: modifiers

- **Bruxism** is not an item. A moderate-risk bruxer may be handled as higher risk: closer review, earlier referral, or TMD care first (Section 5.2.2).
- **Axis II stress** works the same way: closer follow-up, referral, or deferral until stable (Section 5.2.3).
- Modifiers are recorded but not yet quantified (Section 5.2.3).

## Why it matters

- The paper's context: TMD affects 10–15% of people, peaks at 15–45 years, and orthodontics lasts 18–24 months (Section 2).
- A 2025 meta-analysis it cites found TMD in 43% of people with malocclusion (Section 2.1, ref 14).
- It does not say orthodontics causes TMD. It treats orthodontics as a possible aggravating factor, not a cause (Section 2.1).
- Undocumented baseline TMD leaves the orthodontist exposed. It cites a Michigan $850,000 judgment (Sections 2.2, 5.4).
- Referral targets: OFP specialist, oral surgeon, physician, pain psychologist, physical therapist (Section 5.2.1).

## Paper against itself

1. The DJD bands overlap (0–2, 2–3, 3–5), so a score of 2 or 3 fits two bands (Section 4.4.3).
2. One module point (a tender TMJ, or MAD use) means treat first, yet 0–3 on the total is low risk. How module scores join the total is not stated (Sections 4.2, 4.4, derived).
3. Case 1 reports no aggravation, which is an outcome; Section 7 says the cases are not outcome data.
4. Sections 1 and 10 say the tool reduces complications and litigation; Sections 5.4 and 7 call this theoretical.
5. The 38 mm cut-off is cited to DC/TMD, which uses 40 mm assisted opening including incisal overlap (Section 4.1; Schiffman 2014, VERIFIED).

## Think about: how the score sorts patients, and its limits

**How it sorts.**
- Item points are added up (Section 4.2).
- 0–3 = low: proceed. 4–6 = moderate: proceed, 6-monthly review, OFP referral. ≥7 = high: treat the TMD first, then reassess (Sections 4.2, 4.3, 5.2.1).
- Three modules (MAD trismus, DJD, arthralgia) carry their own cut-offs. Any positive arthralgia or trismus score, or DJD 3–5, means treat first (Section 4.4).
- Bruxism and psychosocial stress add no points but can move a moderate patient up a tier (Sections 5.2.2, 5.2.3).

**Limits of an unvalidated framework.**
- No accuracy data: no sensitivity, specificity or reproducibility, and no pilot (Section 7).
- The cut-offs (3/4 and 6/7) and weights are expert choices, not fitted to outcomes (Sections 3.2, 7).
- Not a consensus: no Delphi, no panel (Section 3.1).
- Most weights are not printed, so two clinicians may not reach the same score (derived).
- Internal clashes: overlapping DJD bands; module rules against total tiers (Section 4.4).
- The 38 mm flag departs from the DC/TMD 40 mm rule and its measuring method (Section 4.1).
- TMD fluctuates on its own, so one baseline score may mislead (Sections 2.1, 6).
- Untested core claim: that a high score predicts flare-ups during treatment (Section 5.5).
- Legal protection is assumed, not measured (Sections 5.4, 7).
- Planned fix: pilot and inter-examiner reliability, then criterion validity against full DC/TMD and imaging, then multi-centre predictive studies (Sections 5.5, 8).
