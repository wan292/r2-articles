# Clinical implications — Khaleel & Al-Nimri 2026

Khaleel R, Al-Nimri K. Assessment of three different techniques in correcting deep overbite: a prospective clinical study. Angle Orthod 2026;96(5):528-535. Prospective, non-randomised, single operator (JUST, Irbid), 81 enrolled / 80 completed, nominally 27 per group. Every patient had anterior bite turbos (ABTs; formed in a silicone mould, Mini Mold, Aditek, and bonded with Transbond, so composite by implication, though the paper does not name the material) bonded to the palatal of the upper centrals at the bonding visit, plus an upper 0.014 NiTi. Group I added a lower 0.016 × 0.022 NiTi reverse-curve-of-Spee (RCOS) wire cinched distal to the 7s; Group II had 0.014 NiTi in both arches plus bilateral posterior box elastics; Group III had 0.014 NiTi in both arches only. Endpoint: overbite < 30 % with posterior Shimstock contact, checked every 14 days. Table values below were read from the page images; where the text and a table disagree, both are given.

Attribution on this page: "the paper says" = printed in Khaleel 2026; "the literature says" = other studies checked in the research pass; "my read" = my interpretation.

## What this changes or confirms at the chair

1. **Bite turbos alone take about three months; adding a lower auxiliary cuts that to six to eight weeks.** The paper says (Table 2): ABT alone 94.15 ± 7.08 d, ABT + RCOS 55.72 ± 10.52 d, ABT + box elastics 44.42 ± 10.88 d; Tukey differences versus turbos alone 38.7 d (95 % CI 28.57-48.78) and 50.0 d (40.01-59.95), both P < .001. The Results text prints 94.4 ± 7.1 d and 55.7 ± 20.5 d for the same two means, so quote the table but treat the Group I SD as unsettled. Caveat: allocation was by operator judgement (incisor alignment, inclination, cooperation), and the turbos-alone arm was the oldest (17.35 vs 15.82 / 16.00 y) and most crowded (1.81 vs 1.25 mm, Table 1); the audit in our research pass recomputed the Table 1 ANOVAs from the printed means and SDs: crowding (P ≈ .008–.009) and SNA (≈ .012–.026) fail at any group size; age and overbite come out at ≈ .04 with 27 per arm but match the printed .067/.068 with 29/31/20, which is itself evidence the arms were unequal (see critical appraisal). All of that slows the turbos-alone arm, so the 40-50 d gap is probably inflated. The literature agrees on the anchor: Al-Zoubi 2022 (same unit, randomised, T1 after alignment) had turbos alone at 3.15 ± 0.93 months (~96 d) and RCOS alone at 4.85 ± 1.56 months (~148 d). My read across the two bite-turbo papers from this unit: the combination is what is fast; an RCOS on its own was the slowest of the arms tested in those two studies. Confirms and extends.

2. **Elastics versus RCOS is not a meaningful speed contest.** The paper says 11.3 d in favour of elastics (95 % CI 2.32-20.29, P = .010, Table 2). Caveat: patients were seen every 14 d, so the difference is below the resolution of the measurement; the elastics arm was pre-selected for cooperation; and if the text SD (20.5 d) is the right one, the RCOS arm was the most variable of the three (my arithmetic: coefficient of variation about 37 % versus 24 % for elastics and 8 % for turbos alone). My read: choose between these two on incisor position, compliance and vertical pattern, not on days.

3. **A 0.016 × 0.022 NiTi RCOS as the first lower wire flares the lower incisors about 7.5°, even in pre-aligned arches with the wire cinched.** The paper says LI-CA +7.46 ± 2.52° vs +3.46° (II) and +1.55° (III) (Table 3; P < .001 in the Results text, since Tables 3 and 4 print no P values); incisor tip 1.01 mm forward vs 0.61 mm in Group II (Table 4; P = .027 in the text); L6 +5.99° and L7 +8.94° distal tipping and L4 +4.44° mesial tipping (Table 3), with mean crowding of only 1.25 mm. The literature says the same wire gave +5.74° (Al-Zoubi 2022) and +6.00° (Shakhtour 2025) without turbos, and Proffit (Ch 15) lists a rectangular reverse-curve wire in the lower arch as the classic torque error. Confirms. Caveat: the paper's conclusion is printed as "measurably lower incisor proclination", which can be read backwards; the finding is more proclination, not less.

4. **Do not pick the RCOS to get "true intrusion".** The paper says Point I (0.66 of incisor length from the tip) moved 0.99 (I), 0.71 (II) and 0.86 (III) mm, SDs 0.38-0.44 (Table 4). No between-group test on Point I is printed and no sign convention is stated (the incisal tip is −2.46 and Point I +0.99 on the same tooth). Caveat: the abstract and conclusions credit the RCOS with 1 mm of true intrusion, but the turbos-only arm on a round 0.014 NiTi registered 0.86 mm. The literature is split: Al-Zoubi 2022 found 1.01 vs 0.28 mm (RCOS vs turbos, P = .001); Shakhtour 2025 found ~1 mm with every RCOS wire tested. My read: whatever Point I is registering here, the RCOS-specific share is 0.1-0.3 mm and untested; the incisal-edge change that does differ between groups (−2.46 vs −1.02 / −1.25 mm, P < .001) is mostly tipping.

5. **Box elastics are less incisor-flaring than an RCOS, not incisor-neutral, and they are the only option that extrudes the upper posterior teeth.** The paper says Group II LI-CA +3.46 ± 3.89° and lower incisor tip −1.02 ± 0.53 mm (Tables 3-4) and calls these "not significant", but Tables 3 and 4 print no P values, and from the printed SDs with n ≈ 27 both changes would be significant. Upper premolar and molar cusps extruded 0.28-0.42 mm in Group II versus 0.07-0.15 mm in the other groups (Table 4), which the paper reports as significant. Caveat: the Results text says lower extrusion ranged "0.6 mm at the premolars to 1.5 mm at the molars", but Table 4 shows 0.90-1.19 mm with the premolars extruding more than the molars; use the table.

6. **All three open the mandibular plane 0.7-1.8° within 6-14 weeks; elastics most, turbos alone least; the RCOS arm is not distinguishable from either.** The paper says (Table 5): Mn-Mx +1.35 / +1.84 / +0.69°, FH-Mn +0.97 / +1.31 / +0.32°, LFH +0.97 / +1.56 / +0.41 %; only II vs III differ (Mn-Mx P = .011, FH-Mn P = .026). LFH shows no between-group difference as printed, but the LFH standard errors (0.89-1.00) do not fit the printed SDs (0.43-0.84), so treat that block as unreliable. Caveat: no untreated control, mean age ~16 y despite the paper's "nongrowing" label, and 0.3-0.7° is within tracing error. The literature says: Rozzi 2017 found no skeletal change with continuous archwires in any facial type; Elbarnashawy 2023 found SN-MP +1.9° with turbos in growing children versus −0.9° in untreated controls; Proffit accepts posterior elongation in a short or average face while growth remains. My read: in the faces this paper admitted, 1-2° is acceptable and sometimes wanted; there are no data for long faces.

7. **The amount of correction is the same whichever you choose, because the endpoint is a threshold.** The paper says overbite fell 3.92 ± 0.90 / 3.65 ± 0.92 / 3.57 ± 0.94 mm from 5.51 / 5.33 / 5.13 mm (Results text; Table 1); no test of the difference is printed. Caveat: correction stopped at < 30 % overlap, not at a finished overbite, so finishing, overcorrection and retention are outside the study. Turbo reliability: 1 of 81 patients lost both turbos and was excluded (1.2 %).

## Practical protocol notes

**Bite turbo placement and product**
- The paper says: standard size and shape, formed in a silicone mould (Mini Mold Bite Turbo, Aditek, Brazil), bonded with Transbond (3M Unitek) to the palatal surfaces of both upper central incisors at the same visit as the brackets. Turbo material and brand, turbo height and amount of posterior disocclusion are not stated.
- Reliability: 1 of 81 lost both turbos (the one dropout). The literature says Fahim 2025 (n = 60, Mini-Mold 5 mm) had zero debonds at 1 month; eating difficulty in 27 % and speech difficulty in 20 % at 1 week, resolved by 1 month.
- My read: bond them before the first wire goes in, as the paper did, so posterior disocclusion starts on day one and the duration clock is comparable.

**Brackets and wire sequence**
- 0.022 × 0.028 Roth (GC LEGEND). Upper 6-6, lower 7-7. Bonding the lower second molars is part of the RCOS protocol: the wire is cinched distal to them and they took the largest tip-back (L7 +8.94°).
- Upper arch: 0.014 NiTi in every group.
- Lower arch, Group I: 0.016 × 0.022 NiTi RCOS (GC Orthodontics) as the first and only wire reported, cinched distal to the 7s. The sample was restricted to well-aligned or ≤ 3 mm crowded incisors so this wire could be placed at bonding.
- Lower arch, Groups II and III: 0.014 NiTi.
- Later wires, turbo removal and the finishing sequence are not reported; T2 was taken "after the leveling stage".
- My read, following Proffit: a rectangular reverse-curve wire in the lower arch delivers labial crown torque to the incisors; check inclination at every visit, and do not place it in an arch that is not yet aligned.

**Elastics: size, force and pattern**
- The paper says: 3/16-inch, medium force (3M Unitek), bilateral box from upper premolars and molars to lower premolars and molars, worn with 0.014 NiTi in both arches.
- Not reported: force in grams, hours of wear per day, replacement frequency, hooking points. Use the manufacturer's rating for the batch you stock; the paper cannot tell you. For scale, Proffit's optimum force for extrusion is 35-60 g per tooth (Table 8.3).
- Expected effect (Table 4): lower posterior extrusion 0.90-1.19 mm, upper posterior extrusion 0.28-0.42 mm.
- My read: box elastics on 0.014 NiTi in both arches leave the elastics almost no wire stiffness to work against, which is why the upper posteriors extruded too. Proffit uses posterior box elastics on light round wires for settling "for only a few weeks at most"; this protocol runs about that long (mean 44 d) but as the main bite-opening engine.

**Review interval and endpoint check**
- Every 14 days.
- Corrected = overbite < 30 % of the lower incisor crown AND upper-lower posterior contact confirmed with Shimstock foil. The paper prints "8 mm"; Shimstock is 8 µm.
- From the means (my arithmetic): elastics about 3 fortnightly visits, RCOS about 4, turbos alone about 7.
- The endpoint is a threshold, not the finished overbite; plan finishing and any overcorrection separately. The operator who assigned the groups also judged the endpoint, unblinded.

**When to remove the turbos**
- The paper does not say.
- My read: once Shimstock confirms bilateral posterior contact, the turbos are no longer disoccluding and their bite-opening job is done; whether to keep them through the working wires as a fixed bite plane is a judgement this paper does not inform. The literature says Elbarnashawy 2023 kept turbos for 12.2 ± 3.6 months in growing children, and Proffit (Ch 19) recommends a maxillary bite-plane retainer for years after deep-bite correction.

## What the paper does NOT tell you

- **Stability.** T2 is the end of levelling; there is no debond, retention or follow-up record. The paper cites Rozzi 2019 for stable extrusion in ~20-year-olds, but that stability was in the high-angle subgroup; the low-angle subgroup, which is this paper's phenotype, relapsed through uprighting of flared incisors. Pollard 2012: 1.2-1.4 mm overbite relapse in low- and normal-angle deep bites at 10 years post-retention. Huang 2012 SR (the paper's ref 1): mean overbite 5.3 → 2.6 → 3.4 mm.
- **Comfort, speech, eating, elastic-wear burden.** Not assessed (the authors say so). The literature says: Fahim 2025 gives the first-week figures above; Sotal 2026 (same unit, same turbo + 0.014 NiTi set-up) found grade 2 lower incisor mobility in 74 % with turbos versus 25 % without, with pain peaking within 24 h in both.
- **Root resorption and pulp.** Not measured. Nasrawi 2022 (same department): 0.44-0.88 mm lower incisor apical resorption with rigid reverse-curve wires over 6 months. Mahmoud 2025: a NiTi reverse-curve arch caused more root-volume and labial bone loss than a utility arch or miniscrews. Sangwattanarat 2025: 0.21-0.25 mm lower incisor root-length loss in 6 months under a removable bite plane. Al-Tamimi 2026 and Sotal 2026: pulpal blood flow under turbos largely recovers by 1 month (Al-Tamimi reported the upper incisors still slightly below baseline at 30 d in the turbo group, not significantly different from controls).
- **Growing patients.** Mean ages 15.8 / 16.0 / 17.4 y with SDs of 2-3 y and consent from guardians, yet the Discussion calls the sample "nongrowing". No maturity staging and no untreated control, so vertical change cannot be separated from eruption and growth. Nothing here applies to the mixed dentition; Alsawaf 2023 in 10-year-olds found a fixed bite plane faster and more effective than a utility arch with posterior elastics (7.2 vs 8.2 months; −3.64 vs −2.1 mm), the opposite ordering.
- **Crowding > 3 mm.** Excluded. The paper cannot tell you what an RCOS does after aligning a crowded arch, which is the case in which the wire is most tempting.
- **Aligners.** Not studied. The literature says aligner deep-bite correction is about 35-60 % predictable over months (Husain 2025 SR: bite opening 0.4-3.8 mm, accuracy 33-49 %), and aligner bite ramps do not speed anterior intrusion (Kou 2026: 18.8 % vs 18.4 % accuracy with and without ramps).
- **Elastic force and wear hours, turbo height, per-group n, and which group lost the dropout.** None reported. The Tukey standard errors in Tables 2 and 5 are consistent with about 20 patients analysed in the turbos-alone arm rather than 27 (derived by our audit, not stated in the paper).
- **Whether the turbos-alone arm is an independent sample.** In our check against Al-Zoubi 2022 (PMC8691468), many Group III outcome means and SDs match the 2022 turbos arm to two decimals (for example L6-CA 0.88 ± 4.54°, L7-CA 0.71 ± 3.28°, L5-CA 1.18 ± 2.90°, DC L7 0.86 ± 0.61 mm) despite different n, age, lower wire and turbo material. The paper does not mention any data reuse. Until this is clarified, do not count the turbos-alone result as a second independent confirmation of the 2022 figure.
- **Upper incisor display and smile outcome.** Upper incisor vertical change −0.09 to −0.43 mm; no smile or display data.
- **Total treatment time.** Only time to < 30 % overbite. Al-Zoubi 2022 judged a 1.7-month saving at this stage "questionable" against 24-30 months of total treatment; this paper does not revisit that.
- **Excluded phenotypes.** Class II division 2 with overjet > 6 mm after uprighting, Class III, extraction cases, missing lower molars, adults with periodontal loss, high smile line, long face: all excluded or absent.

## How I would explain the three options to a patient in one sentence each

- **Reverse-curve wire with bite bumps:** "We bond two small bumps behind your top front teeth and fit a curved wire in the lower braces; the bite usually opens in about eight weeks, but the lower front teeth tip forward a little more with this wire, so we check them at every visit."
- **Rubber bands with bite bumps:** "Same bumps, plus small rubber bands at the back on both sides; this is the quickest, about six weeks, but only if the bands are worn as instructed, and the back teeth come down slightly, which lengthens the lower face a little."
- **Bite bumps alone:** "Bumps behind the top front teeth and light wires only; your back teeth settle into contact on their own over about three months, which is the slowest option but tips the lower front teeth the least."
