# Prep sheet — Khaleel & Al-Nimri 2026: deep bite with bite turbos alone, plus a reverse-curve wire, or plus posterior elastics

Khaleel R, Al-Nimri K. Assessment of three different techniques in correcting deep overbite: a prospective clinical study. Angle Orthod. 2026;96(5):528–535. DOI 10.2319/100925-850.1.

Design: prospective, non-randomised, single-operator, operator-allocated | n: 81 enrolled, 80 completed, 27/27/27 nominal | Groups: I turbos+0.016×0.022 NiTi RCOS; II turbos+0.014 NiTi+box elastics; III turbos+0.014 NiTi | Cephalograms at bonding and after levelling.

## 30-second opening

"Everyone had bite turbos; does a reverse-curve wire or box elastics speed bite opening? Yes: elastics 44 days, reverse curve 56, turbos alone 94; an auxiliary halves it. The reverse curve cost 7.5° of incisor flaring and 6–9° of molar tip-back; elastics flared 3.5° but opened the mandibular plane most. The operator chose the arms, partly on cooperation, so I take the halving, not the 11-day ranking."

## Bottom line

Adding a reverse-curve wire or box elastics to bite turbos cut time to a 30% overbite from 94.15 to 55.72 and 44.42 d (Table 2); the price is 7.46° of proclination (reverse curve) or the largest mandibular-plane opening, +1.84° (elastics). The claimed 1 mm of true intrusion is 0.99 mm at Point I against 0.71 and 0.86 mm without a reverse curve, never tested (Table 4).

## Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths
- Prospective; one operator, bracket system and turbo mould; lower arch bonded 7–7.
- Clear endpoint, reviewed every 14 d (monthly in the 2022 RCT, literature).
- Corpus-axis superimposition, Greig's Point I; intraexaminer ICC 0.92–0.98.

Weaknesses
- Operator allocation on alignment and cooperation: Group III oldest (17.35 y), most crowded (1.81 mm, Table 1); Group II compliance-selected.
- Unblinded operator called the endpoint fortnightly, so the 11.3 d gap is inside one visit; no control, no follow-up.
- Standard errors fit arms of about 29/31/20 rather than 27 each, and 14 of 26 turbos-only values match the 2022 RCT's turbo arm (derived; read conflicts.md before raising this).

## What it changes at the chair

- An auxiliary cuts bite opening from three months to 6–8 weeks. Caveat: turbos-alone was oldest and most crowded.
- Choose elastics or reverse curve on incisor position and compliance, not the 11.3 d gap (under one visit). Caveat: compliance-selected arm.
- A rectangular NiTi reverse curve as first wire flares incisors 7.46 ± 2.52° even in aligned arches (crowding 1.25 mm). Caveat: Proffit's textbook torque error.
- Not the reverse curve for intrusion: Point I 0.99 vs 0.71 and 0.86 mm without one, untested. Caveat: the 2022 RCT's turbos arm gave 0.28 mm (literature).
- Elastics open the plane most (+1.84°) and are the only arm with upper posterior extrusion above 0.15 mm (0.28–0.42 mm). Caveat: long faces excluded; extrusion suits short, growing faces (Proffit).

## Three questions to ask

1. "Point I moved 0.99, 0.71 and 0.86 mm, untested between groups; is the millimetre of true intrusion a reverse-curve effect or just turbos on a 0.014 wire?"
*Why it lands: their own marker; Group III had no intrusive mechanics.*

2. "Elastics beat the reverse curve by 11 days, CI 2 to 20, with 14-day reviews and a compliance-selected arm; is that gap real, or do you choose on 3.5° versus 7.5° of flaring?"
*Why it lands: Tables 2 and 3 together; gap under one visit.*

3. "Turbos alone extruded the lower posteriors as much as elastics, 1.30 versus 1.12 mm at the first-molar mesial cusp, yet took twice as long; where does the speed come from?"
*Why it lands: Table 4 read across arms; only Group II extruded upper posteriors beyond 0.15 mm (0.28–0.42 mm).*

## Five questions you will be asked

- **Primary outcome?** Days from bonding to overbite <30% with Shimstock contact: 55.72/44.42/94.15 d; undeclared, unregistered.
- **Baseline balance?** No P<.05 (Table 1), but Group III oldest (17.35 y), most crowded (1.81 mm); Group I ANB 5.14° despite a 5° ceiling.
- **Reverse-curve effects?** LI-CA +7.46°, tip −2.46 mm, 1.01 mm forward, Point I 0.99 mm; L6/L7 +5.99°/+8.94° distal, L4 +4.44° mesial.
- **Who gets elastics?** A compliant, short- or average-faced adolescent with forward incisors: 44.42 d, +3.46°; not long faces, Mn-Mx +1.84°.
- **Evidence level; practice change?** CEBM Level 3. Add an auxiliary when speed matters, chosen on inclination and compliance; claim neither intrusion nor stability.

## Exam radar

### Ten facts most likely to be tested

1. Non-randomised; operator allocated on alignment, inclination, cooperation.
2. Durations (Table 2): 55.72/44.42/94.15 d, all pairs significant; I–II 11.3 d, P=.010.
3. Endpoint: overbite <30% plus Shimstock, fortnightly; reduction 3.92/3.65/3.57 mm from 5.51/5.33/5.13 mm, untested.
4. LI-CA (Table 3): +7.46°/+3.46°/+1.55°, P<.001.
5. Superimposition: corpus axis at Pm (Ricketts), palatal vault (Broadbent); Point I at 0.66 × incisor length from the tip (Greig): 0.99/0.71/0.86 mm, untested.
6. Group I (Table 3): L6 5.99°, L7 8.94° distal; L4 4.44° mesial.
7. Group II (Table 4): lower extrusion 0.90–1.19 mm; upper 0.28–0.42 mm (other arms ≤0.15 mm).
8. Mn-Mx (Table 5): +1.35/+1.84/+0.69°; only II–III differ (P=.011; FH-Mn P=.026).
9. Ages 15.82/16.00/17.35 y, guardian consent, called nongrowing; no untreated control.
10. Inclusion: overbite >40%, ANB 1–5°, average/reduced LFH, ≤3 mm crowding, average smile; overjet >6 mm excluded.

### One likely oral question

"17-year-old woman, Class I, 60% overbite, average face, upright incisors, 2 mm crowding, wants speed: which protocol, what warnings?"

She fits the criteria; turbos with box elastics: 44.42 d against 55.72 and 94.15 d (Table 2). Warnings: elastics work only when worn; posteriors extrude about 1 mm; the mandibular plane opens 1.8° (Tables 4–5). Upright incisors could take the reverse curve's 7.46°, but elastics get there with 3.46° and no molar tip-back (Table 3). No follow-up past a 30% overbite: I promise neither total time nor stability; Proffit's bite-plane retainer for years applies.
