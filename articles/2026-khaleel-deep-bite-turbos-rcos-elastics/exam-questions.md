## Part 1. Multiple-choice questions (single best answer)

### Q1 (R). Study interventions

In Khaleel & Al-Nimri (2026), which statement correctly describes what all three groups had in common and how Group I differed?

A. All groups had anterior bite turbos bonded to the palatal surfaces of the upper central incisors and an upper 0.014-inch NiTi; Group I additionally had a lower 0.016 × 0.022-inch NiTi reverse-curve-of-Spee archwire cinched distal to the second molars.
B. All groups had a lower 0.016 × 0.022-inch NiTi archwire; Group I additionally had anterior bite turbos.
C. All groups had posterior box elastics; Group I additionally had a lower reverse-curve archwire.
D. All groups had bite turbos on the upper lateral incisors; Group I had a 0.019 × 0.025-inch stainless steel reverse-curve archwire.
E. Only Groups I and II had bite turbos; Group III was an untreated control group.

**Answer:** A
**Rationale:** Methods, Interventions: every patient received a standard composite ABT (Mini Mold, Aditek) on the palatal of the upper centrals at bonding plus an upper 0.014 NiTi; Group I had a lower 0.016 × 0.022 NiTi RCOS (GC) cinched distal to the 7s, Group II 0.014 NiTi both arches plus 3/16-inch medium box elastics, Group III 0.014 NiTi both arches only. Limitations state there was no untreated control.
**Tests:** Recall of the design; separating the shared mechanic (ABT) from the group variable.

### Q2 (R). Primary outcome

According to Table 2, the mean time from the start of treatment (T1) to overbite correction (overbite < 30% with posterior Shimstock contact) was:

A. Group I 44 d, Group II 56 d, Group III 94 d
B. Group I 56 d, Group II 44 d, Group III 94 d
C. Group I 94 d, Group II 56 d, Group III 44 d
D. Group I 56 d, Group II 94 d, Group III 44 d
E. About 60 d in all three groups, with no significant difference

**Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Table 2: Group I 55.72 ± 10.52 d, Group II 44.42 ± 10.88 d, Group III 94.15 ± 7.08 d; ANOVA P < .001; Tukey I vs II 11.3 d (95% CI 2.32 to 20.29, P = .010), I vs III 38.7 d and II vs III 50.0 d (P < .001). The Results text prints 55.7 ± 20.5 d and 94.4 ± 7.1 d; the paper does not say which is correct.
**Tests:** Recall of the ranking elastics < RCOS < turbos alone and the size of the gaps.

### Q3 (A). Case selection by incisor inclination

A 16-year-old has a 55% overbite, average lower face height, 2 mm of lower crowding and lower incisors that are already proclined (IMPA 102°). Using Table 3, which of the three studied protocols would add the least lower-incisor proclination during bite opening?

A. ABTs + 0.016 × 0.022-inch NiTi RCOS lower archwire
B. ABTs + 0.014-inch NiTi + posterior box elastics
C. ABTs + 0.014-inch NiTi archwires alone
D. All three add the same proclination because the ABT is the common factor
E. Lower-incisor inclination change was not measured in the study

**Answer:** C
**Rationale:** Table 3, LI-CA change: +7.46 ± 2.52° (I), +3.46 ± 3.89° (II), +1.55 ± 1.39° (III), between-group P < .001. The Discussion (Dental Effects of RCOS) calls for careful case selection in patients with already proclined incisors, and the Bite Turbos Alone subsection and Clinical Implications name turbos alone the conservative option at the cost of time (94 d, Table 2). B is the fastest route with intermediate proclination; the question asks for the least.
**Tests:** Applying the side-effect profile of each mechanic to a patient with a pre-existing dental compensation.

### Q4 (R). Measuring "true" intrusion

How did the paper locate Point I, the landmark used to measure true lower-incisor intrusion?

A. At the root apex of the lower central incisor on the T2 tracing
B. At the incisal edge, corrected mathematically for the change in LI-CA angle
C. On the long axis of the incisor, at 0.66 x the T1 incisor length measured from the incisal tip (Greig), then transferred to the T2 tracing on the mandibular superimposition
D. On the labial surface midway between the bracket and the incisal edge
E. At the labial cemento-enamel junction

**Answer:** C
**Rationale:** Methods, Lateral Cephalometric Measurements: T1 incisor length x 0.66 locates Point I on the long axis, transferred to T2 (Greig, ref 11), on cephalograms superimposed on the corpus axis at protuberance menti (Ricketts, ref 9). My read: this sits near the centre of resistance (Proffit Ch 9: about the mid-point of the embedded root), so crown tipping does not register as intrusion.
**Tests:** Recall of measurement methodology and why a point near the centre of resistance is used.

### Q5 (A). Predicting side effects of a cinched RCOS

You insert a 0.016 × 0.022-inch NiTi reverse-curve archwire in the lower arch at the bonding visit and cinch it distal to the second molars, as in Group I. Based on Tables 3 and 4, which pattern of lower posterior tooth movement should you expect by the time the overbite is corrected?

A. Bodily extrusion of first and second molars of about 1.2 mm with no change in molar angulation
B. Distal crown tipping of the first molar (about 6°) and second molar (about 9°), with the second-molar distal cusp not extruding (-0.64 mm) while the premolars extrude about 1 mm
C. Mesial tipping of both molars with 2 mm of molar extrusion
D. Intrusion of the premolars and extrusion of the incisors
E. No posterior change; the reverse curve acts only on the incisors

**Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Table 3, Group I: L6-CA +5.99 ± 4.75°, L7-CA +8.94 ± 3.45° (Results: distal tipping, P < .001 vs II and III), L4-CA +4.44° (mesial tipping). Table 4, Group I: CT L4 1.06, CT L5 1.05, MC L7 +0.71 but DC L7 -0.64 mm, DC L6 0.35 mm vs 1.08 and 1.15 mm in II and III. My read: this is the tip-back moment at the terminal molar of a tied-back continuous wire (Proffit Ch 15, bypass and utility arches, Figs 15.25 to 15.27).
**Tests:** Predicting the two-couple behaviour of a continuous reverse-curve wire; reading cusp-level vertical data.

### Q6 (N). Reading the "true intrusion" claim against Table 4

Table 4 reports the vertical change at Point I as 0.99 ± 0.38 mm (Group I), 0.71 ± 0.44 mm (Group II) and 0.86 ± 0.41 mm (Group III). The Conclusions attribute approximately 1 mm of true incisor intrusion to the RCOS + ABT protocol. Which interpretation is best supported by the paper's own data?

A. The RCOS produced 1 mm of true intrusion and the other two groups produced none
B. Point I moved by a similar amount in all three groups and no between-group test on Point I is reported, so the roughly 1 mm cannot be attributed specifically to the RCOS wire
C. Group III's 0.86 mm proves that a 0.014-inch NiTi intrudes incisors as effectively as a reverse-curve wire
D. The differences are explained by the larger sample size in Group I
E. The values show that Point I is an unsuitable landmark for adolescents

**Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Table 4 gives within-group means only; the Results report a between-group difference for the incisor tip (-2.46 vs -1.02 vs -1.25 mm, P < .001), which is driven by 7.46° of tipping (Table 3), but none for Point I. Group I exceeds II and III at Point I by 0.13 to 0.28 mm with SDs of about 0.4 mm. C over-reads: without an untreated control (Limitations) Group III's value could be the ABT, the superimposition, or eruption of the whole dentition. D is wrong because a difference in group means is not explained by sample size; in any case the paper describes three equal groups, although the printed standard errors imply Group III was analysed with fewer patients (see critical appraisal), which is a further reason to distrust unreported between-group comparisons.
**Tests:** Distinguishing within-group change from between-group difference; checking a conclusion against the table it cites.

### Q7 (A). Vertical facial pattern and the elastics protocol

A colleague proposes ABTs with posterior box elastics for a 15-year-old with a deep bite, increased lower face height and Mn-Mx of 34°, because it was the fastest method in the study. Which finding is the most relevant caution?

A. Group II showed the largest increases in Mn-Mx (+1.84 ± 1.59°) and FH-Mn (+1.31 ± 1.85°), significantly larger than Group III (P = .011 and P = .026), and the study only included patients with average or reduced lower face height
B. Group II showed a decrease in the mandibular plane angle, which would deepen the bite further
C. Group III showed the largest skeletal change and should be avoided in this patient
D. Skeletal changes were identical across the three groups, so speed is the only consideration
E. Group I showed no skeletal change, so the RCOS wire is the only safe option

**Answer:** A
**Rationale:** Table 5: Mn-Mx +1.35 / +1.84 / +0.69°, FH-Mn +0.97 / +1.31 / +0.32°, LFH +0.97 / +1.56 / +0.41%; all within-group changes significant, only II vs III differs between groups. Inclusion required average or reduced lower facial height, and the Discussion (Treatment Duration; Dental Effects of Posterior Elastics) warns that clockwise rotation and increased LFH are undesirable with an excessive vertical growth tendency. Proffit (Ch 15, Ch 17): extrusion-based leveling suits short or average faces with growth remaining; long faces need intrusion.
**Tests:** Applying generalisability limits; matching leveling mechanics to vertical facial type.

## Part 2. Short-answer questions

### Q8 (R). Endpoint, monitoring and cephalometric method

Describe (a) how the paper defined a corrected overbite, (b) how patients were monitored, and (c) how the T1 and T2 cephalograms were superimposed and which reference lines were used for the linear tooth measurements.

**Model answer:**
(a) Corrected when the overbite was below 30% of the lower incisor crown and posterior contact was confirmed with Shimstock foil (the paper prints 8 mm; Shimstock is 8 micrometres thick, so this is a typographic error, my read). (b) Reviewed every 14 days; days from T1 to correction is the duration outcome in Table 2. (c) T1 at the start of treatment, T2 after the leveling stage, same machine, FACAD tracing with 36 landmarks. Mandibular teeth were measured on a superimposition on the corpus axis registered at protuberance menti (Ricketts, ref 9); maxillary teeth on the palatal vault (Broadbent, ref 10). Linear changes were measured to a horizontal reference line through Sella 7° below SN and a vertical line perpendicular to it through S (Figure 2); as printed, the Methods sentence assigns anteroposterior distances to the horizontal line and vertical distances to the vertical line, which is the wrong way round (my read). Angular changes were the lower incisor and L4 to L7 to the corpus axis and the upper incisor to the maxillary plane. Every measurement was taken twice and averaged; intraexaminer ICC 0.924 to 0.967 (angular) and 0.923 to 0.975 (linear) on 10 cases retraced after one month.

**Marking points (6):** overbite < 30%; Shimstock posterior contact; 14-day reviews; corpus axis at Pm (Ricketts) for the mandible; palatal vault (Broadbent) for the maxilla; horizontal line through S 7° below SN (or Point I at 0.66 x incisor length).

### Q9 (A). Mechanism in the turbos-only group and Proffit's classification

Using Table 4, explain how Group III (ABTs + 0.014-inch NiTi only) reduced the overbite by 3.57 mm, place the mechanism within Proffit's classification of leveling methods, and explain why the paper calls it the conservative option.

**Model answer:**
Table 4, Group III: lower posterior cusps moved occlusally 0.86 to 1.30 mm (CT L4 1.06, CT L5 1.22, MC L6 1.30, DC L6 1.15, MC L7 1.05, DC L7 0.86 mm); lower incisor tip -1.25 mm, Point I 0.86 mm, upper incisor -0.43 mm; upper posterior teeth barely moved (0.07 to 0.08 mm); LI-CA +1.55° (Table 3), the smallest of the three groups. The paper says correction came mainly from passive eruption of the lower posterior teeth after the turbos disoccluded the buccal segments. In Proffit's terms (Ch 15, Leveling, Fig 15.23; 6th ed p. 518) this is leveling by relative intrusion, which he describes as differential elongation of the premolars, for the most part, while the incisors are held; my read is that the ABT is his anterior bite plane made fixed (Proffit does not use the term "bite turbo"), and he states that choosing a bite plane means accepting posterior elongation and some downward-backward mandibular rotation. It is conservative because incisor inclination changed least, skeletal change was smallest (Mn-Mx +0.69°, Table 5) and no elastics or active wire were needed; the price is the longest duration (94 d, Table 2). My read: the incisor contribution in Group III (1.25 mm lower tip plus 0.43 mm upper incisor) is of the same order as the posterior eruption, so "mainly" is fair but not the whole story.

**Marking points (6):** cites lower posterior eruption of about 1 mm with table values; identifies passive eruption after disocclusion; names relative intrusion / anterior bite plane in Proffit; identifies least proclination (1.55°) and smallest skeletal change; identifies the time cost (94 d); notes the incisor component or the absence of a control.

### Q10 (N). Allocation and the primary outcome

The paper says the groups were formed by operator judgment based on lower-incisor alignment and inclination and on patient cooperation, then reports non-significant baseline P values (Table 1) as evidence of group similarity. Appraise this design with respect to the duration outcome.

**Model answer:**
This is a non-randomised comparison in which the treating clinician allocated by variables that predict the outcome: cooperation determines elastic wear in Group II (the fastest group) and incisor alignment determines whether a 0.016 × 0.022 RCOS can be engaged at bonding (Group I), i.e. confounding by indication. Table 1 imbalances run in the direction of the result: Group I had the least crowding (1.25 mm) and deepest bite (5.51 mm); Group III was oldest (17.35 vs 15.82 and 16.00 y), most crowded (1.81 mm) and most brachyfacial (Mn-Mx 22.89°), all of which would be expected to slow passive eruption (my read). Baseline P values of .062 to .095 with about 27 per group are not evidence of equivalence, and no adjusted analysis (ANCOVA on age, crowding, baseline overbite) was run. The endpoint was called chairside every 14 days by the same unblinded operator who allocated (blinding applied only to measurement and analysis), so the 11.3-day I-vs-II gap (95% CI 2.32 to 20.29 d, Table 2) lies within one review interval and within reach of assessment bias; the 38.7- and 50.0-day gaps to Group III are large enough to survive these problems in direction. Randomisation was feasible: the same unit randomised RCOS vs ABT in 2022 (ref 4), and the paper lists its absence as a limitation. My read: the Tukey standard errors in Table 2 (3.759 / 4.229 / 4.173) are not equal, as they would be with 27/27/26 per group, so the analysed n per arm may not be what the abstract states; this is a non-randomised cohort at roughly CEBM level 3, a tier below the group's own 2022 RCT.

**Marking points (6):** names confounding by indication or selection bias; links cooperation to the elastic-dependent outcome; cites Table 1 imbalances with numbers and direction; explains why a non-significant baseline P is not balance; identifies the unblinded operator calling the endpoint at 14-day intervals; separates the robust contrast (versus Group III) from the fragile one (I vs II).

### Q11 (N). The "nongrowing" claim and the skeletal changes

The Discussion states: "The studied sample included nongrowing patients." The Conclusions describe the vertical skeletal changes as modest and primarily dentoalveolar. Using Tables 1 and 5 and the paper's own limitations, evaluate whether the skeletal changes can be attributed to the mechanics.

**Model answer:**
Table 1: mean ages 15.82 ± 2.12, 16.00 ± 2.17 and 17.35 ± 2.92 y; consent came from participants or guardians; no growth staging (CVM or hand-wrist) is reported. With a mean of 16 and SD of 2 a substantial share of Groups I and II were under 15, and males (25 in the text, 31 by Table 1's counts) grow vertically into the early twenties (Proffit Ch 4), so the nongrowing claim is not supported. Table 5: within-group increases are all significant (Mn-Mx +1.35 / +1.84 / +0.69°; LFH +0.97 / +1.56 / +0.41%; FH-Mn +0.97 / +1.31 / +0.32°), but between groups only II vs III differs (P = .011 and .026); Group I is statistically indistinguishable from both. The paper concedes that without an untreated control it cannot separate treatment from physiologic eruption; the observation window was 44 to 94 days, and the oldest group (III) showed the smallest change, so age and mechanics are confounded. Reliability is given only as ICC 0.92 to 0.97, not as an error in degrees; a 0.3 to 0.7° change (Group III) is within usual tracing error for an individual (my read). The dentoalveolar conclusion is nonetheless consistent with the data: about 1 mm of posterior eruption and 1 to 2.5 mm of incisor-tip movement against 1 to 2° of mandibular plane rotation, which is what Proffit expects from extrusion-based leveling in adolescents, where ramus growth compensates (Ch 15, Ch 20).

**Marking points (6):** cites ages and guardian consent; notes absent growth staging; separates within-group from between-group significance; cites the no-control limitation; identifies the age confound in Group III; balanced verdict on the dentoalveolar conclusion.

## Part 3. Case-based oral defence

### Q12 (A). Seventeen-year-old who wants a fast result

**Patient.** A 17-year-old woman, Class I molars, ANB 4°, overbite 60% (about 5.5 mm), overjet 4 mm, lower crowding 2 mm, lower face height 56% (average), Mn-Mx 25°, average smile line with normal upper incisor display, lower incisors upright (IMPA 88°), all lower second molars erupted, no previous treatment, good oral hygiene. Non-extraction fixed appliances are planned. She leaves for university in 12 months and asks for the fastest treatment.

**Examiner question 1. Which of the three Khaleel protocols would you offer, and why?**
Model answer: She fits the paper's inclusion criteria (overbite > 40%, ANB 1 to 5°, average or reduced LFH, crowding 1 to 3 mm, average smile line, overjet not above 6 mm, intact lower arch to the second molars). Because speed is her priority I would offer the Group II protocol: ABTs on the palatal of the upper centrals, 0.014 NiTi in both arches, and bilateral 3/16-inch medium box elastics from premolars to molars. Table 2: 44.42 ± 10.88 d to a corrected overbite versus 55.72 d with RCOS and 94.15 d with turbos alone. Her upright incisors (IMPA 88°) could tolerate the RCOS route's +7.46° proclination, but elastics reach the endpoint faster with +3.46° (Table 3). Proffit's finishing rule (Ch 17; 6th ed p. 562): with normal incisor display and an average face, correct the overbite by moving the lower teeth and accept slight posterior elongation. At 17 a girl has little vertical growth left, so I would add that Proffit favours intrusion in true adults and that her average LFH is what makes 1 to 2° of rotation acceptable.

**Examiner question 2. What will you tell her about timing?**
Model answer: The paper measured days from T1 to an overbite below 30% at the end of leveling, not total treatment time. Six to seven weeks for bite opening with elastics (about 8 weeks with RCOS, 13 weeks with turbos alone) is the honest figure; finishing, settling and Class I detailing are not reported, so I would not promise completion in 12 months on the basis of this study. The literature says the same unit's 2022 RCT (Al-Zoubi & Al-Nimri, ref 4) found turbos alone took 3.15 ± 0.93 months (~96 d), numerically close to the 94 d here, but because the 2026 turbos-alone column reproduces the 2022 turbo arm on most outcome rows to two decimals (see critical appraisal), the two figures cannot be treated as independent replications; the 44-day elastics figure has no external comparator at all.

**Examiner question 3. What side effects should you warn her about, with numbers?**
Model answer: From Group II (Tables 3 to 5): lower premolars and molars extrude 0.90 to 1.19 mm and upper posterior teeth 0.28 to 0.42 mm (significantly more upper extrusion than the other groups); lower incisors procline about 3.5° and the tip moves 1.0 mm; Mn-Mx opens 1.84°, FH-Mn 1.31°, LFH rises 1.56%, so the chin moves slightly down and back. The elastics are worn bilaterally in a box pattern from premolars to molars; the paper does not report the wear schedule, and the 44-day figure was obtained in patients pre-selected for cooperation. The paper did not assess comfort, speech or chewing with turbos, and one of 81 patients lost both turbos and left the study. Proffit (Ch 17) uses posterior vertical elastics mainly for short-term settling and warns that excessive interarch elastics elongate posterior teeth, so wear should stop once the overbite is corrected.

**Examiner question 4. Why not put a reverse-curve rectangular NiTi in the lower arch from day one? What would Proffit say?**
Model answer: Group I data (Tables 3 and 4): LI-CA +7.46° and 1.01 mm forward movement of the lower incisor tip, L7 distal tipping 8.94°, L6 5.99°, L4 mesial tipping 4.44°, and the second-molar distal cusp did not extrude (-0.64 mm). Point I moved 0.99 mm, but 0.71 to 0.86 mm in the groups without a reverse curve, so the wire's specific intrusive contribution is small and untested (Q6). Proffit (Ch 15, Leveling; 6th ed p. 518 to 520) says a rectangular reverse-curve wire in the lower arch is a torque error that moves incisor roots lingually and crowns labially, that a continuous wire levels almost entirely by extrusion, and that true intrusion needs light forces (Table 8.3: 10 to 20 g) from bypass or segmented arches against a consolidated posterior anchor unit. With 2 mm of crowding she would also need alignment before a 16 × 22 wire engages; the paper restricted its sample to well-aligned or minimally crowded (1 to 3 mm) incisors for this reason.

**Examiner question 5. What would change your choice if her lower face height were increased, or her smile line high?**
Model answer: Both were outside the sample: the study included only average or reduced LFH and average smile lines. The elastics group produced the largest mandibular plane opening (Table 5), so in a long-face patient I would avoid extrusion mechanics and, following Proffit (Ch 15, Ch 17, Ch 20), plan absolute intrusion with a segmented or bypass arch or skeletal anchorage. A high smile line calls for upper incisor intrusion rather than lower-arch leveling, and none of the three protocols intruded the upper incisors meaningfully (upper incisor V -0.09 to -0.43 mm, Table 4).

**Examiner question 6. At the first 14-day review she has worn the elastics poorly. What now?**
Model answer: In the paper cooperation was an allocation criterion, so poor cooperators were not in Group II; her expected time now reverts towards the turbos-alone figure (94 d) unless the mechanics change. With aligned incisors I could switch to the RCOS wire (55.72 d, Table 2), accepting its proclination and molar-tipping profile, while remembering that the Results text gives Group I an SD of 20.5 d, i.e. a variable response. Reinforce wear, check the turbos are intact, and re-time the milestone.

**Examiner question 7. How will you retain the corrected overbite?**
Model answer: The paper has no retention or follow-up data (Limitations) and its T2 is the end of leveling, so it cannot inform stability. Proffit (retention after deep-bite correction; Ch 19 in the 7th ed, 6th ed p. 579) recommends an upper removable retainer with a bite plane, often for several years, night-only once other relationships are stable, lower-incisor retention until growth is complete, and overcorrection of the overbite at the finish. The literature says extrusion-based correction in about 20-year-olds was stable in high-angle patients while low-angle patients relapsed through incisor inclination (Rozzi 2019, ref 14), and long-term overbite rebound after deep-bite correction averages about 0.8 mm (Huang 2012, ref 1); she is average-angle on the extrusion route, so a bite-plane retainer is not optional.

**Examiner question 8. What are the limits of using this paper for her decision?**
Model answer: Non-randomised with operator allocation by cooperation and incisor alignment; single operator who also called the endpoint at 14-day intervals; no untreated control; a sample of 15.8 to 17.4 y described as nongrowing; duration measured only to the end of leveling; no comfort, esthetic, functional or stability outcomes; no between-group test on Point I; text-table discrepancies (Group I SD 10.52 vs 20.5 d; Group III 94.15 vs 94.4 d; Group II lower extrusion 0.6 to 1.5 mm in the text vs 0.90 to 1.19 mm in Table 4). The robust message is that adding an active auxiliary (elastics or RCOS) to turbos roughly halves time to bite opening; the ranking of elastics over RCOS (11 d, P = .010) is the fragile part. The same unit's 2022 RCT (ref 4) is higher-level evidence for RCOS versus ABT.
