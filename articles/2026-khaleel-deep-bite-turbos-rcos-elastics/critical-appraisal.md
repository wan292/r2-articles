# Critical appraisal — Khaleel & Al-Nimri 2026

Khaleel R, Al-Nimri K. Assessment of three different techniques in correcting deep overbite: a prospective clinical study. *Angle Orthod.* 2026;96(5):528-535. DOI 10.2319/100925-850.1. Jordan University of Science and Technology (JUST), Irbid. Submitted 9 Oct 2025, accepted 7 Feb 2026, online 20 Mar 2026.

Numbers below come from the printed tables (page images), which take precedence over the running text where the two disagree. Recomputations are mine, were run from the printed means, SDs, standard errors and group sizes, and are labelled as such. Where I lean on the research notes (prior trials, textbook positions, citation checks) I say so.

## Verdict in three lines

1. **Design label:** prospective, single-centre, single-operator, three-arm comparative cohort with allocation by operator judgment (not randomised), no untreated control, outcomes measured at the end of levelling only (6-14 weeks into treatment).
2. **Level of evidence:** Oxford CEBM 2011 Level 3 (non-randomised controlled cohort). GRADE-style certainty, my grading: *low to moderate* that adding a NiTi RCOS wire or posterior box elastics to anterior bite turbos shortens time to a <30% overbite by 40-50 days versus turbos alone (the effect is very large, Cohen d 2.5-5.4, so it survives plausible confounding); *very low* for the 11-day elastics-versus-RCOS difference, for the claim that RCOS produces 1 mm of true intrusion, and for every between-group skeletal claim.
3. **The one thing that limits it most:** allocation by operator judgment using lower-incisor alignment and patient cooperation, so every baseline imbalance (age, crowding, divergence, overbite depth, compliance) runs in the direction of the result. Behind that sits a reporting problem that would supersede it if confirmed: the paper's own standard errors and baseline P values imply arms of about 29/31/20 rather than 27/27/27, and the turbos-alone arm reproduces the ABT arm of the same group's 2022 RCT on 14 of 26 outcomes exactly (mean and SD to two decimals) and on 5 more within 0.02 (see Sample section).

My read: the question is clinically useful and genuinely new in its combinations. The research notes found no earlier trial of either auxiliary combined with turbos, and no comparison of posterior box elastics against other mechanics in adolescents or adults; posterior vertical elastics on their own had been studied prospectively only in growing children (the paper's ref 8, Jariyavithayakul 2019, and Alsawaf 2023 with a utility arch). The group's own 2022 RCT (ref 4) compared RCOS alone with turbos alone, and the 2025 RCT (ref 20) compared three RCOS wires without turbos.

## Design and allocation

The paper says the sample was "divided into three groups according to operator judgment" on the basis of initial alignment and inclination of the lower incisors and patient cooperation. The abstract describes the groups only as three equal groups and does not say how they were formed. The Methods call the study a trial; the Limitations concede that it was not randomised. No allocation rule, no concealment and no protocol registration are given.

What operator allocation does here is confounding by indication: the patient characteristic that decided the arm is also a determinant of the outcome. This was a choice, not a constraint. The same senior author randomised the 2022 (RCOS vs turbos) and 2025 (three RCOS wires) comparisons, and the alignment criterion used for allocation (an RCOS wire needs aligned incisors, which the Discussion gives as the reason for restricting the sample) was already handled by the eligibility limit of ≤3 mm crowding.

### Direction of bias, by headline comparison

- **II vs III, 50.0 d (Tukey 95% CI 40.0 to 60.0, Table 2):** the direction is almost certainly real because the effect is enormous; the magnitude is inflated by cooperation selection and case mix.
- **I vs III, 38.7 d (28.6 to 48.8):** inflated by the age and crowding imbalance, but large enough to survive it.
- **I vs II, 11.3 d (2.3 to 20.3, P = .010):** fragile. Both arms are young; Group I had the deepest bite to correct but the least crowding; the difference is smaller than the 14-day visit interval (see Outcome definition). I would not rank elastics over RCOS on this study.
- **Skeletal II vs III (Mn-Mx P = .011, FH-Mn P = .026, Table 5):** the two arms differed at baseline by 3.3° of Mn-Mx (standardised difference 0.54, my calculation), in the same direction as the result.
- **Lower incisor proclination I > II > III (Table 3):** direction consistent with mechanics and with the 2022 and 2025 trials; the Group I magnitude is a lower bound because the arm was pre-selected for aligned incisors.
- **True intrusion (Point I):** never compared between groups; see Statistics.

### Blinding

The paper says blinding applied only to measurement and analysis. The endpoint that produces the headline (overbite <30% with Shimstock contact) was judged chairside, at 14-day visits, by the unblinded operator who also allocated the patients. The examiner who traced the films is not named; if it was R.K., measurement blinding is nominal. On the T2 film a curved rectangular RCOS wire is distinguishable from a round 0.014 NiTi, so the tracer can identify Group I in any case. The research notes report that the same criticism (unblinded operator judging the correction endpoint) was made publicly of the 2022 RCT.

## Sample, power and attrition

**Enrolment and flow.** The paper says 81 were enrolled and 80 completed; one patient dropped out after both turbos debonded (arm not stated). The Methods give 25 males and 56 females; Table 1 gives 10 + 12 + 9 = 31 males and 50 females. Both sum to 81, so one is wrong. Table 1 describes the 81 enrolled; the outcome tables describe 80 or fewer. There is no flow diagram and no per-arm n anywhere in Tables 2-5.

**Are the arms 27/27/27? Two independent lines of evidence say no.**

*Line 1, the post-hoc standard errors.* Tukey SEs depend only on the pooled mean square error and the two group sizes, SE = √[MSE(1/nₐ + 1/nᵦ)]. With 27/27/26 the three pairwise SEs must be within 1% of each other. The printed SEs are not: 3.759 / 4.229 / 4.173 d (Table 2), 0.3469 / 0.3903 / 0.3852 (Mn-Mx), 0.8873 / 0.9983 / 0.9850 (LFH) and 0.33115 / 0.37258 / 0.36764 (FH-Mn). All four sets give the same squared ratios (1.266 and 1.233) and, constrained to a total of 80, solve to n = 29 / 31 / 20. With SD 20.5 for Group I (the text value) and 29/31/20, the computed duration SEs are 3.755 / 4.225 / 4.169 against the printed 3.759 / 4.229 / 4.173. The same group sizes reproduce the Mn-Mx SEs from the printed Table 5 SDs to within 0.0005 (0.3465 / 0.3899 / 0.3847) and the FH-Mn SEs to four decimals (0.3312 / 0.3726 / 0.3676); at 27/27/26 those SEs would be 0.354 / 0.357 / 0.357 and 0.330 / 0.333 / 0.333.

*Line 2, the baseline P values.* Re-running one-way ANOVA on the printed Table 1 means and SDs with 27 per arm does not reproduce the printed P values (age .046 vs printed .067; overbite .041 vs .068; SNB .070 vs .093). Re-running it with 29/31/20 reproduces ten of the fourteen continuous rows almost exactly: age .066 (printed .067), overbite .069 (.068), Mn-Mx .145 (.145), ANB .129 (.128), SNB .093 (.093), L7 tip .079 (.079), L6 tip .091 (.094), lower incisor tip .147 (.146), L4 tip .695 (.694), LFH .824 (.831). So Table 1's P column appears to have been computed on arms of 29/31/20 even though its gender row lists 27/27/27. Four rows fail at any group size: crowding (.008-.009 vs printed .095), SNA (.012-.026 vs .062), L5 tip (.004-.009 vs .701) and upper incisor tip (.51-.53 vs .130). The gender chi-square from the printed counts is .69, not .11.

*Corroboration.* Two of the three Group III within-group P values in Table 5 fit n ≈ 20 better than 27: my paired-t recomputation gives Mn-Mx (0.69 ± 0.90) P = .003 at n = 20 (printed .003) versus .0005 at n = 27, and FH-Mn (0.32 ± 0.49) P = .009 at n = 20 (printed .007) versus .002 at n = 27. The LFH value (0.41 ± 0.43, printed P = .009) does not reproduce at any n (P = .0004 at n = 20), which is consistent with the LFH standard-error anomaly described under Statistics.

Either the arms were unequal, which is what hand allocation would produce, or some Group III patients were dropped from the analysis without being reported. Either way the abstract's equal groups are contradicted by the paper's own statistics, and the smallest arm (about 20) is the reference arm for every skeletal contrast.

**Sample-size calculation.** The paper says G*Power gave 75 patients for effect size 0.25, power 0.90 and α = .05, with 6 added for 8% attrition. My recomputation: a one-way ANOVA with three groups at f = 0.25 needs about 207 patients for 90% power; 75 patients give 46% power; 75 is what f ≈ 0.42 requires. One reading that returns about 75 (approximate, from the research notes and a rough check of mine) is a repeated-measures within-between interaction with two time points and a modest correlation, an analysis the paper did not run. No primary outcome is named, and f = 0.25 is Cohen's generic medium effect rather than a value derived from the group's own 2022 data. For duration this is moot because the observed effects are huge. For the skeletal contrasts it is not: with 27 per arm, the power to detect the observed I-vs-II Mn-Mx difference (d = 0.34) is about 0.23, and the smallest detectable difference at 80% power is d ≈ 0.78. No difference between Groups I and II in skeletal change is therefore absence of evidence.

**Attrition.** 1 of 81 (1.2%) is negligible numerically. The lost case was a failure of the appliance under test and belongs in a harms count; no per-arm turbo debond rate is reported.

Fourteen of 26 outcome values are identical in mean and SD to two decimals and five more differ by 0.01-0.02. Two independent samples of different size, age and protocol cannot do this. My read: I cannot tell whether this is undisclosed reuse of the 2022 patients, a data-file mix-up or a whole-column transcription error, and the paper discloses nothing. Two further oddities: the 2026 Point I value (0.86 ± 0.41) is the 2022 arm's *horizontal* incisor value, and the analysed n of about 20 implied for Group III by the standard errors is close to the 2022 arm's 21. Until the authors clarify, the Group III column is not an independent measurement, and every comparison against it, including the headline that turbos alone are slower, inherits that uncertainty. The 94-day duration is not among the reproduced values, but its agreement with the 2022 ABT duration (3.15 mo ≈ 96 d) cannot be counted as independent corroboration. The research notes also flag that the 2025 RCT's NiTi arm reproduces the 2022 RCOS arm on several values; I have not checked that myself.

## Outcome definition and measurement

**Endpoint.** Overbite <30% of lower incisor crown height plus posterior contact on Shimstock (printed as 8 mm; Shimstock is 8 µm). This is a threshold, not a quantity, so the amount of overbite reduction at T2 is largely set by the starting depth: Group I started deepest (5.51 mm) and reduced most (3.92 mm). Clinical overbite percentage was not assessed for reliability. The three arms reach posterior contact by different routes (lower extrusion in II and III, incisor tipping and intrusion in I), so equal percent overbite at T2 is not the same occlusal state.

**14-day discretisation.** Patients were seen every 14 days and the endpoint was called at a visit, so days to correction can only take values near multiples of 14 (28, 42, 56, 70, 84, 98, 112). The data are interval-censored with 14-day resolution. Group III's SD of 7.08 d is what results when nearly everyone is called corrected at the 84- or 98-day visit. Means and SDs of such data do not meet ANOVA assumptions, and the I-vs-II difference of 11.3 d is smaller than one visit interval. Missed appointments count as treatment time and are not reported. The appropriate analysis is an interval-censored time-to-event model or, at minimum, a distribution of visit numbers with medians.

**Cephalometric method.** Same machine (Orthophos XG Plus), FACAD 3.11, 36 landmarks, ruler calibration, every measurement made twice and averaged. Mandibular changes on a corpus-axis superimposition registered at protuberance menti (Ricketts); maxillary changes on the palatal vault (Broadbent). Linear changes were then read against a grid through S, 7° below SN. Two problems: (a) the Methods paragraph describes the axes the wrong way round (anteroposterior change measured to the horizontal line, vertical change to the vertical line); (b) it does not say how a cranial-base grid is transferred to a mandibular superimposition (Figure 2 shows a 90° grid drawn on the corpus axis instead). If lower posterior extrusion was in fact read against the S-based grid, it includes mandibular rotation and is not purely dentoalveolar.

**Superimposition.** The paper says protuberance menti was chosen because Xi shifts with growth. It also cites Springate & Jones (ref 15), who found against tantalum implants that the Ricketts corpus-axis method is less valid than Björk's structural method, and then leans on Graf et al. (ref 16) to say no method is definitively superior, which is not the same as saying this one is accurate. Rotational error about Pm scales with distance: 1° gives about 0.2-0.3 mm at the incisors but 0.5-0.7 mm at molars 30-40 mm away, which is the size of several tabulated molar values. No error estimate for the superimposition itself is given.

**Point I.** Located at 0.66 × incisor length from the tip (Greig, ref 11), close to the centre of resistance, so that tipping should not masquerade as intrusion. The concept is sound; the execution is not interpretable. Table 4 states no sign convention. The text says the lower incisor tip "moved downward" (printed −2.46 mm) and that Point I "intruded" (printed +0.99 mm): the same direction, opposite signs. My geometry: with the tip about 14 mm from Point I, a 7.46° rotation moves the tip 1.8 mm perpendicular to the long axis, of which only about 0.1-0.6 mm is vertical depending on the reference frame; that cannot separate the two rows by 1.47 mm (same sign) let alone 3.45 mm (opposite signs). In Group III (1.55°) the rows are only approximately reconcilable, and only if they share a sign. Under the only convention that fits the posterior teeth (positive = occlusal; the L7 mesial cusp +0.71 with distal cusp −0.64 is a coherent 8.9° distal tip), +0.99 would be extrusion. And Point I moved 0.71 mm (Group II) and 0.86 mm (Group III) in arms with a round 0.014 wire and no intrusive mechanics.

**Reliability.** 10 of 80 patients' film pairs (12%) retraced by the same, unnamed examiner after 1 month: ICC 0.924-0.967 for angles, 0.923-0.975 for distances. No Dahlberg error, no Bland-Altman, no test for systematic error, no second examiner. An ICC of 0.92 from ten films has a 95% CI reaching roughly 0.7 and cannot be converted into a minimum detectable change. Typical angular method error for mandibular-plane measures is 0.5-1.0°; the Group III skeletal changes (0.32-0.69°) and the II-vs-III differences (about 1°) sit inside that band.

## Statistics

**What was run.** Shapiro-Wilk (results not reported), paired t within arms, one-way ANOVA with Tukey between arms (SPSS 25). No ANCOVA adjusting for baseline value, age or crowding, which is the minimum for a non-randomised comparison with visible baseline imbalance. P values are printed as .000.

**What is missing.** Tables 3 and 4 carry no P values at all; between-group P values for dental changes appear only in the text for a few variables (incisor tip vertical P < .001, incisor horizontal I vs II P = .027, molar angulation, first-premolar tipping and incisor proclination P < .001). No between-group test is reported for Point I, for overbite reduction (described as comparable without a test), or for posterior extrusion, about which the same subsection says both that it was significantly less in Group I and that it was comparable across the three arms. Table 4 shows the Group I first-molar distal cusp (0.35 ± 0.22 mm) at a third of Groups II and III (1.08, 1.15), so the exception is not only the second molar. The abstract's statement that skeletal measures increased significantly in all groups is a within-group result: a paired t on 27 treated patients will always be significant.

**Multiplicity.** My count: 72 paired t tests (Tables 3-5), 25 ANOVAs, 75 Tukey contrasts and 15 baseline tests, 187 P values, with no family-wise or false-discovery control beyond Tukey's protection within each ANOVA. About nine spurious significant results are expected under the null. The two skeletal between-group results (Mn-Mx P = .011, FH-Mn P = .026) are exactly the kind that evaporate: a Holm correction over just the three II-vs-III skeletal contrasts leaves FH-Mn at .052.

**Clinical versus statistical significance of the skeletal changes.** Within-arm changes were Mn-Mx +1.35 / +1.84 / +0.69°, FH-Mn +0.97 / +1.31 / +0.32° and LFH +0.97 / +1.56 / +0.41% (Table 5). Rotation about the condyle displaces the chin by up to about 2 mm per degree (my geometry, condyle-to-menton about 110 mm), so 1.3-1.8° is a visible 2.5-3.5 mm in Groups I and II over 6-8 weeks. In a sample restricted to average or reduced lower face height that is neutral to favourable and is, in Proffit's terms, the intended outcome of levelling by extrusion. Group III's 0.3-0.7° is inside tracing error. The paper measured no SNB, ANB or overjet change at T2, so the anteroposterior cost of the rotation in a mildly Class II sample is unknown. Significant in all groups describes the sample size; the Conclusions' description of the changes as modest is the fair reading, and the abstract oversells it.

## Confounding and growth

**Ages.** 15.82 ± 2.12 / 16.00 ± 2.17 / 17.35 ± 2.92 y (Table 1). Consent was taken from participants or their guardians, so minors were included; there is no lower age limit and no CVM or hand-wrist staging. With those means and SDs, roughly a fifth to a third of each arm was under 15 and a tenth to a fifth under 14. The Discussion nevertheless describes the sample as nongrowing. That is unsupported, and the Limitations paragraph concedes that vertical changes could include physiological eruption over the study period.

**No untreated control.** The paper acknowledges this. Over 6-14 weeks condylar growth contributes little to the mandibular plane, but adolescent posterior eruption is real and is the entire mechanism of Group III. The observation window differed two-fold (44 vs 94 d), so eruption and growth had twice as long to act in Group III, yet Group III showed the smallest skeletal change; that argues the II-vs-III skeletal gap is case mix (Group III oldest and most brachyfacial) rather than growth, but it cannot be attributed to mechanics either way.

**Baseline balance was asserted, not shown.** The paper says no baseline variable differed (Table 1, all P > .05). On the paper's own means and SDs, crowding (P ≈ .008-.009 at any plausible n) and SNA (.012-.026) do differ, and age and overbite sit at .041-.069 depending on the group sizes assumed. Standardised differences (my calculation): age III vs I 0.6, crowding III vs I 0.8, overbite I vs III 0.7, SNA I vs III 0.7, Mn-Mx II vs III 0.5, all far above the 0.1-0.2 threshold for imbalance. Crowding was the allocation variable, so this is the fingerprint of the allocation, not noise. Baseline significance tests are the wrong tool in any case (STROBE and CONSORT both discourage them), and no adjustment was made.

**Other unaddressed confounders.** Patient cooperation was an allocation criterion and elastic wear is entirely cooperation-dependent, so the Group II duration is confounded by design; the abstract does not mention it. The ANB 1-5° criterion is breached by Group I (5.14 ± 2.16°), so Group I contained more skeletal Class II patterns. Elastic force and wear time per arm, missed visits and turbo debonds per arm are not reported.

**Mis-citations that matter for the growth and stability argument (research notes, checked against abstracts).** The paper cites Rozzi et al. 2019 (ref 14) to reassure that posterior extrusion is stable in patients averaging 19.8 y; the notes report that the stability was in the high-angle subgroup, while low-angle subjects, the phenotype enrolled here, relapsed through incisor inclination. Ref 8 (Jariyavithayakul 2019) is cited for clockwise rotation with box elastics but found no significant mandibular-plane change in growing patients. Ref 21 (Lindauer 2005), cited for vertical elastics, is reported in the notes to have compared an intrusion arch with a bite plate. Ref 20 is printed as 2024;26;95(1), but Angle Orthodontist volume 95 is 2025.

## External validity

**Who this applies to (the paper's inclusion criteria):** adolescents and young adults of roughly 13-20 y (age range not reported; inferred from mean ± SD) in a university clinic, 56-69% female, deep overbite >40% with 5.1-5.5 mm mean overlap, ANB nominally 1-5°, average or reduced lower face height, average smile line, overjet ≤6 mm, lower crowding ≤3 mm, intact lower arch to the second molars, no previous orthodontics, treated non-extraction with 0.022 Roth brackets and turbos from the first visit. Group II additionally applies only to patients pre-judged to be cooperative.

**Who it does NOT apply to:**

- Lower crowding >3 mm: excluded, and the RCOS arm was further restricted to aligned incisors; 7.46° of proclination is a floor for crowded arches.
- Overjet >6 mm, including Class II division 1 with a large overjet and Class II division 2 after incisor uprighting.
- ANB outside 1-5°: Class III, and moderate-to-severe Class II, although Group I itself breached the upper limit.
- Long faces and hyperdivergent patients: excluded, yet they are the patients in whom the elastics arm's 1.8° of mandibular-plane opening would matter most.
- High smile line or gummy display, and any patient whose deep bite calls for upper incisor intrusion: excluded, so the intrusion-versus-extrusion decision that Proffit's finishing chapter turns on was never tested.
- Adults with already proclined lower incisors, a reduced periodontium or missing lower molars: the RCOS arm would flare further, and the sample was adolescent.
- Extraction cases and patients needing posterior anchorage for retraction.
- Unmotivated patients: the elastics result comes from a compliance-selected arm.
- Anyone asking about the result at debond or in retention: T2 is 6-14 weeks into treatment.

The literature says (research notes): the same 0.016×0.022 NiTi RCOS proclined the lower incisors 5.74° in the 2022 RCT and 6.00° in the 2025 RCT without turbos, so 7.46° with turbos and from bonding is in range; a zero-torque 0.019×0.025 SS RCOS in the 2025 RCT produced −0.31°, a proclination-control option the 2026 Discussion does not raise.

## Strengths

- Prospective, with a clinically relevant three-way question that no earlier trial had asked; the combinations with turbos and the adolescent elastics arm are new.
- Standardised bracket system, turbo (silicone mould, standard size), wires and elastics, with the lower arch bonded 7-7 in every arm.
- Single operator, which removes inter-operator variability in bonding, wire engagement and endpoint judgment (at the cost noted above).
- Cephalograms on one machine, traced in FACAD with 36 landmarks, each measurement made twice and averaged; intraexaminer ICC reported (0.92-0.98), even if incompletely.
- A sensible superimposition strategy in principle (corpus axis for the mandible, palatal vault for the maxilla) and use of Greig's Point I to separate tipping from intrusion.
- A clear, reproducible clinical endpoint (overbite <30% with Shimstock contact) and a 14-day review interval, finer than the monthly reviews of the 2022 RCT.
- An a-priori sample-size calculation was attempted, attrition was 1 of 81, and the Limitations section is candid about non-randomisation, the missing control group, the lack of follow-up and the lack of patient-reported outcomes.
- The direction of the large findings is coherent with mechanics and with prior work: adding an active auxiliary to turbos roughly halves time to a 30% overbite; RCOS flares incisors and tips molars distally; elastics extrude upper as well as lower posterior teeth.

## What would have made it a better study

- Randomise, as the same group did in 2022 and 2025, with concealed allocation and a registered protocol naming duration as the primary outcome; eligibility already guaranteed ≤3 mm crowding, so RCOS engagement was not a reason to allocate by hand.
- Have the correction endpoint judged by a second, blinded clinician (or from photographs and casts), and analyse days to correction as interval-censored time-to-event data with medians and a survival model rather than ANOVA on 14-day-quantised means.
- Report a CONSORT/TREND flow diagram with per-arm n at every stage, the arm of the dropout, turbo debonds and elastic-wear compliance per arm.
- Adjust between-group comparisons for baseline value, age, crowding and Mn-Mx (ANCOVA); report between-group tests and CIs for every variable in Tables 3-4 including Point I; and control multiplicity or pre-specify a small family of outcomes.
- Stage skeletal maturity (CVM), drop the nongrowing claim, and either add an untreated or delayed-treatment control or restrict to post-pubertal patients if growth is to be excluded.
- State the sign convention for every vertical measure, reconcile the incisor-tip and Point I rows, report Dahlberg error in degrees and millimetres with a second examiner, and proof the tables against the analysis output (the SD, mean and LFH post-hoc errors would have been caught).
- Add follow-up to debond and to one year, plus pain, mobility and comfort measures, so that the most efficient arm can be weighed against side effects and stability; and disclose any overlap with previously published cohorts.

## Journal-club one-liners

1. The operator chose who got elastics on cooperation and who got RCOS on incisor position, so the arms differ in exactly the things that drive the speed of bite opening.
2. With reviews every 14 days, the 11-day elastics-versus-RCOS advantage is below the resolution of the ruler; the 40-50-day gap to turbos alone is the only duration finding I would take home.
3. Table 2 says the RCOS SD is 10.5, the text says 20.5, and only 20.5 reproduces the printed standard errors: RCOS was not just slower than elastics, it was twice as unpredictable.
4. The standard errors solve to arms of about 29, 31 and 20, and ten of the fourteen baseline P values only reproduce at those sizes; the abstract's three equal groups are contradicted twice by the paper's own numbers.
5. Turbos alone registered 0.86 mm of Point I change with a round 0.014 wire and no intrusive mechanics, the RCOS arm's 1 mm was never tested against it, and the Point I sign is opposite to the incisor tip on the same tooth with no convention stated.
6. Roughly half the sample is under 16 and consent was taken from participants or their guardians; calling them nongrowing and then citing a 19.8-year-old cohort for the stability of extrusion is not a stability argument.
7. Recompute Table 1 from its own means and SDs and crowding comes out P < .01 at any group size; crowding was the allocation variable, and the elastics arm started 3° more divergent than the turbos-alone arm it beat on mandibular-plane change.
8. About 190 P values with no correction: the two skeletal between-group P values of .011 and .026 are what chance produces, and the LFH post-hoc block has its rows swapped and a standard error four times too large.
9. Fourteen of the 26 turbos-alone outcomes are the 2022 RCT's turbo arm to two decimals, and five more are within 0.02, in a different year with a different operator, wire, turbo and n; until the authors explain that, Group III is not an independent comparator and this study is one arm short.
10. This is CEBM Level 3 from a group that had already run the same question as an RCT; what survives is that adding something active to your turbos halves levelling time in a short-faced, mildly crowded adolescent, and nothing about which auxiliary, how much intrusion, or what happens to the face.
