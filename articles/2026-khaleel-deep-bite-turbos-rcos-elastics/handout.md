# Discussion handout — Khaleel & Al-Nimri 2026: deep bite with bite turbos alone, plus a reverse-curve wire, or plus posterior elastics

Khaleel R, Al-Nimri K. Assessment of three different techniques in correcting deep overbite: a prospective clinical study. Angle Orthod. 2026;96(5):528–535. DOI 10.2319/100925-850.1.

Design: prospective, non-randomised, single operator, arms by operator judgment | n: 81 enrolled, 80 completed, 27/27/27 as reported | Groups: I turbos + 0.016×0.022 NiTi RCOS cinched distal to 7s; II turbos + 0.014 NiTi + 3/16-inch medium box elastics; III turbos + 0.014 NiTi | Endpoint: days from bonding to overbite < 30% with Shimstock posterior contact, reviewed every 14 d | Measurement: cephalograms at bonding and end of levelling; corpus-axis and palatal-vault superimposition; Point I at 0.66 of incisor length.

## 30-second opening

"Everyone had anterior bite turbos; the question was whether a NiTi reverse-curve wire or box elastics on top speeds bite opening. It does: elastics took 44 days, the reverse curve 56, turbos alone 94, so an auxiliary roughly halves the time to a 30% overbite. The reverse curve paid with 7.5° of lower incisor flaring and 6 to 9° of molar tip-back; elastics flared 3.5° but opened the mandibular plane the most. The caveat is that the operator chose the arms, partly on cooperation, so the groups differ in the things that drive speed. I take the halving; the 11 days between the two auxiliaries is less than one review interval."

## Bottom line

A lower 0.016×0.022 NiTi reverse-curve wire or box elastics added to bite turbos cut time to a corrected overbite from 94.15 d to 55.72 d and 44.42 d (Table 2), too large a gap for the design to erase. The price is 7.46° of incisor proclination and 6–9° of molar tip-back with the reverse curve (Table 3), or the largest mandibular-plane opening (+1.84°) with elastics (Table 5). The "1 mm true intrusion" credited to the reverse curve was 0.99 mm against 0.71 mm and 0.86 mm without one, never tested between groups (Table 4).

## Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths
- Prospective, one operator, one bracket system and turbo mould in all 81 patients; lower arch bonded 7–7 throughout.
- Clear endpoint (overbite < 30% plus Shimstock contact) checked every 14 d, finer than the 2022 RCT's monthly reviews (literature).
- Corpus-axis and palatal-vault superimpositions with Greig's Point I; intraexaminer ICC 0.92–0.98 on 10 retraced cases.

Weaknesses
- Allocation by operator judgment on alignment and cooperation: Group III oldest (17.35 y) and most crowded (1.81 mm), Group II pre-selected to wear elastics (Table 1).
- Endpoint called every 14 d by the unblinded operator, so the 11.3 d elastics-over-RCOS gap sits inside one review interval; no control, no follow-up past T2.
- Standard errors and a 2022-table match raise questions about the turbos-only arm (see conflicts file); text and Table 2 also disagree on the Group I SD (10.52 vs 20.5 d) and Group III mean (94.15 vs 94.4 d).

## What it changes at the chair

- Turbos alone take about three months; an auxiliary brings bite opening to 6–8 weeks (94.15 vs 55.72 and 44.42 d, Table 2). Caveat: the turbos-alone arm was oldest and most crowded, so the gap is probably inflated.
- Choose between elastics and reverse curve on incisor position and compliance, not speed (11.3 d, P = .010, against a 14 d review interval). Caveat: the elastics arm was chosen for cooperation; 44 d does not apply to a poor wearer.
- A rectangular NiTi reverse curve as first lower wire flares the incisors about 7.5° even cinched in aligned arches (LI-CA +7.46 ± 2.52°, tip 1.01 mm forward, Tables 3–4). Caveat: mean crowding was 1.25 mm; more crowding means more flare, and this is the classic lower-arch torque error (Proffit).
- Do not pick the reverse curve for true intrusion (Point I 0.99 mm vs 0.71 and 0.86 mm with no intrusive mechanics, Table 4). Caveat: no between-group test, no sign convention; the 2022 RCT found 0.28 mm with turbos alone (literature).
- Elastics open the mandibular plane most and alone extrude the upper posteriors (Mn-Mx +1.84°, LFH +1.56%, Table 5; upper 0.28–0.42 mm, Table 4). Caveat: long faces and high smile lines were excluded, and extrusion suits only short or average faces with growth remaining (Proffit).

## Three questions to ask

1. "Point I moved 0.99, 0.71 and 0.86 mm in Table 4 and I could not find a between-group test; is the millimetre of true intrusion a reverse-curve effect, or what turbos on a 0.014 wire do on their own?"
*Why it lands: it turns the paper's own intrusion marker on its own conclusion, and Group III had no intrusive mechanics at all.*

2. "Elastics beat the reverse curve by 11 days with a confidence interval of 2 to 20, and reviews were every 14 days; for borderline incisors, would you trade those 11 days for 3.5° of proclination instead of 7.5°?"
*Why it lands: Table 2 and Table 3 in one breath, and the gap is below the resolution of the ruler.*

3. "Allocation was by operator judgment including cooperation, and elastic wear is what makes Group II work; this unit randomised in 2022 and 2025, so what stopped them here?"
*Why it lands: confounding by indication on the primary outcome, and the 3 mm crowding ceiling already made randomisation feasible.*

## Five questions you will be asked

1. **Primary outcome and how measured?** Days from bonding to overbite < 30% with Shimstock contact at 14-day reviews: 55.72/44.42/94.15 d (Table 2), every pair significant; no primary outcome declared, no registration.
2. **Groups comparable at baseline?** No P < .05 in Table 1, but Group III was oldest (17.35 vs 15.82 y), most crowded (1.81 vs 1.25 mm) and lowest Mn-Mx (22.89°); Group I had the deepest bite (5.51 mm) and ANB 5.14° despite a 5° ceiling. Every imbalance runs toward the result.
3. **What did the reverse curve do?** LI-CA +7.46°, tip −2.46 mm and 1.01 mm forward, Point I 0.99 mm; L6 +5.99° and L7 +8.94° distal, L4 +4.44° mesial; premolars extruded 1.05 mm, L7 distal cusp −0.64 mm (Tables 3–4).
4. **Which patient gets box elastics?** A compliant adolescent with average or reduced face height and incisors already forward: 44.42 d, +3.46° (Tables 2–3). Not a long face: Mn-Mx +1.84°, LFH +1.56% (Table 5), and hyperdivergent patients were excluded.
5. **Level of evidence; change your practice?** CEBM Level 3 cohort. Add an auxiliary when speed matters (39–50 d saved), choose it on incisor inclination and compliance, and do not quote intrusion or stability; the 2022 RCT (turbos alone 3.15 months, RCOS alone 4.85 months) is the higher-level evidence (literature).

## Exam radar

### Ten facts most likely to be tested

1. Prospective, non-randomised, single operator (JUST); arms by operator judgment on incisor alignment, inclination and cooperation.
2. Durations (Table 2): 55.72/44.42/94.15 d; ANOVA P < .001; every pair significant; I vs II 11.3 d, P = .010.
3. Endpoint: overbite < 30% plus Shimstock posterior contact, reviewed every 14 d; T1 bonding, T2 end of levelling.
4. LI-CA (Table 3): +7.46°/+3.46°/+1.55°, P < .001.
5. Point I: 0.66 × incisor length from the tip (Greig) on a corpus-axis superimposition at Pm (Ricketts); 0.99/0.71/0.86 mm, untested between groups.
6. Group I molar distal tipping L6 5.99°, L7 8.94°; L4 mesial 4.44° (Table 3).
7. Group II extrusion (Table 4): lower 0.90–1.19 mm, upper 0.28–0.42 mm, the only arm with upper extrusion.
8. Mn-Mx (Table 5): +1.35/+1.84/+0.69°, all significant within arms; between arms only II vs III (P = .011; FH-Mn P = .026).
9. Overbite reduction 3.92/3.65/3.57 mm from 5.51/5.33/5.13 mm; called comparable, never tested.
10. Inclusion: overbite > 40%, ANB 1–5°, average or reduced LFH, crowding ≤ 3 mm, average smile line; overjet > 6 mm excluded; ages 15.8–17.4 y.

### Three likely MCQ stems

1. Which arm reached overbite < 30% fastest, and by how much over the reverse-curve arm? A. Reverse curve, 11 d. B. Elastics, 11 d. C. Turbos alone, 39 d. D. Elastics, 50 d. **B** — Table 2: 44.42 vs 55.72 d.
2. A 16-year-old with a 55% overbite and already proclined lower incisors: which studied protocol adds the least proclination? A. Turbos + RCOS. B. Turbos + box elastics. C. Turbos + 0.014 NiTi alone. D. All equal. **C** — LI-CA +1.55°, smallest (Table 3).
3. The Conclusions credit the RCOS with 1 mm of true intrusion; which reading fits Table 4? A. Only Group I intruded. B. Point I moved similarly in all arms, untested. C. Group III proves 0.014 NiTi intrudes. D. Reflects group size. **B** — 0.99/0.71/0.86 mm, no test.

### One likely oral question

"A 17-year-old, Class I, 60% overbite, average face height, upright lower incisors, 2 mm crowding, wants the fastest bite opening. Which protocol, and what do you warn her about?"

She meets the inclusion criteria, so I would offer turbos with box elastics: 44.42 d against 55.72 d with the reverse curve and 94.15 d with turbos alone (Table 2). I would warn her that elastics work only when worn, that the lower posteriors extrude about 1 mm and the uppers 0.3–0.4 mm, and that the mandibular plane opens about 1.8° with a 1.6% rise in lower face height (Tables 4–5). Her upright incisors could tolerate the reverse curve's 7.46°, but elastics reach the same endpoint with 3.46° and no molar tip-back (Table 3). The paper measured only time to a 30% overbite in operator-allocated arms with no follow-up, so I would not promise total treatment time or stability, and a bite-plane retainer for years afterwards still applies (Proffit).

## Verdict in one line

Adding a reverse-curve wire or box elastics to bite turbos halves the time to a 30% overbite in short-faced, well-aligned adolescents; the ranking between the two, the millimetre of true intrusion and every skeletal contrast sit inside the noise of a non-randomised, operator-allocated design.
