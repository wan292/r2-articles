## Part 1. Multiple-choice questions (single best answer)

### Q1 (R). Design and reference standard

Which statement correctly describes the material and method of Doppel et al. 1994?

A. Fifty pairs of lateral cephalograms at least 3 years apart from patients with three maxillary tantalum implants at Björk's sites, chosen from about 700 records because all three implants superimposed perfectly; after implant registration, the maximum distance each of 17 anatomical structures deviated between tracings was measured.
B. Eighteen implanted subjects whose landmark displacements were compared under three superimposition methods in an occlusal-plane coordinate system.
C. Fifty untreated subjects with bilateral implants, traced by two blinded observers, with Dahlberg errors reported for each landmark.
D. Twenty patients with bone plates whose CBCT scans were registered voxel-wise on candidate regions.
E. Seven hundred implanted patients followed for 3 years, with structures ranked by a paired t-test against implant position.

**Answer:** A
**Rationale:** Methods: 50 of about 700 records (23 M, 27 F), films at least 3 years apart (mean 4.2 ± 0.96 y, Table I), three implants per Björk, perfect implant orientation plus four film criteria; one operator; maximum distance between contours after implant superimposition, averaged over several points for curved surfaces. B describes Nielsen 1989 and D describes Nguyen 2018 (both in this topic). C is wrong on three counts: the majority were treated, implants were unilateral (the Discussion says bilateral implants were not placed), and there was one unblinded observer. E inverts the sample and invents a test; the paper has no inferential statistics.
**Tests:** Recall of the design; separating this paper from its two companions.

### Q2 (R). Table II

Which option lists structures in the correct order of increasing mean deviation from the implant superimposition (Table II)?

A. Palate-internal 0.85 mm, anterior key ridge 0.95 mm, posterior key ridge 0.97 mm, palate-superior border 1.05 mm, A point 2.13 mm, PTM-vertical 2.39 mm
B. Posterior key ridge 0.85 mm, ANS-vertical 0.95 mm, PTM-horizontal 0.97 mm, palate-internal 1.05 mm, A point 2.13 mm, clinoid 2.39 mm
C. A point 0.85 mm, PNS 0.95 mm, palate-internal 0.97 mm, anterior key ridge 1.05 mm, orbital floor 2.13 mm, infraorbital foramen 2.39 mm
D. Anterior cranial base 0.85 mm, ethmoid triad 0.95 mm, clinoid 0.97 mm, posterior key ridge 1.05 mm, palate-internal 2.13 mm, ANS-horizontal 2.39 mm
E. Infraorbital foramen 0.85 mm, PTM-vertical 0.95 mm, PNS 0.97 mm, A point 1.05 mm, posterior key ridge 2.13 mm, anterior key ridge 2.39 mm

**Answer:** A
**Rationale:** Table II: palate-internal 0.85 ± 0.43, anterior key ridge 0.95 ± 0.57 (N = 40), posterior key ridge 0.97 ± 0.43, palate-superior 1.05 ± 0.52, palate-inferior 1.14 ± 0.60, lateral orbit 1.27, ANS-vertical 1.29, orbital floor 1.40, ANS-horizontal 1.43, PTM-horizontal 1.52, infraorbital foramen 1.67, A point 2.13, PNS 2.27, PTM-vertical 2.39, anterior cranial base 2.52, ethmoid triad 2.52, clinoid 2.64 mm. The three best numbers are all within 0.12 mm of each other, less than the stated tracing precision of ± 0.25 mm (my read).
**Tests:** Recall of the key numbers and the gap between the zygomatic group (about 1 mm) and the rejected points (2.1 to 2.4 mm).

### Q3 (A). Reading an ANS-registered superimposition

A resident superimposes a 12-year-old's cephalograms taken 4 years apart on the palatal plane registered at ANS. Compared with an implant superimposition, how will the result be distorted?

A. The implants appear to move posteriorly and superiorly, the incisors appear to move lingually, the molars appear to descend vertically and the occlusal plane rotates upward and forward; the vertical descent of the maxillary dentition is under-read.
B. The implants appear to move anteriorly and superiorly and both incisors and molars appear to move labially with minimal vertical descent.
C. The implants appear to move downward and posteriorly, the incisors appear to erupt substantially down and lingually and the occlusal plane rotates markedly downward and backward.
D. There is no distortion, because ANS deviated by only 1.29 mm vertically in Table II.
E. The molars appear to intrude and the incisors appear to extrude.

**Answer:** A
**Rationale:** Clinical implications, Fig 1: registration on the palatal plane at ANS makes the implants appear to move posteriorly and superiorly, the incisors lingual, the molars vertical and the occlusal plane up and forward. B is Fig 2 (PTM registration) and C is Fig 3 (infratemporal fossa). D misreads Table II: a 1.29 mm vertical and 1.43 mm horizontal deviation at ANS is the registration error. The size of the under-read is given by Nielsen 1989 (literature within this topic): 1.33 mm at the molar crown and 1.84 mm at the incisor edge over 4 years, about 30 % of molar and 50 % of incisor eruption (Nielsen Table IV).
**Tests:** Applying the figure set to a common clinical error and knowing the direction of the bias.

### Q4 (N). The internal-palate paradox

Table II gives the internal palate the smallest deviation of any structure (0.85 ± 0.43 mm), yet Conclusion 5 calls it of limited value as a registration area. Which statement best reconciles the two?

A. The authors made an arithmetic error in Table II that the Conclusions correct.
B. The maximum-distance metric records local mismatch of contours after implant registration and cannot detect an en-bloc descent or rotation of a palate that remodels in parallel; the authors found empirically that the structures did not superimpose well, and Fig 4 shows the method behaves like registration at ANS.
C. The internal palate was traced in only 27 of 50 films, so its mean is unreliable.
D. The internal palate is reliable and the Conclusions are wrong; the ABO uses it as the primary maxillary registration.
E. The internal palate deviated least because it is closest to the implants, which proves it is stable.

**Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Discussion, "Internal maxilla structure": the measured distance was small but, when viewed empirically, the structures did not superimpose well, and larger structures with a consistent best fit were preferred. Fig 4 (best fit of internal palatal structures) reproduces the ANS artefact: incisors lingual, molars vertical, occlusal plane up and forward. My read: a palate whose nasal side resorbs and oral side apposes keeps its shape while moving down, which a contour-to-contour distance does not see; Nielsen measured that en-bloc error at 1.08 to 2.24 mm over 4 years (Nielsen Table III). C is wrong: N = 50 for palate-internal (27 is PNS). E states the lever-arm caveat the authors raise, but proximity explains a small number, not stability, and the authors rejected the area anyway.
**Tests:** Understanding what the metric measures and why the paper overrides its own best number.

### Q5 (A). Rotation of the palatal plane

Table III reports that the palatal plane (ANS-PNS) rotated +0.92 ± 2.10° relative to the lateral-to-anterior implant line over 4.2 years, range −5° to +8°. What is the correct inference for using ANS-PNS to assess maxillary rotation on serial cephalograms?

A. ANS-PNS is a stable reference because its mean rotation is under 1°.
B. ANS-PNS rotates within the maxilla by a mean of about 1° anterior-down with a 13° range, so a change in the angle between SN and ANS-PNS mixes true maxillary rotation with palatal remodelling; maxillary rotation should be read from the implant line or the zygomatic structural fit, with SN transferred by cranial-base superimposition.
C. The negative values show that ANS resorbs and PNS apposes.
D. The range reflects tracing error of 0.5 mm only.
E. The maxilla did not rotate relative to the cranial base, because SN changed by only −0.56°.

**Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Results: theoretically there should be no angular change between ANS-PNS and the implant line, yet the range was 8° downward and backward to −5° upward and forward, which the authors say demonstrates the unreliability of ANS and PNS for superimposition and interpretation of change; the Discussion attributes it to rotation of the palatal plane and remodelling at both ends. Julius (ref 17) found about 3° of downward and backward rotation at the anterior end. A one-sample t on the printed mean and SD gives t = 3.10, P about .003 (derived), so the drift is systematic as well as variable. E misreads Table III: SN to the implant line was −0.56 ± 2.19° with a range of −6° to +9°, i.e. the maxilla rotated relative to the cranial base in varying directions (Conclusion 3). Proffit Ch 4 [6th p88] (study pages) describes the same thing as internal rotation masked by external remodelling.
**Tests:** Applying Table III to a routine cephalometric measurement; linking it to Proffit's rotation concept.

### Q6 (N). The cranial-base rows

The clinoid process (2.64 ± 1.43 mm), anterior cranial base (2.52 ± 1.13 mm) and ethmoid triad (2.52 ± 2.01 mm) show the largest mean deviations in Table II. The correct interpretation is:

A. The anterior cranial base is unstable during adolescence and should not be used for overall superimposition.
B. These deviations were measured after registration on the maxillary implants, so they record the displacement and rotation of the maxilla relative to the cranial base over 4.2 years, magnified by distance from the implants; they are not evidence of cranial-base instability.
C. Tracing error is larger for cranial-base structures because they are faint.
D. The implants migrated within the maxilla by about 2.5 mm.
E. The clinoid remodels more than the palate.

**Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Methods: the two tracings were registered on the implants and the deviation of every structure, including the cranial base, was measured in that frame; SN was transferred using Björk's cranial-base superimposition, which treats those structures as stable. Table III shows SN rotated −0.56 ± 2.19° relative to the implant line, and the Discussion ("Maxilla rotation") says a parallel relationship between cranial base and maxilla was not found. The authors also note that rotational misfit at closely spaced implants makes apparent error a function of distance (page 167): at 1° of misfit a structure 60 mm away moves about 1 mm (derived, tan 1°). D is excluded by the selection criterion of perfect implant orientation. The ABO cranial-base structures (anterior wall of sella, planum sphenoidale, greater wings, cribriform plate, ethmoidal crests, orbital plates of the frontal bone; literature, VERIFIED) are the ones Doppel used to transfer SN.
**Tests:** Separating the frame of reference from the structure being judged; a classic examiner trap.

### Q7 (A). The recommended method and its vertical rule

According to the Clinical implications and Conclusions, which maxillary superimposition most closely approximates implant superimposition, and what vertical rule accompanies it?

A. Palatal plane registered at ANS, with the palatal plane held level.
B. Best fit of the internal palatal structures, with ANS as the vertical registration point.
C. The anterior and posterior contours of the zygomatic process of the maxilla, allowing the floor of the orbit to rise more than the palatal plane lowers in a ratio of 1.5 : 1.
D. The infratemporal fossa registered at the posterior palate, with PNS held fixed.
E. A perpendicular from the anterior cranial base through the infraorbital foramen, registered at the foramen.

**Answer:** C
**Rationale:** Clinical implications (page 165) and Discussion (page 167): the best method appears to be on the anterior and posterior contours of the zygomatic arches (the paper's wording; the structure is the zygomatic process of the maxilla) with the orbital floor rising more than the palatal plane lowers at 1.5 : 1, citing Fig 7, which is in fact the implant superimposition; no figure shows the combined method and no table measures the ratio. The ratio is Björk & Skieller's 3 : 2 apposition-to-resorption ratio restated (Introduction, ref 4) and the ABO teaches it as 3/5 : 2/5 (literature, VERIFIED). A and B are Figs 1 and 4, D is Riedel's method (Fig 3, abandoned in 1974), E is Moss & Greenberg's infraorbital method (ref 13), which Table II rejects (1.67 ± 1.31 mm, N = 34).
**Tests:** Knowing the practical recommendation and its origin.

## Part 2. Short-answer questions

### Q8 (R). Sample, selection and measurement

Describe (a) the sample and how it was selected, (b) the implant sites, (c) the measurement metric, and (d) how tracing error and head position were controlled.

**Model answer:**
(a) Fifty pairs of lateral cephalograms (23 males, 27 females) from implanted patients treated at the University of Washington from 1967 to 1975; T1 11.9 ± 1.4 years (range 8.7 to 16.0), T2 16.0 ± 1.7 years (11.9 to 20.3), interval 4.2 ± 0.96 years (3.0 to 6.4) (Table I). The majority were treated with edgewise appliances, headgear, Class II or III elastics, with and without extractions; treatment type and timing were disregarded. About 700 records were reviewed and 50 chosen (7 %, derived); most were rejected for loss or change in orientation of one or more implants. The 50 had perfect orientation of all three implants on both films and met four criteria: at least 3 years apart, high film quality, same cephalostat with all facing right, and the same head tilt and rotation. (b) Björk's sites: below ANS (anterior implant), in the zygomatic process of the maxilla (lateral implant), and at the border between the hard palate and the alveolar process medial to the first molar. (c) After registration on the implants, the maximum distance each of 17 structures deviated between the T1 and T2 tracings, measured with digital calipers reading to 0.01 mm; for curved surfaces several points were measured and averaged (Table II). Rotations of ANS-PNS and of SN relative to the lateral-to-anterior implant line were measured separately (Table III), with SN transferred from the first film by Björk's cranial-base superimposition. (d) One operator traced everything with a 3H lead kept to a needle point; two successive tracings of 10 films at 2-week intervals established ± 0.25 mm, stated as a total tracing error of 0.5 mm (Thurow). Head position was judged visually on four areas: inferior and posterior borders of the mandible, the orbits, and the infratemporal fossae, because Julius found the zygomatic implant can shift up to 3 mm with head rotation and patients rotate up to 3° and tip up to 5° in a cephalostat. My read: no Dahlberg error, no second observer and no per-landmark error are reported.

**Marking points (6):** 50 of about 700, selected on perfect three-implant fit; ages and interval from Table I with at least 3 years; majority treated; the three Björk implant sites; maximum distance between contours after implant registration, averaged for curved surfaces; tracing error ± 0.25 mm (0.5 mm) and head-position criteria (or Julius's 3 mm / 3° / 5°).

### Q9 (A). Correcting a resident's reading

A 10-year-old with a Class II division 1 malocclusion is treated with cervical headgear for 2 years. From a superimposition on the palatal plane registered at ANS, a resident reports 1.5 mm of upper first molar extrusion, 2° of upper incisor retroclination and 0.5 mm of molar distalisation. Using Doppel's Fig 1 and Tables II and III, and Nielsen's tables where they help, state the direction of error in each reading and give its likely size where the papers permit.

**Model answer:**
Vertical: Fig 1 shows that ANS registration makes the implants appear to move posteriorly and superiorly, so the whole maxilla's descent is subtracted from the teeth; the true molar extrusion is larger than 1.5 mm, not smaller. Nielsen (literature within this topic) measured the under-read on implants: molar crown 1.33 mm and incisor edge 1.84 mm over 4 years in 10- to 14-year-olds, about 30 % and 50 % of eruption (Nielsen Table IV). Scaling linearly, 2 years of pubertal growth hides about 0.6 to 0.7 mm at the molar and 0.9 mm at the incisor (derived, crude), so the molar probably extruded about 2 mm. Incisor inclination: Fig 1 shows the incisors appearing to move lingually on ANS registration, so 2° of retroclination is partly artefact; the paper gives no degree value for this, and Doppel's Table III shows the palatal plane itself rotated +0.92 ± 2.10° (range −5° to +8°) relative to the implants, so the angular error is of the same order as the reading and its direction varies with the case. Molar distalisation: the horizontal error of palatal registration is case-dependent (Doppel Fig 1 shows a posterior shift, Nielsen's 4-year means show the best fit placing teeth about 1.2 to 1.7 mm more anterior than implants; Nielsen Tables II and IV), so 0.5 mm of distalisation is inside the method's horizontal uncertainty of about 1 mm over 2 years and cannot be claimed in either direction. All three readings are also inside or near the 0.5 mm tracing error Doppel states. The correct superimposition is on the anterior and posterior contours of the zygomatic process with the orbital floor rising 1.5 times what the palatal plane lowers (Clinical implications), after which the molar extrusion will read larger, the incisor retroclination smaller or absent, and the distalisation unresolved.

**Marking points (6):** names the Fig 1 artefact (implants posterior and superior, teeth appear lingual, descent under-read); vertical error direction (extrusion under-read) with Nielsen's 30 to 50 % or 1.3 to 1.8 mm per 4 y; incisor retroclination partly artefact; horizontal direction case-dependent with Table III rotation range; tracing error 0.5 mm against 0.5 mm distalisation; names the zygomatic method with the 1.5 : 1 rule.

### Q10 (N). What the ranking can and cannot support

Appraise the paper's ranking of maxillary structures in Table II. State what the ranking can support, what it cannot, and why, with reference to the metric, the sample, the statistics and the observer.

**Model answer:**
What it supports: a clinically useful three-tier separation. The zygomatic process (0.95 to 0.97 mm) and, numerically, the palate (0.85 to 1.14 mm) stayed within about 1 mm of the implants over 4.2 years, while A point (2.13 ± 1.63), PNS (2.27 ± 1.75), PTM-vertical (2.39 ± 1.69) and the infraorbital foramen (1.67 ± 1.31 mm) drifted 1.7 to 2.4 mm; the gap of 1 to 1.5 mm is two to three tracing errors and survives the paper's weaknesses. The figure set (Figs 1 to 6) shows, on one representative case, the direction in which each anatomical method distorts tooth movement, which agrees with Nielsen's measured under-read (literature within this topic). It is the largest implant series of its kind (50 against Björk & Skieller's 9 and 21, Baumrind's 31 and Nielsen's 18), with a minimum 3-year interval and explicit head-position and film criteria. What it cannot support: (1) Fine ordering. The metric is a maximum for point landmarks but an average of several points for curved surfaces, and an average is never larger than a maximum, so surfaces (key ridges, palate borders, internal palate) win and points (ANS, PNS, A point, PTM, foramen) lose partly by construction; the top three means are within 0.12 mm of each other against a stated precision of ± 0.25 mm; five rows share a minimum of 0.15 mm. (2) Stability as opposed to small drift. Distances are unsigned, so the posterior key ridge, which moved posteriorly in all 50 cases, scores the same as a surface that wobbles at random; the anterior key ridge, with no preferred direction (27.5 / 35 / 37.5 %), is in fact the better registration surface by Doppel's own logic. (3) Any verdict with a threshold. There is no inferential statistic, no reliability threshold and no method error table; the palate borders are "next in reliability" on page 164 and "not reliable" in Conclusion 6 with no change in the data, and the internal palate's best number was overruled by an undocumented visual judgement. (4) Generalisation to routine films or untreated growth. The 50 are the 7 % of records in which the three implants still coincided, which selects pairs without transverse rotation or with compensating head position (the Discussion concedes this); the majority were treated with headgear and elastics, with no count of untreated subjects and no split by treatment, sex or maturity (T1 8.7 to 16.0 years); the interval varied from 3.0 to 6.4 years with no per-year rate. (5) The 1.5 : 1 ratio and the recommended combined method, which appear in no table and are illustrated by Fig 7, the implant superimposition itself. One unblinded operator traced, superimposed and judged best fit. My read: the paper justifies which structures to use and which to avoid, but not the decimals, and the posterior leg should be treated as an appositional guide rather than a registration surface.

**Marking points (6):** three-tier separation of about 1 mm versus 2 mm or more as the robust finding; mixed maximum/average metric favouring surfaces; unsigned distance hides bias (posterior key ridge 100 % posterior); no statistics, no threshold, palate verdict changes between Results and Conclusions; selection on perfect implant fit (7 % of 700) and treated/untreated mix; ratio and recommended method without data; single unblinded observer.

## Part 3. Oral defence

### Q11 (A). "Show me how you would superimpose this case and tell me what could fool you."

**Case.** An 11-year-old boy, Class II division 1, treated with cervical headgear for 18 months then fixed appliances; lateral cephalograms at 11.3 and 15.0 years on the same cephalostat, hand-traced. The examiner lays the two tracings in front of you.

**Examiner question 1. Start. What goes first, and on what?**
Model answer: The cranial-base superimposition, by Björk's structural method: the anterior wall of sella below the anterior clinoids, the planum sphenoidale, the greater wings of the sphenoid, the cribriform plate and ethmoidal crests, and the orbital plates of the frontal bone (ABO list; literature, VERIFIED), with SN drawn on T1 and transferred to T2 after that fit, which is exactly how Doppel transferred SN (Methods). This gives total change of the maxilla and mandible relative to the cranium. Doppel's Table II reminds me what to expect: over 4 years the maxillary implants moved about 2.5 mm away from the cranial-base structures (clinoid 2.64 ± 1.43, anterior cranial base 2.52 ± 1.13 mm), and the maxilla rotated −0.56 ± 2.19° relative to SN; those numbers are maxillary displacement, not cranial-base error.

**Examiner question 2. Now the maxilla. Which structures, and how do you orient it?**
Model answer: Doppel's recommendation, which is the ABO method: register on the anterior and posterior contours of the zygomatic process of the maxilla (key ridge), posterior leg 0.97 ± 0.43 mm and anterior leg 0.95 ± 0.57 mm from the implants over 4.2 years (Table II). Anteroposterior orientation on the anterior surface, because it has no preferred direction of change (27.5 % anterior, 35 % posterior, 37.5 % none; Results), and the posterior surface kept parallel but allowed to sit slightly behind, because it apposed posteriorly in all 50 cases. Vertically, set T2 so the orbital floor rises 1.5 times what the palatal plane lowers (Conclusion 2; ABO 3/5 : 2/5; Björk & Skieller 3 : 2). I do not register on ANS, PNS, PTM, A point or the infraorbital foramen (Table II: 1.29 to 2.39 mm), and I use the superior surface of the palate only as a vertical check, because the internal palate did not superimpose well despite its 0.85 mm number (Conclusion 5).

**Examiner question 3. What could fool you before you even start?**
Model answer: Head position. Julius (Doppel ref 17) found the zygomatic implant shifts up to 3 mm with head rotation and that patients rotate up to 3° and tip up to 5° while held in a cephalostat; Doppel checked tilt and rotation on the inferior and posterior mandibular borders, the orbits and the infratemporal fossae, and I would check the same four areas, in particular that the double contours of the key ridges and orbital floors are the same width on both films. The anterior key ridge could be traced accurately in only 80 % of Doppel's films and PNS was visible in about half (Table II, N = 40 and 27), so if the contour is not clear on both films it cannot register anything. Tracing error is 0.5 mm for one operator (Methods), so differences under about 0.5 mm, and under 1 mm for a landmark 40 mm from the registration area if I am 1° off (derived), are not interpretable.

**Examiner question 4. Suppose you had registered on the palatal plane at ANS. What would the headgear look like?**
Model answer: Doppel's Fig 1: the implants would appear to move posteriorly and superiorly, the incisors lingually and the molars vertically, with the occlusal plane rotating up and forward. The maxilla's own descent would be subtracted from the teeth, so molar extrusion would be under-read and incisor retroclination over-read. Nielsen (literature within this topic) put the vertical under-read at 1.33 mm for the molar and 1.84 mm for the incisor over 4 years, about 30 % and 50 % of eruption, and 1.08 to 2.24 mm at skeletal landmarks (Nielsen Tables III and IV). In a headgear case that matters, because the vertical effect of the headgear on the molar is the thing I am trying to read.

**Examiner question 5. And PTM, which many older papers used?**
Model answer: Fig 2: registration at PTM makes the implants appear to move anteriorly and superiorly and the teeth labial with minimal descent, so headgear distalisation would be under-read and extrusion hidden. Table II gives PTM 1.52 ± 0.99 mm horizontally and 2.39 ± 1.69 mm vertically, and PNS 2.27 ± 1.75 mm with increase in length, because the maxilla grows at its posterior end (Björk & Skieller, ref 4; Riedel abandoned the infratemporal-fossa method in 1974 for this reason, Introduction). Fig 3 shows the fossa method rotating the occlusal plane markedly down and back.

**Examiner question 6. You said the posterior key ridge moved in every case. How can it be a reference?**
Model answer: It is a consistent, small bias rather than a random error: 0.97 ± 0.43 mm over 4.2 years, posterior in all 50 (Results; Conclusion 1), consistent with Björk & Skieller's apposition greatest inferiorly (ref 14). That is why the anteroposterior registration goes on the anterior surface and the posterior surface is used as a parallel guide with the T2 contour allowed to sit behind T1. Doppel's own Fig 6 shows what happens if you register on the posterior leg: slight superior and anterior movement of the implants, more apparent forward movement of the molars and less incisor eruption. Nielsen rejected Riedel's registration on this surface for the same reason; the two papers agree on the biology and differ only in whether to keep it as a guide.

**Examiner question 7. The angle SN to palatal plane increased 2° between the films. Did the maxilla rotate?**
Model answer: Not necessarily. Doppel's Table III shows the palatal plane rotated +0.92 ± 2.10° (range −5° to +8°) relative to the implant line inside the maxilla, and SN rotated −0.56 ± 2.19° (range −6° to +9°) relative to the same line; a 2° change in SN-PP is within both spreads and may be remodelling of the nasal floor at ANS and lengthening at PNS rather than displacement. Julius found about 3° of anterior-down rotation of the palatal plane by remodelling alone (ref 17). To separate true rotation from remodelling I would read the orientation of the zygomatic structural fit relative to the transferred SN, which is what Doppel's implant line stands in for; Proffit's internal versus external rotation of the maxilla (Ch 4 [6th p88], study pages) is the same distinction.

**Examiner question 8. Mandible, briefly.**
Model answer: Björk's structures: the anterior contour of the chin, the inner cortex of the inferior symphysis, the symphyseal trabecular pattern, the mandibular canal and the lower contour of a molar germ before root formation (as listed by Nguyen 2018 and the ABO; literature). Nguyen's 3D test against bone plates kept the chin (0.37 ± 0.16 mm) and the inner symphysis (0.40 ± 0.14 mm) and rejected the third-molar crypt (1.94 ± 0.06 mm), with the canal excluded beforehand. I would not use the lower border or a corpus axis through Xi, because they absorb remodelling and rotate with growth (Springate & Jones 1998; registry, VERIFIED there).

**Examiner question 9. If you had two CBCT scans instead of films?**
Model answer: Voxel-based regional registration on the equivalent stable regions, as in Nguyen 2018: for the mandible chin plus symphysis, with two observers agreeing to 0.12 ± 0.11 mm and ICC 0.998, below the 0.3 mm voxel. For the maxilla the published 3D regions use the zygomatic processes and the palate (Ruellas 2016; literature), which matches Doppel's ranking, but no 3D maxillary region has been validated against implants the way Doppel's structures were. Growth monitoring is not among Proffit's listed CBCT indications (study pages), so the scans would have to exist for another reason.

**Examiner question 10. What would you tell a colleague this paper proves, in one sentence?**
Model answer: That against implants over four adolescent years the only maxillary structures that stayed within about a millimetre were the two contours of the zygomatic process, that ANS, PNS, PTM, A point and the infraorbital foramen drifted 1.3 to 2.4 mm, and that the palate's good number hides an en-bloc descent, so the structural method with the 1.5 : 1 vertical rule is the one to use; and that the ranking is descriptive, selected on perfect implant fit, measured with a 0.5 mm tracing error and never statistically tested.
