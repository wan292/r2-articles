# Critical appraisal — Doppel 1994

Doppel DM, Damon WM, Joondeph DR, Little RM. An investigation of maxillary superimposition techniques using metallic implants. *Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop* 1994;105(2):161-168. University of Washington, Seattle; two MSD theses (Doppel, Damon) combined; supported in part by the UW Orthodontic Alumni Association. Films 1967-1975.

Numbers come from the printed tables (page images of Tables I-III, p. 163-164), which take precedence over the running text where the two disagree. Recomputations are mine from the printed means, SDs and N and are labelled as such. Literature numbers come from the research pack with its VERIFIED / UNVERIFIED tags.

## Verdict in three lines

1. **Design label:** retrospective, descriptive validation of 17 anatomic maxillary structures against a reference standard (three Björk tantalum implants) on archived serial lateral cephalograms; one operator; metric = maximum (or, for curved surfaces, averaged) distance between T1 and T2 contours after implant registration; six anatomic registration methods then illustrated on one representative case (Figs 1-7). No hypothesis test, no comparison group, no pre-specified reliability threshold.
2. **Level of evidence for a methods study:** a diagnostic-accuracy-type study against an imperfect reference standard with a non-consecutive sample selected on the reference standard's behaviour and no blinding, Oxford CEBM level 3b-4 equivalent (my mapping, UNVERIFIED as a formal grade); in journal-club terms a hypothesis-generating implant drift table, large for its type (50 vs Björk & Skieller's 9 and 21, Baumrind's 31, Nielsen's 18).
3. **The one thing that limits it most:** the output is an unsigned drift distance scored by one unblinded operator with a 0.5 mm tracing error, so it cannot separate a consistent bias (posterior key ridge, posterior drift in 50 of 50 cases) from random error, and 52-59 % of the best means (0.85-0.97 mm) is tracing noise (my arithmetic). Every verbal verdict ("reliable", "limited value", "not reliable") rests on a threshold that is never stated and was overruled by eye for the best-scoring row.

## Study question

The paper says: are there "discernable, stable, anatomic landmarks in the maxilla" that can reliably be used for maxillary superimposition, identified by evaluating cephalograms of implanted patients (abstract; p. 162). Operationally: after registering T1 and T2 tracings on three implants, how far does each of 17 structures sit from its own earlier contour (Table II), and how much do the palatal plane and the transferred SN rotate relative to the implant line (Table III)?

My read: the question is the right one and the sample is unrepeatable (tantalum pins in children), which is why the paper is still cited by the ABO as validity evidence for its maxillary superimposition. But it is not a comparison of superimposition methods in Nielsen's sense; it is a drift table under one superimposition, plus pictures. The recommended method (both zygomatic contours with the orbital floor rising 1.5× the palatal lowering) is never itself measured.

## Design, sample and selection (direction of bias)

**Sample (Table I).** 50 film pairs, 23 males, 27 females; T1 11.9 ± 1.4 y (range 8.7-16.0), T2 16.0 ± 1.7 y (11.9-20.3), interval 4.2 ± 0.96 y (3.0-6.4). About 700 implant records reviewed; 50 chosen = 7.1 % (my arithmetic). "The majority" treated (edgewise, headgear, Class II or III elastics, extraction and non-extraction); the number untreated is never given and the Results later call all 50 "treated".

**Selection criteria.** Perfect orientation of all three maxillary implants on both films, then: ≥ 3 y apart; high film quality; same cephalostat, all facing right; same head tilt and rotation, judged visually on the inferior and posterior mandibular borders, the orbits and the infratemporal fossae.

**Direction of bias, selection on the reference standard.** Björk & Skieller (ref 4) showed the anterior-lateral implant distance shortens with growth because the two maxillae rotate transversely; Korn & Baumrind 1990 (literature, VERIFIED abstract) put posterior maxillary widening at 0.43 mm/y. A film pair on which all three implants still coincide is therefore one in which that rotation was absent, small, or cancelled by a change in head rotation. The authors say so (p. 166-167: implants may have "only matched well" because of head-position alteration). The 50 are the pairs in which the gold standard behaved, which plausibly makes the anatomy look more stable than it is and specifically flattens the anterior zygomatic surface, the structure most sensitive to transverse rotation and head rotation. Bias direction: toward smaller drift values for the key ridges, i.e. toward the paper's conclusion.

**Direction of bias, treatment.** Headgear, Class II/III elastics and extractions all act on the maxilla and its dentition. Baumrind 1987 (ref 18) found treated and untreated subjects remodel differently; the authors blame treatment for the rotational spread (SN vs implant line −6° to +9°) and for A-point variability but cannot separate it. Bias direction: toward larger drift and wider rotation ranges for ANS, A point and the palatal plane, i.e. also toward the paper's rejection of those landmarks. The two biases push in the same direction as the conclusions, which is why the conclusions are probably right but the numbers are not transferable.

**Age and interval.** T1 8.7-16.0 y spans late mixed dentition to post-pubertal with no maturity indicator; Kurihara (ref 15) describes resorptive fields changing through the deciduous and mixed dentitions. Interval 3.0-6.4 y, unadjusted: a structure with constant drift per year scores higher in longer pairs; no per-year rate and no correlation with interval was computed.

## The "gold standard" and its own error

- **Implant sites** (Björk, ref 1): below ANS (anterior), in the zygomatic process (lateral), at the palate-alveolar border medial to the first molar. Doppel superimposed exactly on all three; Björk & Skieller registered on the lateral implant and oriented on the implant line to absorb transverse rotation (p. 165). A perfect three-point fit of a rigid tracing on markers that genuinely move relative to each other is only possible when they did not move (see selection above).
- **Lever arm.** The three implants are a few centimetres apart; a small rotational misfit scales with distance, so distant structures (clinoid 2.64 ± 1.43, anterior cranial base 2.52 ± 1.13, PTM vertical 2.39 ± 1.69 mm) accumulate more apparent error than near ones (palate, key ridges). The authors state this (p. 167). Part of the Table II ranking is geometry, not biology; and the cranial-base rows are maxillary displacement relative to the cranial base, not evidence against cranial-base superimposition.
- **Head position.** Julius (ref 17): a zygomatic implant shifts up to 3 mm with head rotation; patients rotate up to 3° and tip 5° in a cephalostat. Doppel controlled this by visual matching of four bilateral structures only; unilateral implants mean transverse rotation could not be detected. 3 mm is three times the best mean drift.
- **Tracing error.** One operator; 10 films retraced after 2 weeks gave "±0.25 mm", restated as 0.5 mm total and equated to Thurow 1951 (ref 23). No table, no Dahlberg, no per-landmark error. Calipers read to 0.01 mm, two orders of magnitude finer than the tracing. Five Table II rows share a minimum of 0.15 mm (anterior key ridge, PTM vertical, ANS vertical, ANS horizontal, A point), which looks like a floor value rather than a measurement.
- **Magnification.** Not mentioned; same cephalostat for both films, so it cancels within a pair.
- **Metric.** "Maximum distance" in the title of Table II, but an average of several points for curved surfaces (Methods p. 163; Results call the internal-palate figure an "average distance between contours"). The table does not say which rows are which; averages are always ≤ maxima, so surfaces (key ridges, palate borders, internal palate, orbital floor) are favoured over points (ANS, PNS, A point, PTM, foramen) by construction.
- **Unsigned.** Distance has no direction: a landmark drifting 1 mm posteriorly in every case (posterior key ridge) scores the same as one wobbling ±1 mm at random, yet the first is a bias and the second is noise.

## Measurement and statistics

**What was reported.** N, mean, SD, minimum and maximum for 17 structures (Table II) and two angles (Table III). Nothing else: no confidence intervals, no tests, no method-error table, no correlation.

**What was not tested.**

| Question the data could answer | Status | My arithmetic from the printed values |
|---|---|---|
| Does the palatal plane rotate systematically relative to the implants? | Not tested; called "quite variable" | One-sample t vs 0: +0.92° ± 2.10, N = 50, SE 0.30°, t = 3.10, df 49, P ≈ .003. A systematic anterior-down rotation of about 0.9° over 4.2 y, same direction as Julius's ~3° |
| Does SN rotate relative to the implant line? | Not tested | −0.56° ± 2.19, SE 0.31°, t = −1.81, P ≈ .08 (NS); 95 % reference range −4.9° to +3.7° |
| Do structures differ from each other? | Not tested; verbal ranking only | SEs of the top four means are 0.06-0.09 mm, so 0.85 vs 0.95 vs 0.97 vs 1.05 mm are not separable; the "reliable / next / unreliable" tiers are within one SE of each other at the boundary |
| Does the mean drift exceed tracing error? | Not tested | 0.5 mm is 59 % of 0.85 mm and 52 % of 0.97 mm; only rows ≥ 1.5 mm are clearly more than three tracing errors |
| Treated vs untreated, male vs female, younger vs older | Not tested; untreated N unknown | Table I gives sex subgroups for age only; pooled SDs (1.40 / 1.65 / 0.96 y) match the printed totals, so Table I is internally consistent |
| Drift vs interval length (3.0-6.4 y) or vs distance from the implants | Not tested | Per-year drift of the best rows 0.20-0.23 mm (0.85 / 4.2; 0.97 / 4.2), crude linear scaling |
| Observer error per landmark | Not reported | Single "±0.25 mm" from 10 retraces |

**Multiplicity.** None, because nothing was tested. The cost is that every verbal verdict ("significant amount of change" for the foramen, "clinically acceptable reliability" for the key ridges, "too low a reliability" for the rest) is a judgement without a threshold or a P value.

**Distribution.** The metric is an unsigned distance, right-skewed by nature; maxima sit 2.1-5.0 SDs above their means (ethmoid triad 11.99 mm is 4.7 SD above 2.52; posterior key ridge 3.12 mm is 5.0 SD above 0.97; my arithmetic). Medians and interquartile ranges would have served better than mean ± SD.

**Direction counts.** The anterior key ridge: 27.5 % anterior, 35 % posterior, 37.5 % no change; of the 40 traceable films that is 11 / 14 / 15 (my arithmetic), though the paper never states the denominator. The posterior key ridge: posterior in 100 %.

**Text-vs-table.** Infraorbital foramen "32 %" traceable in the text vs N = 34 (68 %) in Table II; PNS "52 %" vs N = 27 (54 %); Table III minimum and maximum columns swapped (8° / −5°; 9° / −6°); the 1.5 : 1 ratio printed once as "1:5 to 1 mm" (p. 165). Details in `conflicts.md` section A.

## Generalisability

- **Who:** adolescents 8.7-20.3 y, mostly treated, mostly growing, University of Washington 1967-1975, hand-traced film cephalograms, three unilateral maxillary implants. The results describe landmark drift over about four years of growth plus treatment. They say nothing about adults, where the palate and key ridges are presumably near-stable, or about short treatment intervals: at 0.20-0.25 mm/y the best structures would drift about 0.2-0.25 mm in a 12-month case (my arithmetic, linear scaling), inside the 0.5 mm tracing error.
- **What:** 2D lateral projections. Transverse rotation of the maxillae, which the authors invoke to explain the anterior ridge, is invisible on the film; 3D voxel registration (Nguyen 2018 for the mandible; Ruellas 2016 and Fan 2023 for the maxilla, literature) replaces the question.
- **Why it still travels:** implanted children are an ethically unrepeatable cohort, so Doppel, Nielsen 1989 and the Björk and Baumrind series remain the only direct anatomic-vs-implant evidence; every later validation uses surgical hardware or CBCT reproducibility instead. The ABO cites Doppel and Nielsen as proof that its structural method is "reliable and valid" (ABO page, VERIFIED); Graf 2022 (literature, VERIFIED abstract) reads the same literature as 25 of 27 studies at high risk of bias with no method proved accurate.

## Strengths

- Largest anatomic-landmark-vs-implant series of its time: 50 film pairs against Björk & Skieller's 9 (1977) and 21 (1972), Baumrind's 31 and Nielsen's 18.
- Minimum 3-year interval, so growth changes are large relative to tracing error for the unstable landmarks.
- Strict and explicit inclusion rules (perfect implant orientation, same cephalostat, four head-position checks) and an explicit list of 17 traced structures with a stated tracing convention (posterior and inferior surfaces).
- One operator throughout, a reported retrace, and calipers rather than ruler.
- Directly usable output: millimetres of drift per landmark with range, so "A point 2.13 ± 1.63 mm, up to 6.55 mm" can be quoted at the chair.
- Figures 1-7 are a teaching set: the same case under six anatomic registrations and on implants, showing incisors appearing lingual (ANS), labial (PTM), over-erupted (infratemporal fossa) or mesial (posterior key ridge).
- The Discussion anticipates most of the criticisms above (head position, lever arm, curved surfaces, empirical fit) and the conclusion is conditional ("assuming that superimposition on metal implants is the most accurate method").
- The ranking it produced is the one the ABO still teaches (anterior and posterior zygomatic contours, orbital floor 3/5 to nasal floor 2/5), and it agrees with Nielsen 1989 on everything that changes practice.

## What would have made it better

1. Register on the lateral implant and orient on the implant line (Björk & Skieller's way) or at least report both fits, so transverse rotation does not disqualify 93 % of the archive and does not flatten the anterior ridge.
2. Signed displacement in a defined coordinate system (as Nielsen did, Downs occlusal plane), so bias and noise separate; report medians and IQRs for the unsigned distances.
3. One metric per row, labelled, or a column saying "maximum" vs "average of n points".
4. A pre-specified reliability threshold, and a test of each structure against it and against tracing error; one-sample tests of the two rotations against zero.
5. A second observer and a per-landmark method-error table (Dahlberg or ICC), with the 10-film retrace reported numerically.
6. Count and stratify treated vs untreated, and give a maturity indicator; test drift against interval length and against distance from the implants.
7. Measure the recommended combined method (both zygomatic contours + 1.5 : 1) against the implants and give it a Table II row; measure orbital-floor apposition and nasal-floor resorption so Conclusion 2 has data.
8. Fix the text to the tables (foramen 68 %, PNS 54 %, Table III columns, "1:5", ref 22 "1910") and name the structure consistently (zygomatic process, not arch).

## Journal-club one-liners

1. "This is a 50-case drift table, not a method comparison: nothing was tested, so 'reliable' means 'moved less than about a millimetre in four years'."
2. "The 50 were the 7 % of 700 records on which all three implants still coincided; the sample was selected on the gold standard behaving, which flatters the anatomy."
3. "The best number in Table II, internal palate 0.85 mm, was overruled by eye; if the metric can be overruled for one row it can be questioned for all seventeen."
4. "The posterior key ridge moved back in 50 of 50 cases and is still the most reliable landmark; that is a consistent bias, not stability, and the ABO method uses it knowing that."
5. "Palatal borders are 'next in reliability' on page 164 and 'not reliable' on page 168; the data did not change, the threshold did, and the threshold is never stated."
6. "The 1.5 : 1 orbital-floor to nasal-floor rule appears in the abstract and conclusions but in no table, method or figure; it is Björk's 3 : 2 restated, and once misprinted as 1:5."
7. "Table II compares maxima for points with averages for surfaces; averages are always smaller, so every surface wins and every point loses by construction."
8. "Tracing error is 0.5 mm and the best mean drift is 0.85-0.97 mm; in a 12-month treatment interval the signal would be about 0.2 mm, inside the noise."
9. "The cranial-base rows (2.5-2.6 mm) are the maxilla moving against a stable base, not cranial-base instability; the trap is reading Table II as if every row were a candidate reference."
10. "The palatal plane rotated +0.92° ± 2.10 relative to the implants; the paper calls that variable, but a one-sample t gives P ≈ .003, so it is systematic anterior-down rotation with variability on top."
