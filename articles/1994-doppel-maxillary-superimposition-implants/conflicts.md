# Conflicts and disagreements — Doppel 1994

Use this file when you need to challenge a number, a claim or a citation in Doppel DM, Damon WM, Joondeph DR, Little RM. *Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop* 1994;105(2):161-168. Section A is the paper against itself (every row checked against the page images of Tables I-III and the printed text), B is the paper against the other two papers in this topic (Nielsen 1989, Nguyen 2018), C is the paper against the wider literature, D against Proffit, E against the hub registry (Khaleel 2026), F lists what stays open and G is the verdict. Page numbers are journal pages (161-168). Numbers from Nielsen and Nguyen were re-read from their texts; everything else from the literature comes from the research pack and carries its VERIFIED / UNVERIFIED tag. "The paper says", "the literature says" and "my read / my arithmetic" are used deliberately.

### How to raise these politely

- Lead with the table: "Table II gives N = 34 for the infraorbital foramen, which is 68 %; the text says 32 %. I read the table as primary, is that right?"
- Frame item 3 as a threshold question, not an error: "The Results rank the palatal borders next after the key ridges; the Conclusions call them unreliable. Where would the authors have drawn the line, about 1 mm?"
- For item 4, give the authors credit first: "They flag themselves that the internal palate's number flatters it. The interesting question is what that says about the metric for the other rows."
- For item 5, ask where the ratio comes from: "Is the 1.5 : 1 measured here, or is it Björk & Skieller's 3 : 2 restated? I could not find a table for it."
- For item 11, separate bias from noise: "The posterior ridge moved back in 50 of 50 cases. Does a consistent 1 mm drift count as reliable, or as a known bias we correct for?"
- Keep items 6, 15-19 for one sentence at most; they do not change the science.

## B. Against the other two papers in this topic

### Nielsen 1989 (maxilla, 2D, 18 implanted subjects, 4 ± 0.56 y)

Doppel cites Nielsen as ref 20 and summarises it correctly (30-50 % under-read of eruption with the ANS-registered best fit; the structural method differed from implants horizontally but not vertically). The two papers measure different things: Nielsen measures landmark displacement under three superimpositions and tests the differences (Tables I-IV); Doppel measures how far each structure drifts after one superimposition (implants) and tests nothing.

### Nguyen 2018 (mandible, 3D CBCT, bone plates as regional implants)

Nguyen does not cite Doppel (it is a mandible paper), but the logic is the same: validate anatomic regions against markers that do not grow, then test reproducibility.

| Point | Doppel says | Nguyen says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scale of error | Best 2D maxillary regions drift 0.85-0.97 mm over 4.2 y (Table II) against a 0.5 mm tracing error; one operator, no interobserver test | Chin 0.37 ± 0.16 mm and symphysis 0.40 ± 0.14 mm surface error over about 1 y (Table; abstract prints 0.4 ± 0.15); two observers 0.12 ± 0.11 mm (abstract misprints ± 1.1), ICC 0.998 (95 % CI 0.995-1.000), < 0.25 mm over the whole mandible and < 0.12 mm over 75 % of it | Complementary: Doppel's best structures carry 0.2-0.25 mm/y of drift plus 0.5 mm tracing noise (my arithmetic); Nguyen's voxel registration resolves about 0.1-0.4 mm. The two numbers are not interconvertible (maximum contour deviation vs absolute mean surface distance) |
| Distance from the reference markers | "Slight, unavoidable rotational errors would make the apparent error of a given structure a function of its distance from the implants" (p. 167); cranial-base rows 2.52-2.64 mm | Third-molar crypt 1.94 ± 0.06 mm (P = .0025) far from the plates; Nguyen answers the distance objection with the antegonial notch (less error than the crypt, Fig 5) and the ≈ 2 mm deposition near menton seen on the same registration | AGREES on the lever-arm problem; Nguyen tests it, Doppel only states it |
| Reference standard is loaded by treatment | "majority" treated with headgear and elastics; rotation range attributed to "treatment variation" | All 20 Aim-1 patients had Class III elastics on the plates; immobility "confirmed clinically" only | AGREES that both gold standards sit in treated jaws; neither paper separates treatment from growth |
| What the 2D structural lists survive as | Anterior + posterior zygomatic contours recommended; ANS, PNS, PTM, A point, foramen, palate borders rejected | Of Björk's mandibular list, chin and inner symphysis confirmed, third-molar crypt rejected, mandibular canal excluded a priori (Björk; Krarup 2005) | Together they give the complete structural set: zygomatic contours with 1.5 : 1 for the maxilla, chin + inner symphysis for the mandible, Björk's anterior cranial base for the overall (Doppel's SN transfer, Methods p. 163) |
| Observer error | 10 films retraced, "±0.25 mm", no table | Two independent observers, ICC with sample-size calculation (n = 25 for r0 .75 vs r1 .95, α .05, power .80) | CONFLICTS in standard: Nguyen is what a reliability section should look like; Doppel's is the minimum |

## C. Against the wider literature

All numbers in this section come from the research pack unless marked otherwise; VERIFIED means the pack saw the number in an abstract or full text, UNVERIFIED means recollection or secondary report.

### The implant foundations Doppel builds on

- **Björk & Skieller 1977, Br J Orthod 4:53-64 (Doppel ref 4; VERIFIED via the paper).** Nine boys: anterior zygomatic surface "strikingly stable" sagittally in all nine; orbital apposition : nasal-floor resorption 3 : 2; anterior-lateral implant distance shortens with transverse rotation of the two maxillae. **PARTIAL.** Doppel reproduces the ratio (1.5 : 1) but finds the anterior surface variable in direction in 62.5 % of 40 films. One-liner: "Doppel's 50 treated cases on a three-implant fit versus Björk's nine on a lateral-implant fit: the ratio survives, the 'strikingly stable' surface does not."
- **Björk & Skieller 1972, Am J Orthod 62:339-383 (ref 14; VERIFIED via the paper).** 21 untreated parapubertal subjects at 6-year intervals; compensatory alveolar apposition and nasal-floor resorption vary with maxillary rotation; posterior zygomatic surface appositional, most at its inferior part. **AGREES.** Doppel found posterior apposition in 50 of 50. One-liner: "Doppel's 100 % posterior drift of the key ridge is Björk 1972 confirmed, not a new finding."
- **Björk & Skieller 1977, Trans Eur Orthod Soc 53:51-55 (ref 16; VERIFIED via the paper).** The structural method: transfer SN after anterior-cranial-fossa and sella-wall superimposition, then register on the anterior zygomatic contour; vertical registration acknowledged as weak. **AGREES.** Doppel's SN transfer uses it and his 1.5 : 1 rule is the missing vertical anchor. One-liner: "The 1.5 : 1 rule is Doppel's answer to the vertical gap Björk admitted in 1977."
- **Julius 1971, UW MSD thesis (ref 17; VERIFIED only as reported by Doppel and the ABO deck).** 21 untreated implant subjects: posterior and key-ridge surfaces parallel; palatal plane rotates about 3° down-and-back at its anterior end; heads rotate up to 3° and tip 5° in the cephalostat; a zygomatic implant moves up to 3 mm with head rotation. **AGREES.** Doppel's +0.92° mean is the same direction, smaller; his head-orientation criteria come from here. One-liner: "The 3 mm head-rotation error is three times Doppel's best landmark drift."
- **Korn & Baumrind 1990, J Dent Res 69:1298-1306 (literature, VERIFIED abstract).** 31 implant subjects 8.5-15.5 y: posterior maxillary widening 0.43 mm/y (SD 0.18, P < .001). **AGREES / CONTEXT.** Independent evidence for the transverse rotation Doppel invokes for the variable anterior ridge. One-liner: "A three-point implant fit can only be perfect if the 0.43 mm/y widening was absent or cancelled by head rotation, which is Doppel's own caveat."

### Quantitative implant studies of the palate

- **Baumrind, Korn, Ben-Bassat & West 1987 part 1, AJODO 91:29-41 (ref 18; VERIFIED abstract).** 31 implant subjects, 19 with data 8.5-15.5 y: PNS remodelled downward 2.50 mm (SD 2.23), ANS 1.56 mm (SD 2.92); ANS-PNS rotated 1.1° forward (SD 4.6, range 11.3° forward to 6.7° backward); palate lengthens posteriorly; treated and untreated differ. **AGREES.** Doppel: palatal plane +0.92 ± 2.10° (range −5° to +8°), PNS lengthens, A point variable. One-liner: "Baumrind's 1.1° ± 4.6 and Doppel's 0.9° ± 2.1 say the same thing: the palatal plane is a measurement, not a reference."
- **Baumrind et al. 1987 part 2, AJODO 91:463-474 (ref 19; VERIFIED abstract).** Anatomical best fit understates downward palatal remodelling by about 0.3-0.4 mm/y and makes ANS and A point appear to move forward. **AGREES.** Doppel Fig. 1 shows the same masking. One-liner: "Doppel's Fig. 1 is Baumrind part 2 drawn on one case."
- **Isaacson, Worms & Speidel 1976, Am J Orthod 70:290-303 (literature, VERIFIED abstract).** Tooth movement relative to bone is best measured on implants; bony surfaces remodel too much for small movements. **AGREES.** One-liner: "Isaacson said in 1976 what Doppel's Table II quantifies: no maxillary surface is still enough for sub-millimetre tooth movement."
- **Gu & McNamara 2008, Angle Orthod 78:967-976 (literature, VERIFIED abstract; mm values not in abstract).** 10 untreated implant subjects: the then-ABO maxillary method (lingual palatal curvature plus best fit) underestimated vertical displacement and overestimated forward movement. **PARTIAL.** Supports Doppel's rejection of palatal registration; the current ABO zygomatic + orbital-floor method, which is Doppel's recommendation, was not the version tested. One-liner: "Even the ABO's method is not implant-exact, and the version that failed is the palatal one Doppel also rejects."

### Methods Doppel rejects or illustrates (Figs 1-6)

- **Broadbent 1937 / Ricketts 1960, 1981: palatal plane at ANS (literature, VERIFIED via Nielsen Fig. 1).** **CONFLICTS** (Fig. 1; Conclusion 6). One-liner: "Broadbent himself saw A point move backward with it; Doppel and Nielsen show why."
- **Moore 1959, Am J Orthod 45:323-352: palatal plane at PTM (ref 7).** **CONFLICTS** (PTM vertical 2.39 ± 1.69 mm; Fig. 2). One-liner: "PTM moves 2.4 mm vertically and 1.5 mm horizontally in four years; it is the growth front, not a reference."
- **Riedel 1974, Angle Orthod 44:194-212: infratemporal fossa + posterior palate (ref 12).** **CONFLICTS** (Fig. 3; Riedel abandoned it in 1974). One-liner: "Riedel gave this method up because the maxilla lengthens at PNS; Doppel's Fig. 3 shows the occlusal plane rotating markedly down and back."
- **McNamara 1981, Angle Orthod 51:269-300: best fit of internal palatal structures (literature, VERIFIED via Nielsen Fig. 1e).** **PARTIAL.** Best number (0.85 ± 0.43 mm) but worst empirical fit; Fig. 4 behaves like ANS registration. One-liner: "The internal palate wins Table II and loses the Discussion."
- **Moss & Greenberg 1967, Angle Orthod 37:151: infraorbital foramen constant, 47 Eskimo skulls (ref 13).** **CONFLICTS.** Traceable in 34/50, 1.67 ± 1.31 mm (max 5.79); Björk & Skieller also disputed it. One-liner: "A landmark you cannot see on a third of films and that moves 1.7 mm cannot register anything."
- **Luder 1981, Eur J Orthod 3:205-222: anterior zygomatic contour registered at the key-ridge tip (ref 24, listed but not cited; Nielsen Fig. 1f).** **PARTIAL.** Fig. 5 shows only slight downward implant drift; Nielsen says the method under-reads eruption because the key ridge lowers by apposition. One-liner: "Doppel and Nielsen disagree on how bad Luder's method is: slight drift versus systematic under-read."
- **Kurihara, Enlow & Rangel 1980, Angle Orthod 50:98-106 (ref 15; VERIFIED via the paper).** Resorptive fields on the anterior maxilla and anterior zygomatic surface, person-variable. **PARTIAL.** Doppel's 35 % posterior change is consistent with it; Nielsen found no resorption with implants. One-liner: "Histology says the anterior ridge can resorb; implants say it rarely shows on a cephalogram."

### Mandible and cranial base (context)

- **Springate & Jones 1998, AJODO 113:263-270 (registry, VERIFIED there).** 23 implanted children: Björk's structural method close to implants; Ricketts' corpus axis produced rotation from differential growth at Xi; errors mostly < 2 mm but ≥ 4 mm in about 10 % at molars and incisors; error grows with distance from the core. **AGREES** with Doppel's distance-from-implant argument. One-liner: "Springate is Doppel for the mandible: drift scales with distance from the stable core."
- **Houston & Lee 1985, Eur J Orthod 7:127-135 (literature, VERIFIED abstract, no numbers).** Every cranial-base superimposition method tested showed appreciable error. **CONTEXT.** Doppel's SN transfer carries error that the paper does not quantify. One-liner: "Doppel's cranial-base rows (2.5-2.6 mm) are maxillary displacement, not cranial-base error, but the SN transfer itself has an error Houston measured and Doppel did not."
- **Graf, Dritsas, Ghamri & Gkantidis 2022, Eur J Orthod 44:477-490 (literature, VERIFIED abstract; whether Doppel is among its 10 maxillary studies is UNVERIFIED).** 27 studies, 25 at high risk of bias; no cephalometric superimposition method proved accurate. **CONTEXT.** Doppel's design (selected sample, no blinding, no test) would score high risk on QUADAS-2. One-liner: "Graf 2022 and the ABO cite the same two papers, Doppel and Nielsen, to opposite ends."

### Conventions

- **ABO Cephalometric Review deck, rev. 3.14.2025 (VERIFIED, text extracted).** Maxillary regional superimposition on both zygomatic processes (anterior and posterior legs), the superior surfaces of both orbital floors and the palatal floor; AP orientation on the anterior zygomatic surface; orbital-floor apposition 3/5 to nasal-floor resorption 2/5; head-position warnings cite Damon 1970, Masumoto 1970 and Julius 1971, Doppel's refs 21-22 and 17; the ABO page cites Doppel 1994 and Nielsen 1989 as its validity evidence. **AGREES.** The ABO method is Doppel's Conclusion 1 + 2 written as a protocol. One-liner: "If an examiner asks where the ABO's 3/5 : 2/5 comes from, the answer is Björk & Skieller 1977 confirmed by Doppel 1994."
- **Fan et al. 2023, BMC Oral Health 23:655 (literature, VERIFIED abstract).** Mini-screw-referenced non-rigid registration in 10 CBCT pairs (12.9 → 14.8 y): stable regions were the infraorbital rims, the maxilla around the piriform aperture, the infrazygomatic crest and the hard palate more than 1 cm behind the incisive foramen. **PARTIAL.** The infrazygomatic crest and orbital region agree with Doppel; Fan keeps the posterior hard palate, which Doppel scored 1.05-1.14 mm and called unreliable. One-liner: "In 3D the posterior palate comes back as a stable region; in 2D it was a 1 mm drift, which may be the metric, not the bone."

## E. Against the hub registry (Khaleel 2026)

Khaleel & Al-Nimri 2026 (`2026-khaleel-deep-bite-turbos-rcos-elastics`) superimposed the maxilla on "the palatal vault (Broadbent)" and the mandible on the Ricketts corpus axis Xi-Pm registered at Pm, in 15.8-17.4-year-olds over 44-94 days, and reports upper-arch tooth changes of 0.07-0.42 mm (Table 4) with intra-examiner ICC 0.92-0.98 and no superimposition error estimate (registry; Khaleel `key-numbers.md`). What Doppel implies, stated as inference:

1. **Which method it is.** "Broadbent" is the palatal plane registered at ANS (Nielsen Fig. 1a); "palatal vault" may instead mean the internal-palate best fit (Doppel Fig. 4; McNamara). Doppel says Fig. 4 "is similar to" Fig. 1, so the inference does not depend on which Khaleel used. Khaleel's paper does not state the registration point.
2. **Direction of bias.** Fixed: on either palatal registration the implants "appear to move posteriorly and superiorly" and apparent vertical descent of the teeth is reduced (Fig. 1, Fig. 4). Applied to Khaleel: upper posterior extrusion is under-read, apparent upper-incisor intrusion is over-read. The horizontal direction varies with the case (palatal-plane rotation −5° to +8°, Table III), so no fixed correction to Khaleel's upper-incisor horizontal values (0.46-0.63 mm) can be stated.
3. **Size of bias over 6-13 weeks (my arithmetic, crude, UNVERIFIED for 16-17-year-olds).** Doppel's palatal rows drift 0.85-1.14 mm per 4.2 y, about 0.20-0.27 mm/y if linear, so 0.02-0.07 mm over 44-94 d, and less at 16-17 y than at Doppel's mean 11.9-16.0 y. The method bias is immaterial to Khaleel's tooth values.
4. **Resolution is the real problem.** Doppel's tracing error is 0.5 mm (±0.25 mm duplicate tracings, Methods p. 163) and his best landmarks drift 0.85-0.97 mm with SDs of 0.43-0.57 mm. Khaleel's Group I and III upper posterior values (0.07-0.15 mm) with SDs of 0.07-0.08 mm, and the 0.2-0.35 mm between-arm differences behind "Group II significantly more upper extrusion" (no P printed), sit below that tracing error. The inference: a 2D palatal superimposition cannot establish 0.2-0.4 mm differences in upper posterior extrusion; the direction (elastics extrude upper posteriors) is plausible, the magnitude unmeasurable with this method.
5. **Corpus axis.** Doppel does not study the mandible, but his lever-arm statement (error "a function of its distance from the implants", p. 167) is the mechanism behind Springate & Jones 1998 (registry, VERIFIED there): Pm sits on Nguyen's stable chin, Xi sits in remodelling ramus bone, so rotation about Pm scales to the molars (Khaleel `critical-appraisal.md` already gives 0.5-0.7 mm per degree at 30-40 mm). Over 44-94 d the rotation is probably ≤ 0.2° (my estimate), so again the 0.5 mm tracing error, not the method, is the limit on Khaleel's Point I values of 0.71-0.99 mm (registry line "within this method's error band" stands).
6. **"Nongrowing" at 15.8-17.4 y (registry A15).** Doppel's T2 extends to 20.3 y with remodelling still present, and Björk & Skieller's 3 : 2 ratio runs to age 20 in boys (Nielsen Fig. 2; literature). Over Khaleel's intervals this does not touch the tooth values, but it supports the registry caveat against calling the sample nongrowing.

Suggested registry addition for Khaleel: "Group II upper posterior extrusion 0.28-0.42 mm vs about 0.08 mm, 'significantly more', no P: the 0.2-0.35 mm difference is below the 0.5 mm 2D tracing error (`1994-doppel-maxillary-superimposition-implants`) and palatal registration under-reads maxillary descent (direction only; size over 44-94 d about 0.05 mm, derived)."

## F. Open questions

1. What was the reliability threshold? The implied cut lies between 0.97 mm (accepted) and 1.05 mm (rejected) and is never stated.
2. Which Table II rows are maxima and which are averages of several points? The two metrics are mixed and not labelled.
3. Where does the 1.5 : 1 ratio come from? No orbital-floor or nasal-floor measurement is described.
4. How many of the 50 were untreated, and did treated and untreated differ in drift or rotation? Baumrind (ref 18) says they should.
5. Is the anterior zygomatic surface variable because it remodels (Kurihara), because the two maxillae rotate transversely (Björk & Skieller; Korn & Baumrind 0.43 mm/y widening), or because head rotation moved the implants (Julius, 3 mm)? The paper offers all three and tests none.
6. Does drift scale with interval length (3.0-6.4 y) or with distance from the implants? Neither correlation was run.
7. What is the per-landmark tracing error? One figure (0.5 mm) covers 17 structures, points and surfaces alike.
8. Would the recommended combined method (both zygomatic contours + 1.5 : 1) have a Table II row of its own, and how close to zero would it be? It was never measured.
9. How far does any of this transfer to adults or to 12-18-month treatment intervals, where the signal would be about 0.2-0.25 mm against 0.5 mm of noise (my arithmetic)?

## G. One-paragraph verdict

My read: Doppel 1994 is the largest direct comparison of maxillary anatomic landmarks against metallic implants (50 film pairs, 4.2 ± 0.96 y, Table I) and its ranking is still the basis of the ABO maxillary superimposition: both zygomatic contours, orbital floor rising 1.5 times what the palate lowers, never ANS, PNS, PTM, A point, the infraorbital foramen or the palatal borders. Its weaknesses are also plain on the page: a sample selected on the gold standard behaving (50 of about 700), one unblinded operator, a 0.5 mm tracing error that is 52-59 % of the best mean drift, a metric that mixes maxima with averages and cannot tell a consistent 1 mm posterior drift (posterior key ridge, 50 of 50 cases) from random error, no inferential statistics, a ratio in the conclusions with no table behind it, and a Results-versus-Conclusions reversal on the palatal borders. Against Nielsen 1989 it agrees on everything that matters for practice (palatal registration hides vertical change; PTM and PNS are unusable; the zygomatic contours are the reference) and differs on two points worth a discussion each: whether the anterior zygomatic surface is stable (Nielsen and Björk yes, Doppel variable) and whether the appositional posterior surface should be used at all (Nielsen no, Doppel yes with the apposition allowed for). Against Nguyen 2018 it is the 2D baseline that 3D voxel registration improves by roughly an order of magnitude. For Khaleel 2026 its message is not that the palatal-vault method biased the result but that a 2D tracing cannot resolve the 0.1-0.4 mm upper-arch differences the paper reports.
