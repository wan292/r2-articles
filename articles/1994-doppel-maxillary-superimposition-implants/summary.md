# An investigation of maxillary superimposition techniques using metallic implants (Doppel et al. 1994)

Doppel DM, Damon WM, Joondeph DR, Little RM. Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop 1994;105(2):161–168; University of Washington, Seattle; no DOI printed.

## Bottom line

In 50 implanted adolescents followed for 4.2 ± 0.96 y, no maxillary structure stayed exactly on the implants. The anterior and posterior contours of the zygomatic process came closest, 0.95 ± 0.57 and 0.97 ± 0.43 mm (Table II); ANS, PNS, PTM and A point drifted 1.3–2.4 mm, and the palatal plane rotated −5° to +8° against the implant line (Table III). The recommended method registers on both zygomatic contours with the orbital floor rising 1.5 times what the palate lowers, a rule stated without a table.

## What they did

- 50 film pairs from about 700 University of Washington implant records in which all three tantalum implants had kept perfect orientation; 23 males, 27 females; T1 11.9 ± 1.4 y, T2 16.0 ± 1.7 y; films at least 3 y apart with matched head position (Table I); most subjects treated.
- One operator; 10 films retraced at 2 weeks gave ± 0.25 mm, quoted as a 0.5 mm total tracing error.
- T2 registered on T1 by the implants; maximum deviation of 17 structures measured with calipers; ANS–PNS and the transferred SN measured against the implant line; six anatomic registrations shown on one case against implants (Figs 1–7). No statistical tests.

## What they found

- Table II: internal palate 0.85 ± 0.43 mm; anterior key ridge 0.95 ± 0.57 mm (traceable in 80 %); posterior key ridge 0.97 ± 0.43 mm (traceable in 100 %, posterior in every case); palate borders 1.05–1.14 mm; A point 2.13 ± 1.63, PNS 2.27 ± 1.75 (n = 27), PTM vertical 2.39 ± 1.69 mm; cranial-base rows 2.52–2.64 mm, the maxilla moving relative to the cranium (my read).
- Table III: palatal plane +0.92 ± 2.10°; SN −0.56 ± 2.19° (−6° to +9°).
- Internal palate: best number, poor fit by eye, "of limited value".
- Figs 1 and 4: palatal registrations make the implants appear to move posteriorly and superiorly; incisors look lingual and descent is under-read.

## Why it matters

Palatal superimposition, as in Khaleel 2026 (registry), hides maxillary descent; Nielsen 1989 (cited here) put the error at 30–50 % of eruption. The zygomatic method with a 1.5 : 1 allowance is Björk & Skieller's 3 : 2 and the current ABO maxillary structural method (VERIFIED, ABO workshop deck). Table II supplies the exam reasons ANS, PNS, PTM and A point are never registration points.

## One-line caveat

Descriptive, untested, no threshold, on the 7 % of records in which the gold standard held, one unblinded tracer, and a 1.5 : 1 ratio and combined method that appear in no table.

## Method at a glance

| Method | What is registered | What it gets right | What it gets wrong | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Implants (reference) | Three tantalum implants, all superimposed | Growth-free reference | Closely spaced, so error grows with distance; head rotation can shift the zygomatic implant up to 3 mm (Julius, cited) | Fig 7; Discussion |
| Palatal plane at ANS (Broadbent, Ricketts) | ANS–PNS line, registered at ANS | Easy, landmarks usually visible | Implants appear to move posteriorly and superiorly; incisors look lingual; occlusal plane rotates up and forward; the palatal plane itself rotates −5° to +8° | Fig 1; Table III |
| Palatal plane at PTM (Moore) | ANS–PNS line, registered at PTM | Nothing specific | PTM drifts 2.39 ± 1.69 mm vertically and 1.52 ± 0.99 mm horizontally; teeth appear labial with minimal descent | Fig 2; Table II |
| Infratemporal fossa at posterior palate (Riedel, abandoned 1974) | Posterior zygomatic surface and posterior hard palate | Surface traceable in 100 % | Surface is appositional (posterior change in 50 of 50); maxilla lengthens at PNS; occlusal plane rotates markedly down and back | Fig 3; Results |
| Best fit of internal palate (McNamara) | Trabecular structure above the incisors | Lowest mean deviation, 0.85 ± 0.43 mm | Poor empirical fit; behaves like ANS registration (incisors lingual, descent reduced) | Fig 4; Discussion; Conclusion 5 |
| Anterior zygomatic contour at its inferior point (Luder) | Anterior key ridge | Nearest to implants: only slight downward implant drift, teeth descend vertically | Traceable in 80 %; direction variable (27.5 % anterior, 35 % posterior, 37.5 % none) | Fig 5; Table II |
| Posterior zygomatic contour + internal palate | Posterior key ridge, oriented on the internal palate | Posterior surface always traceable | Implants drift slightly superiorly and anteriorly; molars look more forward, incisors erupt less | Fig 6 |
| Recommended: both zygomatic contours, orbital floor rising 1.5 × palatal lowering | Anterior and posterior key ridge, vertical by the 1.5 : 1 rule | Closest to implants by the authors' judgement; matches Björk & Skieller 3 : 2 | Never measured as a combined method; the ratio has no table | Conclusions 1–2; Clinical implications |
