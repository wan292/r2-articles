# Clinical implications — Doppel 1994

Doppel DM, Damon WM, Joondeph DR, Little RM. An investigation of maxillary superimposition techniques using metallic implants. Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop 1994;105(2):161–168. Retrospective implant study, University of Washington: 50 film pairs (23 M, 27 F) chosen from about 700 implant records because all three maxillary tantalum implants had stayed in perfect orientation; T1 11.9 ± 1.4 y, T2 16.0 ± 1.7 y, interval 4.2 ± 0.96 y (Table I); most subjects treated. T2 was registered on T1 by the implants and the maximum distance each of 17 maxillary and cranial-base structures drifted was measured (Table II); the palatal plane and the transferred SN line were measured against the implant line (Table III); six anatomic registrations were applied to one case (Figs 1–6). No statistical tests.

Attribution on this page: "the paper says" = printed in Doppel 1994; "the literature says" = other sources, marked VERIFIED or UNVERIFIED; "my read" = my interpretation.

## What this changes or confirms when reading a ceph or a CBCT

1. **Register the maxilla on the zygomatic process, not on the palatal plane.** The paper says the posterior key ridge drifted 0.97 ± 0.43 mm (traceable in 100 % of films) and the anterior key ridge 0.95 ± 0.57 mm (80 %), against ANS 1.29 ± 0.86 mm vertically and 1.43 ± 1.14 mm horizontally, A point 2.13 ± 1.63 mm (maximum 6.55), PNS 2.27 ± 1.75 mm (visible in about half the films) and PTM 2.39 ± 1.69 mm vertically (Table II). Confirms Björk & Skieller and Nielsen 1989 (the paper's own refs 4 and 20) and matches the zygomatic-process structures the ABO maxillary method now requires (literature, VERIFIED from the ABO workshop deck). Caveat: the posterior surface moved posteriorly in all 50 cases, so it is a consistent appositional bias rather than a stable surface; the paper uses it as an orientation guide, not a registration point.

2. **ANS–PNS is not a fixed line inside the maxilla, so "palatal plane rotation" over a growth interval is partly remodelling.** The paper says the angle between ANS–PNS and the implant line changed +0.92 ± 2.10°, range −5° to +8° (Table III), when theoretically it should not change at all. My arithmetic: the mean differs from zero (one-sample t = 3.10, P ≈ .003, derived), a small systematic downward-backward tilt of the anterior palate on top of the spread, matching Julius's 3° at the anterior end (cited). At the chair: a 1–2° change in SN–PP or Mx–Mn over 2–4 years of growth is inside this remodelling band and cannot be read as true maxillary rotation; true rotation was −0.56 ± 2.19° (−6° to +9°) when read from the transferred SN against the implants.

3. **A palatal-plane or palatal-vault superimposition hides vertical change and tilts tooth movement in a known direction.** The paper says that with the palatal plane registered at ANS (Fig 1) or a best fit of the internal palate (Fig 4) the implants "appear to move posteriorly and superiorly", incisors look lingual, molars seem to descend vertically and the occlusal plane rotates up and forward. The size of that error is in Nielsen 1989: 30–50 % of maxillary eruption under-read over 4 years (cited in the paper; VERIFIED in a2). Khaleel 2026 (registry) measured upper-arch changes on a "palatal vault (Broadbent)" superimposition; over its 44–94-day intervals the growth component is about 0.05 mm (derived from the drift per year), so the direction, not the size, is the point to raise.

4. **Set the vertical by letting the orbital floor rise about 1.5 times what the nasal floor drops.** The paper says the orbital floor was appositional and the palatal plane lowered by resorption "with a ratio of about 1.5 to 1" (Discussion; Conclusion 2). The literature says the same number three ways: Björk & Skieller 3 : 2 (cited), the ABO's 3/5 : 2/5 (VERIFIED, deck) and Nielsen's 1 : 1 operational version, which left a small vertical over-read (a2). Caveat: no table in this paper measures the ratio; the only tabulated pair that resembles it, orbital floor 1.40 mm over palate superior border 1.05 mm, gives 1.33 : 1 (derived) and is not the same quantity.

5. **Know the resolution of a hand-traced 2D superimposition before you quote a tooth movement.** The paper says tracing error is 0.5 mm (10 films retraced at 2 weeks, ± 0.25 mm) and the best anatomic structures drift 0.85–0.97 mm over 4.2 years. My arithmetic: that is about 0.2 mm per year of registration drift on top of 0.5 mm of tracing noise, so a 12-month pair cannot establish a tooth movement under about 0.5 mm, and 1° of registration tilt is 0.5–0.7 mm at a molar 30–40 mm away (derived). For comparison the literature says voxel-based CBCT registration of the mandible reaches 0.12 ± 0.11 mm between observers (Nguyen 2018, a3, VERIFIED).

6. **Head position is a precondition, not a detail.** The paper says films were accepted only with the same head tilt and rotation, checked on the inferior and posterior mandibular borders, the orbits and the infratemporal fossae, because a zygomatic implant can shift up to 3 mm with head rotation and patients rotate 3° and tip 5° in a cephalostat (Julius, cited). Even so the anterior key ridge could be traced in only 80 %, the infraorbital foramen in 34 of 50 and PNS in 27 of 50 (Table II). My read: this is the 2D problem that voxel registration of a CBCT removes (a3), and the reason the authors ask for bilateral implants they did not have.

## Practical notes

The paper states its recommended method in two sentences and never demonstrates it as a combined registration; the steps below reconstruct it (my read) from the Methods, the Results on each structure, Björk & Skieller's method as the paper summarises it (ref 16) and Fig 7.

1. **Qualify the film pair.** Same cephalostat, same head tilt and rotation. Check four bilateral areas: inferior border of the mandible, posterior border of the mandible, orbits in the same relative position, infratemporal fossae the same distance apart. If the double contours differ, do not superimpose; the anterior zygomatic contour moves up to 3 mm with head rotation (Julius, cited).
2. **Trace with a needle point and a fixed convention.** 3H lead, sharpened throughout. Where a structure has two surfaces, trace the posterior and the inferior one on both films. Trace both contours of the zygomatic process (anterior surface and posterior surface, the key ridge), both orbital floors, the superior border of the palate (nasal floor), ANS, PNS, the incisor, the first molar, plus the cranial-base structures (anterior wall of sella, anterior cranial fossa) for the overall superimposition.
3. **Do the cranial-base superimposition first.** Register T2 on T1 on the anterior cranial fossa and the anterior wall of sella (Björk, ref 16), draw SN on T1 and transfer it to T2. This is how the paper measured maxillary rotation; it keeps the growth of nasion and sella out of the answer.
4. **Anteroposterior registration on the anterior zygomatic contour.** Slide T2 so the anterior surface of the zygomatic process coincides with T1. Expect no change in 37.5 %, slight anterior or posterior change in the rest (Results); if the surface is not traceable, as in 20 % of this sample, say so rather than guess.
5. **Orientation on the posterior zygomatic contour.** Keep the T2 posterior surface parallel to the T1 posterior surface, sitting slightly posterior to it (apposition in 50 of 50 cases). Do not register on it.
6. **Vertical by the 1.5 : 1 rule.** Position T2 so that the orbital floor sits above T1 by about 1.5 units for every 1 unit the T2 nasal floor sits below T1 (Conclusion 2). The literature says Björk & Skieller 3 : 2 and the ABO 3/5 : 2/5 (VERIFIED, deck); Nielsen's 1 : 1 gave a slight vertical over-read of descent (a2).
7. **Read change only after registration.** Tooth movement is read relative to this zygomatic registration; maxillary rotation from the transferred SN against ANS–PNS; palatal remodelling as the gap between the T1 and T2 nasal floors. Never register on ANS, PNS, PTM, A point, the infraorbital foramen or the palatal borders (Conclusion 6).
8. **State the error budget.** Tracing 0.5 mm (the paper's own figure); registration drift about 0.2 mm per year for the best structures (derived); 1° of tilt = 0.5–0.7 mm at the molars (derived). Report any change under 0.5 mm as within error, and retrace a sample of films at an interval, as the paper did with 10 films at 2 weeks.

## What the paper does NOT tell you

- Any number for the recommended combined method (both contours plus 1.5 : 1): Table II scores single structures, Figs 5 and 6 show single contours, Fig 7 is the implant superimposition.
- Where the 1.5 : 1 ratio comes from: no measurement of orbital-floor apposition or nasal-floor resorption is described; the number equals Björk & Skieller's 3 : 2.
- How many subjects were untreated, or whether treated and untreated, boys and girls, younger and older subjects differed; headgear and elastic effects are mixed into every drift and rotation value.
- Whether any difference between structures is statistically real; no test, no reliability threshold, and a Results verdict on the palatal borders ("next in reliability") that Conclusion 6 reverses.
- Which rows are a true maximum and which an average over several points; averages are always smaller, which favours surfaces (key ridges, palate) over points (ANS, PNS, A point, PTM).
- The direction of drift for anything except the key ridges; unsigned distances cannot separate a systematic 1 mm shift from a random ± 1 mm wobble.
- Per-landmark method error, inter-examiner error, or magnification; one operator, no blinding.
- Anything about adults, intervals under 3 years, the mandible, CBCT or 3D registration, or transverse maxillary rotation (implants were unilateral).
- Why five rows share a minimum of 0.15 mm when the calipers read to 0.01 mm.
- Whether the sample overlaps the Julius 1971 and Masumoto 1970 theses from the same implant archive.

## One sentence for a patient or a junior

For a junior: superimpose the maxilla on the anterior and posterior contours of the zygomatic process with the orbital floor rising about 1.5 times what the nasal floor drops, and never on ANS, PNS, PTM or A point, because over four years of growth those points drift 1.3–2.4 mm and the palatal plane itself tilts anywhere from −5° to +8°.

For a patient: when we compare your two x-rays we line them up on the cheekbone part of the upper jaw, because that is the part that changes least while you grow.
