# Busse 2023 · Exam questions

Source: Busse JW, Casassus R, Carrasco-Labra A, et al. Management of chronic pain associated with temporomandibular disorders: a clinical practice guideline. BMJ 2023;383:e076227. doi:10.1136/bmj-2023-076227. Effect sizes come from the linked network meta-analysis: Yao L et al. BMJ 2023;383:e076226 (Fig 3, corrected version).

Design: a BMJ Rapid Recommendation (GRADE) based on 153 RCTs with 8713 adults who had chronic (≥3 months) TMD pain. There are 59 interventions, each compared with placebo or sham.

Nine questions: Q1 to Q6 single-best-answer MCQs, Q7 and Q8 short answer, Q9 a case-based oral prompt. Tags: R = recall, A = application, N = analysis.

## MCQs

**Q1 (R).** In the 2023 BMJ guideline on chronic TMD pain, which intervention has a strong recommendation in favour?
- A. Removable occlusal splint
- B. Botulinum toxin injection
- C. Supervised jaw exercise and stretching
- D. Low-level laser therapy
- E. Arthrocentesis

**Answer: C.** Jaw exercise with stretching is in the strong-for group. Its evidence is high certainty, with −1.55 cm on a 10 cm VAS. The other four options are all conditional against (Busse Abstract; Yao Fig 3).

**Q2 (R).** How does the guideline classify irreversible oral splints?
- A. Strong recommendation in favour
- B. Conditional recommendation in favour
- C. Conditional recommendation against
- D. Strong recommendation against
- E. No recommendation because evidence is insufficient

**Answer: D.** Irreversible oral splints are strong against, together with discectomy and NSAIDs with opioids (Busse Abstract; Understanding the recommendations, p4).

**Q3 (N).** In the linked NMA, the pooled pain estimate for removable occlusal splints was −1.81 cm versus placebo, close to CBT (−1.88 cm). Yet splints received a conditional recommendation against. What best explains this?
- A. Removable splints carry a serious risk of facial nerve weakness
- B. The splint effect rests on low-certainty evidence. Trials at high risk of bias showed larger effects, and longer follow-up showed smaller effects
- C. No randomised trials of splints were found
- D. The splint effect was smaller than the 1 cm MID
- E. Splints were judged too costly for most patients

**Answer: B.** The panel's stated reason for every conditional recommendation is pain evidence of only low or very low certainty, with no confidence of serious harm (Busse, Understanding the recommendations, p4). Removable splints are rated low certainty (Yao Fig 3). Yao's subgroup analysis adds why: high risk-of-bias trials gave larger effects versus placebo, and longer follow-up gave smaller effects versus usual care (Yao, Additional analysis; moderate credibility). Option A is the harm argument used for irreversible splints and discectomy. Option D is wrong because 1.81 cm exceeds the 1 cm MID.

**Q4 (A).** The panel's values-and-preferences survey suggested what pain improvement patients would typically need before accepting an invasive or irreversible procedure?
- A. 0.5 cm on a 10 cm VAS
- B. 1 cm
- C. 2 cm
- D. 3 cm
- E. 5 cm

**Answer: D.** About three times the 1 cm MID (Busse, Values and preferences, p5; 3 cm derived). For conservative care and most drugs, about one MID (1 cm) was enough.

**Q5 (A).** Which patient fits the guideline's target population best?
- A. A 15-year-old with 3 weeks of jaw pain after a football injury
- B. A 30-year-old woman with 6 months of 5/10 masticatory muscle pain
- C. A 40-year-old with an acute closed lock since yesterday
- D. A 28-year-old with painless reciprocal clicking
- E. A 50-year-old with fibromyalgia and previous TMJ surgery

**Answer: B.** The guideline covers adults with moderate (4-6/10) TMD pain lasting ≥3 months. Acute pain (<3 months) is excluded. Fibromyalgia and prior TMD surgery are listed as "may not apply", and painless clicking is not chronic pain (Busse, Who does the recommendations apply to?, p4; infographic).

**Q6 (R).** Which statement about NSAIDs in this guideline is correct?
- A. NSAIDs alone have a strong recommendation in favour
- B. NSAIDs combined with CBT have a conditional recommendation in favour
- C. NSAIDs combined with opioids have a conditional recommendation against
- D. NSAIDs were excluded from the network meta-analysis
- E. NSAIDs alone reduced pain more than CBT

**Answer: B.** NSAIDs with CBT are conditional for (−2.83 cm, low certainty). NSAIDs alone are conditional against (−0.76 cm vs −1.88 cm for CBT), and NSAIDs with opioids are strong against (Busse Abstract and infographic; Yao Fig 3). NSAIDs alone are listed only in the infographic, as "NSAIDs with or without steroids".

## Short answer

**Q7 (R).** Name the four recommendation categories in the guideline, give two examples of each, and state the order in which clinicians should consider them.

Model answer:
- **Strong for**: CBT with or without biofeedback or relaxation; therapist-assisted jaw mobilisation; manual trigger point therapy; supervised postural exercise; supervised jaw exercise and stretching with or without trigger point therapy; usual care (education, reassurance, home exercise and stretching, self-massage, over-the-counter analgesics).
- **Conditional for**: manipulation; supervised jaw exercise with mobilisation; CBT with NSAIDs; manipulation with postural exercise; acupuncture.
- **Conditional against**: removable occlusal splints (alone or combined); arthrocentesis; low-level laser; TENS; gabapentin; botulinum toxin; hyaluronic acid injection; trigger point injection; relaxation therapy; biofeedback; acetaminophen (with or without muscle relaxants or NSAIDs); NSAIDs with or without steroids (infographic); capsaicin; corticosteroid injection; benzodiazepines; beta-blockers; cartilage supplement.
- **Strong against**: irreversible oral splints; discectomy; NSAIDs with opioids.
- **Order**: strong for first, then conditional for, then conditional against. Strong-against options are not offered. Choices are made with the patient, weighing their values, what they have already tried, and access (Busse Abstract; infographic).

**Q8 (N).** The linked NMA gave irreversible oral splints a pain effect of −2.67 cm (RD 37 %). Explain how the panel still reached a strong recommendation against them.

Model answer:
- A strong recommendation normally needs moderate- or high-certainty evidence. GRADE allows five situations in which low-certainty evidence can still support a strong recommendation. One is low-certainty benefit combined with moderate-to-high certainty of a greater risk of important harm (Busse, strength-of-recommendation box, p4).
- Benefit: the irreversible splint estimate is low certainty (Yao Fig 3).
- Harm: trials gave almost no harms data, since only 19 of 59 interventions reported any. The clinical experts were therefore surveyed. They judged that irreversible splints and discectomy may cause serious harm, such as permanent change in range of motion or facial nerve weakness (p4-5).
- Values: patients would want about three times the MID (about 3 cm) from an invasive or irreversible procedure (p5). The irreversible splint point estimate, −2.67 cm, falls short (derived). This is supporting context, not the panel's stated reason: discectomy (−3.12 cm, very low certainty) passes 3 cm and is still strong against.
- Weak spot to name: the harm side rests on a survey of the panel's own clinical experts, not on trial data.
- Process: a strong recommendation needed ≥80 % panel agreement (p4).

## Case-based oral prompt

**Q9 (A/N).** A 26-year-old woman attends for an orthodontic consultation. She has a Class II division 2 malocclusion with a deep bite. She reports 8 months of bilateral cheek and temple pain, 5/10, worse on waking, and a painless click in the right TMJ. A previous dentist suggested "balancing the bite" by selective grinding. She has a hard night guard that she wears sometimes. She asks whether braces will cure her jaw pain. What will you do, and why?

Model answer outline:
1. **Frame the problem.** She has chronic (≥3 months) moderate pain, so she sits inside the Busse population. Examine her and classify the diagnosis with DC/TMD, for example myalgia with or without disc displacement with reduction. DC/TMD comes from the course, not from this reading.
2. **Reassure with evidence, honestly.** Chronic TMD pain is common (6-9 % of adults). In one cohort the guideline cites, 49 % of those followed reported complete recovery at 5 years (36 % lost to follow-up). OPPERA found only 24 % remission at 5 years, with 78 % lost. Disc displacement is found in about a third of people with no symptoms, so a click or an imaging finding alone is a weak basis for treatment (inference from Busse, Current practice, p3).
3. **First-line treatment (Busse, strong for).** Start usual care: education, reassurance, home jaw exercise and stretching, self-massage and simple analgesia. Add at least one active option she is willing to do: supervised jaw exercise and stretching, therapist-assisted mobilisation, manual trigger point therapy, postural exercise, or CBT with or without biofeedback or relaxation. Choose together with her, considering adherence, cost and access (Box 2, p5).
4. **Splint.** Removable splints are conditional against, with low certainty. She may keep using her night guard if she values it, but it is not the main treatment.
5. **Decline occlusal grinding.** Busse did not assess selective grinding. By analogy, it is irreversible, and the one irreversible occlusal intervention the guideline rated (irreversible oral splints) is strong against. Table 1 cites RCDSO 2019 against routine irreversible change to the occlusion or dentition (p8).
6. **Medicines.** Avoid NSAIDs with opioids (strong against). NSAIDs alone are conditional against.
7. **Orthodontics.** The guideline makes no recommendation on orthodontics: none of the 59 interventions was orthodontic, and Yao lists orthodontics among irreversible TMD treatments. So braces cannot be offered as a TMD treatment on this evidence. Orthodontics may still be offered for its own indications (deep bite, aesthetics) with honest consent. Starting active treatment once pain is controlled, recording baseline TMD status, and referring to an orofacial pain clinician if pain persists are standard practice, not points from this reading (outside this reading, UNVERIFIED against Busse).
8. **Limits to state.** The evidence comes from adults with pooled TMD subtypes and short follow-up (median 12 weeks). How it applies to a specific subtype is uncertain (Busse p4; Yao Limitations).
