# Busse 2023 · Management of chronic pain associated with temporomandibular disorders: a clinical practice guideline

Busse JW, Casassus R, Carrasco-Labra A, et al. BMJ 2023;383:e076227. doi:10.1136/bmj-2023-076227. Evidence: the linked NMA, Yao L et al. BMJ 2023;383:e076226.

Access: full text read (the published PDF via the University of Geneva open archive; bmj.com showed only the abstract and infographic). Yao 2023 was read in full on bmj.com, including Fig 3 and both corrections.

## Bottom line

- For adults with chronic (≥3 months), moderate (4-6/10) TMD pain, the guideline makes **strong recommendations for** CBT (with or without biofeedback or relaxation), therapist-assisted jaw mobilisation, manual trigger point therapy, supervised postural exercise, supervised jaw exercise and stretching (with or without trigger point therapy), and usual care (Abstract; p4).
- **Removable (reversible) occlusal splints get a conditional recommendation against**, used alone or with other treatments.
- **Strong recommendations against** irreversible oral splints, discectomy, and NSAIDs with opioids. Their benefit is uncertain and they may cause serious harm.
- Order of use: strong for first, then conditional for, then conditional against. Strong-against options are never offered. The choice is made with the patient (Abstract; infographic).

## What it is and what they did

- **Design**: a BMJ Rapid Recommendation (GRADE method, MAGIC support), built on one linked systematic review and network meta-analysis (NMA) (p3).
- **Panel**: 8 clinical experts, 8 methodologists, 1 patient-liaison expert and 3 patients with TMD pain (Yao, Methods). None of the listed clinical roles is an orthodontist (derived).
- **Evidence**: 210 eligible trials. Of these, 153 RCTs (8713 patients, 59 interventions or combinations) entered the NMA, each compared with placebo or sham (The evidence, p4).
- **Typical trial**: 46 patients, 12 weeks of follow-up, mean age 35 years, 83 % women, baseline pain 5.4/10 (Table 2, p9).
- **Rules**: pain relief was the critical outcome. The MID was 1 cm on a 10 cm VAS. A strong recommendation needed 80 % panel agreement; a conditional one needed a majority (p4-5).
- **Harms**: only 19 of 59 interventions (32 %) had harms data, almost all very low certainty, so the panel surveyed its clinical experts instead (p4).

- The top three treatments reduce pain by about 2× the MID; the next five by about 1.5× the MID (p5).
- Four treatments improve function with moderate certainty: jaw exercise + stretching, manipulation, acupuncture, and jaw exercise + mobilisation (RD 36-43 % for a 5-point SF-36 gain) (Yao, Physical functioning).
- Patients would accept the typical risks of conservative care and most drugs for a 1 cm gain. For invasive or irreversible procedures they would want about 3× the MID (Values and preferences, p5).
- Splints: trials at high risk of bias showed larger effects, and the effect against usual care shrank with longer follow-up. Credibility of both findings was moderate (Yao, Additional analysis).
- Context: disc displacement appears in about a third of people without symptoms. In a cohort the guideline cites, 49 % recovered fully at 5 years (Current practice, p3).

## Why it matters for an orthodontist

- A patient referred for braces who has chronic TMD pain needs usual care (education, reassurance, home exercise) plus supervised exercise or CBT first. Changing the occlusion is not part of that plan.
- A splint is at best a low-certainty, reversible add-on.
- Nothing here supports irreversible occlusal therapy for pain. Yao lists orthodontics among irreversible treatments, but no trial arm tested orthodontics or occlusal adjustment.
- Incidental MRI findings should not drive treatment on their own.
- Analgesia: never give NSAIDs with opioids. NSAIDs alone are conditional against.

## The course's question answered

Six treatment families get a strong recommendation, and all are based on coping or movement: CBT (with or without biofeedback or relaxation), therapist-assisted jaw mobilisation, manual trigger point therapy, supervised postural exercise, supervised jaw exercise and stretching (with or without trigger point therapy), and usual care. Removable occlusal splints are conditional against. Their pain estimate (−1.81 cm, RD 29 %) is close to CBT's, but the certainty is low, biased trials inflate it, and it fades with time. Irreversible oral splints are strong against, alongside discectomy and NSAIDs with opioids, because their benefit is low certainty and a credible risk of serious, permanent harm remains. Occlusal adjustment and orthodontics were not tested. Table 1 cites an Ontario guideline (RCDSO 2019) that advises against routine irreversible change to the joints, jaws, occlusion or dentition.

## What the presenter may ask

1. **What is "chronic", and who is covered?** Pain for ≥3 months, in adults with moderate (4-6/10) pain. All subtypes are pooled. Acute TMD is not covered (p4).
2. **What is the MID?** For pain, 1 cm on a 10 cm VAS. For function, 5 points on the SF-36 physical component score (Yao, Data synthesis).
3. **Splints cut pain by 1.8 cm, so why "against"?** The certainty is low, biased trials inflate the effect, and it fades over time. A conditional recommendation still leaves room for patient choice.
4. **How can a strong recommendation rest on low-certainty evidence?** GRADE allows it when benefit is uncertain and serious harm is plausible, such as permanent loss of motion or facial nerve weakness (p5).
5. **Where do NSAIDs sit?** Alone: conditional against. With CBT: conditional for. With opioids: strong against.
6. **What is "usual care"?** Education, reassurance, home exercise and stretching, self-massage and over-the-counter analgesics. It is strongly recommended (p4).

## Limits

- All TMD subtypes were pooled. Most trials were mixed or did not specify subtype, so subtype effects could not be tested (p4; Yao, Limitations).
- Trials were small and short (median 46 patients, 12 weeks). Most effects are low or very low certainty, and harms data are sparse.
- Only adults were studied. Many trials excluded mental illness, fibromyalgia, rheumatoid arthritis and prior TMJ surgery, so adolescents and complex cases are poorly covered.
- Most comparisons are against placebo or sham, so there is little head-to-head evidence.
- Reporting notes: the text says 134/153 trials were in "high- or middle-income" countries, but Table 2 places all 153 there (134 = 80 high + 54 upper-middle, derived). Pain duration is 42 months in Table 2 but 44 months in Yao's text.
