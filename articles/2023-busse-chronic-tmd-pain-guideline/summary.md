# Busse 2023 · Management of chronic pain associated with temporomandibular disorders: a clinical practice guideline

Busse JW, Casassus R, Carrasco-Labra A, et al. BMJ 2023;383:e076227. doi:10.1136/bmj-2023-076227. Evidence: the linked NMA, Yao L et al. BMJ 2023;383:e076226.

Access: Busse full text via the University of Geneva open archive; Yao full text on bmj.com (Fig 3, both corrections). Rechecked 21 November 2026; numbers agree with the verified key-numbers.md.

## Bottom line

- Scope: adults with chronic (≥3 months) TMD pain of moderate severity (4-6/10), all subtypes pooled. It does not apply to acute TMD pain (Abstract; p4).
- **Strong for**: CBT with or without biofeedback or relaxation, therapist-assisted jaw mobilisation, manual trigger point therapy, supervised postural exercise, supervised jaw exercise and stretching with or without manual trigger point therapy, and usual care (Abstract).
- **Conditional against**: removable (reversible) occlusal splints, alone or combined with other treatments (Abstract).
- **Strong against**: irreversible oral splints, discectomy, and NSAIDs with opioids (Abstract): uncertain benefit, important harms (infographic).
- Order: strong for, then conditional for, then conditional against. Strong-against options are not offered. Decide with the patient (Abstract; infographic).

## What it is and what they did

- **Design**: a BMJ Rapid Recommendation (GRADE, MAGIC support) built on one linked systematic review and network meta-analysis (NMA) (Busse p3).
- **Panel**: 8 clinical experts, 8 methodologists, 1 patient-liaison expert and 3 patient partners. The clinical experts are dentists, orofacial pain and oral medicine specialists, an oral surgeon, a pathologist, an internist and a pharmacologist; none is an orthodontist (Yao, Methods; Acknowledgments; derived).
- **Evidence**: 210 unique eligible trials; 153 RCTs (8713 patients, 59 interventions or combinations) entered the NMA (Busse p4; Yao, Results).
- **Typical trial**: median 46 patients and 12 weeks; mean age 35 years; 83 % women; baseline pain 5.4 cm on a 10 cm VAS (Busse Table 2).
- **Rules**: MID 1 cm on a 10 cm VAS for pain, 5 points on the SF-36 physical component summary for function. A strong recommendation needed ≥80 % panel agreement, a conditional one a majority (Busse p4; Yao, Data synthesis).
- **Harms**: only 19 of 59 interventions (32 %) had harms data, all low or very low certainty, so the panel surveyed its clinical experts (Busse p4-5; Yao, Adverse events).

- The top three strong-for options cut pain by about 2 × the MID, the next five by about 1.5 × (Busse p5).
- Function, moderate certainty: jaw exercise + stretching, manipulation, acupuncture, jaw exercise + mobilisation (RD 36-43 % for a 5-point SF-36 gain) (Yao, Physical functioning).
- Patients accept the typical risks of conservative care and most drugs for about 1 cm of relief; for invasive or irreversible (surgical) procedures they want about 3 × the MID (Busse p5; infographic).
- Context: disc displacement is found in about a third of people without symptoms. In one cited cohort, 49 % of those followed recovered fully at 5 years (36 % lost to follow-up) (Busse p3).

## Why it matters for an orthodontist

- Offer strong-for options first: usual care plus an active option (supervised exercise, mobilisation, trigger point therapy or CBT), chosen with the patient. Changing the occlusion is not a recommended treatment.
- A removable splint comes later, for a patient who values it.
- Yao lists orthodontics among irreversible TMD treatments (Introduction), but no node in the pain network was orthodontics or occlusal adjustment (Fig 2), so there is no recommendation on either.

## The course's question answered

- **Strong for**: the six coping and movement options above, including usual care (education, reassurance, home exercise and stretching, self-massage, over-the-counter analgesics). Moderate- to high-certainty effects of −1.31 to −2.62 cm on a 10 cm VAS; MID 1 cm (Busse p4; Yao Fig 3).
- **Removable (reversible) occlusal splints**, alone or combined: conditional against; −1.81 cm, RD 29 %, low certainty. Yao's subgroups found larger effects in high risk-of-bias trials and smaller effects with longer follow-up (moderate credibility).
- **Irreversible oral splints**: strong against; −2.67 cm, low certainty, because serious harm is possible (permanent change in jaw motion, facial nerve weakness) (Busse p4-5).
- **Selective grinding and orthodontics** were not assessed. Busse Table 1 cites RCDSO 2019 against routine irreversible change to the occlusion or dentition (p8).

## What the presenter may ask

1. **Splints cut pain by 1.8 cm, so why "against"?** Conditional-against means low or very low certainty without confidence of serious harm (Busse p4); splint effects were larger in biased trials and smaller with longer follow-up (Yao). Patient choice remains.
2. **How can low-certainty evidence give a strong recommendation?** GRADE allows it when low-certainty benefit meets moderate-to-high certainty of greater important harm (Busse p4). The surveyed experts judged irreversible splints and discectomy may cause serious harm (p4-5).
3. **Where do NSAIDs sit?** Alone: conditional against (infographic, "NSAIDs with or without steroids"). With CBT: conditional for. With opioids: strong against.

## Limits

- Subtypes pooled: 78 of 153 trials (51 %) enrolled mixed or unreported subtypes, so subtype effects could not be tested (Busse Table 2; Yao, Limitations).
- Small, short trials (median 46 patients, 12 weeks); limited direct evidence for several interventions; most effects and all harms evidence low or very low certainty (Yao, Limitations).
- Adults only (≥18 years). Many trials excluded mental illness (47 %), rheumatoid arthritis (36 %), prior TMJ surgery (32 %) or fibromyalgia (20 %), so the guideline may not apply to them (Yao, Study characteristics; Busse p4).
- Reporting notes: Busse's text puts 134/153 trials in "high- or middle-income" countries, Table 2 all 153 (134 = 80 high + 54 upper-middle, derived). Pain duration: 42 months (Table 2) vs 44 (Yao).
