# Serial extraction in orthodontics: a review (Yezdani et al 2020)

Yezdani A, Nandhini P, Padmavati R. Serial extraction in orthodontics – review article. Eur J Mol Clin Med 2020;7(2):6432-6441. No DOI or dates printed; exactly three authors.

## Bottom line

Serial extraction is the timed removal of primary teeth and then first premolars to relieve severe crowding in a Class I child with a good profile. The review lists the classic rules (arch deficiency of 8-10 mm or more, nine contraindications, sequences coded CD4 and D4C) but gives no data. Proffit sets the bar at more than 10 mm per arch and calls it an adjunct to comprehensive treatment (p 443, VERIFIED).

## What they did

A narrative review with no methods, sample or tables: 10 pages, 9 figure labels, 16 references (10 of the 15 dated ones before 1990, derived).

## What they found (as stated)

- History: Bunon 1743; Kjellgren named it in 1929; Nance (1940) the "father"; Hotz's "guidance of eruption" (p 6433).
- Principles: arch length-tooth material discrepancy and physiologic tooth movement (p 6433).
- Indications led by arch deficiency of 8-10 mm or more in a Class I face with normal overbite; warning signs include no physiologic spacing and unilateral primary canine loss with a midline shift (pp 6433-6434).
- Contraindications include mild to moderate crowding, deep or open bite, severe Class II or III and missing teeth (p 6435).
- Advantages: shorter multibanded phase, "more stable" results, less retention. Disadvantages: 2-3 years of multiple visits, bite deepening, residual canine-second premolar spaces (p 6435).
- Alveodental retrusion: no extraction (dished-in face); Class II maxillary protrusion: maxilla only; Class III: not suitable (p 6436).
- Dewel: deciduous canines at 8.5 y, deciduous first molars a year later, first premolars as they erupt. Tweed and Nance start with deciduous first molars at 8 y (pp 6438-6439).
- One complication listed: anterior crossbite (p 6439).

## Why it matters

It is the exam vocabulary of the topic. Promotion recalls test the 10 mm indication (2021) and the time trade-off (2023, 2024): O'Shaughnessy 2011 found a shorter active phase but longer total time and more visits, with similar final PAR (P = .27; VERIFIED).

## One-line caveat

Level 5 review without data: "more stable" conflicts with Little 1990 (73 % unsatisfactory lower anterior alignment 10 or more years postretention; VERIFIED); as printed, Tweed's caption matches Nance's order (both D4C).
