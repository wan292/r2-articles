# Conflicts and disagreements — Yezdani 2020

Use this file when you need to challenge a claim, a date or a citation in Yezdani A, Nandhini P, Padmavati R. Serial extraction in orthodontics – review article. *Eur J Mol Clin Med* 2020;7(2):6432-6441 (ISSN 2515-8260). Page 1 prints exactly three authors ("Dr. Arif Yezdani.A", "Dr. Nithya Nandhini.P", "Dr. Padmavati.R"), so the citation takes no "et al". No DOI and no received or accepted dates are printed.

The paper is a narrative review of 10 pages (derived) with 16 references and nine figure labels (Fig 1, 2a-d, 3a-c, 4). It has **no tables, no data, no statistics and no P values**. Section A therefore checks the text against itself, its figure captions and its own reference list, using all ten page images (journal pp 6432-6441). Section B checks its claims against the studies it cites and the outcome studies it does not cite. C is the paper against Proffit, with the past promotion questions that touch the same points. D lists what stays open and E is the verdict.

Tags: **VERIFIED** = seen this run (Proffit 7th-edition PDF text on disk, a PubMed or Europe PMC abstract, or a primary scan). **VERIFIED-secondary** = seen only in a full-text copy, an evidence summary or a teaching source. **VERIFIED (notes)** = in the resident's 6th-edition Proffit study pages, not the printed book. **UNVERIFIED** = from memory or not checked. Proffit pages are 7th-edition book pages (PDF page − 11); chapters 1-17 have the same numbers in the 6th edition. Abbreviations: C = primary canine; D = primary first molar; 4 = first premolar; SE = serial extraction; LPE = late premolar extraction; PAR = peer assessment rating.

**Checks that passed** (so you can say so).
- The Fig 3a caption ("3 step"; Ds "a year later"; then the erupting 4s) matches the Dewel bullets on p 6438.
- The Nance bullets (D, 4, C) match the Fig 3c caption.
- "Deep or open bites" as a contraindication (p 6435) fits the indication's "normal overbite" (p 6433) and the bite-deepening disadvantage (p 6435).
- The Proffit definition (p 6433) matches Proffit's wording, timed extraction of primary and then permanent teeth for severe crowding (VERIFIED, p 443).
- Residual spaces between the canine and second premolar (p 6435) match Proffit Fig 12.37 (VERIFIED, p 446).
- Kjellgren 1929 (p 6433) matches Kjellgren's own account (VERIFIED, B1).
- The counts are internally coherent: 12 indications, 9 numbered contraindications, 7 advantages, 7 disadvantages (counts derived except the numbered 9), and the "mainly three methods" are all described.

**How to raise these politely.** This is a short teaching review written to summarise textbook material, and it is useful as exactly that. Lead with what holds: the definitions, the Dewel sequence, the list of OPG uses, and residual space and bite deepening as sequelae. Then raise two points as questions about what to learn, not as criticism.
- **A1.** "On p 6438 Tweed's premolar and canine come out together, but the Fig 3b caption has them in sequence, like Nance. Which version do we use for the exam?"
- **A2.** "Is anterior crossbite meant as a complication, or is Fig 4 showing what happens with over-retraction?"

Keep A3-A13 as footnote-level remarks. Phrase nothing as an accusation: a narrative review is not a report of data.

## B. Against other studies

### B0. Against the hub and the rest of topic 5

- **`2004-kokich-impacted-maxillary-canines`** (registry: extracting the primary canine lets a palatally displaced canine self-correct "with high predictability"; RCTs give 69 % against 39 % and 67 % against 42 %). **Compatible.** Dewel's first step is the same extraction, and Proffit (VERIFIED, pp 396 and 446) says early primary canine extraction lowers the risk of canine impaction. Yezdani never mentions this benefit, so it is a gap, not a conflict. "Dewel's first step is Kokich's interception: take the C and the canine and the incisors both benefit."
- **`2005-stephens-long-term-profile-extraction-nonextraction`** (registry: no difference on 9 profile measures at about 15 y between matched extraction and nonextraction patients, n = 20 / 20; H-line unchanged). **Conflicts** with the Fig 4 caption (flat face, prominent chin, aged look) if that caption is read as a general consequence of extraction. **Compatible** with the paper's narrower rule of no extraction in Class I alveodental retrusion (p 6436), because Stephens matched patients on incisor position. Wilson 1999 (B4) adds the same null finding specifically for serial extraction. "Stephens and Wilson both say the face does not remember the extraction; the dished-in face is a selection mistake, not a sequela."
- **Topic 5, paper 2, `2006-kokich-missing-mandibular-second-premolars` (Kokich & Kokich 2006).** Yezdani lists congenital absence of teeth as a contraindication (p 6435). Kokich 2006 (early hemisection or extraction of the E; space closure when the opposing arch is crowded or the profile protrusive) and Proffit (VERIFIED, p 434: extract the E at 7-9 y when space, profile and jaws are good or somewhat protrusive) both use planned early removal of the E when the 5 is missing. The ideas are linked: missing teeth already provide space, so the classic 4s sequence is not needed, but planned primary-tooth extraction still has a role (B6).
- **Topic 5, paper 4, `2018-kouvelis-extraction-vertical-dimension` (Kouvelis et al 2018).** Yezdani rejects Class II mandibular retrognathism with backward rotation and open bite as "not a good case" (p 6437) and lists open bite as a contraindication. Kouvelis found no specific effect of premolar extraction on the skeletal vertical dimension. The two do not collide: Yezdani's rule is about case selection, not about an extraction effect, and neither paper tests it (B5).
- No other hub claim overlaps. The superimposition papers and Khaleel 2026 are unrelated; Yezdani's bite-deepening disadvantage and Khaleel's deep-bite mechanics share no claim.

### B2. Treatment time, visits and cost

- **Wagner & Berg 2000**, J Orofac Orthop 61:207-216 (the paper's ref 6; VERIFIED abstract). Two comparable extraction groups: SE in the early mixed dentition followed by orthodontics, against extraction and orthodontics in the permanent dentition. The fixed-appliance phase was highly significantly shorter after SE, but appointments were significantly more numerous and total treatment and observation time significantly longer. PAR improved or greatly improved in all cases. **AGREES** with both the advantage (shorter multibanded phase) and the disadvantage (prolonged, multiple visits) on p 6435, although the paper cites it for its lists instead. "Wagner and Berg is the paper's own reference for both sides of the time trade-off: shorter brackets, longer supervision."
- **O'Shaughnessy et al 2011**, AJODO 139:510-516 (VERIFIED abstract; the numbers below VERIFIED-secondary from a full-text copy, which was cut off before Table III). Retrospective chart review, 51 SE against 49 LPE patients, all finished with fixed appliances. Entry: SE patients had more than 8 mm of crowding, estimated visually. T0 (SE only) is the point before extraction or enucleation of the first premolars.
  - PAR: SE 18.24 at T0; at the start of fixed appliances 15.18 (SE) against 31.27 (LPE), P < .001, so SE patients began the fixed phase with about half the score (49 %, derived). At debond 2.61 against 3.08, P = .27.
  - Mean ages: SE 7.95 y (T0), 13.11 y (T1), 14.89 y (T2); LPE 13.41 y (T1), 15.52 y (T2).
  - Derived from those mean ages: fixed phase about 1.8 y (SE) against 2.1 y (LPE), about 4 months shorter; SE observation before brackets about 5.2 y; SE programme from T0 to debond about 6.9 y. The Table III month values recorded in `key-numbers.md` (active 20.6 against 24.6; total 82.9 against 31.8) appear in a colleague's summary of the full text (VERIFIED-secondary) and agree with these derived intervals.
  - Active time shorter with SE (P < .001); total time and appointments greater with SE (P < .001 in the abstract; the full text gives P < .05 for total visits). Total chair time not significantly different (P = .07).
  - **AGREES** in direction (shorter active phase, longer overall). **CONFLICTS** with the paper's "2-3 years", which is less than half of the roughly 6.9 years from T0 to debond (derived), and gives no support to "reduces cost of treatment" (p 6435): chair time was not shown to be lower.
  - "The fixed phase is about 4 months shorter, the programme starts about 5 years before the brackets, and chair time is a wash."
- **Lopes Filho et al 2015**, Angle Orthod 85:510-517 (VERIFIED abstract). Systematic review of 6 retrospective studies (219 early against 199 late patients; VERIFIED-secondary). Four studies found crowding corrected equally well with early and late extraction. Early treatment had two favourable secondary outcomes, less relapse and shorter active treatment, but quality was low to moderate and the authors would not say either protocol was superior. No meta-analysis was done (VERIFIED-secondary). **PARTIAL.** A 2020 McGill evidence summary built on it concluded that the two give similar outcomes and that convenience and patient preference should decide (VERIFIED-secondary). "Same crowding outcome either way; choose by logistics, which is exactly what the past exam asks."

### B3. Stability and retention ("more stable results", "lesser retention period", p 6435)

- **Little, Riedel & Engst 1990**, Angle Orthod 60:255-262 (VERIFIED abstract). 30 patients treated by serial extraction plus first premolars, then edgewise appliances and retention; all clinically satisfactory at the end of active treatment; recalled at least 10 years postretention. 22 of 30 (73 %) had clinically unsatisfactory lower anterior alignment. Intercanine width and arch length fell in 29 of 30. There was no difference from a matched sample extracted and treated after full eruption. **CONFLICTS** with "more stable results" and "lesser retention period". "Little's serial-extraction patients relapsed like late-extraction patients: 73 % unsatisfactory at 10 years."
- **Persson, Persson & Skagius 1989**, Eur J Orthod 11:271-282 (VERIFIED abstract). 44 Class I crowded cases had all four first premolars removed at a mean 10½ y as the only treatment; 42 were re-examined at a mean 30 y 4 mo. Marked spontaneous alignment and residual space closure; average malocclusion similar to an appliance-treated sample; crowding redeveloped to about the level of a nonextraction normal-occlusion sample; no harm to overjet, overbite or marginal bone. **PARTIAL.** It supports the "physiological" self-correction in selected Class I cases, but not "more stable": crowding came back as it does in untreated normal occlusions. The abstract describes first-premolar extraction as the only treatment and mentions no primary-tooth steps, so it is not clearly a serial-extraction sample.
- **Yoshihara et al 1999 and 2000**, AJODO 116:691-696 and 118:611-616 (VERIFIED abstracts). SE without appliances; 32 maxillary and 31 mandibular cast series. Irregularity fell as SE proceeded and did not worsen during observation; after the 4s were removed, canine movement or tipping correlated with the correction of anterior crowding. **AGREES** with the paper's principle of physiologic tooth movement (p 6433). "Yoshihara ties the incisor untangling to canine movement after the 4s come out."
- **Almeida et al 2012**, J Appl Oral Sci 20:486-492 (VERIFIED abstract). One case, SE followed by orthodontics, seen 20 years later: harmonious face, anteroposterior stability, a deep overbite and a small relapse of anterior crowding. The authors say early correction does not guarantee stability and that the low cost suits underprivileged populations. **PARTIAL** (Level 5, single case): it supports bite deepening and low cost, and argues against stability as a guarantee.

### B4. Profile ("dished-in face", p 6436; Fig 4 caption, p 6440)

- **Wilson et al 1999**, Angle Orthod 69:165-174 (VERIFIED abstract). SE without orthodontics (n = 28), SE with orthodontics (n = 30) and late extraction with orthodontics (n = 30). No significant differences in soft-tissue profile between the three groups; late extraction moved the most labial point of the lower incisor further back. **CONFLICTS** with a flat face as a general complication of SE; it does not test the paper's narrower rule of not extracting in retrusive faces. "Wilson found three groups with the same soft-tissue profile, so a dished face is not the price of serial extraction."
- **Stephens 2005** (hub, B0): no profile difference at about 15 y after matched extraction and nonextraction treatment.
- **Proffit Ch 7, p 223** (VERIFIED): lips follow about two-thirds of incisor retraction (3 mm of retraction gives about 2 mm less lip protrusion), with large individual variation. Same direction as the paper's warning, small average size.

### B5. Overbite and the vertical dimension

- **Proffit Ch 12, pp 444-445** (VERIFIED): while the teeth align without appliances, the lower incisors tip lingually and overbite often increases; without later mechanotherapy, a correct rather than deep overbite is usually not achieved. **AGREES** with "tendency to increase over bite" (p 6435).
- **Persson 1989** (VERIFIED): no detrimental effect on overbite at a mean 30 y in selected Class I cases. **PARTIAL**: deepening may be transient or case-dependent.
- **Almeida 2012** (VERIFIED): deep overbite 20 years after SE in one case. **AGREES** (Level 5).
- **Quinzi et al 2023**, Orthod Craniofac Res 26:642-649 (VERIFIED abstract; also summarised in a 2023 Evid Based Dent commentary). Retrospective; 78 children aged 8.5 ± 1.4 y: 52 treated for severe crowding (SE alone or maxillary expansion plus SE) and 26 untreated controls matched for age and observation period. The mandibular and occlusal plane angles decreased in both treated groups. The annualised change in the upper gonial angle differed across the three groups (P = .036): −0.44 ± 0.6 (SE alone) and −0.34 ± 0.5 (expansion plus SE) against −0.04 ± 0.6 (controls); degrees presumed, the abstract gives no unit. **NEW**: SE was followed by a slight closing, not opening, of the vertical, which fits bite deepening; the paper discusses no vertical effect.
- **Tongue thrust** (p 6435). Proffit Ch 5, pp 128-129 (VERIFIED): "tongue thrust" swallowing is largely a misnomer; tongue placement between the incisors is usually adaptive or transitional and seldom a cause of malocclusion. **NUANCE**: tongue posture into extraction spaces is likely an adaptation, not a new habit (my interpretation); the paper gives no source.

**Journal-club line:** "Proffit and a 20-year case report say the bite deepens while the teeth drift, Quinzi shows the vertical closing slightly, and Persson found no lasting overbite harm at 30 years, so the fixed phase has to level the bite."

### B6. Indications, contraindications and case selection

- **Crowding threshold.** Proffit: more than 10 mm per arch, no skeletal problem (VERIFIED, p 443); 5-9 mm is borderline and 10 mm or more almost always means extraction (VERIFIED, Ch 7 p 222). O'Shaughnessy 2011 enrolled more than 8 mm, estimated visually (VERIFIED-secondary). **PARTIAL**: the paper's 8 mm floor has some research precedent, but sits in Proffit's borderline band.
- **Congenital absence of teeth** (contraindications 1 and 7). Proffit (VERIFIED, p 434): with good or somewhat protrusive space, profile and jaws, extract Es without successors at 7-9 y and let the 6s drift; drift varies and opposing-arch extractions may be needed. Modified serial-extraction case reports in lower-5 agenesis exist (Al Hummayani 2015; Chalakkal 2013; VERIFIED per research pack). Mamopoulou 1996 (VERIFIED): 11 children, lower E plus upper 5 extracted; the lower space was 46 % closed at 1 y and 80 % at 4 y, with about 2 mm residual. **PARTIAL**: the classic 4s sequence is unnecessary, but timed primary-molar extraction is an established relative of serial extraction.
- **Unilateral primary canine loss with midline shift** (indication 6, Fig 2c). Proffit Ch 11, pp 396-397 (VERIFIED): such loss more often signals a real space shortage, which a space analysis must confirm. Growth-study data (Christensen et al 2018, Pediatr Dent 40:279-284, cited by Proffit) found the midline shift after early loss of one lower primary canine no greater than with normal eruption (1.3 against 1.0 mm). Balancing extraction of the other canine is no longer considered necessary. **NUANCE, partly CONFLICTS**: a warning sign of crowding, not by itself an indication.
- **Lingual eruption of incisors and absence of spacing** (indications 4 and 5, Figs 2a-b). Proffit (VERIFIED): little developmental spacing and early loss of primary canines are signs of severe crowding (p 439). Lower incisors erupting lingual to retained primary incisors are common, and removing the primary teeth allows spontaneous alignment (Fig 11.37, p 396). Some early incisor crowding is normal incisor liability (p 396). **NUANCE**: these are screening signs that call for a space analysis, not indications on their own.
- **Class III** ("not suitable", p 6436). Proffit (VERIFIED, pp 443-444): lower-only sequences can compensate mild to moderate Class III, as complex treatment. **CONFLICTS** with the absolute rule.

**Journal-club line:** "Proffit's bar is more than 10 mm per arch in a Class I child; lingual incisors, absent spacing or one lost primary canine call for a space analysis, and a missing 5 calls for timed E extraction, not the 4s sequence."

### B7. Root resorption (not discussed in the paper)

- **Kennedy et al 1983**, in Proffit Table 8.4, p 292 (VERIFIED). Average root length change with SE plus treatment against late extraction plus treatment (the table prints no unit; mm is presumed): upper central −1.5 against −2.0, upper lateral −2.0 against −2.5, upper canine −1.0 against −1.5, lower central −1.0 against −1.5, lower lateral −1.0 against −1.0. Root shortening was equal or slightly less with SE for every tooth listed. **Gap**: a possible advantage of SE that the paper does not list. "The one root-resorption table in Proffit slightly favours serial extraction."

## D. Open questions

1. Which sequence is Tweed's and which is Nance's: is the 4 taken with the C or before it? (A1)
2. Is "8-10mm or more" per arch or for both arches, and measured with which analysis (the paper names Carey's for the lower arch and arch perimeter for the upper)? What is done between "moderate" and 8 mm? (A8, C row 2)
3. When exactly is the D taken: a year after the C, at 8 y, or at ½ to ⅔ of the 4's root (Proffit)? (A6)
4. In the lower arch, when the canine is ahead of the 4, does the paper accept enucleation? Proffit advises against it because of the bone defect. (C row 6)
5. Is a lingual arch placed after the C extraction to keep the nonextraction option open? (A12, C row 9)
6. How can a Class II maxilla-only sequence be allowed while Class I is the stated requirement? When is a lower-only Class III sequence worth its complexity? (A4)
7. Where do "more stable results" and "lesser retention period" come from, given Little 1990 (73 % unsatisfactory at 10 years, no different from late extraction)? (A5, B3)
8. Is the "2-3 years" the observation period, the active phase or the total? O'Shaughnessy's SE patients took about 6.9 years from T0 to debond (derived from mean ages). (B2)
9. Is anterior crossbite a complication of serial extraction, a reason to treat early, or a mislabelled figure? What is Fig 4 meant to show? (A2)
10. Does a "dished-in face" follow serial extraction in properly selected Class I cases, given Wilson 1999 and Stephens 2005? (B4)
11. Is "guidance of eruption" a synonym, as the abstract says, or Hotz's alternative to serial extraction, as his 1970 title suggests? (B1)
12. Lower cost for whom, and compared with what? O'Shaughnessy found no significant difference in chair time (P = .07). (B2)

## E. One-paragraph verdict

Read against itself, the review is a clear teaching list with one high-impact slip: two of its three extraction methods carry the same code, and the Tweed caption describes the Nance order, so the most examinable content cannot be learned from it without a second source. Its complications section joins a crossbite heading, a crossbite slide and a flat-face caption that do not describe the same thing, and its case-selection rules contradict each other on Class II, Class III and prognathism. Read against the literature, its descriptive core holds. Proffit's definition, Dewel's C-D-4 order, bite deepening and residual space between canine and second premolar all match Proffit Ch 12, and spontaneous alignment after SE is documented (Yoshihara, Persson). Its evaluative claims do not hold. The shorter multibanded phase is real (about 4 months in O'Shaughnessy, derived) but comes with about five years of observation before brackets and more visits (O'Shaughnessy, Wagner & Berg). Lower cost is unproven. "More stable" with "less retention" is contradicted by Little's 73 % relapse at 10 years, no different from late extraction. The flat face is not supported as a general sequela (Wilson; Stephens in the hub). Against Proffit it is stricter on skeletal patterns (Proffit allows upper-only and lower-only sequences), lower on the crowding threshold (8 mm against more than 10 mm per arch), silent on enucleation, lingual arches and root-formation timing, and out of date on balancing extraction after unilateral primary canine loss. Use it as a vocabulary sheet for definitions, history and lists, cross-checked against Proffit Ch 12 pp 442-446, and take every number and every outcome claim from O'Shaughnessy 2011, Little 1990 and Proffit instead.
