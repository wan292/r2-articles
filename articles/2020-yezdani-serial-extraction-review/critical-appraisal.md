# Critical appraisal — Yezdani 2020

Yezdani A, Nandhini P, Padmavati R. Serial extraction in orthodontics – review article. *Eur J Mol Clin Med* 2020;7(2):6432-6441. ISSN 2515-8260. Page 1 prints three authors ("Dr. Arif Yezdani.A", "Dr. Nithya Nandhini.P", "Dr. Padmavati.R"), all at Sree Balaji Dental College and Hospital, Bharath Institute of Higher Education and Research, Chennai: a professor and director of the orthodontic department, an undergraduate student, and a senior lecturer in orthodontics. No DOI, no received or accepted dates, and no funding, conflict-of-interest or ethics statement are printed.

The paper has no methods section, no tables, no statistics and no data of its own. This appraisal therefore grades it as a narrative review, and grades each claim by the evidence the paper cites and the evidence it does not cite. Page numbers are journal pages. Facts about other papers carry the research pack's tags: **VERIFIED** (seen this run: Proffit 7th-edition text, a PubMed or Europe PMC abstract, or a primary scan), **VERIFIED-secondary** (seen only in a full-text copy, an evidence summary or a teaching source), **UNVERIFIED**. Derived numbers are labelled. The evidence levels and certainty grades are my own mapping to Oxford CEBM 2011 and GRADE-style certainty, not an official grading.

## Verdict in three lines

1. **Design:** an unsystematic narrative review in a low-tier multidisciplinary (non-orthodontic) journal. It consists of definitions, lists (12 indications, 9 contraindications, 7 advantages, 7 disadvantages; counts derived except the numbered 9) and three textbook extraction sequences, with no search, no data and no case material.
2. **Level of evidence:** Oxford CEBM 2011 Level 5 (expert opinion and textbook summary; my mapping). That is the same level as the two Kokich papers of topic 5 (2004 and 2006), which at least illustrate five treated cases each, and below Stephens 2005 (a matched retrospective cohort) and Kouvelis 2018 (a systematic review).
3. **Biggest limitation:** the evaluative claims (shorter multibanded treatment, lower cost, "more stable results", shorter retention, flat face as a complication) are asserted without data. The outcome literature that tests them is not cited (Little 1990, Wilson 1999, O'Shaughnessy 2011, Lopes Filho 2015), and where it exists it contradicts the stability claim (Little 1990: 73 % unsatisfactory alignment at 10 years, no different from late extraction; VERIFIED).

My read: the implied question (does serial extraction beat late extraction for severe crowding?) is a good one and has been studied. The paper describes the intervention well enough for an exam answer, but never names the comparator, so none of its "advantages" can be checked against anything in the text.

## Design, source selection and the direction of bias

**What kind of paper it is.** A narrative review written as a lecture handout: headings, bullet lists and figures. It has no search strategy, databases, dates, eligibility criteria, risk-of-bias assessment, synthesis method or certainty grading; every PRISMA-type item is absent. The two-sentence abstract is a definition and contains no findings. The introduction promises to review limitations and "various adjuncts" (p 6432); no adjunct is described (`conflicts.md` A12).

**Journal and authorship.** European Journal of Molecular & Clinical Medicine is a multidisciplinary title outside the orthodontic and dental literature. Its Scopus coverage was discontinued, with coverage ending in 2020 at volume 7, issue 11 (VERIFIED-secondary, from a listing site; not checked against Scopus itself). The issue runs past page 6400 (derived from the page numbers). Whether the paper was peer-reviewed is not stated (UNVERIFIED). The second author is printed as an undergraduate student.

## Sample, power, attrition

- **This paper:** none. No patients, no cases, no follow-up. Power is not applicable.
- **Sample sizes in the evidence it should rest on** (none appear in the paper):
  - Little, Riedel & Engst 1990: 30 SE cases, at least 10 years postretention (VERIFIED).
  - Persson 1989: 44 cases, 42 re-examined at a mean 30 y 4 mo (VERIFIED).
  - Wilson 1999: 28 + 30 + 30 (VERIFIED).
  - Yoshihara 1999 and 2000: 32 and 31 (VERIFIED).
  - O'Shaughnessy 2011: 51 SE against 49 LPE (VERIFIED).
  - Lopes Filho 2015: 6 retrospective studies, 219 early against 199 late (VERIFIED; group sizes VERIFIED-secondary).
  - Quinzi 2023: 52 treated against 26 untreated (VERIFIED).
- **What that means:** the whole comparative evidence base is retrospective, and the largest pooled sample is about 420 patients across 6 studies (derived). No randomised trial of serial extraction against late extraction was found in the research pack.

## Confounding (in the claims the paper makes)

1. **Case selection.** Children chosen for SE are those with severe crowding in a good skeletal pattern. In O'Shaughnessy 2011 they began fixed appliances at about half the PAR score of late-extraction patients (15.18 against 31.27; VERIFIED-secondary), so a shorter fixed phase is partly a consequence of starting easier, not proof of efficiency.
2. **Growth and maturation.** Spontaneous alignment, mesial drift and overbite change all happen during the transition whatever the treatment. Without untreated crowded controls (only Quinzi 2023 and Persson's normal-occlusion samples come close), "physiological" correction cannot be separated from growth.
3. **What counts as time.** "Treatment time" mixes observation, extraction visits and active treatment. The advantage (shorter fixed phase) and the disadvantage (longer programme) are the same fact seen from two ends (`conflicts.md` A5).
4. **Comprehensive treatment afterwards.** Most outcome studies (Little 1990, O'Shaughnessy 2011, Wagner & Berg 2000) followed SE with full appliances; any stability or profile claim reflects the combination, not SE alone. Persson (first-premolar extraction as the only treatment) and Yoshihara (SE alone) studied extraction without appliances in selected cases.
5. **Era and technique.** Little's patients were treated with standard edgewise mechanics decades ago; modern expansion, TADs and retention change the comparison.
6. **Operator and supervision.** Proffit stresses careful selection and supervision (VERIFIED, p 445); results depend on timing decisions the paper reduces to ages.

## External validity: who it does NOT apply to

- **Borderline crowding (about 5-9 mm).** Proffit's advice is to keep options open with a lower lingual arch (VERIFIED, p 446); the paper's 8 mm floor falls inside this band.
- **Skeletal Class II or III with crowding.** The paper's rules are internally inconsistent and stricter than Proffit's, which allows upper-only or lower-only sequences as complex treatment (VERIFIED, pp 443-444).
- **Patients whose lower canines erupt before the first premolars.** The commonest lower-arch problem in SE (VERIFIED, p 444) is not discussed, nor is enucleation and its bone defect (p 445).
- **Hypodontia.** Listed as a contraindication, but timed E extraction at 7-9 y with mesial drift is an option Proffit describes for missing second premolars when space, profile and jaws are good or somewhat protrusive (VERIFIED, p 434); this is topic 5 paper 2 (Kokich & Kokich 2006).
- **A child with early loss of one lower primary canine.** Current evidence does not support balancing extraction or treating the loss as an indication by itself (Christensen 2018 via Proffit, VERIFIED, pp 396-397).
- **Families who cannot attend for years of supervision.** The paper's "2-3 years" understates the programme; O'Shaughnessy's SE patients were followed for about 5.2 years before brackets (derived). This is the scenario of the 2023 and 2024 promotion recalls (`conflicts.md` C2).
- **Anyone expecting a finished result without appliances.** Proffit: SE alone almost never gives ideal positions or space closure (VERIFIED, p 443).
- **Retention planning.** Nothing in the paper justifies shorter retention (Little 1990, VERIFIED).

## Strengths

1. Clear, teachable structure: history, three definitions, two principles, indications, contraindications, advantages, disadvantages, phases, diagnosis by facial pattern, records, sequences. It reads like the exam's own vocabulary list.
2. Proffit's definition is quoted accurately (VERIFIED, p 443), and Dewel's C-D-4 order matches Proffit's sequence (VERIFIED, p 444).
3. The disadvantages that matter are named: bite deepening and residual space between the canine and second premolar, both shown in Proffit (VERIFIED, pp 444-446).
4. The diagnostic emphasis is right: the ideal patient is Class I with severe crowding and a good profile, and extraction is avoided in retrusive faces.
5. A useful list of OPG uses in mixed-dentition planning: missing and supernumerary teeth, space analysis, dental age, root development, eruption pattern, pathology (p 6437).
6. It names the history examiners ask about: Bunon, Kjellgren's 1929 naming (VERIFIED), Nance, Hotz's "guidance of eruption" (title VERIFIED), Dewel.
7. The two-phase framing (reversible primary-tooth extractions, irreversible permanent extractions) is a helpful way to explain to parents why the first step commits to nothing.

## What would have made it better

1. A described literature search, even a simple one, and a statement of the evidence level behind each claim.
2. The outcome evidence: Little 1990 on stability, Wilson 1999 on profile, O'Shaughnessy 2011 and Wagner & Berg 2000 on time, Lopes Filho 2015 as the summary.
3. One consistent table of the three sequences with distinct codes and matching captions, and a source for the Tweed and Nance versions (`conflicts.md` A1).
4. Timing by dental development (½ to ⅔ first-premolar root for the lower D), not age alone.
5. The lower-arch problem (canine ahead of the 4), the enucleation option and its bone defect, and the risk of premolar impaction.
6. The adjuncts promised in the introduction: a lower lingual arch to preserve leeway space, and comprehensive fixed treatment afterwards.
7. A per-arch threshold with a measurement method, and a rule for the 5-9 mm band.
8. A complications section that matches its figure, with the over-retraction caption either supported or removed (A2).
9. Correct citations (A9-A10) and credits for the figures.
10. Removal of claims that the evidence does not support (stability, retention, cost), or a citation for each.

## Best use for the exam

Treat it as a vocabulary sheet, cross-checked line by line against Proffit Ch 12 pp 442-446 and Ch 11 pp 396-397. Take the definition, Dewel's sequence, the two principles, the indication and contraindication lists and the history from it. Take every number and every outcome statement from Proffit, O'Shaughnessy 2011 and Little 1990 instead. For Tweed against Nance, learn the version usually taught (Tweed: D, then 4 and C together; Nance: D, 4, C in sequence; VERIFIED-secondary) and know that this paper's own caption mixes them.
