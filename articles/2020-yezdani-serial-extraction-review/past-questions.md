### Q1 · Promotion 2023

> Parent came concerned about their child's situation (shown in the photo: lower arch with arrow at the canine region).

- **Recalled answer:** Early loss of primary canine · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Early (unilateral) loss of the primary canine from crowding, causing a midline shift to that side and loss of space for the permanent canine (Yezdani 2020 p 6434, Fig 2c and the indications list (unilateral loss of a deciduous canine with a shift to the same side))
- The review lists early unilateral loss of a deciduous canine as a sign of severe arch-length deficiency and an indication for serial extraction. It causes the midline to shift and the space for the permanent canine to close.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles

### Q2 · Promotion 2023

> Parent lives far from the clinic and is confused about serial extraction versus late extraction. Which option is correct?

- **Recalled answer:** Serial extraction has a shorter active phase of orthodontic appliance (underlined) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Serial extraction shortens the active (multibanded) appliance phase, but the whole programme needs many visits over 2-3 years (Yezdani 2020 p 6435 (advantages: shorter multibanded treatment; disadvantages: prolonged treatment with multiple visits over 2-3 years, and repeated extraction visits))
- The review states both sides: the fixed-appliance phase is shorter, but the overall programme is long and needs many visits. A family living far away needs to hear both. Last year's tag (O'Shaughnessy, folder 6) is not in this year's list, and Yezdani is this year's source.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; annotated 'folder 6, file3'

### Q3 · Promotion 2024

> Patient lives far away from the clinic and is concerned about the number of visits, asking about the difference between late premolar extraction and early serial extraction?
>
> - a) Serial: longer treatment time
> - b) Late premolar extraction: longer treatment time

- **Recalled answer:** a) Serial longer treatment time · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a) Serial extraction: longer overall treatment time with more visits (the active fixed-appliance phase is the shorter one) (Yezdani 2020 p 6435 (disadvantage: prolonged treatment with multiple visits over 2-3 years; advantage: shorter multibanded treatment))
- For a patient worried about travelling to visits, the relevant fact is that serial extraction stretches treatment over 2-3 years of supervision and extraction visits. The resident's side note is also true: late premolar extraction has the longer active appliance phase.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 6, 3 Efficiency of serial extraction and late premolar extraction cases treated with fixed appliances, Kevin W. O'Shaughnessy (note beside b: 'longer active tx time')

### Q4 · Promotion 2024

> 8-year-old patient presented with the following (picture: lower permanent incisors erupting lingual to retained primary incisors). What is the appropriate treatment approach?
>
> - a. Extraction of primary incisors
> - b. Extraction of primary incisors then 2x4
> - c. Extraction of primary incisors then expansion

- **Recalled answer:** a. Extraction of primary incisors. · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a. Extraction of the primary incisors (Yezdani 2020 p 6434 Fig 2b (permanent incisors erupting lingual to retained primary incisors); p 6435 (reversible phase: extracting anterior deciduous teeth lets the permanent incisors align))
- The review's first, reversible step for this picture is to remove the retained primary incisors so the permanent incisors can align on their own. It does not call for an appliance or expansion at this stage.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 6, 2 Serial Extraction In Orthodontics - Review Article, Dr. Arif Yezdani

### Q5 · Promotion 2021

> 9-year-old woman came with crowding of 10 mm (OPG with extracted Cs and Ds and erupting canines). What is the best treatment?
>
> - Serial extraction
> - Expansion

- **Per the paper:** Serial extraction (continue the sequence by removing the first premolars) (Yezdani 2020 p 6433 (indications: severe crowding with an arch deficiency of 8-10 mm or more), p 6435 (mild to moderate crowding is a contraindication), pp 6438-6439 (Dewel/Tweed/Nance sequences))
- The review limits serial extraction to severe crowding of about 8-10 mm or more in the mixed dentition. A 9-year-old with 10 mm of crowding and the primary canines and first molars already removed fits that, so the next step is to extract the first premolars, not to expand.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)
