# Key numbers — Yezdani 2020 (serial extraction in orthodontics, review)

Yezdani A, Nandhini P, Padmavati R. Serial extraction in orthodontics – review article. Eur J Mol Clin Med 2020;7(2):6432-6441. Every number below was read from the page images (journal pages 6432-6441) and checked against the extracted text.

**What kind of paper this is.** This is a short narrative review in a multidisciplinary journal, written as definitions, lists and textbook extraction sequences. It has **no methods section, no sample, no tables, no measurements, no statistics and no P values.** Every number in it is a threshold, an age, a date, a count or a citation. The sections below therefore list the paper's stated numbers, decision rules and figures in place of results tables. Page numbers are journal pages. "Derived" means computed here from the printed text.

VERIFIED-secondary = number seen only in a full-text copy, a colleague's summary or a later paper citing it. VERIFIED (notes) = in the resident's 6th-edition Proffit study pages, not the printed book. UNVERIFIED = not checked. Proffit page numbers are 7th-edition book pages.

### Contraindications (p 6435, numbered 1-9, ref 6)

1. Congenital absence of teeth providing space. 2. Mild to moderate crowding (no millimetres given). 3. Deep or open bites. 4. Severe Class II or III of dental or skeletal origin. 5. Cleft lip and palate. 6. Spaced dentition. 7. Anodontia / oligodontia. 8. Midline diastema. 9. Dilacerations.

Items 1 and 7 overlap, as do 6 and 8 (derived).

### Advantages and disadvantages (p 6435)

| Advantages (7, ref 7) | Disadvantages (7, refs 7-8) |
|---|---|
| Psychological trauma avoided | Long-term procedure needing knowledge of growth, eruption sequence and calcification |
| Shorter multibanded treatment | Prolonged treatment with multiple visits (**2-3 years**) |
| "Physiological" treatment using physiological forces | Patient cooperation essential |
| Better oral hygiene | Possible tongue thrust |
| Lower cost | Overbite tends to increase (bite deepening) |
| More stable results | Residual spaces between canine and second premolar |
| Shorter retention | Multiple progressive extraction visits |

No data are given for any advantage or disadvantage.

## Derived numbers

- Pages: 6441 − 6432 + 1 = 10.
- Dewel's deciduous first molar extraction: 8.5 + 1 = about 9.5 y.
- D-first sequences (Tweed, Nance) start at 8 y, 0.5 y before Dewel's canine extraction.
- Indication bullets 12 (4 + 6 + 2 across pp 6433-6435); advantages 7; disadvantages 7; diagnostic categories 5.
- References: 16 in all; 7 textbooks (refs 1, 3, 4, 8, 9, 11, 12) and 9 journal or society items. 15 are dated; 10 of the 15 (67 %) are before 1990; median year 1982. The newest is 2008 (ref 4, Tandon textbook); the newest journal item is 2004 (ref 15, Travess). Ref 12 (Proffit, 2nd edition) has no year.
- Kjellgren: 1929 (name) to 1947 (ref 14) = 18 years between the dated event and the cited source.
- Threshold gap: the paper's lower bound of 8 mm sits inside Proffit's 5-9 mm borderline band, 2 mm below his "more than 10 mm per arch".
- Time gap: the paper's 2-3 years against about 5.2 y of observation before fixed appliances in O'Shaughnessy's serial-extraction group (13.11 − 7.95 y; VERIFIED-secondary inputs).
