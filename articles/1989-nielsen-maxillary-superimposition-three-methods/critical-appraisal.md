# Critical appraisal — Nielsen 1989

Nielsen IL. Maxillary superimposition: a comparison of three methods for cephalometric evaluation of growth and treatment change. *Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop* 1989;95(5):422-431. Single author, Department of Growth and Development, University of California San Francisco. No funding, ethics or conflict statement (none was customary in 1989).

Numbers come from the printed tables (page images, journal pages 426-428), which take precedence over the running text where the two disagree. Recomputations are mine, run from the printed means, SDs and n = 18 (script `checks.py` in this folder), and are labelled as such. Where I lean on the research notes (Björk, Baumrind, the ABO, Graf 2022) or on the other two topic papers (Doppel 1994, Nguyen 2018) I say so. Axis convention: Fig 6 puts +X anterior and +Y upward, so a negative Y is descent or eruption.

## Verdict in three lines

1. **Design label:** retrospective, within-subject method-comparison (criterion-validity) study on 18 pairs of serial lateral cephalograms with maxillary tantalum implants, taken 4 y apart at about 10 and 14 y; two anatomic superimpositions (palatal "best fit" registered at ANS; Björk-Skieller "structural" on the anterior surface of the zygomatic process) compared against the implant superimposition on the same films, plus a two-observer reliability check of the structural method only. No clinical outcome, no randomisation, no control group needed or present.
2. **Level of evidence for a methods study:** Oxford CEBM diagnostic level 3-4 (non-consecutive, quality-selected sample against a reference standard; the mapping is mine, UNVERIFIED). QUADAS-2 domains, my grading: patient selection high risk (films chosen for the index test's own visibility needs), index test unclear (method error measured, not reported), reference standard unclear (implant-stability criterion not stated, not all bilateral), flow and timing unclear (n behind the implant comparisons not reconcilable with 18). GRADE-style certainty: high that a palatal best fit under-reads vertical change in growing children (t = 3.6-9.1 in all 16 cells, biologically predicted by Björk's nasal-floor resorption, replicated by Isaacson 1976 and Baumrind 1987, literature); moderate for its size (28-59 % by landmark); low for the direction of the structural method's 0.5 mm horizontal bias (the paper states it both ways); very low for "reliable" (one second observer after brief instructions, no ICC, 0.5-0.9 mm vertical disagreement at ANS and the incisors).
3. **The one thing that limits it most:** the 18 films were chosen because the zygomatic process, ANS and orbital floors were clear and 3-4 stable implants were visible on both films, so the structural method was tested on the films it needs; and the implant rows of Tables III-IV cannot be rebuilt from Tables I-II (gaps up to 0.38 mm), so the number of subjects behind the headline comparison is unknown.

## Study question

| | The paper says |
|---|---|
| **Aim 1** (p 424) | Compare three superimpositions of the same serial films: (1) traditional best fit along the palatal plane registered at ANS, (2) implant, (3) structural (Björk & Skieller, anterior surface of the zygomatic process). |
| **Aim 2** | Examine the validity and reliability of the structural superimposition. |
| **Material** | 18 of 35 UCSF implant subjects (Matthews' series, ref 21; the same material as Baumrind's 1983 maxillary-traction study); 6 boys, 12 girls; T1 10 ± 0.37 y; interval 4 ± 0.56 y; treated and untreated, mostly Class II, some Class I, no Class III. |
| **Index tests** | Best fit: optimal fit of the hard palate with the nasal floors aligned, registered at ANS and the ANS-to-A-point region. Structural: both zygomatic processes, orbital floors and palatal floor traced; registered on the anterior zygomatic surface with orbital-floor apposition kept equal to nasal-floor resorption (1 : 1). |
| **Reference** | Implant superimposition: implants traced, the distance between them bisected; unstable implants excluded. |
| **Outcome** | T2 − T1 displacement (mm) of four skeletal (PNS, ANS, ANS-Harvold, point A) and four dental (U6C, U6A, UIE, UIA) landmarks in a Downs occlusal-plane coordinate system fixed to the T1 film (Fig 6), by each method (Tables I-II); between-method differences with significance marks (Tables III-IV); interobserver differences for the structural method (last row of Tables III-IV). |
| **Implied null** | No difference in landmark displacement between an anatomic method and the implant method; no difference between observers. |

My read: the question is the right one and could only be answered with implants, which cannot be placed in children today, so the dataset is irreplaceable. The design is a bias study (does the anatomic method agree with the truth on average?), not an agreement study (how far can one patient's superimposition be from the truth?), and it should be read as such.

## Design, sample and selection (direction of bias)

**Source and selection.** 35 subjects with maxillary implants placed by open surgery (Taylor and Ware); 18 usable (51 %, derived). Exclusions: poor visibility of the zygomatic process, ANS or orbital floors; excessive double contours from mal-positioning; fewer than 3-4 stable zygomatic implants visible on both films. The paper calls this selection "on the basis of the quality of the films only", but the criteria are exactly the structural method's own requirements (clear bilateral zygomatic processes, matched double contours) plus clean implant registration. This is selection on the index test. Agreement in routine clinical films will be worse than reported.

**Who they were.** 6 boys and 12 girls (67 % girls, derived), 10 ± 0.37 y at T1, 4 ± 0.56 y later at T2 (about 14 y). The age SD is implausibly tight for a sample selected on film quality (nearly all between 9.3 and 10.7 y if it is an SD); it may be a standard error or an undeclared age band. Treated and untreated pooled, numbers not given; given the source (Baumrind's maxillary-traction material, ref 21), a substantial fraction probably had headgear (literature inference, UNVERIFIED for these 18). Mostly Class II, some Class I, no Class III. No sex, treatment or growth-stage subgrouping "because of the limited sample size".

**Direction of bias on the main finding.** The 4-year window straddles the pubertal peak for most of the girls, so nasal-floor resorption and eruption are at their maximum and the best-fit vertical error measured here (1.08-2.24 mm skeletal, 1.33-1.84 mm dental) is near the top of its range. In an adult the same method's error shrinks toward zero. Treated subjects may remodel differently (the author concedes this "cannot be ruled out"), and the molar horizontal values (best fit +3.02 mm, implant +1.31 mm in 4 y, Table II) cannot be read as normal growth because headgear may be in them.

**Direction of bias on the structural-vs-implant comparison.** Film selection flatters the structural method. Running it at 1 : 1 instead of Björk's 3 : 2 pushes its vertical readings slightly past the implant values (over-read of 0.04-0.33 mm, Tables I-II, derived; NS) and, by the author's own mechanism, produces the 0.5 mm horizontal shift.

| Feature | What was done | Direction of bias |
|---|---|---|
| Sample selected for clear zygomatic processes and matched double contours | 18 of 35 | Flatters the structural method; routine-film error larger |
| Pubertal interval (10 to 14 y) | 4 y | Maximises the best-fit error; not transferable as a percentage to other ages |
| Treated and untreated pooled | counts not given | Unknown effect on remodelling; absolute displacements not normal growth |
| 1 : 1 apposition-resorption rule | Methods p 424 | Structural method slightly over-reads descent and shifts horizontally |
| Unstable implants excluded per film | criterion not stated | Reference standard cleaner than nature; n may vary by landmark |
| Second observer "after brief instructions", structural method only | n = 1 observer | Reliability tested for the favoured method only; no best-fit reliability arm for comparison |

**Blinding.** The best-fit and structural tracings were made without the implants drawn, which blinds the superimposition step to the reference. The same operator (the number of primary observers is never stated) traced all three methods, knew the subject and the film order; film order cannot be blinded in growth studies. Landmark digitising was presumably by the same person. A second observer repeated only the structural superimposition, twice, after brief instructions.

## The "gold standard" and its own error

**What it is.** Tantalum implants in the zygomatic process ("posterior (lateral) implants", Fig 4), at least 3-4 stable ones on both films, registration on the bisector of the inter-implant distance. Any implant "determined to be unstable" was excluded; the criterion is not stated. Not all subjects had bilateral implants, and the author concedes bilateral implants would have reduced errors from head-position differences.

**What it cannot remove.**
- Landmark identification error, which the author says dominated the variance for all three methods (Baumrind & Frantz 1971, ref 25). The SDs in Tables III-IV therefore mix method disagreement with landmark noise.
- Film-to-film head rotation, handled only by excluding gross double contours. Doppel 1994 (topic paper) cites Julius 1971: heads rotate up to 3° and tip up to 5° in a cephalostat, moving a zygomatic implant by up to 3 mm (UNVERIFIED thesis). Korn & Baumrind 1990 (literature, VERIFIED abstract) found posterior maxillary widening of 0.43 mm/y, so a unilateral zygomatic contour drifts even with a perfectly repositioned head.
- 2D projection of 3D remodelling, and magnification.
- Tracing error, not quantified here; Doppel found ± 0.25 mm on duplicate tracings, 0.5 mm total, with the same kind of material.
- Implant movement within growing bone, assumed zero by the Björk method.

**The n problem.** All 16 structural-minus-best-fit cells of Tables III-IV rebuild from Tables I-II to within 0.01 mm; the 32 cells involving the implant method do not (point A Y 2.24 printed vs 1.86 computed; point A X 1.15 vs 0.92; UIA X 1.30 vs 1.09; UIE Y implant minus structural −0.20 vs 0.00; point A X implant minus structural 0.41 vs 0.13). For paired data the mean of the differences equals the difference of the means, so the implant comparisons were run on fewer subjects for some landmarks (films where an implant was excluded, probably) or on a different determination. "N = 18" under Figs 8 and 9 cannot hold for every implant cell. The gaps are largest at point A and the incisors, the landmarks the paper itself calls hard to locate.

**Consequence.** The mean bias of best fit is interpretable and large. The individual-level agreement of the structural method with implants is not interpretable from this paper, because the reference's own noise, the landmark noise and the method's error are not separated.

## Measurement and statistics

**Coordinate system.** X axis = Downs occlusal plane of the T1 film (bisecting the occlusion of first molars and central incisors); Y axis = perpendicular through the mesial cusps of the first molars; origin at U6C (Fig 6). The frame is fixed to the T1 tracing and carried to the T2 tracing by each superimposition, so the frame itself is not a source of between-method difference. Landmark definitions are given (p 425), including Harvold's ANS at 3 mm bony thickness; ANS and ANS(H) behaved alike (implant vertical 1.62 vs 1.56 mm), which the paper notes.

**Procedure.** Separate tracings per film per method on mat acetate (Baumrind, Miller & Molthen technique, refs 22-23); registration points, fiducial points (A-D, Fig 5) and landmarks digitised; UCSF computer-aided analysis; means and SDs by SAS.

**Tests.** Never named. From the layout (one mean ± SD per method-pair per landmark-axis, asterisks at three thresholds) it is a paired t on the per-subject differences. No statement that it was paired, no normality check, no confidence intervals, no correction for 64 comparisons (4 rows × 8 landmark-axis columns × 2 tables). The Table IV footnote (\*\* P < 0.001, \*\*\* P < 0.0001) cannot be right for its own numbers; Table III's thresholds (\*\* P < 0.01, \*\*\* P < 0.001) fit both tables.

**Recomputation from the printed means and SDs (paired t, n = 18, df 17; my arithmetic).**
- Implant vs best fit: t = 3.58 (PNS X) to 9.08 (UIE Y); P ≤ 0.002 in all 16 cells. Bonferroni at 0.05/64 = 0.0008: all eight vertical cells survive (P ≤ 0.0002); of the eight horizontal cells five survive and PNS X (P = 0.002), U6A X (P = 0.001) and ANS X (P = 0.0008) sit at the threshold.
- Implant vs structural, horizontal: t = 2.64-3.42, P = 0.003-0.017 (point A 0.017, unstarred); none survives Bonferroni. Vertical: t = −0.14 to −1.62, P = 0.12-0.89, none significant, as printed.
- Structural vs best fit, vertical: t = 6.3-9.3, all P < 0.0001; horizontal: t = 2.62-3.06, P = 0.007-0.018, of which Table III stars only point A and Table IV stars all four dental cells.
- Interobserver: ANS(H) X t = 3.07, P = 0.007 (unstarred, and the text says no horizontal difference was significant); ANS(H) Y P = 0.011 and ANS Y P = 0.010 (printed \*\*); point A Y P = 0.034; UIE Y P = 0.011; UIA Y P = 0.009; U6C X and UIE X P = 0.058. None survives correction.

**What was not analysed.**
- Agreement at the individual level. The paper argues from mean bias. The SD of implant-minus-structural differences is 0.66-1.19 mm (skeletal) and 0.68-0.87 mm (dental). If those differences are roughly normal, 95 % of patients fall within about ± 1.3 to ± 2.3 mm of the implant answer (1.96 × SD, derived): a structural superimposition can be 2 mm off in one patient while the group mean is unbiased. No limits of agreement, no ICC, no per-subject plot.
- Method error of the double determinations (measured, never reported), and whether one determination or the mean of two went into the tables.
- Treated vs untreated, sex, growth rate, tooth inclination change (all acknowledged, p 430).
- Reliability of the best-fit method, so "structural is reliable" has no comparison level.
- Whether the horizontal structural bias depends on how much vertical growth occurred, which the author's own explanation predicts it should.
- The direction of that bias: the Discussion says posterior; Tables I-II put every structural landmark 0.13-0.75 mm anterior to the implant value (mean 0.51 mm, derived).

## Generalisability

**Applies to:** growing patients of roughly 9-15 y; 2D lateral cephalograms traced by hand; any maxillary superimposition that registers on the palate at ANS versus one that uses the anterior zygomatic surface with the orbital-floor against nasal-floor rule; mostly Class II faces; mixed treatment.

**Does not transfer directly to:** adults (little remodelling, so the best-fit error is small, though Björk & Skieller's ratio runs to 20 y and Doppel's sample shows change to 20.3 y); Class III, surgical, RME or protraction patients; digital internal-palate best fits (McNamara's method is drawn in Fig 1e but was not tested; Doppel found it statistically good and empirically poor); 3D voxel registration (Nguyen 2018 is the mandibular 3D analogue, with 0.12 ± 0.11 mm observer error and no implant standard).

**The percentage is not portable.** The masked quantity is nasal-floor resorption per unit time, not a fraction of tooth movement, so "30-50 %" applies to 4 years of pubertal growth and to nothing else; quote the per-year bias (0.27-0.56 mm/y skeletal, 0.33-0.46 mm/y dental, derived) when scaling to another interval, and discount it for age.

**Radiation and availability** are not at issue: the structural method is a tracing rule applied to films already taken, which is why it is still teachable and why the ABO (research notes, VERIFIED deck) requires it, citing this paper as validation.

**Registry.** Khaleel 2026 superimposed the maxilla on the palatal vault (Broadbent) at mean ages 15.8-17.4 y over 44-94 d. By Nielsen's per-year bias the masked descent is under 0.1 mm, so the bias is immaterial; but Nielsen's landmark SDs (0.53-2.90 mm) and interobserver differences (0.5-0.9 mm at the incisors) are larger than Khaleel's upper-arch values (0.07-0.42 mm) and between-arm differences (0.2-0.35 mm), which is the real inference (see `conflicts.md`, Section E).

**Today's relevance.** The structural method validated here is the comparator for automated 2D superimposition (Kim 2022, literature) and gave tooth movements equal to mini-screw-referenced 3D registration in Fan 2023 (literature, VERIFIED abstract). Graf 2022 (literature) rates the field as high risk of bias with no method proved accurate; this paper is the best maxillary evidence with implants, and still a small, selected sample.

## Strengths

1. An implant reference standard on the same films, the only kind of data that can arbitrate between anatomic superimpositions, and a dataset that cannot be regenerated ethically.
2. Three methods applied to identical tracings in one coordinate system; the comparison is within-subject and the frame is fixed to T1.
3. The anatomic tracings were made without the implants drawn, so the superimposition step was blinded to the reference.
4. Double determinations for all methods and a second observer for the index method, even if the numbers were not all reported.
5. The main result is biologically predicted (Björk's nasal-floor resorption and orbital-floor apposition, Fig 2), agrees in direction and size with Björk & Skieller, Isaacson 1976 and Baumrind 1987 (literature), and is large: t ≥ 4.8 for every vertical implant-vs-best-fit cell.
6. Each rejected method is rejected for a stated mechanism: PTM and the infratemporal fossa because PNS grows backward and the posterior zygomatic surface is appositional; the key ridge because it is lowered by apposition; ANS because of anterior-dominant resorption.
7. The author states his own method's weak points: film quality, bilateral double contours, a short zygomatic contour that "can create a rotational effect", unilateral implants, the 1 : 1 assumption.
8. The horizontal structural bias, although mis-signed in the Discussion, is reported at all; a less careful paper would have stopped at "no vertical difference".

## What would have made it better

1. Report n per landmark for the implant comparisons, and reconcile Tables III-IV with Tables I-II.
2. Name the test, state that it was paired, give confidence intervals, and either correct for multiplicity or declare the implant-vs-best-fit vertical comparison as primary.
3. Report the intra-observer error of the double determinations (Dahlberg or ICC) and say which determination entered the tables.
4. Give limits of agreement or a per-subject plot for structural vs implant, so the reader can see how far one patient can be from the truth.
5. State the sign convention of the difference tables, and resolve the direction of the 0.5 mm horizontal bias in one sentence.
6. Run the structural method at Björk's 3 : 2 as well as at 1 : 1 and report both.
7. Report how many subjects were treated, with what, and compare treated with untreated, or at least headgear with no headgear.
8. Add an interobserver arm for the best-fit method, so "reliable" has a comparison level, and use more than one second observer with a defined training step.
9. Give the age range and the number of subjects with bilateral implants; state the criterion for calling an implant unstable.
10. Correct the Table IV footnote, the Fig 8 incisor label and the ref 18 pagination before print.
