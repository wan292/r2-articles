# Past exam questions · Nielsen 1989

Previous years' promotion questions as residents recalled them (R2 exam 2019; promotion 2021–2024; last year's recall; the R2 end-of-year exam), linked to this paper and checked against it. The recalled answers are memory, not an answer key: where the paper disagrees, the line is marked ⚠️ and the paper wins.

**8 questions** (9 recalls) linked directly to this paper · ⚠️ = the recalled answer differs from the paper.

### Q1 · R2 end-of-year exam

> According to Björk's structural method, the lowering of the orbital floor is limited by:
>
> - A. Certain bone deposition toward the orbit
> - B. Equal to nasal drop

- **Recalled answer:** A. Certain bone deposition toward the orbit · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** A. Certain bone deposition toward the orbit (Nielsen 1989 p423 text and Fig 2 (O 6.4 mm, Su 11.2 mm, Re 4.6 mm; apposition:resorption about 3:2); Doppel 1994 p162 and p165 (orbit rises about 1.5:1 vs palatal lowering))
- Apposition at the orbital floor offsets part of the sutural lowering, which limits how far the floor drops. Option B mixes up the biology with the structural-method convention, where films are oriented so apposition equals nasal-floor resorption; the measured ratio is about 3:2, not equal.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Thilander P:149

### Q2 · R2 end-of-year exam

> The space for new molars is created by:
>
> - A. Anterior apposition on tuberosity
> - B. Posterior apposition on tuberosity
> - C. Apposition on alveolar ridge

- **Per the paper:** B. Posterior apposition on tuberosity (Nielsen 1989 p429 Discussion (PNS moved 2.3 mm posteriorly on implant superimposition = maxillary lengthening by sutural growth plus tuberosity apposition, after Björk & Skieller); Doppel 1994 p161-162 (maxilla lengthens at PNS))
- Both papers put maxillary growth in length at the back of the maxilla: PNS moves posteriorly through tuberosity apposition. Calling this the space for the erupting molars comes from Proffit, not from the papers.

### Q3 · R2 end-of-year exam

> Growth of the palate is by:
>
> - A. Its own drift & displacement of maxilla
> - B. Its own displacement & drift of the maxilla
> - C. ……
> - D. …….

- **Per the paper:** A. Its own drift & displacement of maxilla (Nielsen 1989 p422 (resorptive lowering of nasal floor, greater anteriorly) and p423 Fig 2 (Su 11.2 mm sutural lowering + Re 4.6 mm resorption); Doppel 1994 p165 (Björk & Skieller 1972: sutural growth plus alveolar apposition plus nasal-floor resorption))
- The palate descends through the whole maxilla being displaced by sutural growth and through its own remodelling drift (resorption on the nasal floor). Option A puts these the right way round.

### Q4 · Growth MCQ list

> According to Björk, the maxilla is lowered by a combination of displacement and alveolar growth: what proportions?

- **Recalled answer:** 43% displacement and 57% of alveolar length. · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Sutural lowering (displacement) about 43% and alveolar growth about 57% of the increase in maxillary height. Derived from Fig 2: 11.2 mm and 14.6 mm of 25.8 mm. (Nielsen 1989 p423 Fig 2 (Björk & Skieller 1977, 9 boys aged 4-20 y: Su = 11.2 mm, A = 14.6 mm); Doppel 1994 p165 gives the qualitative statement)
- Fig 2 prints only millimetres, but 11.2/(11.2+14.6) gives 43% and 14.6/25.8 gives 57% (derived), which reproduces the recalled split exactly. The paper never states the percentages in words.

### Q5 · Growth MCQ list

> According to Björk, the orbital floor moves down by what percentage, and how is this explained?

- **Recalled answer:** 25% explained by apposition at orbital floor. · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** The orbital floor drops less than the maxilla because 6.4 mm of orbital-floor apposition offsets 11.2 mm of sutural lowering (net about 4.8 mm, derived). The paper gives no percentage; 6.4 mm is about 25% of the 25.8 mm total (derived). (Nielsen 1989 p423 Fig 2 and text (apposition:resorption about 3:2, ages 4-20 y); Doppel 1994 p162)
- The explanation (apposition at the orbital floor) matches the paper. The recalled 25% matches the apposition's share of the total height increase. The floor's net drop is about 19% of that total, or 43% of the sutural lowering (both derived), so treat the percentage as UNVERIFIED.

### Q6 · Growth MCQ list

> According to Enlow: maxillary anterior surface? chin surface?

- **Recalled answer:** Max ant surface = resorptive; Chin surface = variable, resorption and deposition. · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Anterior surface of the maxilla: resorptive (Enlow). Chin surface: not stated in the paper. (Nielsen 1989 p422 (Enlow, Kuroda & Lewis: anterior maxillary surface resorptive); Doppel 1994 p162 (Kurihara, Enlow & Rangel: resorptive fields on anterior maxillary arch). Nguyen 2018 p751 covers the chin only from Björk's view (little or no remodelling at pogonion).)
- The maxillary half of the recall matches what both papers attribute to Enlow. The chin half (mixed deposition and resorption) comes from Enlow/Proffit and is not in any topic-1 paper, so it is UNVERIFIED here.

### Q7 · Growth + biomechanics list

> How much do the nasal floor and the orbital floor drop (Björk)?

- **Recalled answer:** The nasal floor drops 61%, the orbital floor is lowered by 25% · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Fig 2 in mm: the nasal floor drops 15.8 mm (11.2 sutural + 4.6 resorption), about 61% of the 25.8 mm total; the orbital floor drops a net 4.8 mm (about 19%); its 6.4 mm apposition is about 25%. All percentages derived. (Nielsen 1989 p423 Fig 2 (Björk & Skieller 1977, 9 boys aged 4-20 y: O 6.4, Su 11.2, Re 4.6, A 14.6 mm))
- The recalled 61% for the nasal floor reproduces exactly from Fig 2. The recalled 25% for the orbital floor matches its apposition share rather than its net drop, so learn it as 'apposition about 25%'. The paper prints mm values and the 3:2 ratio, not percentages.

### Q8 · Growth + biomechanics list

> Growth of the maxilla: mechanism (floor of the nose).

- **Recalled answer:** floor of the nose resorption · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Resorptive lowering of the nasal floor, greater anteriorly than posteriorly (Re = 4.6 mm in Fig 2) (Nielsen 1989 p422 (resorptive lowering of nasal floor, greater anteriorly) and p423 Fig 2; p429 (ANS drops about twice as much as PNS); Doppel 1994 p162 and p165)
- Both papers report, from Björk's implant studies, that the nasal floor is lowered by resorption while the orbital floor builds up. Nielsen adds that this is greater anteriorly, which is why best-fit palatal superimposition hides it.
