# Conflicts and disagreements — Nielsen 1989

Use this file when you need to challenge a number, a claim or a citation in Nielsen IL. Maxillary superimposition: a comparison of three methods for cephalometric evaluation of growth and treatment change. *Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop* 1989;95(5):422-431. Section A is the paper against itself (every row checked on the page images of Tables I-IV and Figs 1-2 and 7-9, journal pages 423-429, with the arithmetic re-run from the printed means and SDs; script `checks.py` in this folder), B is the paper against Doppel 1994 and Nguyen 2018, the other two papers in this topic, C is the paper against the wider literature, D is the paper against Proffit, E is the paper against the hub registry (Khaleel 2026), F lists what stays open and G is the one-paragraph verdict. Page numbers are journal pages. Numbers from other papers carry the research pack's VERIFIED or UNVERIFIED tag; nothing outside the three topic papers was re-read here. Axis convention throughout: Fig 6 (p 426) puts +X anterior (toward ANS and UIE) and +Y upward, so a negative Y is descent or eruption and a negative X is posterior; the text reads PNS X −2.34 as a posterior displacement of 2.3 mm, which confirms it. "The paper says", "the literature says" and "my read / my arithmetic" are used deliberately.

## A. Internal inconsistencies (the paper vs itself)

Everything the audit listed was confirmed on the page images; nothing had to be dropped. Severity: **High** = changes what a reader takes from the paper or cannot be reconciled with n = 18; **Moderate** = a number or direction a consultant could challenge; **Low** = a reporting slip; **Trivial** = cosmetic. Recomputed t and P values assume a paired t test on 18 subjects (df 17), which is what a row labelled "implant minus best fit ± SD" implies; the paper never names its test (A14).

One finding frames several rows. All 16 "structural minus best fit" cells in Tables III-IV rebuild to within 0.01 mm from the means in Tables I-II (PNS X: −1.81 − (−1.18) = −0.63, printed 0.63; ANS Y: −1.72 − 0.28 = −2.00, printed 2.00; U6C Y: −4.99 − (−3.40) = −1.59, printed 1.59; UIE Y 1.99). The 32 cells that involve the implant method do not (gaps up to 0.38 mm). For paired data the mean of the differences must equal the difference of the means, so the implant comparisons were not run on the same 18 subjects, or not on the same determinations, as Tables I-II.

**Checks that passed** (so you can say so). n = 18, six boys and 12 girls, 18 of 35 usable: consistent on p 422, 424 and 430. Results p 427 vs Table I: ANS implant 1.6 mm about twice PNS 0.7 mm (−1.62, −0.70); PNS horizontal 2.3 vs 1.2 mm (−2.34, −1.18); point A down 0.5 vs 2.3 mm (−0.47, −2.33); point A horizontal 0.1 mm with best fit, SD ± 2.2 (0.14 ± 2.21); point A 0.8 mm posterior on implants (−0.78). Best fit shows PNS, ANS and ANS(H) slightly upward (0.36, 0.28, 0.37) and point A slightly downward (−0.47), as the text says. Structural vertical values are all more negative than implant (−0.04 to −0.33 mm skeletal, −0.20 to −0.29 mm dental), matching "tended to slightly overestimate". The "about 50 %" under-read of PNS horizontal growth: 1 − 1.18/2.34 = 50 %. The four Fig 9 values match Tables III-IV. The Fig 1 and Fig 3 page credits fall inside the cited references' page ranges. The implant-vs-best-fit effects reproduce at t = 3.6-9.1 (P ≤ 0.002 in every one of 16 cells).

**How to raise these politely.** The author cannot be written to about a 1989 single-author paper, so these are session points, not a letter. Lead with what holds: the vertical best-fit bias is large, biologically predicted and reproducible from the tables. Then raise the three findings that change how the paper is used, each as a question. First (A1): the structural-minus-best-fit rows rebuild exactly from Table I and the implant rows do not; does anyone read this as fewer than 18 subjects in the implant comparisons? Second (A7): the tables put the structural landmarks 0.5 mm anterior to the implant ones and the Discussion says posterior; which sign do we teach? Third (A3): the 30-50 % is 28 % at the molar cusp and 59 % at the incisal edge; is the 50 % an incisal-edge number? Keep A2, A9-A12 and A18 as footnote-level remarks (the Table IV footnote and the Fig 8 incisor label are misprints) and do not spend session time on A19-A22. Phrase nothing as an accusation: hand-typeset tables from 1989 carry slips, and the primary finding does not depend on any of them.

## B. Against the other two papers in this topic

Doppel et al. 1994 (a1: 50 implanted adolescents, maximum deviation of 17 maxillary structures from a three-implant fit, Table II) and Nguyen et al. 2018 (a3: 20 + 25 growing patients, 3D voxel registration of the mandible against bone plates). Doppel cites Nielsen as ref 20 and summarises it correctly (30-50 %; structural vs implant differed horizontally only; apposition set equal to resorption). Nguyen does not cite Nielsen. The three metrics cannot be converted into one another: Nielsen measures mean landmark displacement under each superimposition; Doppel measures the maximum local mismatch of a contour after implant registration; Nguyen measures mean surface distance after registration.

## C. Against the wider literature

Format: **citation** (pack tag) — what it found — VERDICT — one journal-club sentence. Nothing here was re-read against a source; tags are the research pack's.

### Björk's implant work, the paper's own foundation

- **C1. Björk 1955, Acta Odontol Scand 13:9-34 (ref 12)** — VERIFIED citation only. Introduces tantalum implants for serial superimposition. CONTEXT. "Everything in this paper rests on a tantalum pin in the zygomatic process not moving; Björk 1955 is the reference, not the finding."
- **C2. Björk & Skieller 1972, Am J Orthod 62:339-383 (ref 15)** — VERIFIED citation. 21 untreated subjects at 6-year intervals; differential nasal-floor resorption with maxillary rotation; Isaacson re-analysed four of the 21. AGREES. "Nielsen's 18 treated and untreated children reproduce what Björk and Skieller saw in 21 untreated ones."
- **C3. Björk & Skieller 1977, Br J Orthod 4:53-64 (ref 17)** — VERIFIED abstract (PMID 273440). Nine boys; anterior zygomatic surface sagittally stable; orbital-floor apposition to nasal-floor resorption about 3 : 2 (the abstract words it as sutural lowering about twice the orbital-floor apposition and nasal-floor resorption about one-third of alveolar apposition; 3 : 2 is Nielsen's reading of the Fig 2 values 6.4 and 4.6); transverse rotation of the two maxillae. AGREES (Nielsen adopts the structural rule) and PARTIAL (he ran it at 1 : 1). "Nielsen cites the 3 : 2 and then sets apposition equal to resorption; the small vertical over-read is the price."
- **C4. Björk & Skieller 1977, Trans Eur Orthod Soc (ref 18, pagination misprinted; A20)** — VERIFIED via the two topic papers. The structural method itself: SN transferred after cranial-base superimposition (NSL_A and NSL_B in Fig 3), then registration on the anterior zygomatic contour; vertical registration acknowledged as the weak step. AGREES. "The method's authors called the vertical step the weak one; Nielsen found the vertical step clean and the horizontal one biased by 0.5 mm."
- **C5. Korn & Baumrind 1990, J Dent Res 69:1298-1306** — VERIFIED abstract. 31 Björk-implant subjects 8.5-15.5 y; posterior maxillary widening 0.43 mm/y (SD 0.18, P < .001). CONTEXT. "Half a millimetre of widening a year is why Nielsen wanted bilateral implants and why a unilateral zygomatic contour can drift between films."

- **C6. Isaacson, Worms & Speidel 1976, Am J Orthod 70:290-303 (ref 20)** — VERIFIED abstract. AGREES. "Isaacson showed the direction in four cases; Nielsen put a percentage on it in 18."
- **C7. - **C8. Baumrind et al. 1987 part 2, AJODO 91:463-474** — VERIFIED abstract (PMID 3473926). ## E. Against the hub registry: Khaleel & Al-Nimri 2026

Khaleel's methods, from the hub's `key-numbers.md` for `2026-khaleel-deep-bite-turbos-rcos-elastics` (registry): maxillary superimposition "on the palatal vault (Broadbent)", mandibular on the Ricketts corpus axis Xi-Pm registered at Pm; mean ages 15.82 / 16.00 / 17.35 y; intervals 44.42 / 55.72 / 94.15 d (0.12 / 0.15 / 0.26 y, derived); intra-examiner ICC 0.92-0.98 for repeated measurement, no superimposition error estimate; upper-arch changes (Table 4): incisors V −0.09 / −0.14 / −0.43 mm, H 0.46 / 0.55 / 0.63 mm; upper premolar and molar cusps 0.07-0.15 mm in Groups I and III and 0.28-0.42 mm in Group II, SDs 0.07-0.29 mm. Everything below is inference from Nielsen's tables to a different age band and interval, and is stated as such.

1. **Which method Khaleel used.** "Broadbent" in Nielsen's Fig 1a is the palatal plane registered at ANS, the best fit his tables condemn; "palatal vault" may instead mean an internal-palate best fit (Nielsen Fig 1e, McNamara; Doppel Fig 4). Doppel states the two behave alike, so the inference does not depend on which one it was. Khaleel does not describe the registration point.
2. **Direction of the bias is fixed.** Every maxillary landmark appears less descended than it is (Nielsen Tables I-IV). Applied to Khaleel: upper posterior extrusion is under-read, and upper-incisor apparent intrusion is over-read, by the same offset (whether Khaleel's negative sign means intrusion is itself unresolved; Khaleel `conflicts.md` A10). Horizontally the method tends to show maxillary teeth more anterior than they are (Nielsen 1.2-1.4 mm per 4 y) but Doppel shows the direction varies with the case, so no correction to Khaleel's upper-incisor H values (0.46-0.63 mm) can be stated.
3. **Size over Khaleel's intervals (my arithmetic; UNVERIFIED for 16-17-year-olds).** The masked quantity is remodelling per unit time, not a share of tooth movement. Nielsen's masked descent at 10-14 y is 1.08-2.24 mm skeletal and 1.33-1.84 mm dental per 4 y, that is 0.27-0.56 and 0.33-0.46 mm per year. Scaling with no age discount gives 0.04-0.06 mm (Group II, 44 d), 0.05-0.07 mm (Group I, 56 d) and 0.09-0.12 mm (Group III, 94 d). Remodelling at 15.8-17.4 y is slower than at the pubertal peak (Björk & Skieller's ratio runs to 20 y in boys, Fig 2 legend, literature; Doppel's sample runs to 20.3 y), so the realistic offset is of the order of 0.05 mm or less. The 30-50 % must never be quoted as a percentage correction to Khaleel's values.
4. **The bias is unequal across arms but still negligible.** Group III has the longest interval (94 d) and would carry the largest masked descent, which would under-read its upper posterior values (0.07-0.08 mm) relative to Group II (0.28-0.42 mm); Group III is also the oldest arm, which pulls the other way. Net effect well under 0.1 mm.
5. **What Nielsen really says about Khaleel's upper-arch numbers is about resolution, not bias.** Nielsen's landmark SDs are 0.53-2.90 mm (Tables I-II) and his two observers differed by 0.52-0.88 mm vertically at the incisors and ANS on a validated method; Doppel's duplicate-tracing precision is ± 0.25 mm (total 0.5 mm). Khaleel's Group I and III upper posterior values (0.07-0.15 mm, SDs 0.07-0.08 mm) and the 0.2-0.35 mm between-arm difference behind "Group II significantly more upper extrusion" (no P printed) are below that resolution. My read: a 2D palatal superimposition cannot establish 0.2-0.4 mm differences in upper posterior extrusion over 6-13 weeks; the direction (elastics extrude upper posteriors) is biologically plausible, the magnitude is unmeasurable with this method.
6. **"Nongrowing" (Khaleel `conflicts.md` A15).** Nielsen's own window is 10-14 y, so he does not speak to 16-17-year-olds directly; the Björk & Skieller ratio he reproduces in Fig 2 runs to 20 y, and Doppel's sample shows continuing change to 20.3 y. Over Khaleel's 1.5-3-month intervals this is immaterial to the tooth values but relevant to the label and to the Mn-Mx changes (+0.69 to +1.84°), which Nielsen does not bear on.
7. **Repeatability is not validity.** Khaleel reports intra-examiner ICC 0.92-0.98 for repeated measurement by one operator. Nielsen shows that two observers using the same validated superimposition still differed by 0.5-0.9 mm at the landmarks that matter for a deep-bite paper (ANS, A point, incisal edge, incisor apex); Khaleel has no inter-examiner or superimposition-error figure at all.
8. **Mandible.** Nielsen is silent; the analogue for Khaleel's corpus-axis choice is Springate & Jones 1998 (registry B30; about 10 % of subjects > 4 mm at molars and incisors) and Nguyen 2018 (Pm sits on the stable chin, Xi sits in remodelling ramus bone).

Registry lines this paper touches: (a) Khaleel's "about 1 mm true intrusion (Point I) ... 0.99 / 0.71 / 0.86 mm in all three arms": add "within the 0.5-0.9 mm observer disagreement Nielsen measured at the incisors on a validated 2D method"; (b) Khaleel's "nongrowing at 15.82 / 16.00 / 17.35 y": add "Björk & Skieller's maxillary remodelling ratio runs to 20 y (Nielsen Fig 2, literature)"; (c) a new Khaleel line for "Group II upper posterior extrusion 0.28-0.42 vs about 0.08 mm, significantly more, no P": conflicts with "a palatal best fit under-reads maxillary descent (direction only; size over 44-94 d about 0.05 mm, derived) and the difference is below the landmark SDs of 0.53-2.90 mm (Nielsen) and the 0.5 mm tracing error (Doppel)".

## F. Open questions

- How many subjects contributed to each implant comparison in Tables III-IV? The implant rows do not rebuild from Tables I-II (gaps up to 0.38 mm at point A) while the structural-minus-best-fit rows do.
- Which way does the structural method's 0.5 mm horizontal bias run? The tables say the structural landmarks sit anterior to the implant ones; the Discussion says posterior.
- What statistical test produced the asterisks, and was it paired? Four cells with t ≈ 2.6-3.1 are unstarred and three with P ≈ 0.011 carry double stars.
- What was the intra-observer error of the double determinations, and was one determination or the mean analysed?
- How many of the 18 were treated, with what, and does the best-fit bias differ between treated and untreated? The author concedes treatment "cannot be ruled out" as an influence on remodelling.
- Is the age SD really 0.37 y, and what was the age range?
- How many subjects had bilateral implants, and what was the criterion for calling an implant unstable?
- Does the vertical bias of best fit scale with the amount of growth in the interval, as the mechanism predicts? No per-subject plot or regression is given.
- What would the structural method have shown with the 3 : 2 ratio Nielsen cites instead of the 1 : 1 he used? The over-read of −0.04 to −0.33 mm predicts a smaller residual, untested.
- Why does ANS lower more than PNS here (1.62 vs 0.70 mm) and less than PNS in Baumrind 1987 (1.56 vs 2.50 mm) in a sample from the same city? Age window (10-14 vs 8.5-15.5 y), treatment or landmark definition?
- What is the agreement at the individual level? With difference SDs of 0.7-1.2 mm, 95 % limits of about ± 1.3-2.3 mm (derived) mean a single structural superimposition can sit 2 mm from the implant answer while the group mean is unbiased.
- Does the structural method's reliability hold in routine films? Only 18 of 35 implant films met its visibility requirements.
- What is the correct pagination of ref 18, the paper that defines the structural method?

## G. One-paragraph verdict

Relative to its own tables, the paper's main claim stands and its secondary claims wobble: a palatal best fit registered at ANS hides 1.1-2.2 mm of skeletal and 1.3-1.8 mm of dental descent over four pubertal years (28 % at the molar cusp, 39-40 % at the apices, 54-59 % at the incisal edge), with t values of 3.6-9.1 that no typesetting slip can undo; but the implant rows cannot be rebuilt from the displacement tables, so the n behind them is unknown, the structural method's 0.5 mm horizontal bias is reported in both directions, the test is unnamed, 64 comparisons are uncorrected, the method error of the double tracings is never given, the Table IV footnote and the Fig 8 incisor label are misprints, and the abstract omits the one significant finding against the structural method. Relative to the other two papers, it agrees with Doppel 1994 on every rejected structure (ANS, PNS, PTM, A point, palate) and on the anterior zygomatic surface as the reference, differs from him on whether that surface remodels (stable in 18, variable in 50) and on the vertical ratio (1 : 1 executed here, 1.5 : 1 found there, 3 : 2 in Björk & Skieller), and is the maxillary counterpart of Nguyen 2018's mandibular result, with 2D observer disagreement of 0.5-0.9 mm against Nguyen's 0.12 mm. Relative to Proffit it is the growth chapters measured, and relative to the registry it shows that Khaleel 2026's palatal-vault superimposition has a known bias direction, a negligible bias size over 6-13 weeks, and a resolution of about half a millimetre that its 0.07-0.42 mm upper-arch values do not reach. Use it to teach why the maxilla is superimposed on the zygomatic process and never on the palate; do not use its 30-50 % as a correction factor, its "valid and reliable" as a statement about individual patients, or its 0.5 mm horizontal sign without saying the paper gives both.
