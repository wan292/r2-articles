# Clinical implications — Nielsen 1989

Nielsen IL. Maxillary superimposition: a comparison of three methods for cephalometric evaluation of growth and treatment change. Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop 1989;95(5):422-431. Eighteen UCSF children with maxillary tantalum implants (6 boys, 12 girls; T1 10 ± 0.37 y; interval 4 ± 0.56 y; treated and untreated pooled, mostly Class II), each film pair superimposed three ways: on the implants, on a palatal best fit registered at ANS, and on the Björk-Skieller structural method (anterior surface of the zygomatic process, orbital-floor apposition set equal to nasal-floor resorption). Landmark displacement was measured in a Downs occlusal-plane grid fixed to the first film (Tables I-IV).

Attribution on this page: "the paper says" = printed in Nielsen 1989; "the literature says" = other sources checked in the research pass (Doppel 1994 and Nguyen 2018 are the two companion papers of this topic); "my read" = my interpretation.

## What this changes or confirms when reading a ceph or a CBCT

1. **A palatal superimposition in a growing patient under-reads every vertical change in the upper arch, and always in the same direction.** The paper says: implant minus best fit, vertical, PNS 1.08, ANS 1.88, A point 2.24 mm (Table III) and U6C 1.33, U6A 1.46, UIE 1.84, UIA 1.71 mm (Table IV), all P < .001, over 4 years; best fit even showed PNS and ANS moving *up* 0.36 and 0.28 mm while implants showed them 0.70 and 1.62 mm *down* (Table I). My read: upper-molar "extrusion" read on a palatal best fit is a floor, not the value, and upper-incisor "intrusion" read the same way is inflated by the same amount; the share hidden was 28 % at the molar cusp and 59 % at the incisal edge (derived from Table II).

2. **The palatal plane itself rotates and descends, so it is not a stable line to measure against.** The paper says: on implants ANS descended 1.62 ± 0.83 mm and PNS 0.70 ± 1.14 mm in 4 years (Table I), "twice as much" anteriorly, from resorption that is greater at the front of the nasal floor. My read: an incisor-to-maxillary-plane angle compared across a growth interval is measured against a line that tipped; Doppel 1994 (a1) put the palatal-plane rotation relative to implants at +0.92 ± 2.10°, range −5° to +8° (its Table III).

3. **Best fit also mis-reads the horizontal, by about 1.2 mm.** The paper says: every landmark sat about 1.2 mm more anterior on best fit than on implants (Discussion; Tables III-IV 1.09-1.69 mm), and PNS moved back only 1.18 mm instead of 2.34 mm, half the growth in length (Table I). The first-molar cusp read 3.02 mm forward on best fit against 1.31 mm on implants (Table II). My read: in a growing Class II patient a palatal superimposition can turn 1.3 mm of mesial molar drift into 3 mm, or hide 1.7 mm of distalisation; the paper's sample mixed treated and untreated subjects, so the absolute molar values are not "normal growth".

4. **The structural method is vertically trustworthy and horizontally about half a millimetre off.** The paper says: implant minus structural, vertical, −0.04 to −0.33 mm, none significant (Tables III-IV); horizontal 0.53-0.66 mm, P < .01, at 7 of 8 landmarks (A point 0.41 mm, NS). The Discussion calls the shift posterior; Tables I-II put the structural landmarks anterior to the implant ones, so take the 0.5 mm as a magnitude of unknown sign. My read: on a structural superimposition, do not report a horizontal tooth change under about 0.5 mm as real, and remember the vertical slightly over-reads descent because apposition and resorption were set 1 : 1 instead of Björk's 3 : 2.

5. **ANS, A point and the incisor are the landmarks that move between observers, not the superimposition.** The paper says: a second observer differed vertically by 0.88 mm at ANS, 0.87 at ANS-Harvold, 0.77 at A point, 0.86 at the incisal edge and 0.52 at the incisor apex (Tables III-IV), nowhere horizontally, and the SDs were about the same for all three methods, so variability came from locating landmarks. My read: define ANS by Harvold's 3 mm rule (the two definitions gave 1.88 and 1.87 mm, Table III) and treat any single-tracing change at ANS or A point under 1 mm as noise.

6. **Film quality decides whether the method is usable, and only half of these films qualified.** The paper says: 18 of 35 implant films could be used because the structural method needs both zygomatic processes visible with the same amount of double contour on both films, and a short zygomatic process "can create a rotational effect". My read: for serial cephalograms, same machine, same head position, and if the zygomatic processes cannot be matched the honest statement is that the maxillary superimposition is unreliable. For CBCT the same principle transfers: register on a stable region, not on the palate; Nguyen 2018 (a3) reached 0.12 ± 0.11 mm between observers with voxel registration on the chin and symphysis, which is the standard a 2D tracing cannot approach (Doppel 1994 tracing error 0.5 mm).

## Practical notes: performing the structural maxillary superimposition the paper validates

The steps are the paper's Methods and Fig 3 (Björk & Skieller), with the ratio and checks from the literature where marked.

1. **Films.** Same cephalostat and head position for both films; reject or flag a pair in which the zygomatic processes show different amounts of double contour (the paper excluded such films). Contrast and density must show the anterior surface of the zygomatic process, the orbital floors and the nasal floor (Fig 4). The paper says the method "is greatly dependent on" film quality.
2. **Trace T1.** On one acetate: both zygomatic processes with their anterior surfaces, the orbital floors, the nasal floor and hard palate, ANS (Harvold: where the spine is 3 mm thick), PNS, A point, the first molar and central incisor outlines with cusp, edge and apices, plus fiducial marks so the tracing can be re-registered (Fig 5).
3. **Trace T2** the same way on a separate acetate. The paper traced each method separately and left the implants off the anatomic tracings; the equivalent today is to trace blind to the T1 measurements.
4. **Register anteroposteriorly.** Lay T2 on T1 along the anterior surface of the zygomatic process, both sides treated as one bilateral contour. This fixes the horizontal position and the angular orientation. Björk & Skieller found this surface sagittally stable (literature, as cited by the paper); Doppel 1994 found it the second most reliable maxillary structure, 0.95 ± 0.57 mm from implants, with a variable direction of change in 62.5 % of cases, so use the full length of the contour and not one point.
5. **Set the vertical.** Slide T2 along that surface until the T2 orbital floor sits above the T1 orbital floor (apposition) and the T2 nasal floor sits below the T1 nasal floor (resorption). The paper set the two distances equal (1 : 1) and got a slight over-read of descent (−0.04 to −0.33 mm, NS) and a 0.5 mm horizontal shift. Björk & Skieller's ratio is about 3 : 2 (Fig 2: 6.4 : 4.6), Doppel 1994 found the orbital floor rising 1.5 times the palatal lowering, and the ABO teaches 3/5 : 2/5 (literature, VERIFIED deck). My read: use 3 : 2 and say which ratio you used.
6. **Check the result against the known biology.** On a correct fit the T2 palate lies below the T1 palate with ANS lowered more than PNS (paper: 1.62 vs 0.70 mm per 4 y), PNS lies further back (2.34 mm per 4 y), the key ridge appears lowered by apposition and the posterior surface of the zygomatic process shows apposition (Discussion). If the T2 palate sits level with or above T1, the tracing has been pulled into a best-fit position.
7. **Measure in a frame fixed to T1.** The paper used Downs' occlusal plane of the first film as X with the perpendicular through the molar mesial cusps as Y (Fig 6); any T1-fixed frame will do, but report vertical and horizontal tooth displacement separately and state the frame.
8. **Repeat.** Trace twice on separate occasions and average (the paper made double determinations but did not report the error). Expect a second observer to differ by 0.8-0.9 mm vertically at ANS and the incisal edge (Tables III-IV).
9. **Report the limits.** Horizontal changes under 0.5 mm and ANS/A-point changes under 1 mm are inside the method's own noise; say so rather than tabulating them to two decimals.
10. **Do not** register on the palatal plane at ANS (best fit), at PTM, on the infratemporal fossa, or on the key ridge (Discussion; Fig 1 a-d, f).

## What the paper does NOT tell you

- **Which test was used,** whether it was paired, or any confidence interval; 64 comparisons with no correction (derived count). The implant rows of Tables III-IV do not rebuild from Tables I-II (gaps up to 0.38 mm at A point), so the number of subjects in each implant comparison is unknown.
- **The method error of a single tracing.** Double determinations were made for all three methods, but no intra-observer statistic is printed and the tables do not say whether one determination or the mean was analysed.
- **Individual agreement.** Only mean biases are reported; from the printed SDs an individual structural superimposition can sit about 2 mm from the implant answer (95 % limits, derived).
- **The sign of the 0.5 mm structural offset.** Discussion says posterior; the tables say anterior.
- **Treated vs untreated, boys vs girls, fast vs slow growers.** All pooled; the author says the treatment effect on nasal-floor remodelling "cannot be ruled out".
- **Tooth inclination.** Only landmark positions were compared; changes in incisor and molar inclination between methods were not examined (stated limitation).
- **Adults, Class III, surgical or expansion patients, short treatment intervals.** The sample is 10-14 y, Class II and I only, over 4 years.
- **Best-fit reliability.** The second observer repeated only the structural method, so "reliable" has no comparison arm.
- **Internal-palate best fit (McNamara, Fig 1e).** Drawn in the figure but not tested; Doppel 1994 found it numerically the closest to implants (0.85 ± 0.43 mm) yet empirically a poor fit.
- **Anything three-dimensional.** Head rotation was handled only by excluding gross double contours; the author notes unilateral implants left head-position error in the gold standard itself.
- **Why the age SD is 0.37 y** for 18 children chosen on film quality alone; possibly a standard error printed as an SD. Flag, do not assert.

## One sentence for a patient or a junior

- **To a junior:** "Never register the maxilla on the palate in a growing patient, because the palate itself drops 1-2 mm in four years and takes a third to a half of the tooth eruption with it; register on the anterior surface of the zygomatic process and let the orbital floor rise a little more than the nasal floor falls."
- **To a patient:** "Your upper jaw remodels as you grow, so to see what the braces did we line up the two X-rays on the part of the cheekbone that stays put, not on the roof of the mouth, which moves."
