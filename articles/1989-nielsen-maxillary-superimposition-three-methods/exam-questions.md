# Exam questions: Nielsen 1989, three maxillary superimposition methods against implants

Source: Nielsen IL. Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop 1989;95(5):422-431. UCSF Department of Growth and Development. Retrospective method comparison: palatal best fit and the Björk-Skieller structural method tested against metallic-implant superimposition on serial cephalograms of 18 implanted children over 4 years, plus a two-observer reliability check of the structural method.

Eleven questions: Q1 to Q7 single-best-answer MCQs, Q8 to Q10 short answer, Q11 case-based oral defence. Tags: R = recall, A = application, N = analysis (4 R, 4 A, 3 N). Table numbers refer to the paper. Where the paper's text and tables disagree, the table value is used and the discrepancy is flagged. Anything from outside the paper is labelled (literature), (Proffit), (registry) or my read, and is UNVERIFIED unless a source was seen.

## Part 1. Multiple-choice questions (single best answer)

### Q1 (R). Sample and selection

Which statement correctly describes the material of Nielsen 1989?

A. Eighteen of 35 implant subjects (6 boys, 12 girls), mean age 10 ± 0.37 y at the first film and a 4 ± 0.56 y interval, selected for film quality (clear zygomatic processes, ANS and orbital floors, no excessive double contours) and at least 3 to 4 stable zygomatic implants on both films; treated and untreated mixed, mostly Class II.
B. Nine untreated boys with implants followed from 4 to 20 y.
C. Eighteen Class III children randomised to three superimposition methods.
D. Twenty-one untreated subjects with implants studied at 6-year intervals through puberty.
E. Fifty treated adolescents selected from about 700 implant records for perfect orientation of three maxillary implants.

**Answer:** A
**Rationale:** Methods, p 424. B is the Björk and Skieller series behind Fig 2 (literature), D is Björk and Skieller 1972 as cited in the Introduction (ref 15), and E is Doppel 1994 (registry). There was no randomisation; the three methods were applied to the same films.
**Tests:** Recall of the sample; separating this series from the implant studies it cites and from Doppel.

### Q2 (R). The structural method

Which option describes the "structural method" as Nielsen executed it?

A. Superimposition along the palatal plane registered at ANS, with the nasal floors aligned.
B. Superimposition on the anterior surface of the zygomatic process, left and right, with the orbital floors and palatal floor traced and the second film oriented so that resorptive lowering of the nasal floor equals the apposition at the orbital floor.
C. Superimposition on the infratemporal fossa and the posterior part of the hard palate.
D. Superimposition on the anterior border of the zygomatic process registered at the tip of the key ridge.
E. Best fit of the internal palatal structures.

**Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Methods, p 424, and Fig 3 (Björk and Skieller). A is the best-fit method tested in the same paper (Fig 1a, Broadbent); C is Riedel (Fig 1c), D is Luder (Fig 1f) and E is McNamara (Fig 1e), all rejected in the Discussion. Note that Nielsen kept apposition and resorption equal (1 : 1) although the Introduction cites about 3 : 2 (Fig 2); he attributes the structural method's slight vertical over-read to this (Discussion, p 429).
**Tests:** Recall of the registration structure and the orientation rule.

### Q3 (A). Reading Table II

A growing patient's maxillary first-molar mesiobuccal cusp is measured as having moved 3.4 mm occlusally over 4 years on a palatal best-fit superimposition registered at ANS. Based on Nielsen's Tables II and IV, the implant-referenced value is most likely:

A. About 2.3 mm, because best fit over-reads eruption.
B. About 3.4 mm, because best fit is unbiased for the molars.
C. About 4.8 mm, because best fit under-reads molar vertical movement by roughly a quarter to a third.
D. About 6.8 mm, because best fit under-reads eruption by half at every landmark.
E. It cannot be estimated, because the two methods were not compared.

**Answer:** C
**Rationale:** Table II: U6C vertical −3.40 mm on best fit, −4.75 mm on implants; Table IV: implant minus best fit 1.33 ± 0.82 mm, P < .001. The missed share is 28 % (my arithmetic); the Discussion says "nearly 30 %" for the molars. D applies the incisal-edge figure (59 %, Table II) to the molar. The correction is a group mean over 4 years of pubertal growth and does not transfer as a percentage to other ages or intervals (my read).
**Tests:** Applying the direction and size of the best-fit bias to a measured value.

### Q4 (A). Mechanism of the best-fit error

Why did the best-fit superimposition show PNS, ANS and ANS (Harvold) moving slightly upward (+0.28 to +0.37 mm) while the implants showed them moving downward (−0.70 to −1.62 mm), and produce an average 1.2 mm horizontal difference at every landmark?

A. ANS is an unreliable landmark, as shown by the difference between ANS and ANS (Harvold).
B. The palate itself descends by resorption of the nasal floor, greater anteriorly than posteriorly, with apposition on the oral side; registering on the palate subtracts that remodelling, and the anterior-dominant resorption rotates the maxilla en masse within the coordinate system.
C. The implants migrated within bone during the 4 years.
D. Tracing error of about 0.5 mm accumulates over two films.
E. The occlusal plane rotates as the teeth erupt, so the X axis is unstable.

**Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Discussion, p 429: best fit "emphasizes the palatal structures" and does not allow for the resorptive lowering of the nasal floor, which is greater anteriorly; the result is an en masse rotational shift and a 1.2 mm average horizontal difference (Table III skeletal mean 1.18 mm, my arithmetic). A fails because ANS and ANS (Harvold) differ by only 0.06 mm vertically on implants (Table I). Unstable implants were excluded (Methods), the X axis is fixed to the first film (Fig 6), and tracing error is not quantified in this paper.
**Tests:** Linking the implant biology (Björk) to the measurement artefact.

### Q5 (N). Structural method against implants

Which statement about the structural method is best supported by Tables III and IV?

A. The structural and implant methods agreed in both planes at every landmark.
B. The structural method showed no significant vertical difference from implants (−0.33 to −0.04 mm skeletal, −0.29 to −0.20 mm dental, a slight over-read of descent) but a systematic horizontal difference of 0.53 to 0.66 mm, significant at seven of eight landmarks (point A 0.41 mm unstarred); the abstract reports only the vertical result.
C. The structural method under-read eruption in the same direction as best fit, by about 15 %.
D. The structural method was significantly biased vertically at ANS only.
E. Horizontal differences from implants were significant only for the dental landmarks.

**Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Table III, implant minus structural: PNS 0.53** / −0.33, ANS (Harvold) 0.56** / −0.17, ANS 0.56** / −0.17, point A 0.41 / −0.04; Table IV: U6C 0.62** / −0.29, U6A 0.56** / −0.25, UIE 0.66** / −0.20, UIA 0.58** / −0.20. Results, p 427, confirm "only in the horizontal displacement". The abstract states the vertical null and omits the horizontal finding (my read). The Discussion calls the offset a posterior shift of 0.5 mm; the Table I and II means place structural landmarks less posterior than implant ones, so the direction cannot be rebuilt from the paper, only the size.
**Tests:** Reading significance patterns across two tables; checking an abstract against its tables.

### Q6 (A). Posterior registration

A colleague assesses headgear effects in a 10-year-old by superimposing along the palatal plane registered at the pterygomaxillary fissure (Moore). According to Nielsen's results and Discussion, the main problem is:

A. The fissure is obscured by the developing third molars.
B. The maxilla lengthens posteriorly at PNS, 2.34 mm in 4 years on implants against 1.18 mm on best fit, by sutural growth and tuberosity apposition, so a posterior registration subtracts the growth and introduces vertical and horizontal error in the tooth movements; Riedel's infratemporal-fossa method adds the problem that the posterior zygomatic surface is appositional.
C. The palatal plane rotates 3° at its anterior end during growth.
D. PTM registration over-reads incisor eruption by 50 %.
E. The fissure can be identified on only half of the films.

**Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Table I (PNS X, implant −2.34 vs best fit −1.18) and the Discussion, p 429-430, which says the best fit under-reads the apposition at PNS by about 50 % and that registration at the pterygomaxillary fissure "will introduce considerable error in both vertical and horizontal directions"; the posterior zygomatic surface "has been shown to undergo appositional growth". C is a Julius/Doppel figure (registry) and E is Doppel's PNS visibility of 52 % (registry), not findings of this paper.
**Tests:** Applying the maxillary lengthening finding to a method choice.

### Q7 (N). Reliability and individual precision

Which conclusion about the reliability of the structural method is best supported by the paper?

A. Interobserver differences were non-significant at every landmark, so the method locates individual teeth to within 0.1 mm.
B. Significant interobserver differences occurred only in the vertical plane, at ANS (0.88 ± 1.29 mm), ANS (Harvold) (0.87 ± 1.30), point A (0.77 ± 1.42), UIE (0.86 ± 1.28) and UIA (0.52 ± 0.75); the author attributes the variability to landmark location rather than to the superimposition and says it was equally great for all three methods; with between-method SDs of about 0.7 to 1.2 mm, a non-significant mean difference does not guarantee agreement within 1 mm in an individual patient.
C. Reliability was assessed for all three methods by intraclass correlation.
D. Horizontal interobserver differences were significant for the molars.
E. The second observer's superimpositions were shown to be more accurate than the first observer's.

**Answer:** B
**Rationale:** Tables III and IV, observer rows; Results, p 428; Discussion, p 430 (variability "related more to variability in locating the actual landmarks"). The second observer was tested only for the structural method and only against the first observer, not against implants, so E has no basis. No ICC, method error or limits of agreement are reported; the ± 1.96 SD bands (my arithmetic: point A −2.4 to +2.3 mm) are the individual-level caveat.
**Tests:** Distinguishing mean bias from individual agreement; recognising what reliability statistics are missing.

## Part 2. Short-answer questions

### Q8 (R). Methods

Describe (a) the sample and how it was selected, (b) how each of the three superimpositions was made, (c) the coordinate system and landmarks, and (d) the steps taken against observer bias.

**Model answer:**
(a) Pairs of head films from 18 of 35 subjects with maxillary implants in the UCSF series collected by Matthews (ref 21); 6 boys and 12 girls; T1 10 ± 0.37 y; interval 4 ± 0.56 y. Films were chosen for quality: clear zygomatic processes, ANS and orbital floors; no excessive double contours from malpositioning; at least 3 to 4 stable implants in the zygomatic process identifiable on both films; implants not bilateral in every subject. Treated and untreated mixed, mostly Class II, some Class I, no Class III; no division by sex or treatment because of the sample size. (b) Best fit: optimal fit of the hard palate with the nasal floors aligned, registered at ANS and the ANS to point A region. Implant: implants traced on both films, the inter-implant distance bisected; any implant judged unstable excluded. Structural (Björk and Skieller): both zygomatic processes, the orbital floors and the palatal floor traced, superimposed on the anterior surface of the zygomatic process with the second film oriented so that nasal-floor resorption equalled orbital-floor apposition. (c) Reference frame on the first film: Downs occlusal plane (bisecting the occlusion of the first molars and central incisors) as X axis, a perpendicular through the mesial cusps of the first molars as Y axis (Fig 6); registration and fiducial points carried forward and digitised with the UCSF computer-aided head-film analysis (Baumrind and Miller). Landmarks: ANS, ANS (Harvold, 3 mm thickness), PNS, point A; U6C, U6A, UIE, UIA. (d) Separate tracings for each method; the best-fit and structural tracings omitted the implants; double determinations for all three methods on separate occasions; a second observer repeated the structural superimposition twice after brief instructions. Means and SDs by SAS; the statistical test is not named (my read: a paired comparison on 18 subjects, with 64 tests and no correction).

**Marking points (6):** 18 of 35, 6 boys and 12 girls, 10 y and 4 y; selection on film quality and 3 to 4 stable zygomatic implants; three methods correctly described including the 1 : 1 orientation rule; Downs occlusal-plane coordinate system; implants omitted from the anatomic tracings; double determinations and a second observer for the structural method.

### Q9 (A). The best-fit error and its clinical meaning

Using Tables I and II and the biology described in the paper, explain (a) why palatal best fit under-reads vertical change and PNS lengthening, and (b) what that would do to the assessment of a growing Class II patient treated with cervical headgear over 4 years if the maxillary superimposition were a palatal best fit.

**Model answer:**
(a) Björk's implant studies (refs 12 to 18) showed the nasal floor resorbing downward, more anteriorly than posteriorly, with apposition at the orbital floor at about 3 : 2 (Fig 2, nine boys 4 to 20 y), and the maxilla lengthening at PNS by sutural growth and apposition at the tuberosity (Melsen and Melsen, ref 24). A superimposition registered on the palate rides down with it and subtracts the remodelling: on best fit PNS, ANS and ANS (Harvold) appear to rise 0.36, 0.28 and 0.37 mm and point A to descend 0.47 mm, while the implants show them descending 0.70, 1.62, 1.56 and 2.33 mm (Table I). Because the resorption is greater anteriorly the maxilla also rotates en masse in the frame, giving an average horizontal difference of 1.2 mm at every landmark (Discussion; Table III skeletal mean 1.18 mm, my arithmetic). PNS moved back 2.34 mm on implants and 1.18 mm on best fit, a 50 % under-read of growth in length. (b) Dental consequences from Table II: molar cusp descent read as 3.40 instead of 4.75 mm (28 % missed), molar apex 2.33 instead of 3.80 (39 %), incisal edge 1.40 instead of 3.39 (59 %), incisor apex 2.60 instead of 4.36 (40 %); horizontally the molar cusp reads 3.02 mm anterior instead of 1.31 and the incisal edge 1.22 anterior instead of 0.46 posterior. In a headgear case the examiner would therefore under-read molar and incisor eruption by roughly a third to a half, over-read mesial molar movement by about 1.7 mm (or under-read distalisation by that amount), under-read maxillary lengthening at the tuberosity by half, and might read ANS and point A as stable or rising when they descended 1.6 to 2.3 mm. My read: the headgear's vertical effect on the molar and its restraint of maxillary growth are exactly the quantities a palatal best fit distorts most. Caveat from the paper itself: treated and untreated were pooled and the effect of treatment on nasal-floor remodelling "cannot be ruled out", so the percentages are for this mixed pubertal sample over 4 years.

**Marking points (6):** nasal-floor resorption greater anteriorly with orbital apposition (3 : 2); palate registered on itself subtracts its own descent, with Table I numbers; en masse rotation and 1.2 mm horizontal difference; PNS lengthening 2.34 vs 1.18 mm (50 %); dental under-read with at least two Table II values and the landmark-specific percentages; correct direction of error for molar vertical and horizontal change in a headgear case.

### Q10 (N). Appraising "valid and reliable"

The Discussion concludes that the structural method "is a valid and reliable method" for maxillary growth and treatment change. Appraise this claim: list the strengths that support it, the weaknesses and internal inconsistencies that limit it, and state the level of evidence and what survives.

**Model answer:**
Supports: an implant reference standard on the same films, which is the only arbiter between anatomic methods and cannot ethically be regenerated; three methods applied to identical tracings in one coordinate system, within subject; the anatomic tracings made without the implants drawn; double determinations and a second observer for the index method; a large, biologically predicted effect for the main comparison (implant minus best fit vertical 1.08 to 2.24 mm skeletal and 1.33 to 1.84 mm dental, P < .001; paired t of about 3.6 to 9.1 on the printed means and SDs, my arithmetic, surviving any multiplicity correction); mechanisms given for each rejected method; limitations acknowledged (treatment, sex, growth rate, short zygomatic processes, double contours, unilateral implants). Limits: 18 of 35 films selected on the structural method's own requirements, so its errors here are a best case; the structural method differed from implants horizontally by 0.53 to 0.66 mm at seven of eight landmarks (P < .01), which the abstract omits; the Discussion gives that offset as posterior while the Table I and II means run the other way (structural minus implant +0.13 to +0.75 mm, mean +0.51, my arithmetic); the implant rows of Tables III and IV do not equal the differences of the Table I and II means (point A vertical 2.24 printed vs 1.86 computed; point A horizontal 1.15 vs 0.92; UIA horizontal 1.30 vs 1.09) whereas every structural-minus-best-fit cell does, so the implant comparisons were probably not run on the same 18 subjects or the same determination; the test is unnamed, 64 comparisons are uncorrected, and Table IV's footnote (** P < .001) contradicts Table III's (** P < .01) and its own values; double determinations were made but no method error is reported and it is not stated which determination entered the tables; "reliable" rests on one second observer after brief instructions, with significant vertical differences of 0.52 to 0.88 mm at ANS, point A and the incisor and no ICC or limits of agreement, so with between-method SDs of 0.7 to 1.2 mm an individual superimposition can sit about 2 mm from the implant answer (± 1.96 SD, derived) while the group mean is unbiased; the 30 to 50 % headline is 28 to 59 % by landmark and is a 4-year pubertal figure, not a correction factor; treated and untreated pooled, mostly Class II, no Class III, no growth staging; Fig 8 plots the incisor-apex value (1.71 ± 0.84) at the incisal edge (Table IV UIE 1.84 ± 0.86). Level: a retrospective, quality-selected method comparison against a reference standard, n = 18, about Oxford diagnostic level 3 to 4 (literature mapping, UNVERIFIED). What survives: the direction and approximate size of the best-fit error, the absence of a vertical bias in the structural method at group level, and the biological explanation; what does not is any claim of sub-millimetre precision for an individual patient or a single transferable percentage.

**Marking points (6):** implant reference standard and within-subject design; blinded anatomic tracings and second observer; selection of 18 of 35 on the method's own criteria; the horizontal offset omitted from the abstract and its unresolved direction; the implant-row arithmetic or the unnamed test and uncorrected multiplicity; mean bias versus individual agreement with the SDs, and a level-of-evidence statement.

## Part 3. Case-based oral defence

### Q11 (A). "Show me how you would superimpose this case and tell me what could fool you."

**Case.** An 11-year-old girl, Class II division 1, overjet 9 mm, ANB 6°, average lower face height, in the late mixed dentition at the first cephalogram. She was treated with cervical headgear for 18 months followed by fixed appliances. A second cephalogram is taken at 14 y 6 months, same machine. You are asked to describe the skeletal and dental changes.

**Examiner question 1. Which superimpositions will you make, and on what?**
Model answer: Three. Overall, on the anterior cranial base using Björk's stable structures (anterior wall of sella, planum sphenoidale, cribriform plate, the greater wings and the inner cortex of the frontal bone, as the ABO lists them; ABO deck, seen; Proffit Ch 6 Figs 6.58 to 6.60, study pages), to show how the maxilla and mandible moved relative to the cranium. Maxillary regional, by the structural method of Björk and Skieller as Nielsen tested it: both zygomatic processes, the orbital floors and the palatal floor traced, registered anteroposteriorly on the anterior surface of the zygomatic process, with the second film oriented so that the palatal plane ends slightly below the first, orbital-floor apposition to nasal-floor resorption about 3 : 2 (Nielsen Fig 2; ABO 3/5 to 2/5). Mandibular regional on the symphysis and inner cortex (Björk; Nguyen 2018 confirmed the chin and inner symphysis in 3D, registry). The ABO logic is that the overall superimposition gives total change, the regional ones give tooth movement within each jaw, and the difference is growth or orthopaedic effect (ABO deck, seen).

**Examiner question 2. Walk me through the maxillary one.**
Model answer: On the first tracing I draw the anterior surface of the zygomatic process on both sides, the orbital floors, the nasal floor and oral palate, and the teeth. I lay the second tracing so the anterior zygomatic contours coincide, which fixes the anteroposterior position; I then slide vertically so that the nasal floor sits lower on the second film and the orbital floor higher, by about three parts up to two parts down. Nielsen kept these equal; he reports that this made the structural method over-read descent slightly (PNS by 0.32 mm, others by 0.24 mm or less, Tables I and II) and may have produced the 0.5 mm horizontal offset through the slope of the zygomatic process, so I would use the 3 : 2 rule rather than 1 : 1. I would then read molar and incisor change relative to the first film's occlusal plane, as Nielsen did with Downs' plane and a perpendicular through the molar cusps (Fig 6).

**Examiner question 3. If you had superimposed on the palate at ANS instead, what would you have told me about her molars and incisors?**
Model answer: I would have under-read their eruption. In Nielsen's 18 children over 4 years, palatal best fit gave molar cusp descent of 3.40 mm against 4.75 mm on implants (28 % missed), incisal edge 1.40 against 3.39 mm (59 %), incisor apex 2.60 against 4.36 mm (40 %) (Table II). I would also have read the molar as moving 3.02 mm mesially instead of 1.31 mm, which in a headgear case means under-reading the distalising effect by about 1.7 mm, and I would have called ANS and PNS stable or rising when they descended 1.6 and 0.7 mm (Table I). The paper's headline, that best fit hides 30 to 50 % of eruption, is for this pubertal interval and would be smaller in an adult.

**Examiner question 4. What could fool you?**
Model answer: (1) Head position: the structural method superimposes bilateral structures, so the double contours of the two zygomatic processes must be minimal or the same on both films; Nielsen rejected 17 of 35 films for this and for visibility of the zygomatic process, ANS and orbital floors (Methods; Discussion). (2) A short anterior zygomatic surface, which Nielsen says "can create a rotational effect" that misreads tooth movement. (3) Landmark location: the paper attributes most of its variability to finding ANS, point A and the incisor points, with observer differences of 0.52 to 0.88 mm vertically at those points (Tables III and IV); horizontally nothing was significant. (4) The ratio: 1 : 1 over-reads descent slightly and 3 : 2 is a mean from nine boys, so an individual's palatal rotation may differ (Doppel 1994: palatal plane to implant line +0.92 ± 2.10°, range −5° to +8°, registry). (5) The structural method's own 0.5 mm horizontal offset, whose direction the paper states both ways, which is about the size of a tracing error (Doppel 0.5 mm, registry); a 1° registration error at a molar 30 to 40 mm from the registration area is about 0.5 to 0.7 mm (my arithmetic). (6) Treatment: headgear changes molar position and possibly palatal remodelling, and Nielsen could not separate treated from untreated. (7) Posterior landmarks: PNS is obscured by second-molar germs and moves back 2.3 mm in 4 years, so nothing should be registered on PNS, PTM or the key ridge, which lowers by apposition (Discussion).

**Examiner question 5. Your structural superimposition shows the upper first molar 2 mm more distal at the second film. How sure are you?**
Model answer: Not to the millimetre. The structural method differed from implants horizontally by 0.62 ± 0.79 mm at the molar cusp (Table IV, P < .01), so the systematic part is about 0.6 mm, in a direction the paper does not settle; the random part gives individual limits of about ± 1.5 mm (1.96 × 0.79, my arithmetic). I would report the direction as distal and the size as about 2 mm with an uncertainty of the order of a millimetre, and I would add the overall superimposition to separate maxillary restraint from molar movement. Two observers would agree horizontally (no significant horizontal interobserver differences) better than vertically.

**Examiner question 6. Would a CBCT at both time points have solved this?**
Model answer: It would reduce the observer and head-position problems: Nguyen 2018 (registry) registered growing mandibles on the chin and symphysis with 0.12 ± 0.11 mm between observers and an ICC of 0.998, below the 0.3 mm voxel. But that is reproducibility, not validity; Nguyen's gold standard was bone plates checked clinically, and systematic reviews of 3D cranial-base superimposition found no study with a true gold standard (Ponce-Garcia 2018; literature). Proffit (study pages, Ch 6) lists four evidence-based CBCT indications, none of them growth monitoring, with doses of about 0.05 to 0.15 mSv against 0.005 mSv for a cephalogram, and says implant radiography has been "largely superseded" by 3D imaging (Ch 2). For this patient a well-positioned lateral cephalogram and the structural method are the standard; CBCT would be justified only by another indication.

**Examiner question 7. She is now 17 and you want to measure 3 months of bite opening with a fixed appliance. Does the choice of maxillary superimposition still matter?**
Model answer: The bias of a palatal best fit scales with nasal-floor remodelling per unit time. Nielsen's masked vertical change was 1.08 to 2.24 mm skeletal and 1.33 to 1.84 mm dental over 4 years at 10 to 14 y, about 0.3 to 0.6 mm per year; over 3 months at 17 y that is under 0.1 mm and probably less, since remodelling slows after puberty (my arithmetic; UNVERIFIED for that age). So the systematic bias is negligible, and the direction is known: upper molar extrusion would be slightly under-read. What matters at that interval is resolution: tracing error of about 0.5 mm (Doppel, registry) and Nielsen's landmark SDs of 0.5 to 2.9 mm mean that sub-half-millimetre tooth movements cannot be established from a 2D cephalogram whichever superimposition is used. That is the caveat for Khaleel 2026 (registry), which superimposed on the palatal vault at 15.8 to 17.4 y over 44 to 94 days and reported upper posterior changes of 0.07 to 0.42 mm.

**Examiner question 8. What did Nielsen's paper not tell you that you would want to know?**
Model answer: The size of the structural method's error on routine films rather than the best 18 of 35; its error in an adult or in a Class III patient; whether treatment alters the apposition-to-resorption ratio; the method error of a single observer, since double determinations were made but not reported; individual-level agreement (no limits of agreement or ICC); whether the implant comparisons used all 18 subjects, since the implant rows of Tables III and IV do not rebuild from Tables I and II; and the sign of the 0.5 mm horizontal offset. Doppel 1994 (registry) later ranked the maxillary structures by maximum deviation from implants in 50 subjects (anterior zygomatic surface 0.95 ± 0.57 mm, ANS 1.29 to 1.43, PNS 2.27, point A 2.13 mm) and reached the same practical method with a 1.5 : 1 ratio.
