# Long-term profile changes in extraction and nonextraction patients (Stephens et al. 2005)

Stephens CK, Boley JC, Behrents RG, Alexander RG, Buschang PH. Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop 2005;128(4):450-7. doi:10.1016/j.ajodo.2004.04.034.

## Bottom line

When extraction and nonextraction patients finish with the same incisor position and lip line, their profiles age the same way over about 15 years. In both groups the lips fell about 1.5 mm further behind the E-line and the profile straightened, because the nose and chin outgrew the lips; sex, not treatment, decided how much.

## What they did

- 40 white Class I and Class II patients from one orthodontist: 20 four-premolar extraction, 20 nonextraction, 6 males and 14 females in each (Table I).
- Matched at the end of treatment on age, sex, U1-SN, L1-NB and the H-line; extraction cases chosen first "in the normal range" (pp 451-452).
- Posttreatment and long-term cephs (mean 15.1 to 29.7 years of age, derived from Table I) measured on nine profile measures (Table II); landmark changes by sex (Table V).
- Paired photos rated by 105 orthodontists and 225 laypeople for amount of change (106 mm visual analogue scale) and preference (Tables VI-VII).

## What they found

- No extraction-nonextraction difference at debond, at follow-up, or in change: LS-E −1.5 vs −1.6 mm (P .82), N'-Sn-Pg' +4.0° vs +2.9° (P .33) (Table III).
- Five of nine measures changed within both groups; the H-line did not (+0.1 / −0.4 mm) (Table III).
- Males changed more: LS-E −3.2 vs −0.9 mm (P < .01); pronasale forward 6.7 vs 3.1 mm and soft-tissue pogonion 7.9 vs 4.0 mm (P .01) (Tables IV-V).
- Raters saw 33.9-39.2 % change (P < .01), with no extraction or sex difference; only 1 of 18 perception-ceph correlations was significant (r = 0.34). Preferences split roughly in thirds (p 454; Tables VI-VII).

## Why it matters

It answers the "extraction dishes in the face later" argument: post-treatment ageing is the same once the finish is the same. Lip support should be planned at debond for the nose and chin growth still to come, especially in males.

## One-line caveat

The groups were made equal at debond by design, so the study says nothing about what extraction does during treatment, and 20 per group could miss differences under about 1.9 mm (derived).

## Mechanism at a glance

| Structure | What happened over about 15 years | Number (source) |
|---|---|---|
| Upper lip vs E-line | Further behind the line, same in both groups | −1.5 vs −1.6 mm (Table III) |
| Soft-tissue convexity N'-Sn-Pg' | Angle opened: straighter, less convex profile | +4.0° vs +2.9° (Table III) |
| Lip fullness (H-line) | No significant change | +0.1 vs −0.4 mm (Table III) |
| Nose (Pn-h) | Grew forward, about twice as much in males | 6.7 vs 3.1 mm, males vs females (Table V) |
| Chin (Pg'-h) | Grew forward, about twice as much in males | 7.9 vs 4.0 mm, males vs females (Table V) |
| Perception | Change seen, but unrelated to ceph change | 33.9-39.2 % of the scale; 1 of 18 correlations (Table VI; p 454) |
