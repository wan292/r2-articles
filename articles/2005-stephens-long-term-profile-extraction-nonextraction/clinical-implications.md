# Clinical implications — Stephens 2005

Stephens CK, Boley JC, Behrents RG, Alexander RG, Buschang PH. Long-term profile changes in extraction and nonextraction patients. Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop 2005;128(4):450-7.

**The study in brief:**

- **Patients:** 40 white Class I and Class II patients treated by one orthodontist. 20 had four premolars extracted and 20 were treated without extraction; each group had 6 males and 14 females.
- **Matching:** at the end of treatment, on age, sex, U1-SN, L1-NB and the H-line.
- **Follow-up:** about 15 years, from a mean 15.1 to 29.7 years of age (derived from Table I).
- **Outcomes:** nine cephalometric profile measures (Tables III-IV), landmark changes by sex (Table V), and a photograph survey of 105 orthodontists and 225 laypeople (Tables VI-VII).

**Attribution on this page:** "the paper says" = printed in Stephens 2005. "The literature says" = another source checked in the research pass, marked VERIFIED or UNVERIFIED. "Proffit" = the user's Proffit study pages (VERIFIED in those notes, not checked against the printed book). "My read" = interpretation.

## What this changes or confirms at the chair

1. **If the finish is the same, extraction does not change how the profile ages.**
   - The paper says: the profile changes over 15 years were the same in both groups. N'-Sn-Pg' +4.0° vs +2.9° (P .33); LS-E −1.5 vs −1.6 mm (P .82); LI-E −1.3 vs −1.6 mm (P .66) (Table III). The groups were also no different at follow-up on any of the nine measures (P .10-.99).
   - Caveat: the groups were made equal at debond by design. The study therefore answers "does extraction change ageing?" and not "does extraction flatten the face?".
   - The literature says the treatment effect itself is real but small. A systematic review pooled 1.26 mm more upper-lip and 1.96 mm more lower-lip retraction and 4.21° more nasolabial angle with extraction, at very low certainty (Konstantonis 2018, VERIFIED abstract).
   - My read: the "dished-in face later" argument is not a reason to avoid extraction. What decides the long-term profile is where the incisors and lips are left at debond.

2. **On average the lips fall further behind the E-line after treatment, extraction or not.**
   - The paper says: LS-E changed −1.5 / −1.6 mm and LS-S −1.0 / −1.2 mm in the extraction / nonextraction groups, all significant within the group (Table III). That is roughly 0.1 mm per year (derived).
   - Proffit: the nose and chin become more prominent after adolescence and the lips do not, so lip support is planned at the end of treatment (Ch 2).
   - My read: a profile that looks "just right" at 15 years will have about 1.5 mm less upper-lip prominence relative to the nose and chin by about 30 years. A face finished already flat will look flatter.

3. **The lips do not flatten in themselves; the nose and chin grow past them.**
   - The paper says: the H-line (upper-lip sulcus depth) did not change (+0.1 / −0.4 mm, NS; Table III).
   - The lips moved **forward**, but less than the nose and chin. In males / females: Ls-h 4.1 / 1.8 mm against Pn-h 6.7 / 3.1 mm; Li-h 5.0 / 2.5 mm against Pg'-h 7.9 / 4.0 mm (Table V). The nose ended up 2.6 / 1.3 mm further ahead of the upper lip, and the chin 2.9 / 1.5 mm further ahead of the lower lip (derived).
   - Caveat: lip thickness was not measured. Proffit says adult lips flatten and thin (Ch 4).
   - The H-line runs from the upper lip to the soft-tissue chin, so it is independent of the nose but not of the chin. The direct evidence for "nose and chin" is Table V, not the H-line.

4. **Sex matters more than extraction: young males have most of the profile change still ahead.**
   - The paper says: the male upper lip moved 3.2 mm further behind the E-line (LS-E) and the female 0.9 mm (P < .01). LI-E was −3.0 vs −0.8 mm (P < .01) and N'-Sn-Pg' +5.5° vs +2.6° (P .02) (Table IV).
   - Male pronasale moved forward 6.7 mm and soft-tissue pogonion 7.9 mm (Table V).
   - The authors' explanation: at about 15 years males are only about a year past peak velocity, with more growth to come (p 456).
   - Proffit: male nasal growth peaks at about 13-14 years and continues to 16-18 years and beyond (Ch 4).
   - My read: in a borderline adolescent male, the expected 3 mm of relative lip retrusion against the E-line over 15 years (about 0.2 mm per year, derived) argues against finishing the lips already retrusive. Proffit's guideline points the same way: a big nose or chin argues for keeping the incisors forward (Ch 7).

5. **People see the change, but do not consistently prefer the younger or the older face.**
   - The paper says: raters marked 33.9-39.2 % of the scale as change (P < .01), with no extraction or sex difference (Table VI). Preferences split roughly in thirds: posttreatment, long-term, neither (Table VII).
   - For individual patients the preferences were strong: chi-square was significant for 87.5 % of patients (35 of 40, derived). About 90 % of orthodontists preferred the long-term photo of one patient, and about 80 % preferred the posttreatment photo of another (p 454).
   - My read: some faces age well and some do not, and nothing measured here predicts which. The authors say as much.

6. **What people see on photographs is not what the ceph measures.**
   - The paper says: only 1 of 18 correlations between perceived change and ceph change reached significance. That was N'-Pn-Pg' with orthodontists, r = 0.34, P .03 (p 454), which is about what chance alone would produce (0.9 false positives expected, derived).
   - Orthodontists and laypeople agreed closely with each other (r = 0.89, P < .001).
   - My read: judge the soft-tissue outcome on the face and on profile photographs, not on E-line millimetres alone. The ceph lines are diagnostic tools, not measures of how the face is seen.

7. **No long-term esthetic penalty for extraction in this sample.**
   - The paper says: perceived change and preferences did not differ between extraction and nonextraction patients (Tables VI-VII).
   - The literature says:
     - Two studies agree at about 15 and 35 years. Mendes 2019: 4-premolar 4.53 → 4.33 and nonextraction 4.76 → 4.32 on a 10-point scale. Bravo Vallejo 2024: alike at about 35 years (both VERIFIED abstracts).
     - At the end of treatment the picture is different: orthodontists preferred extraction profiles and general dentists nonextraction profiles (Vu 2026, VERIFIED abstract).
   - Caveat: raters compared two photos of the **same** patient at about 15 and about 30 years. They did not compare extraction faces with nonextraction faces. Blinding to time point is not reported; with about 15 years between photos, age cues were probably visible (my read).

8. **The incisors were not fully stable in males.**
   - The paper says: male U1-SN increased 2.6° over 15 years (significant), and female U1-SN fell 0.8° (not significant); the sex difference in change was P .02. At follow-up, males had U1-SN 109.6° against 103.0° in females (P .01) and L1-NB 2.3 against 4.0 mm (P .02) (Table IV).
   - U1-SN and L1-NB were matching variables, and males and females did not differ on them at debond (P .15 and .17; Table IV). The paper does not discuss the change.
   - My read: it may reflect incisor relapse, a change in the S-N reference with growth, or both. It cannot be separated here. Retention was not reported.

## Practical protocol notes

1. **Decide on the target, not the label.** This study's groups were made equal on U1-SN, L1-NB and the H-line at debond. Plan the final incisor position and lip support first, then choose extraction or nonextraction to reach it. Proffit's crowding guide is a starting point (Ch 7): under 4 mm rarely extract, 5-9 mm either, 10 mm or more almost always.
2. **Record the soft-tissue profile at debond:**
   - LS-E, LI-E, N'-Sn-Pg' and the H-line from the final ceph.
   - Standardised profile photographs.
   - Those are the numbers to compare at a long-term recall.
3. **Build the expected ageing into the finish:**
   - About 1.5 mm of relative upper-lip retrusion against the E-line over 15 years for the average patient (Table III).
   - About 3 mm in males and about 1 mm in females (Table IV).
   - About +3-4° of N'-Sn-Pg' straightening (Table III; +5.5° in males and +2.6° in females, Table IV).
4. **Use the right line for the right question:**
   - The H-line tracks lip curvature without the nose, and did not change here (Table III).
   - The E-line includes the nose tip and the chin, so it moves with their growth.
   - Judging a lip as "retrusive" on the E-line in a 30-year-old male partly measures his nose.
5. **Do not read N'-Sn-Pg' the wrong way round.** An increase means a straighter, less convex profile; the paper's Results text calls it an increase in convexity, but the angle and the Discussion say otherwise. Total convexity including the nose (N'-Pn-Pg') did not change, because nose and chin growth roughly offset each other (my read).
6. **Judge esthetics on the face.** Ceph changes and perceived changes did not correlate (1 of 18 correlations), and orthodontists and laypeople agreed closely with each other (r = 0.89). Profile photos and the patient's own view are the outcome; the cephalometric lines are the diagnosis.
7. **Counsel as part of consent.** Tell families that the nose and chin keep growing into the twenties, more in males, and the lips will look relatively flatter with time whichever way the case is treated.
8. **For audits and long-term recalls,** compare to untreated norms, not only to the debond values. This study had no untreated control and assumed normal growth from the literature (refs 15, 17, 18). Rathod 2015 found premolar-extraction profiles similar to those of untreated controls at the end point (VERIFIED abstract; the follow-up length and the source of the controls are UNVERIFIED).

## What the paper does NOT tell you

- **Whether extraction changes the profile during treatment.** The groups were matched at debond and no pretreatment records were analysed.
- **Why each patient was treated as they were:** pretreatment crowding, protrusion, Class I / II split, Class II division, and which premolars were extracted.
- **Equivalence.** The 95 % confidence interval for the extraction-minus-nonextraction difference in LS-E change runs from −1.22 to 1.42 mm (derived). Differences of more than 1 mm cannot be excluded. With 20 per group only differences of about 1.5-1.9 mm in lip change were detectable (derived).
- **Growth vs ageing vs treatment.** There was no untreated control; "normal" change was assumed from the literature.
- **Patients outside the sample:**
  - adults treated after growth;
  - non-white patients, Class III, bimaxillary protrusion and two-premolar extraction;
  - faces finished outside the normal range.
- **Other soft-tissue features:** lip thickness, nasolabial angle, incisor display, lip competence, vertical dimension.
- **Retention and dental stability.** Retention was not reported. Male U1-SN increased 2.6° and the sexes diverged on U1-SN and L1-NB, which the paper does not discuss.
- **How the long-term ceph was superimposed,** beyond measurement from posttreatment sella on S-N −7°, and whether tracings were blind.
- **Rater blinding.** Not reported; the photos were taken at about 15 and about 30 years of age, so age cues were probably visible (my read). Photo standardisation over 15 years is not described.
- **What drove the individual preferences** that were strong for 87.5 % of patients. The authors say they found nothing that explained them.
- **What happens after 30 years.** The literature says profiles kept changing into the forties in males (Formby 1994) and that extraction and nonextraction faces were still rated alike at about 35 years of follow-up (Bravo Vallejo 2024), both VERIFIED abstracts.
- **Recall bias.** How many patients were eligible or recalled is not reported, so selection at follow-up cannot be judged.

## One sentence for a patient or a junior

- **To a junior:** "If you finish an extraction case and a nonextraction case to the same incisor position and lip line, they age the same way over 15 years: the upper lip drops about 1.5 mm further behind the E-line in both, about 3 mm in boys, because the nose and chin keep growing, so plan lip support at debond and stop blaming the extractions."
- **To a parent:** "Taking out the premolars will not make the face sink in later; everyone's nose and chin keep growing into their twenties, boys more than girls, so the lips look a little flatter with time whether or not teeth were removed."
