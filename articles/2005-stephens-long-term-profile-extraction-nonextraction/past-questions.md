### Q1 · Promotion 2023

> 25-year-old who did not have orthodontic treatment before came complaining of retrusion of the lips after 10 years. Why?

- **Recalled answer:** Because of nose growth (handwritten '?' beside it; Proffit Fig 4.9 and Michigan Growth Study excerpt on upper-lip lengthening/thinning pasted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Growth of the nose (and chin) makes the lips look more retrusive (Stephens 2005 Discussion pp 455-456 (lip retrusion relative to the E and S lines comes from relatively greater nose and chin growth, as in untreated subjects); conclusion 2 p 457 (H-line, which is independent of the nose, did not change); Table V p 454 (pronasale moved forward 6.7 mm in males and 3.1 mm in females))
- The lips moved back relative to the E and S lines while lip fullness on the H-line stayed the same, so the change came from the nose and chin growing forward, not from the lips. The paper says this pattern also occurs in untreated people. The Proffit lip-thinning excerpt is a secondary point.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; Proffit Ch 4 Fig 4.9 excerpt pasted

### Q2 · Promotion 2024

> 35-year-old patient had 4 premolars extracted 10 years ago; he came complaining that he is unhappy with his smile and his profile. Cause?
>
> - a) Growth of the nose
> - b) Excessive retraction of incisors
> - c) Thinning of upper lip

- **Recalled answer:** a) Growth of the nose · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a) Growth of the nose (Stephens 2005 abstract p 450 and conclusion 1 p 457 (extraction and nonextraction changed alike); Discussion pp 455-456 and conclusion 2 p 457 (nose and chin growth retrude the lips relative to the E line; H-line unchanged))
- Ten to fifteen years after treatment, extraction and nonextraction patients flattened in the same way, so the extractions are not the cause. The lips look more retrusive because the nose and chin keep growing, while lip fullness itself did not change.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 7, 1 Long-term profile changes in extraction and nonextraction patients, Corbett K. Stephens

### Q3 · Promotion 2022

> 12 years old complains of upper teeth sticking out; facial convexity without nose:
>
> - a. increases with age
> - b. decreases with age

- **Recalled answer:** b. decrease with the age (marked '?') · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b. Decreases with age: the profile straightens (Stephens 2005 Table III p 453 (N'-Sn-Pg' rose from 163.6° to 167.6° in the extraction group, +4.0°, and by +2.9° in the nonextraction group, both significant); Discussion pp 455-456 (convexity decreased considerably); Table IV (males +5.5° vs females +2.6°))
- The facial convexity angle excluding the nose opened by 3-4° between ages 15 and 30, so the face became less convex. The Results text calls this an 'increase in facial convexity' because it describes the angle's value, which is an easy trap. Total convexity including the nose (N'-Pn-Pg') did not change significantly, so the link note's 'total convexity increased' is wrong.
