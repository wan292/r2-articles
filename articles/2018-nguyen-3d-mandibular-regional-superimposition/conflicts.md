# Conflicts and disagreements — Nguyen 2018

Use this file when you need to challenge a number, a claim or a citation in Nguyen T, Cevidanes L, Franchi L, Ruellas A, Jackson T. Three-dimensional mandibular regional superimposition in growing patients. *Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop* 2018;153(5):747-754 (Techno Bytes; DOI 10.1016/j.ajodo.2017.07.026): Section A is the paper against itself (every row checked against the page images of pp. 747-753, including the single Table on p. 749 and Figures 1-8), Section B is the paper against the other two papers in this topic (Doppel 1994, Nielsen 1989), Section C is the paper against the wider implant and 3D literature, Section D is the paper against Proffit, Section E is the paper against the hub registry (Khaleel 2026), F lists what stays unresolved and G is the one-paragraph verdict. Page numbers are journal pages (747-754). Numbers from other studies come from the research pack, which tagged each as VERIFIED or UNVERIFIED; I re-ran the arithmetic behind every derived number below (script: `checks.py` in this folder) and read nothing else at source. "The paper says", "the literature says" and "my read / my arithmetic" are used deliberately.

## A. Internal inconsistencies (the paper vs itself)

Derived values assume n = 20 per region in aim 1 and n = 25 in aim 2, because the paper never states n per region. **How to raise these politely.** Order the queries by what the authors can settle from their own output, and ask each as a question. First the ones that change a number: which SD is right for the interobserver error (A1) and for the symphysis (A2), and what the Fig 8 thresholds are (A11); these are proofing errors and the authors will thank you. Second the statistics: what test produced the P column in the Table and what it tests (A4), what quantity went into the ICC (A6), and which formula gave 25 (A5); phrase it as "I could not reproduce these from the printed means and SDs". Third the design: whether the 5 Class III patients in aim 2 are 5 of the 20 in aim 1 (A14), whether Fig 4 was registered on the plates or on the symphysis (A9), and how plate stability was checked beyond palpation. Keep the proximity argument (A8, A9) factual: the figures show one subject each and the lower-border error was never tabulated. In the session, lead with A1 and A11 (they are on the page and nobody disputes a misprint), then A4 as the one that matters, and do not attribute motive; a Techno Bytes report is a technique note and was edited as one. If the resident wants a project, a short letter asking for the test behind the Table and the ICC variable would be legitimate.

## B. Against the other two papers in this topic

The three papers do not compete on the same structure (Doppel and Nielsen are maxillary 2D, Nguyen is mandibular 3D), and Nguyen cites neither of them (Doppel cites Nielsen as ref 20). The comparison is therefore about principles, methods and magnitudes. Numbers for Doppel and Nielsen are from their tables as recorded in the topic pack.

1. **Reference standard: AGREES in logic, differs in rigour.** All three treat metal as truth. Doppel: three tantalum implants in Björk's sites (below ANS, zygomatic process, palate/alveolar border medial to U6), all three fitted exactly, 50 cases chosen from ≈ 700 records for perfect implant orientation and film quality (Methods). Nielsen: ≥ 3-4 stable implants in the zygomatic process, unstable implants excluded (Methods). Nguyen: bone plates "confirmed to be immobile at T1 and T2 clinically" (Methods p749), no radiographic check, no plate-on-plate residual. My read: the 2D papers used unloaded pins in basal bone whose stability was checked on the films themselves; Nguyen used loaded orthopaedic anchors in alveolar bone checked by palpation.

2. **Error grows with distance from the reference: AGREES, and both authors name the confound.** Doppel: with closely spaced implants the error of a distant structure is "a function of its distance from the implants" (Discussion); his cranial-base rows (anterior cranial base 2.52 ± 1.13, ethmoid triad 2.52 ± 2.01, clinoid 2.64 ± 1.43 mm, Table II) are maxillary displacement, not error. Nguyen: crypt 1.94 ± 0.06 mm at 30-40 mm from the plates against chin 0.37 ± 0.16 mm next to them (Table). Doppel measures around the problem (he ranks everything and explains the far structures); Nguyen argues around it with Figs 4 D and 5 (A8, A9).

3. **Landmarks versus surfaces: AGREES, and Nguyen is the reply to Nielsen.** Nielsen: SDs were about the same for all three methods (UIE vertical ± 2.30 / ± 2.72 / ± 2.64 mm, Table II) and variability was due to locating the landmarks, not to the superimposition; his second observer differed significantly at ANS 0.88 ± 1.29, ANS-Harvold 0.87 ± 1.30, A point 0.77 ± 1.42, UIE 0.86 ± 1.28 and UIA 0.52 ± 0.75 mm (Tables III-IV, vertical only). Doppel: duplicate tracings of 10 films ± 0.25 mm, total tracing error 0.5 mm (Methods, citing Thurow). Nguyen: thousands of voxels, two observers 0.12 ± 0.11 mm, ICC 0.998. The precision chain for the session is 0.5 mm (2D tracing) → 0.5-0.9 mm (2D interobserver at landmarks) → 0.12 mm (3D voxel interobserver), with the caveat that Nguyen's 0.12 mm is two runs of the same algorithm from the same observer-1 orientation of T1 (Methods p750), not anatomy known to 0.12 mm.

4. **Selection on image quality: AGREES, all three do it.** Doppel 50 of ≈ 700 (7 %, derived); Nielsen 18 of 35 (51 %, derived), on film quality only; Nguyen from "previous research and clinical databases" minus syndromes, asymmetry and motion artefact, with the number screened not given. All three error figures are lower bounds for routine records.

5. **"Best by number" is not "best for use": AGREES by analogy, and the trap runs both ways.** Doppel's internal palate had the smallest maximum deviation (0.85 ± 0.43 mm, Table II) yet was "of limited value" because a local-contour metric cannot see the en-bloc descent of a palate that remodels in parallel; Nielsen measured that descent (best fit under-reads vertical skeletal change by 1.08-2.24 mm per 4 y, Table III). Nguyen's absolute mean surface distance has the mirror-image blind spot: a region registered on itself always fits (Fig 4 caption), so the Table's 0.37-0.40 mm means what it claims only if the registration was on the plates. My read: all three papers teach that the metric must match the question.

6. **Treated samples: AGREES, shared weakness.** Doppel: "the 50 treated cases" (edgewise, headgear, Class II and III elastics; Results). Nielsen: treated and untreated mixed, treatment effect "cannot be ruled out". Nguyen: all treated (Herbst, Class II elastics, bone-anchored Class III). No paper in this topic validates a structure under growth alone.

7. **Age, interval and what "stable" means: PARTIAL.** Doppel 4.2 ± 0.96 y, Nielsen 4 ± 0.56 y, Nguyen ≈ 1.2 y (aim 1, derived from the mean ages) and 12.6 ± 0.9 months (aim 2). The 2D deviations are over a full adolescent growth period; Nguyen's are per year. My arithmetic: a true remodelling rate of 0.3-0.4 mm/y at the chin is invisible inside a 0.3 mm voxel over one year but would reach 1.2-1.6 mm over Doppel's interval. Nguyen's "stable" means stable over one year in 12-13-year-olds.

8. **Strength of the verdict: CONFLICTS in tone.** Doppel: no area in the maxilla is completely reliable (Conclusions), despite best values of 0.95-0.97 mm. Nielsen: the structural method is "valid and reliable". Nguyen: regions "stable", registration "reliable", at 0.37-0.40 mm. Doppel's maxima include tracing and head-position error; Nguyen's means exclude segmentation error (never measured). The three metrics are not interconvertible: maximum contour deviation (Doppel) ≠ landmark-displacement difference (Nielsen) ≠ absolute mean surface distance (Nguyen).

9. **Which regions to drop: AGREES in principle.** Doppel and Nielsen reject ANS, PNS, PTM, A point and the palatal borders because they are growth fronts or remodelling surfaces (Doppel Table II: 1.05-2.39 mm; Nielsen Tables I-IV). Nguyen rejects the third-molar crypt (1.94 mm, on the growing posterior body) and excludes the canal a priori. Same rule in both jaws: register on surfaces that neither resorb nor appose, not on structures that relocate with growth.

10. **The remodelling pattern seen on the recommended registration: AGREES.** Doppel: posterior zygomatic surface appositional in 100 % of cases. Nielsen: nasal-floor resorption greater anteriorly (ANS 1.62 vs PNS 0.70 mm per 4 y, Table I). Nguyen: anterior ramus resorption and posterior ramus deposition (Fig 6), ≈ 2 mm deposition at the inferior symphysis (Fig 4 D), pogonion unchanged. All three recover Björk's and Enlow's patterns, which is face validity for the registrations.

11. **Head position: AGREES that 3D removes it, and Doppel supplies the numbers.** Doppel: head rotation moved the zygomatic implant up to 3 mm, and heads rotate up to 3° and tip 5° in a cephalostat (Julius thesis, literature); Nielsen rejected 17 of 35 films for double contours (derived). Nguyen: T1 oriented to Frankfort by observer 1, then voxel registration that is independent of head position.

12. **The complete "structural" set for the session: COMPLEMENT.** Maxilla from Doppel and Nielsen (anterior zygomatic contour for AP; orbital floor rising 1.5× the palatal lowering; never ANS, PNS, PTM or A point). Mandible from Björk via Nguyen (anterior chin + inner symphyseal cortex; not the third-molar crypt; canal excluded in 3D). Cranial base on Björk's anterior-cranial-fossa structures (Doppel transfers SN that way; Nguyen cites Cevidanes 2005-2009 for the voxel version).

## C. Against the wider literature

Format: **Citation** (verification tag as in the research pack) — what it found — VERDICT — one sentence for journal club.

### Björk's implant foundations

**C1. Björk 1955 Acta Odontol Scand 13:9-34; Björk 1963 J Dent Res 42:400-411; Björk 1969 Am J Orthod 55:585-599; Björk & Skieller 1972 Am J Orthod 62:339-383; Björk & Skieller 1983 Eur J Orthod 5:1-46** — citations VERIFIED; the structure list is VERIFIED only as reported by Nguyen, the ABO deck and Krarup 2005, not against the originals.
- What they found: stable mandibular structures for superimposition: anterior chin contour above pogonion; inner cortical plate of the inferior symphysis; symphyseal trabecular pattern; mandibular canal; lower contour of a molar germ before root formation. Björk 1963 is also Nguyen's own source for the canal changing with growth.
- VERDICT: AGREES on the chin and inner symphysis (0.37 ± 0.16 and 0.40 ± 0.14 mm); CONFLICTS on the third-molar germ (1.94 ± 0.06 mm, P = .0025) and on the canal (excluded a priori).
- Journal club: Nguyen keeps Björk's first two structures and retires the last two; this is a 3D edit of Björk, not a new list.

### 2D mandibular methods tested against implants

**C2. Springate SD, Jones AG. AJODO 1998;113:263-270** — VERIFIED abstract; registry B30.
- What it found: 23 growing children with tantalum implants, ≈ 2 y of treatment; Björk's structural method close to implants; Ricketts' corpus axis (Xi-Pm) produced a rotational error from differential growth at Xi; most discrepancies < 2 mm but ≈ 10 % of subjects > 4 mm at molar and incisor landmarks; error larger horizontally and growing with distance from the central core.
- VERDICT: AGREES (same distance effect that Nguyen's crypt shows in 3D).
- Journal club: Springate found in 2D what Nguyen found in 3D: fit the symphysis core and the error grows with every millimetre away from it.

**C3. Baumrind S et al. AJODO 1992;102:134-142 and 227-238** — VERIFIED abstracts.
- What they found: 31 Björk-implant subjects 8.5-15.5 y; anatomic best fit vs implants differed by ≤ 1 mm at every landmark over 7 y except the condyle (2.70 mm horizontal, 1.90 mm vertical) and gonion (1.52 mm vertical); individual differences considerable.
- VERDICT: AGREES (symphysis-region registration adequate; ramus and condyle least reliable).
- Journal club: Baumrind's condyle and gonion are the 2D version of Nguyen's crypt: far from the core and on a growth front.

**C4. Gu Y, McNamara JA. Angle Orthod 2008;78:967-976** — VERIFIED abstract; millimetre values not in the abstract.
- What it found: 10 untreated subjects with maxillary and mandibular implants, CS1-CS6; symphysis inner cortex plus inferior alveolar canals approximated the implants (lower anterior symphysis border possibly better); lower-border superimposition did not reflect true growth.
- VERDICT: AGREES on the symphysis core; PARTIAL on the canal (Gu kept it in 2D; Nguyen excluded it in 3D without testing it).
- Journal club: The canal survived Gu and McNamara's implant test in 2D; Nguyen never tested it, he excluded it.

**C5. Buschang PH et al. Eur J Orthod 1986;8:152-156** — VERIFIED abstract; Nguyen ref 15.
- What it found: 120 replicate superimpositions on Björk's cranial-base and mandibular structures; greatest technical error in the vertical orientation of posterior structures; the mandible needs more training than the cranial base.
- VERDICT: AGREES (the step Buschang found hardest is the one voxel registration automates; interobserver 0.12 ± 0.11 mm).
- Journal club: Buschang's hardest step, orienting the posterior mandible by eye, is exactly what the software now does.

**C6. Houston WJ, Lee RT. Eur J Orthod 1985;7:127-135** — VERIFIED abstract; Nguyen ref 14.
- What it found: several cranial-base superimposition methods all showed appreciable error; none significantly better.
- VERDICT: CONTEXT (cited by Nguyen for reference-line irreproducibility; it concerns the cranial base, which 3D handles as a separate registration).
- Journal club: Nguyen cites Houston to justify going 3D; Houston is about the cranial base, not the mandible.

### 3D cranial-base registration (the pipeline Nguyen inherits)

**C7. Cevidanes LH et al. Dentomaxillofac Radiol 2005;34:369-375** — VERIFIED; Nguyen ref 16 (mis-paginated).
- What it found: 10 maxillary-surgery patients; cranial-base voxel registration; average surface displacement 0.77 ± 0.17 mm at 0.60 mm resolution; interobserver 0.02 ± 0.01 mm.
- VERDICT: CONTEXT / AGREES (same pipeline; interobserver error far below the voxel, as in Nguyen).
- Journal club: 0.02 mm in 2005 and 0.12 mm in 2018 say the same thing: the algorithm, not the observer, decides the fit.

**C8. Cevidanes LH et al. AJODO 2009;136:94-99** — VERIFIED; Nguyen ref 17.
- What it found: 3 Class III miniplate patients, 11.4 y, 1-y interval; registration on the anterior cranial fossa (anterior wall of sella, anterior clinoids, planum, lesser wings, cribriform plate, orbital roofs, inner frontal cortex); 0.5 mm voxels; three observers; interobserver range 0.4 mm at the chin, condyles, rami and lower border.
- VERDICT: AGREES (same group, same patient type; the chin agreed to 0.4 mm on a cranial-base registration, to 0.12 mm on a chin registration).
- Journal club: Nguyen's reliability number is the regional version of what the same group showed on the cranial base in 2009.

**C9. Ghamri M et al. J Clin Med 2021;10:5429; Kanavakis G et al. J Clin Med 2022;11:3536** — VERIFIED abstracts.
- What they found: in growing CBCT pairs the total anterior cranial base registered more reliably than its middle part alone; larger reference = better registration.
- VERDICT: AGREES in principle (the reason chin and symphysis were merged for aim 2).
- Journal club: Bigger reference, better fit, in the cranial base and in the mandible alike.

**C10. Huang X, Anchling L, ... Nguyen T, ... Cevidanes L. AJODO 2026;169(6):753-765** — VERIFIED abstract; Nguyen is a co-author.
- What it found: 22 Class II patients; conventional vs semi-automated vs fully automated open-source workflows; cranial-base change differences −0.3 to 0.7 mm; regional maxilla and mandible −1.7 to 1.1 mm; the reference region must be stated when reporting change.
- VERDICT: AGREES / CONTEXT (regional registrations vary between workflows far more than Nguyen's 0.12 mm between two observers on one workflow).
- Journal club: The same group in 2026 finds regional registrations differ by up to 1.7 mm between workflows; Nguyen's 0.12 mm is two observers on one.

**C11. Ponce-Garcia C et al. Angle Orthod 2018;88:233-245** — VERIFIED.
- What it found: SR of 6 studies of 3D anterior-cranial-base superimposition; voxel differences ≤ 0.5 mm, landmark 1.24 mm; no study had a gold standard.
- VERDICT: CONTEXT (Nguyen's aim 1 is the gold-standard attempt the review asked for, transposed to the mandible).
- Journal club: Ponce-Garcia asked for a gold standard; Nguyen offers a loaded bone plate.

### 3D regional mandibular superimposition

**C12. Krarup S et al. J Anat 2005;207:669-682** — VERIFIED; Nguyen ref 25.
- What it found: 10 Apert children, 49 CTs from 1 week to 14.5 y, 2 mm slices; ICP superimposition on the symphysis and mandibular canals; method error 0.357-0.816 mm; the canal kept its profile curvature and lengthened posteriorly but was relocated laterally in all 10 (buccal resorption, lingual apposition); symphysis stable in profile; tooth buds stable before root formation.
- VERDICT: PARTIAL. Nguyen cites it for the canal changing with growth, which is true transversely; in profile Krarup used the canal for registration, which AGREES with Björk 1983 and the ABO. Krarup's stable buds also soften the crypt rejection: Nguyen's crypts were in 12-13-year-olds near root formation and their stage is never stated.
- Journal club: Krarup's canal moved sideways and stayed put in profile, which is why it works in 2D and is dropped in 3D.

**C13. Koerich L et al. Int J Oral Maxillofac Surg 2016;45:662-669** — VERIFIED abstract; Nguyen ref 23.
- What it found: two dry skulls and 15 patients; regional maxillary and mandibular voxel registration in 10-15 s; intraobserver ICC ≥ 0.980; highest RMS 0.241 mm; interobserver RMS 0 mm in 23 of 34, the rest ≤ 0.25 mm.
- VERDICT: PARTIAL (Nguyen describes it as whole-mandible registration in non-growing patients; neither statement is in the abstract).
- Journal club: Nguyen's gap statement about Koerich may be right, but it is not what Koerich's abstract says.

**C14. Ruellas AC et al. PLoS One 2016;11(6):e0157625** — VERIFIED full text; Nguyen ref 24.
- What it found: 16 growing subjects 9-13 y, T1-T2 ≥ 18 months, 0.4 mm voxels; three masks; the Björk mask completed in only 2 of 16 (14 failures, 87.5 %); mandibular-body mask ICC 0.83-0.99 with Bland-Altman mean differences 0.10-0.38 mm; failures attributed to a small reference region and possibly to growth displacement of canal, symphysis, chin and/or crypt.
- VERDICT: CONFLICTS on the citation (Nguyen prints "16 of 18" and attributes failure to the crypts; the paper says 14 of 16 and names the crypt as one of several possible causes); AGREES on principle (larger region, which Nguyen applied by merging chin and symphysis).

## E. Against the hub registry: Khaleel & Al-Nimri 2026 (`2026-khaleel-deep-bite-turbos-rcos-elastics`)

Everything here is inference from Nguyen's results to Khaleel's method; Nguyen never studied the corpus axis and Khaleel never used 3D. Khaleel's methods, from that folder's `key-numbers.md` (Cephalometrics and measurement): mandibular changes read on a Ricketts corpus-axis (Xi-Pm) superimposition registered at protuberance menti, with Pm chosen "because Xi drifts posteriorly and superiorly with growth"; maxillary changes on the palatal vault (Broadbent); sample 15.82 / 16.00 / 17.35 y; intervals 44.42 / 55.72 / 94.15 d (0.12 / 0.15 / 0.26 y, derived); intra-examiner ICC 0.92-0.98 for repeated measurement; no superimposition error estimate; Table 4 lower values: Point I 0.99 / 0.71 / 0.86 mm, lower incisor V −2.46 / −1.02 / −1.25 mm, lower posterior cusps 0.35-1.30 mm.

1. **Where Pm sits.** Protuberance menti lies on the anterior chin contour between B point and pogonion, which is exactly Nguyen's validated chin region (0.37 ± 0.16 mm per ≈ 1.2 y) and Björk's first structure. The *registration point* of Khaleel's method is therefore on bone that Nguyen's data call stable. The *orientation* through Xi is the problem: Xi is a constructed centre in ramus bone that Nguyen's Fig 6 shows remodelling (anterior ramus resorption, posterior deposition), and Khaleel's own Methods concede Xi relocates. The corpus axis uses none of the inner symphyseal cortex or trabecular pattern that Björk and Nguyen rely on; it is a two-point line, not a structural best fit. Springate & Jones 1998 (registry B30, VERIFIED there; not re-read here) measured the consequence against implants: errors usually < 2 mm but ≥ 4 mm in ≈ 10 % at molars and incisors over ≈ 2 y in growing children.

2. **Direction of the bias (geometric inference, my read).** If Xi relocates superiorly and posteriorly between T1 and T2, aligning the T2 corpus axis on the T1 axis rotates the T2 tracing about Pm with its posterior end down. Lower molars then read slightly *more extruded* and the lower incisal edge slightly *more posterior* than truth; Point I, a few millimetres from Pm, is barely affected by the rotation.

3. **Size of the bias over Khaleel's intervals (derived; UNVERIFIED for 16-17-year-olds).** The rotation equals the Xi drift divided by the Pm-Xi length. Over 44-94 d at 16-17 y the drift is a fraction of a millimetre; my arithmetic with a Pm-Xi of about 65 mm (UNVERIFIED) gives 0.09° for 0.1 mm of drift and 0.26° for 0.3 mm, i.e. 0.05-0.16 mm at a molar 35 mm from Pm. The method bias is therefore small over these intervals; the dominant uncertainty is the 0.5 mm 2D tracing error (Doppel 1994, Methods), which is of the same order as Khaleel's Point I values (0.71-0.99 mm) and lower posterior values (0.35-1.30 mm). This supports, and does not change, the existing registry caveat that the ≈ 1 mm Point I change "is within this method's error band".

4. **What Nguyen adds as a benchmark.** The achievable standard for a mandibular registration is 0.12 ± 0.11 mm between two observers and an ICC of 0.998 for a surface registration, against Khaleel's intra-examiner ICC 0.92-0.98 for repeated *measurement* on 10 film pairs (repeatability of the operator, not validity of the superimposition; no inter-examiner check, no Dahlberg error). Nguyen's ≈ 2 mm/y deposition near menton (Fig 4 D) also means any reference touching the lower border would be far worse than Pm; registering at Pm was the right choice within the Ricketts method.

5. **Growth at 16-17 y.** Nguyen's remodelling (2 mm/y at menton, ramus resorption) was measured at 12-13 y near the pubertal peak; Khaleel's sample is 3-5 y older. The hub already records that Khaleel's "nongrowing" label is unsupported (that folder's `conflicts.md` A15); Nguyen does not bear on that directly, because he has no subjects over about 14 y (derived from the mean ± SD).

6. **The maxillary side.** Khaleel's palatal-vault (Broadbent) superimposition is the best-fit method Nielsen 1989 showed under-reads vertical change by 30-50 % of eruption over 4 y of growth; that cross-check belongs to the Nielsen file, and over 6-13 weeks at 16-17 y the offset is of the order of 0.05 mm (derived there). One sentence in the session is enough.

## F. Open questions the paper leaves unresolved

- What test produced the three P values in the Table, and what null hypothesis does it test? Against zero, against the voxel, between regions, or is it the Kolmogorov-Smirnov normality test the next sentence mentions?
- Is the third-molar SD really 0.06 mm (CV 3 %) when the Discussion says the error varied with growth pattern? If not, what is it, and does 1.94 mm still hold?
- How many subjects contributed to each region, and at what developmental stage were the third-molar crypts (before or after root formation, Björk's criterion)?
- Which variable went into the ICC, and which formula gave 25 subjects from ρ0 0.75 and ρ1 0.95?
- Are the 5 Class III bone-anchor patients in aim 2 among the 20 of aim 1? If so, how was metal artefact in the registration field handled?
- How was plate immobility established beyond clinical palpation? What was the plate-on-plate residual after registration, and was plate position compared on T1 and T2 volumes?
- Was Fig 4 registered on the plates or on the symphysis, as its caption says? If the latter, what does the 0.40 mm symphysis value mean?
- What is the segmentation error of the ITK-SNAP models, which feeds directly into a surface-distance metric?
- What are the range and maximum of the chin, symphysis and interobserver errors, and the per-subject values behind the 0.25 mm / 75 % figures from one case?
- Which sign is outward (apposition) and which inward (resorption) on the −2.00 to +2.00 mm maps, and is "absolute mean surface error" point-to-surface or surface-to-surface, averaged over T1 or T2 vertices?
- Does chin + symphysis stay within a voxel over 4-5 years, in untreated subjects, in adults, in Class I, and under miniscrew or Herbst loading of the symphysis region? Chen 2020 answers part of the first (literature); nobody has answered the rest against a metal reference.
- Would the canal have passed in profile, as it does in 2D (Gu & McNamara 2008; Krarup 2005)? It was excluded, not tested.
- How long does the method take per case, who did the manual rough approximation in aim 1, and was aim 1 ever repeated by a second observer or session?

## G. One-paragraph verdict

Relative to Björk and Proffit, this paper is a 3D edit of the classical mandibular superimposition rather than a discovery: the anterior chin and the inner symphyseal cortex hold at about one voxel (0.37 and 0.40 mm, Table) over roughly a year of treated adolescent growth, the remodelling it maps on that registration (stable pogonion, deposition at the inferior symphysis, anterior ramus resorption, posterior deposition) is the pattern every growth chapter teaches, and its real contribution is subtractive: the third-molar crypt fails (1.94 mm) and the canal is excluded, so the Björk/ABO four-structure list becomes a two-region field in 3D. Relative to the other two papers in this topic, it closes the precision gap they left open (0.5 mm tracing error in Doppel, 0.5-0.9 mm interobserver at landmarks in Nielsen, 0.12 ± 0.11 mm here) while reopening the gold-standard question they had settled with tantalum pins: its reference is a loaded Class III bone anchor in alveolar bone, judged stable by palpation and sitting next to the two regions it certifies, and its two answers to that objection are single-subject colour maps, one of them from a registration whose caption says it was on the symphysis. Relative to itself, the abstract misprints the interobserver SD (1.1 for 0.11) and the plate location, the Figure 8 label misprints its threshold (0.025 for 0.25 mm), the only inferential test in aim 1 is unnamed and incompatible with the printed SDs, and the ICC describes an undefined variable. Use it in an exam as the 3D confirmation of Björk's chin and symphysis and as the reason the crypt and canal are dropped from a voxel protocol; quote 0.37 / 0.40 / 1.94 mm from the Table and 0.12 ± 0.11 mm from the Results, never from the abstract; and do not use it to say chin + symphysis are stable beyond one year, in untreated growth, or in adults, because none of that was tested.
