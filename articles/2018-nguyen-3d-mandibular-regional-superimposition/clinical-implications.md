# Clinical implications — Nguyen 2018

Nguyen T, Cevidanes L, Franchi L, Ruellas A, Jackson T. Three-dimensional mandibular regional superimposition in growing patients. Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop 2018;153(5):747-754 (Techno Bytes). Two retrospective arms on archived CBCT pairs at 0.3 mm voxels. Aim 1: 20 Class III bone-anchor patients (12.1 ± 1.3 → 13.3 ± 1.1 y) whose plates and screws at the lateral-incisor/canine apices were used as regional implants; T2 was registered to T1 on the plates and the absolute mean surface error of three Björk regions was measured. Aim 2: 25 treated growing patients (12.7 ± 1.4 y, CVM 2-5, 12.6 ± 0.9 months) registered by two observers on the combined chin + symphysis field. Table values below were read from the page image of p. 749.

Attribution on this page: "the paper says" = printed in Nguyen 2018; "the literature says" = other studies checked in the research pass (status given); "my read" = my interpretation.

## What this changes or confirms when reading a ceph or a CBCT

1. **Register the mandible on the anterior chin and the inner symphyseal cortex, and on nothing else.** The paper says these two regions moved 0.37 ± 0.16 mm and 0.40 ± 0.14 mm relative to the plates over about a year (Table), which is about one 0.3 mm voxel (1.2-1.3 voxels, derived), and that they were merged into one registration field because the values were "almost identical". Confirms Björk's first two 2D structures. Caveat: one year, treated adolescents, plates judged immobile clinically; the literature says Chen 2020 found distances from the inner symphysis to pogonion and the mental foramen changed by < 1.0 mm over 4.6 years (VERIFIED, abstract), which is the longer-interval support this paper lacks.

2. **Leave the third-molar crypts out of the registration field.** The paper says the crypt floors erred 1.94 ± 0.06 mm (Table, P = .0025), 5.2 × the chin error (derived), and cites Ruellas 2016 as 16 of 18 failed superimpositions when crypts were included. Caveat: the Ruellas paper itself reports 14 of 16 Björk-mask failures and names the crypt as one of several possible causes (literature, VERIFIED full text); the paper never states the developmental stage of the crypts, so Björk's "before root formation" condition is untested in 12-13-year-olds. My read: Björk included the germ when it was a shell with no root; the paper's subjects were at the age when roots start, which is the likeliest reason the structure failed.

3. **Do not register on the mandibular canal in 3D.** The paper says the canal was excluded before any measurement because Björk 1963 and Krarup 2005 showed it changes with growth (Methods). The literature says Krarup saw the canal relocate laterally in all 10 children while keeping its profile curvature, and Chen 2020 found the mandibular foramen 3.0-3.4 mm away from the symphysis reference over 4.6 years (both VERIFIED). Caveat for 2D: the ABO deck still lists the inferior alveolar canal, and in a lateral projection the canal's profile is the stable part (Krarup); the paper did not test it. My read: canal trabeculae remain a 2D orientation aid at best; in voxel work they add transverse drift.

4. **Never anchor on the lower border, menton or the ramus, and treat the corpus axis as a line through remodelling bone.** The paper says the plate registration showed about 2 mm of deposition near menton (Fig 4D, read from a colour map), resorption at the anterior ramus and deposition at the posterior ramus (Fig 6). The literature says Baumrind 1992 part 2 found condyle (2.70 / 1.90 mm) and gonion (1.52 mm) the only landmarks off by more than 1 mm between anatomic best fit and implants (VERIFIED, abstract), and Springate & Jones 1998 found Ricketts' Xi-Pm registration gave > 4 mm errors at molars and incisors in about 10 % of implanted children (registry, VERIFIED). My read: Pm (suprapogonion) lies inside this paper's stable chin region, so Khaleel 2026's registration point is sound; its orientation through Xi, a constructed ramus centre, is where the error enters.

5. **Know the noise floor before calling a change real.** The paper says region stability is about 0.4 mm and two observers differ by 0.12 ± 0.11 mm (Results; the abstract misprints ± 1.1); over one subject's whole mandible the observers agreed within 0.25 mm, and within 0.12 mm over 75 % of the surface (Fig 8). The literature says 2D duplicate tracings differ by about 0.5 mm (Doppel 1994, VERIFIED paper text). My read: on a 3D regional superimposition, anything under about 0.4 mm is within method error; on a 2D structural superimposition, anything under about 0.5 mm is. Khaleel 2026's upper-arch differences of 0.2-0.35 mm and lower Point I values of 0.7-1.0 mm sit at or inside those floors (registry).

6. **Read the colour map as growth plus treatment relative to the chin, and expect nothing to show inside the registration region.** The paper says the chin + symphysis fit is forced by the registration, and the displacement maps then show deposition at the inferior symphysis, ramus remodelling and condylar change (Figs 4, 6, 7). My read: tooth movement and condylar growth read off this registration are relative to the chin, which is what Björk's 2D method also gives; an overall (cranial-base) registration is still needed to separate displacement of the whole mandible from remodelling within it (the paper cites Cevidanes 2005-2009 for that step, refs 16-18).

## Practical notes

How to perform the superimposition the paper recommends, step by step, following its Methods (pp. 749-750). Steps marked "my read" fill gaps the paper leaves.

**1. Acquire and screen the scans**
- Same CBCT machine for T1 and T2, 0.3 mm isotropic voxels (i-CAT or NewTom 3G in the paper). Exclude scans with motion-artefact double images, craniofacial syndromes and significant asymmetric growth, as the paper did.
- My read: match field of view and voxel size between T1 and T2; a different voxel size at T2 adds interpolation error the paper never had.

**2. Convert and orient**
- Convert DICOM to the working format (gipl in the paper; current Slicer pipelines use nrrd, my read).
- Orient T1 to Frankfort horizontal in a known 3D coordinate system (done by observer 1 in the paper). T2 is then brought to T1, not the reverse.

**3. Rough manual approximation**
- Translate and rotate T2 so that analogous anatomic regions overlap approximately. This is the only manual step in registration and it is done by eye.

**4. Define the registration mask**
- Chin: anterior surface of the bony chin from pogonion up to B point, laterally to a plane through the distal-incisal points of the lateral incisors.
- Symphysis: internal cortical bone of the lingual surface from the inferior border up to the level of the centre of the two mental foramina, laterally to the limit of the lingual surface.
- Combine the two into a single field (the paper merged them to provide maximum surface area for the software).
- Exclude the teeth and alveolar bone, the mandibular canal, the third-molar crypts, the lower border beyond the symphysis, the body and the rami. My read: the mask should be bone only; alveolar bone around erupting incisors remodels with tooth movement and will pull the fit.

**5. Run the voxel-based registration**
- Slicer CMF version 3.1 in the paper (3D Slicer with the CMF extensions; the registration is automated once the mask and the rough approximation are set). The algorithm matches grey-level voxels inside the mask, not landmarks or surfaces.

**6. Segment and build surface models**
- Automated thresholding (Intensity Segmenter in Slicer CMF), then manual refinement with the Active Contour and Adaptive tools in ITK-SNAP (version 3.6 in the paper). Build surface models with Model Maker.
- My read: segment T1 and T2 with the same threshold; segmentation error is not reported in the paper and directly enters any surface-distance figure.

**7. Quantify**
- Model to Model Distance plus Mesh Statistics in Slicer. Use the absolute mean surface distance (direction ignored) for region error, as the paper did; signed maps (−2 to +2 mm scale in Figs 4 and 7) for direction of remodelling.
- Check the registration region first: expect residuals of about 0.4 mm or less (Table). Then read the regions outside it (inferior symphysis, ramus, condyle, dentition) as change relative to the chin.

**8. Reliability check**
- A second observer repeats steps 3-5 independently on the same oriented T1; overlay the two registered T2 models and read the surface error between them. The paper's figure is 0.12 ± 0.11 mm with ICC 0.998.

**9. Translating to 2D when only cephs exist (my read, consistent with the paper and Björk)**
- Register on the anterior chin contour and the inner cortex of the symphysis; let the canal-related trabeculae and a pre-root third-molar germ orient but not anchor the fit; never use the lower border or the corpus axis. Expect about 0.5 mm of tracing error (Doppel 1994) on top of whatever the structures themselves move.

## What the paper does NOT tell you

- **Whether the plates themselves moved.** Immobility was "confirmed ... clinically"; no radiographic or plate-on-plate residual is reported. The plates were loaded by Class III elastics for the whole interval and sit in alveolar bone at the lateral-incisor/canine apices.
- **n per region, the range or maximum of any error, confidence intervals, per-subject data.** The Table has means, SDs and three P values with no named test. The third-molar SD of 0.06 mm (CV 3 %, derived) does not fit the printed P of .0025 by any standard test (derived).
- **What the ICC measured.** The only interobserver outcome is a surface distance, which is one number per subject and cannot feed a two-rater ICC on its own.
- **Which 25 subjects and whether 5 of them are from the 20.** Overlap between the arms is not stated; if the 5 Class III bone-anchor patients in aim 2 are among the aim-1 20, they carry metal next to the registration field.
- **Untreated growth.** Every subject was under a Herbst, Class II elastics or bone-anchored Class III mechanics. Stability under treatment load is what was shown.
- **Anything beyond about one year or outside 11-14 years.** Björk's structures were followed for up to 25 years. CVM is reported only for aim 2 (stages 2-5); aim 1 has ages only.
- **Segmentation error, intra-observer repeatability, metal-artefact handling, time per case, observer experience and blinding.** None reported.
- **The third-molar crypt stage.** Björk's condition was "before root development"; the paper never says whether its 12-13-year-olds met it.
- **Dose, cost and indication for serial CBCT.** The paper is a method validation on existing scans; it makes no recommendation about taking serial CBCTs for growth assessment. Proffit's four CBCT indications do not include it (VERIFIED via study pages only).
- **Transverse and asymmetric change.** The Introduction motivates 3D with asymmetry and the condyles, but asymmetric growers were excluded and no condylar or transverse measurement is reported.
- **2D equivalence.** No cephalometric arm; the implications for tracing are by analogy with Björk, not measured here.
- **Whether the result holds in other software.** Only Slicer CMF and ITK-SNAP were used.

## One sentence for a patient or a junior

- **For a junior:** "When you superimpose a growing mandible, fit the front of the chin and the inside of the symphysis and nothing else; the crypts, the canal, the lower border and the ramus all remodel, and anything you read under about half a millimetre on a tracing, or about one voxel on a CBCT, is noise."
- **For a patient:** "To see how much your jaw has grown and how much the braces have moved the teeth, we line up the two scans on the one part of the chin that does not change shape, so everything else that has changed shows up as real growth or tooth movement."
