## Bottom line

- Well-done orthodontics has not been shown to raise TMD risk, but calling it 'unrelated' is too simple [§2].
- TMD has five etiologic factors (occlusion, trauma, emotional stress, deep pain input, parafunction) plus patient adaptability. Orthodontics routinely changes only occlusion [§2].
- The orthodontist should therefore finish in **orthopedic stability**: maximum intercuspation at the same time as the stable joint position [Abs; Int-2018 Q2].
- Stability lowers a risk but does not guarantee freedom from TMD. Orthodontics is not TMD prevention [§2; Int-2018 Q2].

- **Design:** invited narrative review by one author, an orofacial pain programme director (University of Kentucky) writing as a nonorthodontist [Intro; PubMed]. May 2015 AJODO supplement (a centennial issue, UNVERIFIED).

- **Structure:** 5 sections: history; role of orthodontics in TMD; functional treatment goals; future; conclusions [Intro].

## What it says

**Why orthodontists should care [Intro].** Orthodontics changes the whole occlusion, like a full-mouth reconstruction in natural teeth, mostly in young people who keep the result for life.

**History [§1]**

| When | What happened | Joint considered? |
|---|---|---|
| 1900 | Angle opens his school in St Louis, coins the term malocclusion, defines Classes I, II and III | No; the aim was esthetics |
| Later | Andrews' six keys become the finishing guideline | No reference to joint position |
| Routine practice | Casts trimmed and set in maximum intercuspation | No joint reference |
| Mid-1970s to early 1980s | Roth's 5 goal groups: facial esthetics, dental esthetics, functional occlusion, periodontal health, stability; urges articulators (controversial) | Yes, first time in orthodontics |
| 1987 | A patient wins a lawsuit claiming her orthodontist caused her TMD, with sizable compensation | Orthodontic organisations fund research |
| Mid-1990s | Studies find orthodontics is not a significant TMD risk factor | Anxiety falls |

**Why 'unrelated' is too simple [§2]**

| Reason | Okeson's point |
|---|---|
| Who treated | Almost all long-term studies came from supervised university graduate programmes; poorly finished treatment might carry risk |
| Who was treated | Young, healthy, adaptive patients; a developing system may adapt to new occlusal and joint positions |
| Many causes | Occlusion is one of at least 5 factors; at least 4 lie outside the orthodontist's control |
| Adaptability | COMT gene variation sorts people into 3 pain-sensitivity clusters. In 186 post-orthodontic women, the pain-sensitive cluster developed more TMD symptoms: the vulnerability was in the patient, not the treatment |

**Orthopedic stability [Abs; Int-2018 Q2, Q3, Q13]**

- **Stable joint position** (musculoskeletally stable, MS): condyles most superior and anterior in the fossae, against the posterior slopes of the articular eminences, discs interposed. The elevators set it: temporalis seats the condyles up, masseter and medial pterygoid up and forward, lateral pterygoid forward [Int-2018 Q2, Fig 1].
- **Stable occlusal position:** maximum intercuspation. Orthopedic stability = both at once.
- **Size of the mismatch:** most people have 1-2 mm, not linked to TMD. Slides over 3-4 mm relate more to symptoms [Int-2018 Q3].
- **The occlusion he means** (his splint criteria): even, simultaneous contacts with the condyles in MS, forces along the long axes, anterior guidance in excursions [Int-2018 Q13]. Whether section 3 sets these as finishing goals: (UNVERIFIED, full text not read).
- **Loading:** his textbook adds that instability matters mainly when the joints are loaded (clenching, bruxism), and that an acute occlusal change can trigger protective muscle co-contraction (UNVERIFIED, full text not read).

## Why it matters for an orthodontist

- Screen before treatment: history, muscle and joint palpation, range of movement. Record and discuss the findings; they are the baseline if the braces are blamed later [Int-2018 Q4].
- Manage significant TMD first. A stabilisation splint helps find the stable joint position. If the splint fails, occlusion is unlikely to be the cause [Int-2018 Q5].
- Finish with maximum intercuspation matching the seated condyles, without a large slide (Okeson's 2015 finishing wording: UNVERIFIED, full text not read).
- Do not promise that braces will prevent or cure TMD [Int-2018 Q2].

## The course's question answered

Orthopedic stability means the teeth are in maximum intercuspation while the condyles are in the MS position: superoanterior, against the eminences, discs in place. Orthodontics can create or break that match, so the abstract makes it a routine treatment goal. Occlusion is one of five factors and matters only in a minority:
- a large slide from MS into intercuspation (Okeson: over 3-4 mm; Pullinger 1993: RCP-ICP over 2 mm);
- anterior open bite, unilateral maxillary lingual crossbite, overjet over 6-7 mm, or 5-6 or more missing posterior teeth (Pullinger 1993);
- low adaptability, such as the pain-sensitive genotype [§2];
- per his textbook, joints loaded by parafunction or a sudden occlusal change (UNVERIFIED).

Even then, occlusion explains little of the variation in TMD (Pullinger 1993).

## What the presenter may ask

1. **CR (the MS position) in Okeson's terms?** A joint position, not a tooth position: condyles superoanterior against the eminences, discs interposed.
2. **The five etiologic factors, and which one orthodontics changes?** Occlusion, trauma, emotional stress, deep pain input, parafunction. Only occlusion.
3. **Does orthodontics cause TMD?** The mid-1990s studies found no significant risk, but in well-controlled cases and adaptive teenagers.
4. **Gnathology or evidence: where does Okeson stand?** In the middle. Occlusion is not *the* cause, but a stable joint-occlusion match is still the goal.
5. **Which occlusal features carry risk?** Pullinger's five. Anterior open bite in osteoarthrosis is a consequence, not a cause.
6. **A TMD patient wants braces to fix the pain.** Manage the TMD first, then decide. No promises.

## Limits

- Expert opinion: no systematic search, no new data, selective citation in the parts read.
- Orthopedic stability is logical, but the sections read cite no trial showing that finishing in MS prevents TMD. Sections 3-5, where the finishing criteria sit, were not read.
- The MS position cannot be located exactly at the chair, and CR definitions have changed over time (UNVERIFIED).
- Newer evidence moves away from occlusal treatment for chronic TMD pain: the 2023 BMJ guideline is conditionally against reversible occlusal splints and strongly against irreversible oral splints (abstract).
