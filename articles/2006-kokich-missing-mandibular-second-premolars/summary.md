# Congenitally missing mandibular second premolars: clinical options (Kokich & Kokich 2006)

Kokich VG, Kokich VO. Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop 2006;130(4):437-44. doi:10.1016/j.ajodo.2006.05.025. Clinician's Corner; University of Washington, Seattle.

## Bottom line

Profile and crowding decide whether a missing lower second premolar space is closed or kept. If it is kept for an implant, protect the ridge until vertical growth stops: keep and narrow a healthy E, extract an ankylosed one early in a growing child, skip the space maintainer, and let moving teeth build bone.

## What they did

Expert opinion illustrated by five patients (four girls aged 8 y 3 mo to 14 y 6 mo and one adult woman; Figs 1-5) and 22 references. No methods section, measured outcomes or statistics.

## What they found

- Patient 1's E was 13 mm wide against 7.5 mm for an average lower 5; it was reduced mesially and distally, covered with composite, and the space closed to Class I (p 437). Rule: cut the E to its CEJ width from a bitewing, about 2 mm per side, leaving about 7-8 mm (pp 441-442).
- Flat interdental bone means the E is not ankylosed; oblique bone confirms ankylosis. Significant growth left: extract. Little growth and slight submergence: keep (p 443).
- No space maintainer: the 4 and 6 drift together and are later pushed apart, leaving bone (orthodontic implant-site development; Patient 2; p 443).
- After E extraction the ridge narrows 25 % in 4 y and 30 % by 7 y, more facially, so the implant sits lingually (citing Ostler, p 441).
- Implant when serial cephs show vertical growth has stopped: about 17 y in girls, 21 y in boys (citing Fudalej, p 441); Patient 5's at 17 y.
- Narrow ridge: push the 4 into the 5 site and place the implant in the 4 site, no graft (Patient 5). Adult missing the 5 and 6: one implant anchors protraction of the 7 (Patient 3).
- No crowding and a normal profile: close only with extra anchorage. A chincup and elastics took 4 years in Patient 4 (13 y 8 mo, 5-mm overjet, three missing 5s), with no restorations (pp 439-440).

## Why it matters

A decision framework for the tooth most often affected by agenesis (Polder 2004, VERIFIED). Proffit Ch 12 gives the same rules plus decoronation (notes, VERIFIED). A 2025 split-mouth RCT found only minimal, clinically irrelevant differences between hemisection and plain extraction, with more complications and cost (Abdul Jabbar, VERIFIED).

## One-line caveat

Level 5 opinion from five selected successes, all female; 13 − 2 × 2 = 9 mm, not 7-8 (derived); the source abstract gives 25 % ridge loss within 3 y, then 4 % (VERIFIED).

## Mechanism at a glance

| Strategy | When the paper uses it | Why it works | Figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep and narrow the E | Healthy E (flat bone), growing patient, implant planned | Holds ridge width; the diverging roots resorb and bone replaces them | Fig 1 |
| Extract the ankylosed E early, no space maintainer | Oblique bone, significant growth left | Avoids a vertical defect; the drifting, erupting 4 and 6 bring bone up | Fig 2 |
| Orthodontic implant-site development | 4 and 6 pushed apart to reopen the 5 space | Separating roots leave bone the width of the teeth | Fig 2 |
| Move the 4 distally | Late extraction, narrow ridge | The moving tooth brings bone to the deficient site; implant in the vacated 4 site, no graft | Fig 5 |
| Implant as anchor | Adult missing the 5 and 6; space between one and two tooth widths | Immobile anchor protracts the 7; fewer restorations | Fig 3 |
| Close with extra anchorage | No crowding, normal profile | Molars come forward; incisors and lips stay | Fig 4 |
| Early hemisection of the E | Child seen early and monitored | The 6 erupts mesially without moving the lower incisors | — |
