# Key numbers — Kokich & Kokich 2006 (congenitally missing mandibular second premolars)

Kokich VG, Kokich VO. Congenitally missing mandibular second premolars: clinical options. Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop 2006;130(4):437-44. doi:10.1016/j.ajodo.2006.05.025. Every number below was read from the page images (journal pages 437-444) and checked against the extracted text. Page numbers are journal pages; "col 1" and "col 2" are the left and right text columns. "Derived" means computed here from the printed text.

**What kind of paper this is.** A Clinician's Corner: expert opinion illustrated by five patients, one per figure. It has **no tables, no methods section, no defined sample, no measured outcomes, no statistics, no P values and no SDs.** Its numbers are patient ages, one tooth width set against an average premolar width, one overjet, a reduction rule, a treatment duration and two sets of figures cited from other studies (ridge resorption, end of facial growth). The sections below therefore list those numbers, the decision rules and the five cases in place of results tables.

## Decision rules as printed (the paper's "protocol")

### Keep the space or close it (p 443, col 2 to p 444)

| Situation | Paper's recommendation | Cited support |
|---|---|---|
| Crowding in the opposing arch, or a protrusive profile | Closure "would be advantageous" | Ref 19 (Fines 2003, a case report) |
| No crowding and a normal profile | Closure "could produce an undesirable facial profile"; additional anchorage is needed | None |
| Extraoral anchorage | Protraction facemask or chincup (cites Fig 4; Patient 4 used a chincup with elastics, no facemask) | None |
| Intraoral anchorage | Miniscrews and mini-implants | Ref 20 (miniscrews) |
| Child seen early and monitored | Hemisect the E early; the 6 erupts mesially without moving the lower incisors; "especially attractive" | Refs 21, 22 (Northway 2004, 2005) |

### Keeping the space for an implant (pp 440-441)

| Item | As printed |
|---|---|
| Two keys | Create the correct amount of space; leave the ridge in ideal condition (abstract; p 440, col 2 to p 441) |
| Restoration of choice | Single-tooth implant (ref 6). Full-coverage bridges in young patients risk pulp devitalisation (ref 2); resin-bonded posterior bridges have questionable survival (refs 3-5) (p 441, col 1) |
| When | At the cessation of vertical facial growth, judged by comparing serial cephalometric radiographs for the end of ramus growth (p 441, col 2) |
| Averages | Girls about 17 y; boys about 21 y (ref 9) |
| Ridge after E extraction | Narrows 25 % in the first 4 y and another 5 % by 7 y (30 % total), yet still broad enough for an implant. More facial than lingual loss, so the implant sits lingually and the restorative dentist alters buccal and lingual cusp loading to prevent abutment or crown fracture (refs 7, 8; p 441, col 1) |

### Keeping and reducing the E (pp 441-442)

| Step | As printed |
|---|---|
| Why | Deciduous molars are too wide mesiodistally and could affect the fit of the posterior teeth; reduce to the size of a second premolar (ref 1) |
| Instrument | Sharp carbide fissure bur or diamond bur |
| Guide | Mesiodistal width at the CEJ on a bitewing (Fig 1), marked on the occlusal surface with a pencil or marking pen |
| Cut | Follow the line toward the gingiva, removing a wafer of enamel and the underlying dentin on the mesial and the distal |
| Amount | About 2 mm from each surface, leaving a crown about 7-8 mm wide |
| Limit | Enough to create space, not enough to cause pulpal necrosis |
| Protection | Light-cured restorative composite on the mesial and distal surfaces (exposed dentin is hard to clean once spaces close) |
| Build-up | The same composite builds up the occlusal surface of the "typically short" E, so it occludes and the opposing tooth does not supererupt |
| Then | Close the interproximal spaces; the E functions as a premolar (Fig 1) |
| Roots | Diverging roots usually resorb as the socket walls of the permanent teeth reach them; bone replaces them, called an "ideal" implant-site preparation (ref 1) |

### Ankylosed E (pp 442-443)

| Situation | As printed |
|---|---|
| Ankylosis while young and growing | The E submerges relative to the erupting neighbours; the ridge may be compromised vertically and need a graft (ref 10); vertical grafting is "often unpredictable" and an added expense (ref 11) |
| Diagnosis | Bone level between the E and the adjacent 6 and 4 (ref 1): flat = erupting evenly; oblique, more apical around the E = ankylosis confirmed (Fig 2) |
| Little growth left, slight submergence | Keep the E to preserve alveolar width |
| Significant growth left | Extract to prevent a significant ridge defect |
| After extraction | No space maintainer "in most of these situations", especially when implants are planned; the 6 and 4 erupt together and are later pushed apart (longer treatment). The moving roots leave bone "that equals the width of the premolar and molar": orthodontic implant-site development (Fig 2) |
| Extraction decided too late (narrow ridge, vertical defect) | Graft, or push the 4 into the 5 position and place the implant in the 4 site. A wider root can be moved through a narrow ridge without compromising bone support (refs 12, 13); done "on several occasions" (Fig 5) |

### Implant as an orthodontic anchor (p 443, col 2)

| Item | As printed |
|---|---|
| Indication | 5 and 6 missing, with drift leaving a space too large for 1 tooth and too small for 2 |
| Sequence | Discussion: implant placed "before orthodontic treatment". Patient 3: initial alignment, then wax-up, implant and provisional crown |
| Planning | Diagnostic wax-up and precise positioning for orthodontic, surgical and restorative goals |
| Benefit | Fewer restorations for the patient; an immobile anchor for the orthodontist (ref 14) |
| Loading | Traditionally delayed until full integration (ref 15); early or immediate loading possible (refs 16, 17), especially in orthodontic patients (ref 18), because the orthodontic load is continuous and one-directional. "In most orthodontic situations": load soon after the temporary restoration |

## Measurement method, reliability, statistics

None. The only measured values are the 13 mm E width (method not stated; the cited Fig 1C has no scale) and the 5 mm overjet. ## Derived numbers

- **Patient 1's excess:** 13 − 7.5 = 5.5 mm.
- **The reduction arithmetic:** 13 − (2 × 2) = 9 mm, not 7-8 mm. Reaching 7.5 mm from 13 mm needs 2.75 mm per side; a 7-8 mm result after 4 mm removed implies an 11-12 mm E. On Bjerklin's mean E of 10.53 mm (VERIFIED), 2 mm per side would leave about 6.5 mm, narrower than a premolar. The paper's guide cuts the crown down to its own CEJ width, so per side = (crown width − width at the CEJ) ÷ 2; reaching premolar size takes (E width − premolar width) ÷ 2.
- **Ostler as cited vs the source abstract:** 25 % at 4 y vs 25 % within 3 y; +5 % by 7 y (30 %) vs +4 % over the next 3 y (about 29 % by about 6 y, derived by simple addition).
- **Patient 5:** 17 y − 14 y 6 mo = about 2 y 6 mo from presentation to implant.
- **Patient 4:** tracings from 13 y 8 mo to 17 y 11 mo = 4 y 3 mo; she finished at about the 17 y average the paper cites for girls.
- **Sample:** 5 of 5 female; mean adolescent age at presentation (8.25 + 12.33 + 13.67 + 14.5) ÷ 4 = 12.2 y; 8 missing second premolars (7 mandibular, 1 maxillary); 3 implants placed, 1 planned.
- **Figures and references:** 42 panels; 5 of 22 references (22.7 %) include V. Kokich.
- **Closure without a restoration:** in a Class I arch, closing one lower 5 space by molar protraction alone moves the lower 6 forward by up to a premolar width, toward Class III by up to a full unit if the upper molar stays (derived). This is why Proffit says opposing-arch extractions may be needed unless all four 5s are missing; in Patient 4 the upper and lower 6s were both protracted.

## Number traps (details in `conflicts.md`)

- "About 2 mm per side leaves 7-8 mm" does not fit Patient 1's 13 mm E (9 mm left, derived).
- The Ostler percentages differ from the source abstract (above; VERIFIED).
- The 17 y and 21 y averages are not in the published Fudalej abstract (UNVERIFIED as cut-offs).
- Patient 4: Class II with a 5-mm overjet (text) vs "end-to-end malocclusion" (caption).
- Patient 3's implant: second-premolar site (text, and Fig 3E/H) vs "first premolar position" (caption).
- Patient 5's text cites "Fig 3, C-E" where Fig 5 C-E is meant.
- Patient 2's text says "second molars" for the deciduous molars; reference 7's printed title says "second molars", the published title says "second premolars" (VERIFIED).
