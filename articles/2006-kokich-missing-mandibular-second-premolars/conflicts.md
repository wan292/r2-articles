# Conflicts and disagreements — Kokich & Kokich 2006

Use this file when you need to challenge a claim, a figure legend or a citation in Kokich VG, Kokich VO. Congenitally missing mandibular second premolars: clinical options. *Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop* 2006;130(4):437-44, doi:10.1016/j.ajodo.2006.05.025. Volume, issue and pages were confirmed on page 1 and the running heads (Volume 130, Number 4; October 2006). It is a **Clinician's Corner** article, submitted March 2006 and revised and accepted May 2006 (p 437). It has 22 references and five patients (Figs 1-5, 42 panels, derived). It has **no tables, no statistics, no methods section and no measured outcomes**. Section A therefore checks the text against itself and against its own figure legends, on all eight page images. Section B checks what the paper says its references found against those references, then against later studies and the papers already in the hub. C is the paper against Proffit, followed by the previous-year exam items that use this paper. D lists what stays open and E is the verdict.

Page numbers are journal pages (437-444); "col 1" and "col 2" are the columns. Tags: **VERIFIED** = seen in this run, in a PubMed abstract (fetched through NCBI E-utilities), on the paper's page images, on the user's Proffit study pages (wan292/proffit-study-pages) or in the recalled-exam files of the `promotion/` folder; **UNVERIFIED** = from memory, from a full text that was not read, or not checked. Proffit positions come from the study pages, which summarise the 6th edition with boxes marking 7th-edition additions; they were not checked against the printed book. Chapter numbers are the **6th edition** (as in the process-article skill); where the 7th differs it is given in brackets (6th Ch 18/19/20 = 7th Ch 19/20/22; Ch 1-17 are the same). `topics/05-treatment-rationale-1/kokich-2006-qa.md` labels its chapters by the 7th edition. "The paper says", "the source says" and "my read" are used deliberately. Abbreviations: E = mandibular deciduous second molar; 4, 5, 6, 7 = first premolar, second premolar, first molar, second molar; CEJ = cementoenamel junction; RBB = resin-bonded bridge; TAD = temporary anchorage device.

**Checks that passed** (so you can say so).
- The abstract and the opening paragraph are the same text, and its scope (at least one missing lower 5) fits all five patients. Patient 3 is also missing a lower 6 and Patient 4 an upper 5.
- Patient 4's tracings are labelled 13-8 and 17-11 (Fig 4C, 4F), a span of 4 y 3 mo (derived). That fits "4 years of orthodontic treatment" (p 439, col 2).
- Patient 4's three missing premolars agree between the text (p 439, col 1; p 440, col 1) and the legend (p 441).
- Patient 5's implant at 17 y (p 440) matches the cited average end of growth in girls, about 17 y (p 441, col 2), and was placed after serial cephalograms, as the Discussion prescribes.
- Flat bone in Patient 1 (Fig 1B) and oblique bone in Patient 2 (Fig 2B) are used exactly as the diagnostic rule on p 443, col 1 describes them.
- Patient 2's management (no space maintainer, 4 and 6 drift together, bone rises with eruption, then the teeth are pushed apart) is the sequence the Discussion recommends (p 443, col 1). Fig 2C-D match it.
- Patient 4 had only a minor arch-length deficiency (p 439, col 1) and a profile judged ideal (Fig 4C legend). The closure rule (close freely only with crowding in the opposing arch or a protrusive profile, p 443, col 2) therefore points to added anchorage, which is what was done.
- The paper is candid about costs: longer treatment when the 4 and 6 are pushed apart (Discussion, p 443, col 1), 4 years for Patient 4 (p 439, col 2), and an unopposed upper 7 in Patient 3 (p 438, col 2).
- Refs 1-6, 8, 10-13 and 15-22 match their PubMed records in journal, year, volume and first page (VERIFIED). Ref 7 (title) and ref 9 (in press, B2) differ; ref 14, a book chapter, was not checked.

**How to raise these politely.** This is a Clinician's Corner by two of the best-known names in interdisciplinary orthodontics, and its decision framework still matches Proffit. Lead with what holds, then raise at most three points, each as a question.
- **A1.** "I read Patient 3's implant as a second-premolar implant, as in the text and Fig 3E, but the legend says first-premolar position. Did I read Fig 3 right?" Treat it as a legend slip.
- **A2.** "Two millimetres a side from a 13-mm E leaves 9 mm, not 7 or 8. Is the CEJ width on the bitewing the real guide?" This is a teaching question.
- **Ostler and Duyck (B2, B1).** "The abstract of reference 7 gives 25 % within 3 years and 4 % over the next 3. Does the full paper report the 4- and 7-year figures?" and "The rabbit study found that static load did no harm. Is there human evidence that orthodontic load builds bone?"

Keep A3-A17 as footnote-level remarks. Phrase nothing as an accusation: a Clinician's Corner is written as expert guidance, not as a report of data.

### B3. Keeping the E (later and contemporary evidence)

- **Bjerklin & Bennett 2000**, Eur J Orthod 22:245-55 (VERIFIED). 41 subjects (13 male, 28 female), 59 retained Es, followed from 11-12 y to a mean of 20 y 6 mo (SD 3.62). 2 exfoliated and 5 were extracted, 2 of them replaced by upper third-molar transplants; 7 of 59 lost (11.9 %, derived), none after age 20. Infraocclusion rose from 0.47 ± 1.13 mm at 11-12 y to 1.43 ± 1.13 mm at 17-18 y; at 19-20 y, 45 % showed none. Mean E width 10.53 ± 0.51 mm. **AGREES** with keeping the E. Their average E is about 3 mm wider than Kokich's 7.5-mm premolar (derived), not 5.5 mm. "If an E is still there at 20, it tends to stay."
- **Sletten et al 2003**, AJODO 124:625-30 (VERIFIED). 20 adults, 28 retained Es, followed 12.4 ± 7.7 y: 24 (86 %) still functioned; 4 were lost at a mean age of 51.0 y, from caries or periodontal breakdown; root shortening 0.16 mm. **AGREES**. "A healthy adult E lasts about as long as some prostheses."
- **Hvaring et al 2014**, Eur J Orthod 36:26-30 (VERIFIED). 188 Es in 111 hypodontia patients (8.4 missing teeth on average): clinically significant infraocclusion in 43.6 % of patients, severe in 18.8 %; infraocclusion predicts prognosis better than root resorption. **PARTIAL**: Kokich's keep-or-extract rule uses infraocclusion ("submerged only slightly", p 443) but gives no millimetres. "Hvaring says watch the infraocclusion, not the roots."
- **Kurol & Koch 1985**, Am J Orthod 87:46-55 (VERIFIED). 15 children, 23 pairs of infraoccluded deciduous molars **with** successors: 10 of 23 progressed but all exfoliated on time with normal bone height; extraction caused space loss in 14 of 15. **PARTIAL**: a more conservative stance, in a different population (successors present). "With a successor, an infraoccluded molar usually sorts itself out; without one, Kokich's growth rule applies."
- **Otsugu et al 2026**, Jpn Dent Sci Rev 62:262-73 (VERIFIED). Systematic review, 14 studies: infraocclusion prevalence 1.3-24.8 %; not all infraoccluded primary molars are ankylosed. **PARTIAL** against "oblique bone confirms ankylosis" (p 443): the sign is reasonable but has never been validated as a diagnostic test. "Low is not the same as fused."
- **Baril et al 2026**, Eur Arch Paediatr Dent 27:997-1006 (VERIFIED). Systematic review, 37 studies, mostly case reports: preserving infraoccluded molars without successors could maintain alveolar bone. **AGREES**.

### B4. Extraction, drift and hemisection

- **Mamopoulou et al 1996**, Eur J Orthod 18:589-600 (VERIFIED). 11 subjects (mean 11.8 y), normal occlusion, lower E and upper 5 extracted on the agenesis side, followed 4 y. Lower space 46 % closed at 1 y and 80 % at 4 y (residual 2 mm), by mesial movement, rotation and tipping of the 6 and distal movement and tipping of the 4. Unilateral extraction shifted the lower midline; overjet, overbite and incisor inclination were unchanged. **PARTIAL**: supports Patient 2's spontaneous drift, but with tipping and a midline shift that the paper does not mention. "Spontaneous closure happens mostly in the first year, by tipping, and leaves about 2 mm."
- **Abdul Jabbar et al 2025**, Eur J Orthod 47:cjaf043 (VERIFIED). Registered split-mouth RCT, 40 children (25 boys, 15 girls), mean 10.03 ± 1.07 y, 4.2-y follow-up, no losses. Residual 6-4 space 2.04 ± 1.67 mm with hemisection vs 2.39 ± 1.86 mm with extraction (P = .053; difference 0.35 mm, derived); no difference in molar or premolar angulation; more complications with hemisection (P = .0176) and higher costs and chair time (P < .0001). The authors do not recommend hemisection. **CONFLICTS** with "especially attractive" (p 444). "The only RCT found hemisection no better than plain extraction, and dearer."
- **Nawaia et al 2025**, Eur J Orthod 47:cjaf095 (VERIFIED). Same trial (40 patients aged 9-12) against 29 untreated controls: lower incisors retroclined 4.5° and retruded 1.8 mm, overjet +1.2 mm, overbite +1.5 mm; no detrimental skeletal effect; caution in deep-bite tendencies. **PARTIAL**: confirms the incisor retraction Kokich warns about, although the authors judged it favourable. "Early E extraction does tip the lower incisors back, about 4.5°."
- **Naoumova et al 2026**, Eur J Orthod 48:cjag048 (VERIFIED). Same trial, 40 vs 40 matched controls at 4 y: total CPQ score no different (P = .218); emotional (P = .018) and social (P = .045) scores higher in the treated group; hemisection recalled as more painful (P < .001); 82.5 % did not want their residual spaces treated. Background.

### B5. Orthodontic implant-site development

- **Eliášová et al 2014**, AJODO 145:333-40 (VERIFIED). 55 patients, 71 sites: lower 4s distalised into the missing 5 position. During treatment, ridge width +28.5 % and height +1.1 mm; over 2-5 y of retention, width −4.2 % and height −0.07 mm. **AGREES** with Patient 5's strategy (p 440, p 443) and gives the numbers the paper lacks. "Moving the 4 back built about 28 % more ridge width, and it stayed."
- **Hom & Turley 1984** (B2): the adult molar-space version, with ridge width +1.2 mm and crestal loss in most cases. **PARTIAL**.
- Proffit 6th Ch 19 [7th Ch 20] (C): closing into an old, narrowed ridge is slow, about 3 years. **NUANCE**: possible, but slow.

### B6. Implant timing: does waiting for growth to stop prevent infraposition?

- **Bernard et al 2004**, J Clin Periodontol 31:1024-8 (VERIFIED). 28 patients, 40 anterior maxillary implants, reviewed a mean 4.2 y after placement: 14 young adults (15.5-21 y) and 14 mature adults (40-55 y). **Every** patient in both groups showed a vertical step beside the implant crown: 0.1-1.65 mm in the young group and 0.12-1.86 mm in the mature group. **PARTIAL / CONFLICTS** with the implicit promise of the 17 y / 21 y rule: waiting reduces the risk but does not abolish it, because eruption continues in adults. Anterior maxilla, not lower premolar sites. "Even 40- to 55-year-olds developed up to 1.86 mm of step beside an implant."
- **Heij et al 2006**, Int J Oral Maxillofac Implants 21:867-78 (VERIFIED; **V.G. Kokich is a co-author**). Review: implants behave like ankylosed teeth as the jaws grow; postpone placement until growth is complete in normal faces; in short- or long-face patients, continued eruption of adjacent teeth is a serious risk even after age 20. **PARTIAL**: the same senior author refined the rule within months by facial type. "Kokich's own later review adds: in long and short faces, 20 is not safe either."
- **Fudalej 2007** (B2): growth after the second decade is clinically insignificant on average. Proffit Ch 4: increases in facial height and eruption continue throughout life, with the decline to adult levels in the early 20s in boys and earlier in girls (VERIFIED, notes). **AGREES** in direction with Bernard and Heij: averages, not guarantees.

### B7. Options and comparisons the paper does not discuss

- **Autotransplantation.** Josefsson et al 1999 (Acta Odontol Scand 57:111-5; VERIFIED): 80 patients, 110 teeth transplanted to the lower 5 region; 4-y success 92 % with incomplete roots (99 teeth) and 82 % with complete roots (11 teeth); the main donor was the upper 5; in growing patients the transplant maintains alveolar growth. Andreasen et al 1990 (Eur J Orthod 12:14-24; VERIFIED): 370 premolars in 195 patients aged 7-35, followed 1-13 y; survival 95 % (incomplete roots) and 98 % (complete); pulp healing 96 % vs 15 %. Czochrowska et al 2002 (AJODO 121:110-9; VERIFIED): 33 teeth in 28 patients (mixed recipient sites), transplanted at a mean 11.5 y and followed a mean 26.4 y (17-41 y): survival 90 %, success 79 %. Atala-Acevedo et al 2017 (J Oral Maxillofac Surg 75:35-50; VERIFIED): 21 studies, all of weak quality; open-apex success 89.68 %, survival 98.21 %; premolars fail less than molars (OR 0.46, 95 % CI 0.25-0.84). Chung et al 2014 (J Clin Periodontol 41:412-23; VERIFIED): complete roots, 5-y survival 90.5 %. The paper's own ref 19 lists the option, and Bjerklin replaced 2 lost Es with transplants (B3). **GAP**. "In a growing child a transplant keeps the alveolus growing; an implant cannot."
- **Decoronation.** In Proffit Ch 12 as the alternative to extracting an ankylosed primary molar without a successor, increasingly indicated when the growth spurt is still ahead (VERIFIED, notes). **GAP** (C, and the 2020 exam item in C-bis).
- **Modern resin-bonded bridges.** Thoma et al 2017 (Clin Oral Implants Res 28:1421-32; VERIFIED): 29 studies, 2300 RBBs; survival 91.4 % (95 % CI 86.7-94.4) at 5 y and 82.9 % (73.2-89.3) at 10 y; better with zirconia frameworks and single retainers, worse posteriorly; debonding 15 % at 5 y. **PARTIAL** against "questionable survival" (p 441): bonded bridges have improved since refs 3-5, but posterior bridges still do worse. "Bonded bridges now last better than the paper's references suggest, but still less well in the back of the mouth."
- **Single implants.** Jung et al 2012 (Clin Oral Implants Res 23 Suppl 6:2-21; VERIFIED): 46 studies; implant survival 97.2 % (95 % CI 96.3-97.9) at 5 y and 95.2 % (91.8-97.2) at 10 y; crown survival 96.3 % and 89.4 %; 5-y screw loosening 8.8 % and aesthetic complications 7.1 %. **AGREES** with "first choice" in adults; not specific to agenesis or lower premolar sites. "Implants survive, but one in eleven has a screw loosen within five years."
- **Comparative evidence on the central choice.** Thilander 2008 (J Oral Rehabil 35 Suppl 1:64-71; VERIFIED): a MEDLINE search (1990-2007) found only 11 relevant articles comparing space closure with implants (1 prospective, 3 retrospective, 2 reviews, 5 case reports); both options have advantages and disadvantages. **CONTEXT**: no trial shows either pathway to be superior. "Two years after this paper, a systematic review found no trial comparing closure with implants."

### B8. Prevalence and when agenesis is certain

- **Polder et al 2004**, Community Dent Oral Epidemiol 32:217-26 (VERIFIED). Meta-analysis: agenesis in Europe 4.6 % (males) and 6.3 % (females); North American Caucasians 3.2 % and 4.6 %; females 1.37 times males; the lower 5 is the most affected tooth; 83 % of affected people miss 1-2 teeth. **AGREES** with "affects many" (p 437), which the paper never quantifies. The female excess makes an all-female case set plausible, but leaves boys untested (D9).
- **Aasheim & Øgaard 1993**, Scand J Dent Res 101:257-60 (VERIFIED). 1953 Norwegian 9-year-olds: of those registered as missing a 5 at 9 y, 11.3 % of boys and 2.9 % of girls showed late mineralisation between 9 and 12. **NUANCE** for Patient 2 (8 y 3 mo; how agenesis was confirmed is not stated). "Repeat the radiograph before an irreversible step in a young child, especially a boy."
- **Kurol 1981**, Community Dent Oral Epidemiol 9:94-102 (VERIFIED). 1059 children aged 3-12: infraocclusion in 8.9 %, peak 14.3 % at 8-9 y; mandibular primary molars affected more than 10 times as often as maxillary; 18.1 % in siblings. Background for Patient 2's age.

## E. One-paragraph verdict

Read against itself, the paper is a coherent piece of expert guidance whose cases follow its own rules, with a cluster of legend and citation slips rather than contradictions of substance. The two worth knowing are Patient 3's implant (text and photographs: second-premolar site; legend: first premolar) and the reduction arithmetic (2 mm a side does not turn a 13-mm E into a 7-8 mm crown; the CEJ width is the operative guide). The rest are minor: a figure cited for the wrong patient, a growth check that is not shown, and an overjet whose correction is not explained. Read against its references, the restorative argument holds best where the paper cites it least prominently: Creugers' trial shows that a mandibular site and agenesis each raise the failure risk of posterior bonded bridges. Ostler's abstract gives different time points from the text (25 % within 3 years, then 4 %, not 25 % at 4 and 30 % at 7). Duyck's rabbits show that static load is harmless, not that it stimulates bone. The grafting and root-movement citations come from other sites and defects. Read against later evidence, the core rules survive: keep a healthy E (Sletten: 86 % of adult Es still working after about 12 years; Bjerklin: 52 of 59 kept to a mean age of 20 y 6 mo, derived), let moving teeth build the ridge (Eliášová +28.5 % width), and wait for growth to stop, knowing that adults still erupt (Bernard: steps up to 1.86 mm at 40-55 y) and that Kokich's own later review extends the caution past 20 in long and short faces. "Especially attractive" hemisection did no better than plain extraction in the only RCT. Against Proffit it agrees almost point for point; Proffit adds decoronation, pulp therapy for hemisection, the slow pace of closure into narrowed ridges, the chincup's side effects and the retainer that holds the space. The past exams have already drawn twice on Fig 1 of this paper (2023, 2024), and the 2020 item on an ankylosed E is where the recalled key (decoronation) and this paper (extraction) part ways. Use it for the decision framework (growth stage, ridge preservation, crowding and profile, interdisciplinary planning), and present its specific numbers as the authors' rules of thumb, not as measured results.
