### Q1 · Promotion 2023

> Kokich missing premolar article (Fig 1: submerged-looking retained deciduous mandibular second molar, radiograph, occlusal view): management?

- **Recalled answer:** Reduce width and maintain · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Reduce the E's mesiodistal width, protect it with composite and maintain it, closing the residual space to keep the ridge for a later implant (Kokich & Kokich 2006 Patient 1 p 437 (12 y 4 mo girl; E 13 mm wide vs 7.5 mm for a premolar; flat bone levels mean not ankylosed); Fig 1 legend p 438; reduction technique pp 441-442 (about 2 mm off each side))
- In Fig 1 the deciduous molar was not ankylosed (flat interproximal bone), so it was narrowed, built up with composite and kept to preserve buccolingual bone until growth ended. This is exactly the recalled answer.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles (Kokich Fig 1 pasted)

### Q2 · Promotion 2024

> 14-year-old girl presented with congenitally missing lower 5 and retained primary molar (photo and radiograph). Doctor decided to extract it. What is the effect?
>
> - a) Large bony defect
> - b) Excessive residual space
> - c) Tipping of adjacent teeth
> - d) Supra-eruption of opposing

- **Recalled answer:** a) Large bony defect · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a) Large bony defect: the ridge resorbs, narrowing vertically and buccolingually (Kokich & Kokich 2006 Patient 5 p 440 (14 y 6 mo girl, ankylosed E extracted, marked vertical and buccolingual ridge narrowing; Fig 5B p 442); p 441 (Ostler & Kokich: ridge 25% narrower within 4 years, 30% by 7 years, more on the facial side))
- The stem matches Kokich's Patient 5 almost exactly: after the deciduous molar was extracted the ridge collapsed in height and width. Kokich avoided a bone graft by moving the first premolar distally to regenerate bone.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 5, 4 Congenitally missing mandibular second premolars: Clinical options, Vincent G. Kokich
