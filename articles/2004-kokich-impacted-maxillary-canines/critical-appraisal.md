# Critical appraisal — Kokich 2004

Kokich VG. Surgical and orthodontic management of impacted maxillary canines. *Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop* 2004;126(3):278-83, doi:10.1016/j.ajodo.2004.06.009. Single author, Professor, Department of Orthodontics, University of Washington, Seattle. It appeared in the section "Advances in Orthodontics & Dentofacial Surgery" as a written version of a talk at the AAO/AAOMS Symposium (February 6-8, 2004, Palm Springs), "Submitted and accepted, June 2004" (p 278). No funding or conflict-of-interest statement is printed.

The paper has no methods section, no tables, no statistics and no data of its own. This appraisal therefore grades it as a narrative expert review, and grades each recommendation by the evidence it cites and the evidence that came later. Page numbers are journal pages. Facts about other papers carry the research pack's tags: **VERIFIED** (abstract read this run), **VERIFIED-secondary** (number seen only through papers citing it), **UNVERIFIED**. Derived numbers are labelled. Recommendations are graded with my own mapping to Oxford CEBM 2011 and GRADE-style certainty, not with an official grading.

## Verdict in three lines

1. **Design:** a narrative expert review written up from a symposium talk, illustrated by five single-patient cases (Figs 1-5). There is no research question, no sample, no outcome measured and no statistics. It sets out a four-criterion algorithm for uncovering labial canines and a timing-and-technique recommendation for palatal ones.
2. **Level of evidence:** Oxford CEBM 2011 Level 5 (expert opinion and mechanism-based reasoning; my mapping). The cited support is retrospective contralateral-controlled studies (Vermette 1995, Woloshyn 1994, Becker 2002), one uncontrolled prospective series (Ericson & Kurol 1988) and one unpublished thesis (Schmidt 2004). Randomised trials published since (Level 1-2) support interceptive extraction, with a lower success rate than "will" implies. They also find early open exposure equivalent to closed exposure, not superior.
3. **Biggest limitation:** the paper's central palatal claim (early uncovering gives better periodontal and esthetic results in less time, p 283) rests on an unpublished thesis from the author's department, with no numbers given, and on an implied historical comparison with Woloshyn's conventionally treated cohort. The treatment-time part of the claim has no source at all.

| **O** | Width of attached gingiva (at least 2-3 mm over the crown after eruption), crown length, reintrusion, gingival margin, bone level, labial recession | Bone and attachment levels on the canine and lateral incisor, lateral-incisor root resorption, esthetics (whether judges can identify the impacted side), overall treatment time |
| **Measured here?** | No | No |

My read: the questions are the right ones, and the labial algorithm is the paper's lasting contribution. None of the outcomes is defined or measured in the paper, so every O above is something the author asserts or cites, not something he reports.

## Design, case selection and the direction of bias

**What kind of paper it is.** A narrative paper written up from a symposium talk. It has no search strategy, no inclusion rule for references, no statement of the evidence level behind each recommendation, and no description of how the five cases were chosen. Submission and acceptance in the same month fit an invited paper; whether it was invited or externally peer-reviewed is not stated (UNVERIFIED).

**Selection of illustrative cases.** All five cases (Figs 1-5) end in success: normal gingival margins (Fig 1D), canine placed in position (Fig 2F), adequate gingiva (Fig 3D), resembles the contralateral canine (Fig 4H), and hard to tell from the contralateral canine (Fig 5H). No failure, no ankylosis, no reintrusion, no early-uncovered canine that did not erupt by itself is shown. Each figure shows that a technique *can* work; none shows how often it does.

**Selection of evidence (allegiance).** 3 of the 13 references are by the author (refs 6, 7, 11; 23 %, derived). Adding the University of Washington thesis (ref 13) makes 4 of 13 (31 %, derived). These four include the studies behind the two main recommendations: Vermette (APF against CE) and Schmidt (early uncovering, compared with conventional uncovering only by implication; see "Historical comparison" below). Contrary or qualifying evidence is not presented: Ericson & Kurol's failure rate, and Vermette's bone loss against the contralateral tooth with both techniques. Becker's restriction to incisors is stated (p 281) but not flagged where its canine claim is made (p 279) (all in `conflicts.md`, A6 and B1).

**Historical comparison.** The palatal recommendation compares Woloshyn's conventional cohort (n = 32) with Schmidt's early cohort (size not given). The two differ in era, operators, case selection and follow-up. The comparison is implied, never tested.

| Feature | What the paper does | Direction of bias |
|---|---|---|
| Five illustrative cases, all successes | Figs 1-5 | Overstates predictability of every technique |
| 4 of 13 references from the author or his department, including the studies behind both main recommendations | Refs 6, 7, 11, 13 | Favours the author's preferred techniques (CE for high labial canines; early palatal uncovering) |
| Central palatal evidence an unpublished thesis | Ref 13 | Unreviewed, no numbers; the 2007 peer-reviewed report is less favourable (B1; whether the samples are identical is UNVERIFIED) |
| Historical comparison, conventional against early palatal uncovering | Woloshyn against Schmidt | Era, operator and selection differences all favour the newer method |
| Canine claims supported by incisor or mixed-tooth studies | Becker (central incisors); Vermette (maxillary anterior teeth) | Unknown; extrapolation |
| Probabilistic findings reported as deterministic | "Will result in normal eruption" (p 281) | Overstates interceptive extraction |
| Time and superiority claims without a source | p 283 col 2 | Favours early uncovering |

**Blinding.** Not applicable to the paper. For the cited esthetic judgements, whether the judges were blinded is not stated in the paper ("judges could identify", p 283).

## Sample, power, attrition

- **This paper:** none. No patients were studied; the five cases are illustrations. Power is not applicable.
- **Sample sizes the paper gives:** one, Woloshyn n = 32 (p 283). Everything else comes from the research pack:
  - Vermette 1995: n = 30 anterior teeth (APF 18, CE 12) (VERIFIED).
  - Becker 2002: n = 21 central incisors (VERIFIED).
  - Ericson & Kurol 1988: 46 PDCs in 35 children (VERIFIED-secondary).
  - Schmidt & Kokich 2007: 16 unilateral + 6 bilateral (VERIFIED). Whether this equals the thesis sample is UNVERIFIED.
- **Attrition and follow-up:** the paper gives no follow-up length for any cited study. The research pack gives:
  - Woloshyn: mean 3 y 7 m after treatment (VERIFIED).
  - Becker: median 4.5 years after retention (VERIFIED).
  - Vermette: recall at least 3 months after treatment (VERIFIED).
  - Schmidt & Kokich 2007: mean 2 y 11 m (VERIFIED).
  - "Long term" (p 279) and "after orthodontic treatment" (p 283) are therefore undefined in the paper.

## Confounding and growth

1. **Age and root development.** Early-uncovered canines belong to younger patients (late mixed dentition), whose open apices carry residual eruptive potential. Proffit's notes (VERIFIED in the study pages) say spontaneous movement is rare once the root is complete. Better outcomes in the early group could come from age alone, whatever the technique.
2. **Two variables change at once.** The early protocol changes the timing (before appliances) and the surgery (all bone removed to the CEJ, window in the flap, free eruption). The paper itself blames slow conventional cases on insufficient bone removal (p 282). Which change produces any benefit cannot be separated.
3. **Severity of impaction.** Depth, angulation, sector, distance from the occlusal plane and position relative to the lateral root are not described for any case or cited cohort. Mild impactions are easier by any method.
4. **Operator.** Surgeon and orthodontist skill, and the surgeon's instructions, are central to the paper's argument (p 278) and uncontrolled in its evidence.
5. **Sex.** Lateral-incisor resorption from ectopic canines is about 3× more common in girls (Ericson & Kurol 1988b, VERIFIED); the paper does not mention sex.
6. **Space.** The conventional pathway opens space during 6-9 months of alignment; whether space existed before early uncovering is not stated. Bazargani 2014 (VERIFIED) found arch perimeter lost after primary-canine extraction without a palatal arch.
7. **Era.** Woloshyn's patients were treated earlier than Schmidt's, with different brackets, wires and surgical habits.

## External validity: who it does NOT apply to

- **Adults and patients with complete canine roots.** Autonomous eruption depends on eruptive potential (Proffit Ch 15, VERIFIED in the notes); the paper's early protocol is for the late mixed dentition.
- **Canines with active lateral-incisor resorption.** Proffit (Ch 12 box, VERIFIED in the notes) says expose and move the canine away at once; a 6-8-month wait for free eruption may not be appropriate.
- **Ankylosed, dilacerated or transmigrated canines, and mandibular canines.** Not discussed. Ankylosis is called "low" in incidence, without a number (p 282).
- **Very high or deep palatal canines.** A dressing is mentioned only "occasionally" (p 283); what to do when the window closes over a deep crown is not discussed.
- **Interceptive extraction outside age 10-13, in crowded arches, or without space maintenance.** The paper sets no conditions. Williams' Class I uncrowded condition, the age window in Cobourne 2025, and the space loss in Bazargani 2014 (all VERIFIED) are missing.
- **Diagnosis by CBCT.** The paper predates routine small-field CBCT and relies on periapical tube shift, which gives relative, not exact, position (Eslami 2017, VERIFIED).
- **Labial impactions of incisors.** Becker's incisor data are used to support canine claims, not the reverse. The canine algorithm should not be assumed to transfer to incisors without checking.
- **Settings where the surgeon chooses the technique.** The whole argument assumes the orthodontist tells the surgeon which technique to use (p 278).

## Strengths

1. A clear, teachable four-criterion algorithm for labial and intra-alveolar canines. Each criterion comes with its reason: bone over the crown, gingiva after eruption, reintrusion after APF, and access over the lateral root.
2. One concrete threshold in the labial algorithm that a clinician can check: at least 2-3 mm of attached gingiva over the crown after eruption.
3. Biological reasoning that later evidence and Proffit share. Enamel has no resorbing cells, so after the follicle is removed the bone in the path must be removed surgically. Most "ankylosed" canines are under-uncovered canines.
4. An explicit force-direction rule: a vertical vector into the centre of the ridge (ballista loop), never a labial pull. It ties mechanics to the periodontal outcome (dehiscence, recession).
5. It names the mechanism of APF reintrusion: the flap heals to mucosa that is drawn coronally during eruption and pulls the crown back afterwards.
6. Good case illustrations, each mapped to a criterion (Fig 1 excision; Figs 2-3 APF; Fig 4 CE; Fig 5 early palatal uncovering).
7. It frames the uncovering decision as the orthodontist's responsibility, which is still the right message for a referral letter.
8. The open, free-eruption approach it promotes for palatal canines was later tested in RCTs (Björksved; Parkin, whose open-arm protocol is UNVERIFIED) and found equivalent to closed exposure. That makes it a reasonable option, even though it is not a superior one.

## What would have made it better

1. Label the evidence behind each recommendation (expert opinion, retrospective study, case series), and separate opinion from cited findings.
2. Quantify "significantly apical" (millimetres above the MGJ, or a landmark) and give a priority order when the criteria conflict.
3. Report the cited numbers: Ericson & Kurol's success rates by zone, Vermette's bone-level findings against the contralateral tooth, Woloshyn's identification rate and relapse, and the Schmidt thesis's sample, comparator and results.
4. Cite a canine source for "no reintrusion with CE", or state that Becker studied incisors.
5. Give indication criteria for early palatal uncovering: age, root development, depth, angulation, space, and whether the lateral root is resorbing.
6. Say what to do when an early-uncovered canine does not erupt in 6-8 months, and how often that happens.
7. Support or drop the treatment-time claim.
8. Include at least one failure or complication, so the reader sees the limits of each technique.
9. Correct "compared favorably" (p 283) and harmonise the above/below and apical/coronal wording (`conflicts.md` A1, A3).
10. Declare the author's link to refs 6, 7, 11 and 13.

## Journal-club one-liners

1. "This is Level 5 evidence: an expert's algorithm with five success stories and no data, and still the clearest decision rule we have for labial canines."
2. "Four questions before you refer: labial or mid-alveolus, above or below the MGJ, how much gingiva, and is the crown over the lateral root?"
3. "Excision only if the crown is coronal to the MGJ and you will still have 2-3 mm of attached gingiva after eruption."
4. "APF on a high canine buys you gingiva and costs you a long crown and vertical relapse; that is Vermette's photograph, not a periodontal index."
5. "Becker is the long-term evidence for closed eruption, but in central incisors."
6. "Ericson and Kurol's line is the midline of the lateral root, not its mesial surface, and the RCT number is 69 % against 39 %, not 'will'."
7. "Most 'ankylosed' palatal canines are under-uncovered: no follicle, no resorbing cells, so take the bone off down to the CEJ."
8. "Read p 283 with 'unfavorably': Woloshyn's judges could pick the dragged canine three times in four."
