# Key numbers — Kokich 2004 (surgical and orthodontic management of impacted maxillary canines)

Kokich VG. Surgical and orthodontic management of impacted maxillary canines. Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop 2004;126(3):278-83. doi:10.1016/j.ajodo.2004.06.009. Every number below was read from the page images (journal pages 278-283) and checked against the extracted text.

**What kind of paper this is.** This is a narrative symposium paper illustrated with five single-patient cases. It has **no tables, no sample, no measurements, no statistics, no P values and no SDs.** Every number in it is a clinical threshold, a timing, a count, or a figure Kokich cites from another study. The sections below therefore list the paper's stated numbers, decision rules and figures in place of results tables. Page numbers are journal pages. "Derived" means computed here from the printed text.

## Sample

None. The paper reports no patients of its own beyond the five illustrated cases (one patient per figure; ages, sex and follow-up not given). The only sample size printed is a cited one: Woloshyn n = 32 (p 283).

## Decision rules as printed (the paper's "protocol")

### Labial or intra-alveolar canine: the four criteria (pp 278-279)

| # | Criterion | Finding | What the paper says to do | Figure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Labiolingual position | Labial | Any of the 3 techniques (little if any bone over the crown) | Fig 2C |
| 1 | | Centre of the alveolus | Excision and APF "generally more difficult" (extensive labial bone removal) | Fig 4 |
| 2 | Vertical position vs MGJ | Most of the crown coronal to the MGJ | Any of the 3 techniques | Fig 1 |
| 2 | | Crown apical to the MGJ | Excision inappropriate (no labial gingiva after eruption) | Figs 2, 3 (as cited in the text) |
| 2 | | Crown significantly apical to the MGJ | APF also inappropriate (crown instability, possible reintrusion, ref 7); closed eruption gives adequate gingiva and no long-term reintrusion (ref 8) | Fig 4 |
| 3 | Amount of gingiva | Insufficient | APF is the only technique that predictably produces more gingiva | Fig 3 |
| 3 | | Enough to leave at least 2-3 mm attached after eruption | Any of the 3 techniques | — |
| 4 | Mesiodistal position | Crown mesial, over the lateral incisor root | APF (complete exposure needed to move it through the alveolus); closed eruption or excision generally not recommended | Fig 2 |

No cut-off separates "apical" from "significantly apical", and no order of priority is given when two criteria point to different techniques.

### Orthodontic mechanics (p 279)

| Situation | Mechanics | Reason given |
|---|---|---|
| Closed eruption (Fig 4) | Erupt into the centre of the alveolar ridge; ballista loop gives a vertical force vector (Fig 4E-G) | Mimics normal eruption; normal labial gingival relationships |
| Any labial impaction | Avoid mechanics that draw the tooth labially | Bony dehiscence, accelerated migration of the gingival margin, labial recession |
| APF for a crown mesial over the lateral root (Fig 2) | Elastomeric chain directed toward the centre of the edentulous ridge; tooth then moved distally (Fig 2E) | Guides the crown gradually into the arch |

No force magnitudes, wire sizes, attachment types or activation intervals are given.

### Palatal canine (pp 281-283)

| Step / pathway | As printed |
|---|---|
| Interception | Early extraction of the deciduous canine (Ericson & Kurol, ref 10); works if the crown on periapicals is over the lateral root but not past its mesial surface; does not work if well beyond it |
| Conventional uncovering | After the first 6-9 months of maxillary alignment; space created; surgeon uncovers; traction ("dragging") toward the edentulous site begins soon after surgery |
| Why it can stall | Crown often in intimate contact with the lingual surfaces of the central and lateral incisor roots; insufficient bone removal; after the follicle is removed, enamel has no cells to resorb bone; resorption occurs slowly through pressure necrosis |
| Ankylosis | Incidence "low" (ref 11); no number |
| Early uncovering (Kokich & Mathews, ref 11) | Before orthodontic treatment, in some cases in the late mixed dentition; full-thickness mucoperiosteal flap; all bone over the crown removed to the CEJ; flap repositioned with a hole over the crown; occasionally a dressing if the canine is high in the palate |
| Autonomous phase | Erupts without orthodontic force to the occlusal plane in about 6-8 months |
| Alignment | Bracket placed at the occlusal plane; root moved labially through bone as the crown is translated into the arch (Fig 5G) |

### Radiographic localisation (p 281)

| Item | As printed |
|---|---|
| Rule | Buccal object rule (Richards 1980, ref 9) |
| Films | 2 adjacent periapical radiographs taken at slightly different horizontal angles |
| Reading | Buccal object moves opposite to the beam; palatal crown moves in the same direction as the beam |
| Mnemonic | SLOB: same lingual, opposite buccal |

## Measurement method, reliability, statistics

None. No outcome is defined or measured, no reliability is reported, no sample-size calculation is made, and no test is used. The cited findings are reported without values: "no significant differences" (Vermette), "high predictability" (Ericson & Kurol), "low" (ankylosis), "improved" and "little to no" (Schmidt).

## Derived numbers

- Palatal : labial = 2 : 1, i.e. about 67 % vs 33 % (from 1/3 and 2/3).
- Figure panels: 4 + 6 + 4 + 8 + 8 = 30.
- Side of the impacted canine: 4 of 5 cases right (Figs 1, 3, 4, 5), 1 left (Fig 2); 4 labial or intra-alveolar, 1 palatal.
- References co-authored by Kokich: 3 of 13 (refs 6, 7, 11) = 23 %; with the University of Washington thesis (ref 13), 4 of 13 = 31 %.
- Reference span 1967-2004; 4 of 13 references (31 %) dated 2001 or later (refs 4, 8, 11, 13).
- Total treatment time for either palatal pathway: **cannot be derived**. The paper gives only the 6-9 months before conventional uncovering and the 6-8 months of autonomous eruption; neither traction time nor total time is printed, so the conclusion that overall time is reduced has no number behind it.
- Interceptive age gap: Williams 8-9 y (p 278) vs the Ericson & Kurol cohort aged 10-13 y (literature, VERIFIED-secondary) vs Proffit's about 10 y trigger (user's notes): Kokich's earliest age is 1-2 years below the trial ages.
