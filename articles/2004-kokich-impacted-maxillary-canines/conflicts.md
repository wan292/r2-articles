# Conflicts and disagreements — Kokich 2004

Use this file when you need to challenge a claim or a citation in Kokich VG. Surgical and orthodontic management of impacted maxillary canines. *Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop* 2004;126(3):278-83, doi:10.1016/j.ajodo.2004.06.009. It is a written-up symposium talk in the section "Advances in Orthodontics & Dentofacial Surgery", presented at the AAO/AAOMS Symposium (February 6-8, 2004) and "Submitted and accepted, June 2004" (p 278). It has 13 references and five single-patient cases (Figs 1-5, 30 panels, derived). It has **no tables, no statistics, no P values, no SDs and no data of its own**. Section A therefore checks the text against itself and against its own figure legends (all six page images, journal pp 278-283). Section B checks what the paper says its references found against those references, and against the trials that came later. C is the paper against Proffit, D lists what stays open and E is the verdict.

Page numbers are journal pages; "col 1" and "col 2" are the columns. Tags: **VERIFIED** = abstract read in this run's research; **VERIFIED-secondary** = number confirmed only through later papers citing it; **UNVERIFIED** = from memory or not checked. Proffit positions were checked against the user's study pages (wan292/proffit-study-pages, 7th-edition chapter numbering), not against the printed book. "The paper says", "the source says" and "my read" are used deliberately. Abbreviations: MGJ = mucogingival junction; APF = apically positioned flap; CE = closed eruption; PDC = palatally displaced canine; CEJ = cementoenamel junction.

**Checks that passed** (so you can say so).
- The 1/3 labial or intra-alveolar against 2/3 palatal split (p 278 col 1) fits "the most common impaction encountered by orthodontists is the palatal impaction" (p 281 col 2). The two ranking statements are compatible: the canine is the most common impaction after third molars (p 278), and the palatal canine is the most common from the orthodontist's point of view (p 281).
- Each "any of the 3 techniques" (criteria 1, 2 and 3) is conditional on the other criteria. The Fig 2C legend applies the criteria in exactly that sequence: labial, so any technique; but cusp tip above the MGJ and mesial, so APF.
- Fig 1 meets the three conditions for excision that its legend names (labial, coronal to the MGJ, sufficient gingiva), and Fig 1D shows the adequate zone of gingiva that the 2-3 mm rule aims at.
- "APF is the only technique that predictably produces more gingiva" and "CE will provide adequate gingiva" (both p 279 col 1) are compatible: APF adds tissue, CE preserves it.
- The SLOB rule is stated correctly: a buccal object moves opposite to the beam, a lingual object moves with it (p 281 col 1-2).
- The bone-removal mechanism (p 282 col 2: no resorbing cells in enamel) matches Fig 4D (bone removed around the crown in CE) and Fig 5C (palatal bone removed down to the CEJ).
- The Fig 5 legend follows the p 283 text step by step: flap, bone to the CEJ, hole in the flap, autonomous eruption, bracket at the occlusal plane, root moved labially.
- Woloshyn n = 32 (p 283) matches the published abstract (VERIFIED).

**How to raise these politely.** This is a 2004 symposium paper by one of the best-known names in interdisciplinary orthodontics. Lead with what holds: the four-criterion labial algorithm is clear, teachable and still matches Proffit and the systematic reviews. Then raise three points, each as a question.
- **A1.** "On p 283 I read 'compared favorably' as 'unfavorably', because the judges could pick the impacted side. Did I misread it?" Treat it as a likely typesetting slip.
- **A4 and A5.** "How far above the MGJ is 'significantly apical', and which criterion wins when they conflict?" This is a teaching question, not a criticism.
- **A9 and A10.** "What did the thesis compare against, and was treatment time measured?" Let the room bring up the 2007 publication and the trials.

Keep A2, A3, A6-A8, A11 and A12 as footnote-level remarks. Phrase nothing as an accusation: symposium papers are written as expert guidance, not as reports of data.

## B. Against other studies

### B0. Against the hub

No claim in `registry/claims.md` is confirmed or contradicted by this paper. The registry so far holds superimposition claims (Nielsen 1989, Doppel 1994, Nguyen 2018) and deep-bite claims (Khaleel 2026), and none of them concerns canines. The only canine mention anywhere in the hub is a discussion question in `articles/2018-nguyen-3d-mandibular-regional-superimposition/discussion-questions.md` about superimposing a CBCT taken for an impacted canine, and it makes no claim. The other paper in this presentation, Stephens 2005 (`2005-stephens-long-term-profile-extraction-nonextraction`), asks a different question (long-term soft-tissue profile after extraction and nonextraction treatment) and shares no claim with this one. The only link is a contrast in evidence type: Stephens reports matched groups with tables and P values, and Kokich reports none.

### B2. Interceptive extraction of the primary canine (later evidence)

- **Naoumova, Kurol & Kjellberg 2015, Part I**, Eur J Orthod 37:209-18 (VERIFIED). RCT, 67 children aged 10-13 (45 unilateral, 22 bilateral), CBCT at 0, 6 and 12 months, 24 months of observation. Spontaneous eruption 69 % after extraction against 39 % in controls: a difference of 30 percentage points, NNT about 3.3 (derived). Eruption time 15.6 ± 5.6 against 18.8 ± 5.8 months. No difference in resorption of adjacent teeth. **PARTIAL**: extraction works, but about 1 in 3 still fails, so "will result in normal eruption" (p 281) is too deterministic. "The randomised trial says extraction turns 39 % into 69 %, which is good, and not 'will'."
- **Naoumova et al 2015, Part II**, Eur J Orthod 37:219-29 (VERIFIED). Same RCT, predictors. The canine erupts without extraction if the cusp tip lies 11 mm from the midline, 2.5 mm from the arch plane, or at a mesioangular angle of 103°. It needs exposure despite extraction at 6 mm, 5 mm and 116°. **PARTIAL**: quantitative cut-offs where Kokich gives a qualitative "beyond the mesial surface" rule. "Naoumova gives the millimetres that Kokich's rule lacks."
- **Bazargani et al 2014**, Angle Orthod 84:3-10 (VERIFIED). Split-mouth RCT, 24 bilateral patients, 48 PDCs, mean age 11.6 ± 1.2 y, 18 months. Success 67 % against 42 %: 25 percentage points, NNT 4 (derived). Better in younger patients. Arch perimeter was lost on the extraction side, so a palatal arch is recommended. **PARTIAL**: agrees on the benefit and adds age dependence and space maintenance, which Kokich does not mention. "If you extract the C, hold the space; Bazargani lost arch perimeter without a palatal arch."
- **Cochrane CD004621** (Parkin et al 2009 and 2012; withdrawn 2018) (VERIFIED). Two RCTs, about 128 children, high risk of bias. The 2012 conclusion: no evidence of an effect in 10- to 13-year-olds, because trial reporting was inadequate. **CONFLICTS** with Kokich's certainty, but it reflects the quality of the evidence at the time, not proof that extraction fails. "Absence of evidence in 2012, not evidence of absence; the 2015 trials filled the gap."
- **Habib et al 2023**, Eur J Orthod 45:370-81 (VERIFIED). Meta-analysis of 5 RCTs, 238 patients. Canine position improves more after extraction; extraction plus headgear adds benefit; double extraction does not. Evidence moderate at best. **PARTIAL**. "Extraction helps; adding headgear helps more; extracting the D as well does not."
- **Araujo et al 2023**, Braz Oral Res (VERIFIED). Network meta-analysis, 9 RCTs, 506 patients, 730 PDCs. Every interceptive option beats no treatment. RME against extraction alone RR 3.07; extraction plus headgear RR 1.38. Low certainty. **PARTIAL**: extraction is not the only interceptive option. "Kokich offers one interception; the network meta-analysis ranks several."
- **Cobourne, Seehra & Papageorgiou 2025**, Br Dent J 239:463-70 (VERIFIED). Guideline with meta-analysis. Extraction is "unpredictable"; a favourable outcome needs age 10-13, the mixed dentition, space, and a crown **not beyond the lateral-root midline**. **PARTIAL**: confirms the midline threshold rather than the mesial-surface one. "The 2025 guideline keeps Ericson and Kurol's midline, adds age 10-13 and space, and calls the result unpredictable."
- **Ericson & Kurol 1988b**, AJODO 94:503-13 (VERIFIED). 40 resorbed lateral incisors against 118 ectopic canines without resorption. Resorption about 3× more common in girls; risk when the cusp lies medial to the midline of the lateral root; eruption path about 10° more mesial. **AGREES** on the danger zone, with the midline threshold again. "The same midline predicts both failure of self-correction and lateral-incisor resorption."

### B3. Early open exposure with autonomous eruption against closed exposure (palatal canines)

- **Parkin et al 2012**, J Oral Maxillofac Surg 70:2026-34 (VERIFIED). Multicentre UK RCT, n = 71. Operating time 34.3 against 34.3 min (P = .986); no difference in patient-reported outcomes. **Neutral**. "Open and closed exposure take the same time in theatre."
- **Parkin et al 2013**, AJODO 144:176-84 (VERIFIED). Same RCT, periodontal outcomes; 62 analysed (closed 29, open 33), 3 months after debond. Open against closed attachment level: MD 0.1 mm (95 % CI −0.2 to 0.5). Operated against contralateral side: 0.5 mm. Palatal recession on 20 of 62 operated sides against 4 of 62 unoperated sides. **CONFLICTS** with "periodontal results are superior" (p 283): no difference between techniques, and both cost a little attachment. Whether the open arm matched Kokich's pre-orthodontic timing is UNVERIFIED. "Open against closed differed by 0.1 mm of attachment; the surgery itself cost 0.5 mm either way."
- **Cochrane CD006966, Parkin et al 2017** (VERIFIED). 3 RCTs, 146 participants. Success RR 0.99 (95 % CI 0.93-1.06); no differences in esthetics, pain, periodontal health or treatment time; low-quality evidence. **CONFLICTS** with the superiority claim. "Cochrane 2017: open and closed are the same on every outcome measured."
- **Björksved et al 2018 and 2021** (two 2021 reports), Eur J Orthod (VERIFIED). Multicentre Swedish RCT, 119-120 patients aged 9-16. The open arm was the closest trial match to Kokich: flap, bone removal, glass ionomer over the crown and autonomous eruption. The closed arm had a chain under the flap. All canines were aligned. Total treatment time MD −0.1 months (95 % CI −3.2 to 2.9; P = .93). More pain in the closed group during traction. No clinically significant difference in root resorption or periodontal status. Costs equal. **CONFLICTS** with "overall treatment time is reduced" (p 283). Applicability is partial: exposure was not necessarily before orthodontic treatment in the late mixed dentition. "The Swedish trial timed it: open exposure with free eruption saved 0.1 of a month."
- **Björksved et al 2026**, Int Orthod 24:101104 (VERIFIED). Esthetic survey of 62 trial cases 4 years after debond. Exposed canines were identified at below chance overall; orthodontists rated closed-technique canines as more appealing (OR 4.3, 95 % CI 1.8-10.2). **PARTIAL**: agrees the treated side can be hard to spot, conflicts with esthetic superiority of the open approach. "Four years out nobody could pick the exposed canine, and orthodontists preferred the closed ones."
- **Færøvig et al 2025**, AJODO 167:382-98 (VERIFIED, partial abstract). Two-centre RCT, 92 analysed. More pain on VAS after open exposure (coefficient 8.58). **Neutral or against open**. "Open exposure hurt more after surgery in the Norwegian trial."
- **Aldoseri et al 2026**, Cureus (VERIFIED; lower-tier journal). Meta-analysis, 6 studies, 450 patients. Closed exposure: less pain on day 1 (SMD −0.39), but eruption time 3.37 months longer and surgery 5.09 min longer; no difference in recession. **PARTIAL**: faster eruption with the open method, but no total-time benefit shown. "Open erupts faster; whether the whole treatment ends sooner is a different question, and the RCT says no."

### B4. Labial uncovering technique

- **Incerti-Parenti et al 2016**, AJODO 149:463-72 (VERIFIED). Systematic review, 3 studies. Excisional uncovering: bleeding 29 % against 7 %, keratinised gingiva 2.6 against 4.1 mm. APF comparable to untreated teeth. No canine-only CE study exists; evidence insufficient. **PARTIAL**: supports the warning against excision when gingiva is limited (pp 278-279), and shows that Vermette mixed incisors with canines. "The systematic review backs Kokich's warning about excision, and finds no canine-only study of closed eruption."
- **Xenou et al 2025**, Eur Arch Paediatr Dent (VERIFIED). Systematic review, 7 studies. Excision had the worst soft-tissue outcomes, but not clinically significant; CE and APF similar to untreated teeth. **PARTIAL**: the APF's periodontal (not esthetic) outcome is acceptable. "Periodontally all three are close to untreated teeth; the difference Kokich cares about is crown length and relapse."

### B5. Localisation

- **Eslami et al 2017**, AJODO 151:248-58 (VERIFIED). Systematic review, 8 studies. Accuracy of localisation: CBCT 50-95 % against conventional radiography 39-85 %. Kappa between modalities 0.20-0.82; treatment-planning kappa 0.36-0.72. CBCT recommended for difficult cases. **PARTIAL** against "the exact labiolingual position" from the buccal object rule (p 281; A12): parallax is still first-line, and CBCT is preferred when the views are equivocal. "SLOB tells you which side; CBCT tells you where."

## D. Open questions

1. When the criteria conflict (a high canine, mesial over the lateral root, with little gingiva), which one wins: criterion 2 (APF inappropriate) or criteria 3 and 4 (APF the only option)? (A5)
2. How far apical to the MGJ is "significantly apical"? Is there a millimetre or a landmark, or is the deciding factor really intra-alveolar position, as the Fig 4 legend implies? (A4)
3. Where does the 2-3 mm of attached gingiva come from? No source is cited for it (p 279).
4. What age, dental stage or root development makes a palatal canine suitable for early uncovering, and what happens when the apex is already closed? (A8; Proffit Ch 15)
5. What did the Schmidt thesis compare against, with how many patients and over what follow-up, and is it the same sample as Schmidt & Kokich 2007? (A10, B1)
6. Was total treatment time ever measured for the early pathway? Fig 5E-F show appliances on the adjacent teeth during autonomous eruption. (A9)
7. What happens to an early-uncovered canine that does not erupt by itself within 6-8 months, and how often does that happen? The paper does not say.
8. When is the dressing used for a canine "high in the palate", and does the window in the flap re-cover? (p 283; not described)
9. Do the mesial-surface rule (Kokich) and the midline rule (Ericson & Kurol as cited by Proffit; Cobourne 2025) give different decisions in practice, and on which film? (A11, B2, C)
10. If lateral-incisor resorption is already present, does the 6-8-month wait for autonomous eruption still apply? (C, last row)
11. Was the Woloshyn sentence meant to read "unfavorably"? (A1)
12. Does the labial algorithm hold for canines alone? Vermette and Becker both include or are limited to incisors, and Incerti-Parenti 2016 found no canine-only CE study. (A6, B4)

## E. One-paragraph verdict

Read against itself, the paper is a clear, consistent piece of expert guidance with one meaning-reversing slip ("compared favorably", p 283, almost certainly "unfavorably"), a vertical rule without a threshold ("significantly apical"), no priority order when the four labial criteria conflict, and two concluding claims (shorter treatment, superior periodontal and esthetic results) that rest on no data in the paper and, for time, on no cited source at all. Read against its references, the labial message survives (Vermette's long crowns and vertical relapse after APF, Becker's normal crown length after CE), but with caveats the paper omits: both of Vermette's techniques lost bone against the contralateral tooth and its sample mixed incisors with canines, Becker studied central incisors, Ericson & Kurol's threshold is the lateral-root midline with 64 % success beyond it, Woloshyn named premolar rather than canine roots, and the Schmidt thesis, once published in 2007 with Kokich as co-author, reported shorter lateral roots, lower lateral-incisor bone and 78.9 % identification of the treated canine. Read against later evidence, interceptive extraction helps (69 % against 39 % in Naoumova's RCT, 67 % against 42 % in Bazargani's) but is not deterministic, and early open exposure with autonomous eruption is equivalent, not superior, to closed exposure for success (RR 0.99), attachment (MD 0.1 mm) and total treatment time (MD −0.1 months). Against Proffit it agrees on mechanism (no follicle, no bone resorption; erupt through attached gingiva) and differs in the details: CBCT over parallax films, the midline over the mesial surface, and moving the canine away at once when the lateral root is already resorbing. Use it for the four-criterion labial algorithm, the force direction and the bone-removal biology; present the palatal "earlier, faster, better" conclusion as the author's 2004 opinion that later randomised trials found to be no worse and no better than closed exposure.
