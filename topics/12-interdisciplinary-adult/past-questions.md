## Endodontic-orthodontic relationships a review of integrated treatment planning challenges

### Q1 · R2 exam 2019

> RCT during ortho

- **Recalled answer:** clean and shape and CaOH filling · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Clean and shape the canals, place interim calcium hydroxide (sealed coronally) during active movement, and obturate after orthodontics (Hamilton & Gutmann 1999, p351 (section and Conclusions on moving endodontically treated teeth))
- The review advises interim calcium hydroxide for a tooth needing RCT during ortho, with final obturation at the end. Note that Kindelan 2008 (topic 4) prefers definitive gutta-percha before prolonged movement.
- *Source tag in the recall:* R2 exam 2019 recall (typed list)

### Q3 · R2 exam 2019

> Regarding root resorption, tooth with RCT is
>
> - a. ↓ risk of root resorption
> - ↑ risk of root resorption

- **Recalled answer:** a · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a. Decreased risk of root resorption (Hamilton & Gutmann 1999, Abstract p343 and Conclusions p350 (Spurrier 1990; Mirabella & Artun 1995))
- The review concludes, though not conclusively, that root-filled teeth show less apical resorption, provided they are well obturated and free of coronal leakage. Kindelan 2008 (topic 4) reports no significant difference.

### Q4 · R2 exam 2019

> Ortho Tx causes pulpal changes; tooth with incomplete [root formation]
>
> - a. Tooth with incomplete [root] ↑ risk of pulpal changes
> - b. Tooth with incomplete [root] ↓ risk of pulpal changes

- **Per the paper:** b. A tooth with incomplete root formation has a decreased risk of pulpal changes (Hamilton & Gutmann 1999, Abstract p343 and pulp Conclusions p347)

### Q5 · R2 exam 2019

### Q7 · Promotion 2023 · Promotion 2024

### Q8 · Promotion 2023

> Case with a periapical showing tooth #21 with open apex and radiolucency with gutta-percha tracing, and an intraoral photo showing the patient has braces. What is the management?
>
> - a) Inactivate ortho treatment
> - b) Endo and ortho treatment

- **Per the paper:** b) Endodontic (calcium hydroxide / apexification) and continued orthodontic treatment (Hamilton & Gutmann 1999, p351 (RCT during ortho with interim calcium hydroxide during active movement; Anthony 1986 apexification during movement p349) and p353 (slow or halt movement only if inflammatory resorption appears))

Stopping force is advised only if inflammatory resorption appears. Kindelan (topic 4) also says a sinus tract needs endodontic referral.

### Q24 · R2 exam 2019

> [Exam-content note, item 6] Know 1-, 2-, 3-wall [periodontal] defects and what to do in each

- **Per the paper:** Two-wall crater: will not improve with ortho; maintain if shallow (4-5 mm pocket) or remove with osseous resective surgery before ortho. Three-wall intrabony defect: regenerative surgery (bone graft plus membrane) before ortho, starting once stable for 3-6 months. One-to-two-wall hemiseptal defect: corrected orthodontically (upright/erupt a tipped tooth, intrude a supererupted one) after inflammation is controlled, with no pre-ortho surgery. (Mathews & Kokich 1997, Preorthodontic Osseous Surgery p24-26 (Figs 4-6))
- The paper sorts defects by wall count into a treatment choice. Hamilton (t12_1, p356) adds that crestal bone formed during extrusion can reclaim 1-, 2- and 3-wall defects.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Handwritten exam-content note by reading item (sheet items 1-27, photo)

- The paper defines M/F as countermoment over force, equal to the distance to the equivalent force point, and gives the movement thresholds.

### Q41 · Growth + biomechanics list

> Direction of moment on the molar (figure: intrusion arch, 50 gm intrusive force at incisors, 20 mm lever, 1000 gm-mm at molar).

- **Per the paper:** Tip-back moment (crown distal, roots mesial) of 1000 g-mm at the molar, with a 50 g extrusive force (Liaw et al. 2024, Fig 2C p592)
- In a one-couple intrusion arch, the incisor intrusive force times the lever length (50 g x 20 mm) appears at the molar tube as a tip-back moment. The paper stresses that this tip-back moment is large.

### Q42 · Growth + biomechanics list

> Intrusion of molar (figure-based, same one-couple intrusion-arch figure).

- **Per the paper:** The molar does not intrude; it receives an extrusive reaction force (plus tip-back), while intrusion occurs at the incisors (Liaw et al. 2024, Fig 2C p592)
- The intrusive force on the anterior segment is balanced by an equal extrusive force on the posterior anchor. The paper notes the posterior segment may extrude, which is why miniscrews are preferred in high-angle cases.
