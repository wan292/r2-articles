## Ghoneima2011

### Q1 · Promotion 2024

> Which suture will open during expansion?
>
> - a. Pterygomaxillary
> - b. Zygomaticotemporal
> - c. Zygomaticomaxillary
> - d. Nasofrontal

- **Recalled answer:** d. Nasofrontal · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** d. Nasofrontal (frontonasal) (t14_1 Abstract; Results p516-517; Table I p518 (frontonasal +0.2 mm, P<0.001))
- Ghoneima's CT data show significant widening of the frontonasal, internasal, nasomaxillary, frontomaxillary and intermaxillary sutures. The pterygomaxillary, zygomaticomaxillary, zygomaticotemporal and frontozygomatic sutures did not change significantly, which the authors put down to heavy interdigitation.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 15, 2 Effects of rapid maxillary expansion on the cranial and circummaxillary sutures, Ahmed Ghoneima

### Q2 · Promotion 2021

> 8-year-old patient treated with Hyrax expansion and was instructed to activate twice daily. What's the expected adverse effect?

- **Recalled answer:** Nasal distortion (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Nasal effects: the nasal-bone sutures open (internasal 0.6 mm, nasomaxillary 0.4 mm, frontonasal 0.2 mm). The paper calls this nasal widening; calling it 'nasal distortion' or an adverse effect is not stated in the paper. (t14_1 Methods p512 (2 quarter turns twice daily, Hyrax, ages 8-15); Table I p518; Discussion p517)
- Ghoneima's Hyrax protocol matches the stem: twice-daily activation in children aged 8-15. CT showed that the forces open the anterior and nasal sutures, while the zygomatic and pterygomaxillary sutures stay closed. The authors read the nasal changes as a wider nasal cavity rather than a harm. 'Nasal distortion' is the closest option, but the label comes from the exam (and possibly Proffit), not from this paper.

## Long-term dental arch changes after rapid maxillary expansion treatment a systematic review.

### Q3 · Promotion 2023

> Lateral ceph showing CVM stage 5 or 6 (not sure), asking about the effect of expansion to treat posterior crossbite. Which statement is true?

- **Recalled answer:** Increased maxillary and mandibular intermolar width · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Increased maxillary and mandibular intermolar width (mainly dentoalveolar after the pubertal peak) (t14_2 Results 'Cephalometric radiographs assessment (frontal view)' p158; Discussion p159)
- The review summarises Baccetti's PA-ceph study. The later-maturing group had significant gains in both maxillary and mandibular intermolar width compared with controls. The early group gained only maxillary width. After the peak, expansion is mostly dentoalveolar rather than skeletal, which fits a patient at CS5-6.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; annotated '15, 3' with Lagravere 2005 systematic review abstract pasted

### Q4 · Promotion 2023

> Effect of RME?

- **Recalled answer:** No vertical nor anterior-posterior effect · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** No anteroposterior or vertical dental changes (long term) (t14_2 Conclusions p159; Results (lateral ceph, Garib 2001) p158)
- Lagravere concluded that RME had no long-term AP or vertical dental effects, based on a lateral-ceph study that found no molar extrusion or incisor change. Mucedero (t14_5) notes that short-term studies report the maxilla moving down and the mandible rotating back after RME. The 'no vertical effect' therefore applies to long-term dental outcomes.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles

### Q5 · Promotion 2024

> Two siblings presented to the clinic and will undergo expansion using Haas. What is the expected treatment outcome?
>
> - a. More nasal and lateronasal expansion is expected in older sibling
> - b. More intermolar expansion is expected in older sibling
> - c. More intermolar expansion is expected in younger sibling

- **Per the paper:** b. More intermolar expansion is expected in the older sibling (t14_2 Results p158 (early 12.7 vs late 13.5 mm Um-Um) and Discussion/Conclusions p159 (0.8 mm difference; skeletal before peak, dentoalveolar after))
- Baccetti's Haas data, as reported in the review, show slightly more transverse dental change after puberty than before. The difference is about 0.8 mm and may not matter clinically. Skeletal expansion was larger in the younger, prepubertal group, which makes option a wrong.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 15, 3 Long-Term Dental Arch Changes After Rapid Maxillary Expansion Treatment: A Systematic Review; 15, 4 Rapid Maxillary Expansion - Tooth Tissue-Borne Versus Tooth-Borne Expanders: A Computed Tomography Evaluation of Dentoskeletal Effects, Daniela G. Garib; handwritten '?' after stem

### Q6 · Promotion 2021

> Retention for RPE is?

- **Recalled answer:** 4 months · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** About 3-4 months of passive retention (the SR gives 2-4 months; Ghoneima and Garib both used 3 months) (t14_2 Discussion p159 (retention period 2-4 months); t14_3 Methods p549 and t14_1 Methods p512 (3 months))
- Lagravere's review puts the usual post-RME retention at 2 to 4 months. The two CT studies in this topic each kept the expander in place for 3 months, so the recalled 4 months is within the range. Mucedero (t14_5) is an outlier at 8 months or more because of the open-bite protocol. Proffit's 3-4 months is UNVERIFIED.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)

### Q7 · Promotion 2021

> Patient came after orthodontic treatment and expansion after 10 years. What is the expected outcome?

- **Recalled answer:** Increased transverse arch (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** A lasting increase in transverse arch width: maxillary molar about 3.7-4.8 mm and canine about 2.2-2.5 mm, plus arch-perimeter gain in adolescents (maxillary 6 mm, mandibular 4.5 mm) (t14_2 Results pp157-158 and Conclusions p159)
- Against untreated controls, the systematic review found clinically meaningful residual width gains years after RME followed by fixed appliances. The follow-ups lasted about 5-8 years, not 10. Part of the perimeter gain may come from the fixed-appliance phase rather than from RME alone.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25); flagged *** in source

### Q8 · Promotion 2023

> Amount of nasal (floor) expansion if you activate the screw 9 mm?
>
> - a) 1-2.5 mm
> - b) 3-4.5 mm

- **Recalled answer:** b) 3-4.5 mm (underlined; pasted Garib CT conclusions: transverse increase at nasal floor = one-third to one-half of screw activation) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b) 3-4.5 mm (one-third to one-half of a 9 mm activation) (t14_3 Discussion p555 and Conclusions p556; Table 1 (NF +2.4 mm molar, +2.5 mm premolar for 7 mm screw))
- In the CT study, nasal-floor widening was on average about a third of screw activation, and some patients exceeded half. Applied to 9 mm, that gives 3 to 4.5 mm. Note the internal inconsistency: the abstract gives only 'one-third', while the conclusions give 'one-third to one-half'.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; annotated '15, 4'

### Q9 · Promotion 2024

> You decided to expand using a banded expander. Which tooth will have the most tipping?
>
> - a. Canine
> - b. 1st premolar
> - c. 2nd premolar
> - d. 1st molar

- **Recalled answer:** c. 2nd premolar · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** c. 2nd premolar (t14_3 Table 5 p554 (P2 6.7°, P1 2.3°, M1 2.5°); Discussion p556; Conclusions p557)
- The second premolars are not banded and are pushed only by the connecting bar, far from their centre of resistance. They therefore tipped buccally about 6.7°, roughly three times as much as the banded first premolars and first molars. This held in both the Haas and Hyrax groups.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 15, 4 Rapid Maxillary Expansion - Tooth Tissue-Borne Versus Tooth-Borne Expanders: A Computed Tomography Evaluation of Dentoskeletal Effects, Daniela G. Garib

### Q10 · Promotion 2024

> Expected outcomes of treating a young girl with Haas?
>
> - a. Molar expansion by 5.5 mm
> - b. Premolar expansion by 5.5 mm
> - c. Less stable
> - d. Buccal displacement of the palatal shelves

- **Recalled answer:** d. Buccal displacement of the palatal shelves · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** d. Buccal displacement of the palatal shelves (lateral rotation of the maxillary halves) (t14_3 Discussion p555 (lateral rotation of maxillary halves, fulcrum near frontomaxillary suture); Table 2 p553 (Haas group))
- Garib describes the hemimaxillae rotating outward, so the hard palate widened along with the teeth. The 5.5 mm figure in Table 2 is the buccal alveolar crest width at the molar, not molar (dental) expansion, which was about 7-8 mm. Garib also refutes the claim that one appliance is less stable. Option a is a plausible trap if the examiner read 5.5 mm as molar expansion.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 15, 4 Rapid Maxillary Expansion - Tooth Tissue-Borne Versus Tooth-Borne Expanders: A Computed Tomography Evaluation of Dentoskeletal Effects, Daniela G. Garib

### Q11 · Promotion 2022

> Patient is a mouth breather with constricted maxilla; which treatment do you recommend?
>
> - a. Haas, 3 months retention
> - b. Hyrax, 6 months retention

- **Recalled answer:** b. Hyrax 6 month retention (annotation 'Biedermann') · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** a. Haas, 3 months retention (not stated in the paper as a recommendation). The 3-month retention is what Garib used, and Haas and Hyrax gave similar orthopedic and nasal-floor effects. (t14_3 Abstract, Methods p549 (3-month retention), Tables 2-4 (NF changes), Discussion p556)
- Garib found no orthopedic advantage for either appliance: nasal-floor width rose about one-third of the screw opening with both. So appliance type does not decide the question, and the retention period does. The papers in this topic support about 3 months (2-4 months in the review), not 6. The recalled answer (Hyrax, 6 months) is not backed by the topic's papers, so this question should be treated as uncertain.
- *Source tag in the recall:* annotation 'Biedermann'

### Q12 · Promotion 2021

> 11-year-old patient came with bilateral crossbite. What's the ideal treatment?
>
> - Hyrax retained for 3 months
> - Haas retained for 6 months
> - Quadhelix

- **Recalled answer:** Hyrax retained for 3 months (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Hyrax retained for 3 months (t14_3 Methods p549 (3-month retention, girls aged 11-14); Table 4 and Conclusions p556-557)
- Garib treated girls aged 11-14 with bilateral or unilateral crossbite and kept the appliance for 3 months. Hyrax and Haas gave similar orthopedic results. Haas tipped the anchor first premolars more, and the Hyrax is easier to keep clean. Ghoneima and Marshall also use the Hyrax with about 3 months of retention. A quad-helix is mainly a dental expander.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

## Early Transverse Treatment.

### Q13 · Promotion 2021

> 5-year-old patient came into clinic with bilateral posterior crossbite, what is the appropriate treatment?

- **Recalled answer:** W-arch · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** W-arch (Porter W) or another fixed expander if treating now. The paper itself prefers to defer expansion to the early mixed dentition; at age 5 it does only occlusal adjustment for a functional shift. (t14_4 Treatment section, 'When should early treatment for crossbite begin?' and 'How should crossbites be treated?' (pp134-135))
- Marshall et al name the Porter W appliance as a fixed option that reliably opens the suture. For 4 mm or less of expansion they rate it as effective as a quad-helix or RME. They also advise postponing correction until the first permanent molars erupt because young children cooperate poorly and relapse. W-arch fits as the appliance choice (also Proffit's primary-dentition choice, UNVERIFIED), but the paper's timing advice is to wait.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)
