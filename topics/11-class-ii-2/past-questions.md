## Cacciatore2014

### Q1 · Promotion 2023

> Effect of Forsus?

- **Recalled answer:** Significant restriction of maxilla (not sure) (highlighted) · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** Class II correction is mainly dentoalveolar. The maxilla shows only a slight, temporary 'headgear effect' (SNA -1.7° at end of treatment) that relapses, so there is no significant skeletal change long term (Cacciatore 2014, Abstract (p1010); Table 2 (p1013); Table 3 (p1014); Table 4 (p1015); Discussion and Conclusions (p1016))
- Restriction of the maxilla was significant only over the active-treatment interval. SNA relapsed +1.4° afterwards, leaving a non-significant -0.3° net change at about 2 years post-treatment. The authors' take-home message is a mainly dentoalveolar effect with only a slight headgear effect, so 'significant restriction of maxilla' is true only for the treatment period.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; annotated '11, 5' with Cacciatore 2014 abstract pasted

### Q2 · Promotion 2024

> What is true regarding Forsus?
>
> - a. Minimal dento-alveolar effect
> - b. Significant restriction of maxilla
> - c. Minimal effect on maxilla

- **Recalled answer:** c. Minimal effect on maxilla · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** c. Minimal effect on maxilla (the paper supports this as the long-term answer; b is true only for the active-treatment period) (Cacciatore 2014, Abstract (p1010); Table 2 (p1013), Table 4 (p1015); Conclusions (p1016))
- Over the full observation the Forsus corrected Class II mainly dentoalveolarly: overjet -3.8 mm and molar relationship +3.7 mm, with no significant skeletal change (net SNA -0.3°, NS). The maxillary restraint seen at the end of treatment (SNA -1.7°) was described as a slight headgear effect and largely relapsed. Option a is clearly wrong, but the question is ambiguous between b and c because the abstract does call the treatment-period maxillary restriction significant.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 11, 5 Treatment and posttreatment effects induced by the Forsus appliance: A controlled clinical study, Giorgio Cacciatore

### Q3 · Promotion 2021

> Photo of class II elastics. What is the relevant factor?
>
> - Depends on patient's compliance
> - Vertical intrusion forces that can change occlusal plane

- **Recalled answer:** Depends on patient's compliance (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Depends on patient's compliance (Cacciatore 2014, Introduction (p1010) and last Discussion paragraph (p1016); see also George 2021 Discussion p340 on elastic side effects)
- Cacciatore says the Forsus effects look like those of Class II elastics, with one main difference: the Forsus does not depend on patient compliance and elastics do. The other option is wrong because Class II elastics extrude teeth (molars and upper incisors) rather than intrude them; George notes molar extrusion and lower incisor proclination as their side effects.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

## Janson2009

### Q4 · Promotion 2023

> Case scenario asking about the difference between option 1 (upper 4's extraction) vs option 2 (upper and lower 4's extraction) in Class II.
>
> - a) Option 1 has better molar stability
> - b) Option 1 has more increase in apical base

- **Recalled answer:** b) Option 1 has more increase in apical base (highlighted and underlined; abstract annotated 'Wits appraisal' and 'SNA') · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b) Option 1 (upper 4s only) has more increase in apical base (Janson 2009, Abstract (p154.e1); Table IV (p154.e6): SNA +1.90 vs +0.39°, P = 0.002; ANB +1.08 vs +0.06°, P = 0.025; Discussion p154.e6-e8)
- After treatment, the 2-premolar group showed more forward maxillary displacement (SNA) and a larger increase in the apical-base discrepancy (ANB) than the 4-premolar group. Note that Wits did not differ (P = 0.706), despite the 'Wits' annotation. Option a is not clean either: the 4-premolar group did relapse more in molar relationship (P = 0.038), but this did not hold in the subgroups, and the authors conclude overall stability was similar.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; annotated '10, 5' with Janson 2009 abstract pasted

### Q5 · Promotion 2024

> Which is correct regarding U4s vs U&L4s extraction?
>
> - a. U4s is more stable long term
> - b. U&L4s is more stable long term
> - c. U4s has more increase in apical base
> - d. U&L4s has more increase in apical base

- **Recalled answer:** c. U4s has more increase in apical base · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** c. U4s has more increase in apical base (Janson 2009, Abstract (p154.e1); Table IV (p154.e6): ANB +1.08 vs +0.06°, P = 0.025; Conclusions (p154.e9))
- Janson found similar long-term stability for the 2- and 4-premolar protocols, so options a and b are wrong. The 2-maxillary-premolar group had more post-treatment forward maxillary displacement and a greater increase in the apical-base relationship (ANB), which the authors call too small to matter clinically.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 10, 5 Long-term stability of Class II malocclusion treated with 2- and 4-premolar extraction protocols, Guilherme Janson

## Keys to Class II correction.

### Q6 · Promotion 2023 · Promotion 2024

> 15-year-old with Class II div 1 with increased overjet and mild crowding in the lower arch, lower second molar not erupted yet. What is the best extraction pattern?
>
> - a) Upper 4's
> - b) U4's and L4's
> - c) U4's and L5's
> - d) U5's and L4's

- **Recalled answer:** c) U4's and L5s · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** c) U4's and L5's (George 2021, Methods inclusion criteria (p334); Table V (p337); Discussion p337 and p340; Conclusions (p341))
- For a growing Class II div 1 with mild crowding and acceptable lower incisor position, George shows that 4/5 extraction protracts the lower molars more (+0.7 mm) and retracts the lower incisors less, which helps the Class II molar correction without flattening the profile. Neither pattern changed the mandibular plane angle (no 'wedge effect'). Janson 2009 would argue for upper 4s only when lower crowding is minimal, but the wording follows George's sample.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles

### Q7 · Promotion 2022

> Patient with Class II; two extraction patterns offered: all 4s, or upper 4s and lower 5s. What is true?
>
> - a. no difference between the two patterns
> - b. all-4s extraction distalizes lower central incisors more

- **Recalled answer:** b. All 4s Exo distalize lower centrals more · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b. All-4s (4/4) extraction distalizes (retracts) the lower central incisors more (George 2021, Abstract (p333); Table V (p337): L1t 3.37 vs 1.81 mm, P = 0.001; Discussion p337)
- Taking out lower first premolars retracted the lower incisors about 1.5-1.6 mm more than taking out lower second premolars, and this held after adjusting for starting incisor position. So 'no difference' is wrong. The lower molars moved forward 0.7 mm more with 4/5 (P = 0.041).

### Q8 · Promotion 2021

> Patient with class II molar relationship, and can be treated with either extraction of upper and lower first premolars or upper first premolars and lower second premolars. What is the difference?
>
> - Extraction of first would result in more distal incisal movement
> - No significant difference

- **Recalled answer:** Extraction of first would result in more distal incisal movement (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Extraction of first premolars (4/4) gives more distal (retractive) incisor movement, significant for the mandibular incisors (George 2021, Abstract (p333); Table V (p337); Discussion p337-338; Conclusion 1 (p341))
- Lower incisors were retracted about 1.6 mm more after 4/4 than 4/5 extraction (3.37 vs 1.81 mm, P = 0.001), and the gap held after ANCOVA. The upper incisors also went back about 1.4 mm more with 4/4, but that difference was not significant (P = 0.057), so the 'significant difference' answer is about the lower incisors.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)
