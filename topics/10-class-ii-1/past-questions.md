## Burkhardt2003

### Q1 · Promotion 2023

> Pendulum vs Herbst?

- **Recalled answer:** Herbst leads to more projection of chin (highlighted and underlined) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Herbst gives slightly more anterior chin (pogonion) projection than the pendulum, with no significant difference in mandibular length (Burkhardt 2003, Abstract p108; Table IV p112 (Pog to N-perp and SNB significantly different, Co-Gn NS); Clinical significance p114; Conclusions p115)
- At the end of comprehensive treatment both Herbst groups had pogonion slightly further forward than the pendulum group, even though mandibular length grew by a similar amount (~6 mm) in all three groups. The authors put the difference down to the pendulum group's slight downward-backward mandibular rotation, which hid its growth at the chin.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; annotated '10,4'

### Q2 · Promotion 2024

> Patient presented for treatment of his Class II malocclusion. You presented two options, Herbst and Pendulum appliances. Which one of the following is correct regarding the intra-arch appliance?
>
> - a. Provides less mandibular growth than Herbst
> - b. Provides more mandibular growth than Herbst
> - c. Less increase in mandibular length
> - d. Increase in mandibular plane angle exceeds Herbst

- **Recalled answer:** d. Increase in mandibular plane angle exceeds herbst · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** d. Increase in mandibular plane angle exceeds Herbst (Burkhardt 2003, Abstract p108; Results p112 and Table IV p112 (MPA +1.2° pendulum vs slight closure in both Herbst groups, P<.01; Co-Gn 6.2 vs 6.4 vs 6.4 mm, NS); Conclusions p115)
- Over the full treatment the pendulum group's mandibular plane angle opened by about 1.2°, while both Herbst groups closed slightly. The difference was significant. Mandibular length gains did not differ significantly among the three groups, so options a, b and c (less or more growth or length than Herbst) are not supported.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 10, 4 Maxillary molar distalization or mandibular enhancement: A cephalometric comparison of comprehensive orthodontic treatment including the pendulum and the Herbst appliances, Donald R. Burkhardt

### Q3 · Promotion 2021

> Mother came with her male child and her child needs growth modification for mandible. Mother doesn't understand what this means. How best to explain the treatment?
>
> - Accelerated growth of mandible in a given time
> - More mandibular growth than normal

- **Per the paper:** Accelerated growth of mandible in a given time (Burkhardt 2003, Discussion 'Skeletal changes' p113 (Herbst mandibular growth 4.6 / 4.5 mm in phase I vs 1.8 / 2.3 mm in phase II; overall Co-Gn gain 6.4 mm Herbst vs 6.2 mm pendulum, NS, Table IV p112); Abstract p108)
- Burkhardt found that the Herbst groups' extra mandibular growth was packed into the active phase, after which growth slowed, so overall mandibular length ended up no greater than with the pendulum. That means growth was sped up, not increased. Siara-Olds 2010 agrees: growth surged during functional treatment, then caught down ('mortgage' on growth, p23). The accurate thing to tell the mother is that the appliance speeds up jaw growth over a given period. It does not promise a bigger jaw than nature would give.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)

## Cozza2006

### Q4 · Promotion 2024

> 12-year-old boy with Class II malocclusion; you decided to treat him with a functional appliance. Which of the following is correct?
>
> - a. Short-term amount of supplementary mandibular growth
> - b. Long-term amount of supplementary mandibular growth

- **Recalled answer:** a. Short term amount of supplementary mandibular growth · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a. Short-term amount of supplementary mandibular growth (is significantly larger when treatment includes the pubertal growth spurt) (Cozza 2006, Abstract p599.e1; Discussion p599.e9–e10 (all 'peak' samples ≥2 mm except Tümer, no prepeak sample clinically significant); Conclusion 3 p599.e11; only 1 of 22 studies (Faltin) followed patients to the end of growth, p599.e11)
- The review says functional appliances produce supplementary mandibular growth in the short term, and that it is larger when treatment covers the pubertal peak (a 12-year-old boy is close to that window). It cannot speak to long-term supplementary growth, because only one included study followed patients to the end of growth.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 11, 1 Mandibular changes produced by functional appliances in Class II malocclusion: A systematic review, Paola Cozza

### Q5 · Promotion 2024

> Patient was treated using Twin-block appliance, wearing it full time for one year. The orthopedic increase will be:
>
> - a. 0.88
> - b. 1.88
> - c. 2.8
> - d. 2.88

- **Recalled answer:** c. 2.8 · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** c. 2.8 (derived: 0.23 mm/month × 12 months = 2.76 mm) (Cozza 2006, Results p599.e6 and Discussion p599.e11 (Twin-block coefficient of efficiency 0.23 mm/month from 7 samples; Herbst 0.28, bionator 0.17, activator 0.12, Fränkel 0.09, average 0.16 mm/month); Table V p599.e10)
- Cozza defines the coefficient of efficiency as the extra mandibular length over controls divided by months of active treatment. For the Twin-block this was 0.23 mm per month, so one year of full-time wear predicts about 2.76 mm, which rounds to 2.8 mm. The 12-month multiplication is derived; the paper itself reports only the per-month figure. Siara-Olds' Twin Block figure (~1.5 mm/year over controls) does not match any of the options.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 11, 1 Mandibular changes produced by functional appliances in Class II malocclusion: A systematic review, Paola Cozza (Twin-block coefficient of efficiency 0.23 mm/month; handwritten 0.23 x 12 = 2.76); 11, 4 Long-Term Dentoskeletal Changes with the Bionator, Herbst, Twin Block, and MARA Functional Appliances, Nicole J. Siara-Olds

## Siara-olds2010

### Q6 · Promotion 2023

> What is true in comparing four functional appliances (Siara-Olds article)?

- **Recalled answer:** Change of occlusal plane in Herbst and Twin Block (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** The occlusal plane changed significantly in the Herbst and Twin Block groups (abstract result 3) (Siara-Olds 2010, Abstract Results item 3 p18; Table 3 OccPlane-SN p22 (Herbst T2–T1 P<.0001; Twin Block T3–T2 P=.05; MARA T2–T1 P=.05); Conclusions p28)
- The recalled answer is exactly the abstract's third result. The paper contradicts itself, though: its Results text and Conclusions say Herbst and MARA gave the steeper occlusal plane, while the Twin Block's occlusal-plane change appears only after phase II. Other statements about the four appliances that would also count as true: MARA temporarily restricted maxillary growth, Twin Block had the best vertical control and the most lower-incisor flaring, and no group differed from controls in the long term.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; annotated '11, 4' with Siara-Olds 2010 abstract pasted

### Q7 · Promotion 2024

> Question asking about the effect of the MARA appliance:
>
> - a. Minimum constriction of maxilla
> - b. Results in steep mandibular plane
> - c. Greatest vertical control
> - d. Proclination of lower incisors

- **Recalled answer:** b. Results in steep mandibular plane · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b. Results in steep mandibular plane (best option; the paper actually says Herbst and MARA gave a steeper occlusal plane, and MARA opened SN-GoGn vs controls during phase I) (Siara-Olds 2010, Conclusions p28 (Herbst and MARA restricted maxillary growth and steepened the occlusal plane); Table 3 p22 (MARA SN-GoGn +0.93°/yr vs controls T2–T1, P=.01; IMPA +2.46°/yr, P=.0002); Discussion p28 (vertical increase explains MARA's SNB decrease; incisor flaring temporary))
- For MARA the paper reports temporary maxillary restriction, a steeper occlusal plane, and a phase-I increase in vertical dimension (SN-GoGn) that it blames for the drop in SNB, which fits option b. Options c (greatest vertical control) and d (greatest long-term lower-incisor flaring) describe the Twin Block. MARA's lower-incisor proclination was significant in phase I but had resolved by the end of fixed appliances. Option a contradicts the reported maxillary restriction. One caution: the conclusions say occlusal plane, not mandibular plane.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 11, 4 Long-Term Dentoskeletal Changes with the Bionator, Herbst, Twin Block, and MARA Functional Appliances, Nicole J. Siara-Olds
