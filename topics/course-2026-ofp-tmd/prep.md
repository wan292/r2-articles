# OFP & TMD course · Quick prep

> For the road. Sources are the four readings plus the checks listed on the Course map. **UNVERIFIED** = not checked against a source.

## The 60-second version

- **Occlusion is a weak, mostly non-causal TMD factor.** No orthodontic approach is proven to cause, prevent or cure TMD (Michelotti 2025). Adolescent orthodontics neither raises nor lowers later TMD risk (McNamara 1995).
- **Okeson's orthopedic stability** = teeth in MIP while the condyles sit in the musculoskeletally stable position. A sound goal, not a promise of TMD prevention (Okeson 2018).
- **TMD is a pain condition.** Chronic means ≥3 months (BMJ 2023) or over 3 months (ICD-11). Chronic TMD pain affects 6–9 % of adults (Busse 2023). In Jeddah, 27.2 % of 10–18-year-olds had an RDC/TMD diagnosis (Al-Khotani 2016).
- **Diagnose with DC/TMD.** Familiar pain on palpation (1 kg for 2 s) or on movement. Assisted opening under 40 mm with a history of locking that interfered with eating = disc displacement without reduction, with limited opening (Schiffman 2014).
- **Manage by BMJ 2023.** Education, jaw exercise, manual therapy and CBT first. Reversible splints: conditional against. Irreversible splints: strong against (Busse 2023).
- **Before orthodontics:** screen (3Q/TMD), score (Jbara: 0–3 / 4–6 / ≥7), treat painful TMD first, record the baseline, pause active forces if TMD flares (Jbara 2026; Michelotti 2018).

## DC/TMD hands-on cheat card

Step order follows the DC/TMD examination form (order UNVERIFIED).

| Step | What to do | Measure or threshold | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Screen | TMD Pain Screener (6 items) or 3Q/TMD | Screener sensitivity and specificity ≥0.95. 3Q/TMD (adults 20–69): 74 % of positives vs 16 % of negatives met DC/TMD pain or dysfunction criteria (DDwR and DJD excluded) | Schiffman 2014; Lövgren 2016 |
| 1. Pain location | Patient points; examiner confirms jaw, temple, ear area | History must show pain modified by jaw movement, function or parafunction | Schiffman 2014 |
| 2. Incisal relationship | Record vertical overlap, horizontal overlap, midline | Add the vertical overlap to every opening value | Schiffman & Ohrbach 2016 |
| 3. Opening pattern | Straight, corrected or uncorrected deviation | Deviation is no longer a criterion for disc displacement without reduction | Schiffman & Ohrbach 2016 |
| 4. Opening | Pain-free, maximum unassisted, maximum assisted (passive stretch); ask "pain? familiar pain?" each time | Assisted <40 mm + locking that interfered with eating = DDwoR with limited opening (sensitivity 0.80, specificity 0.97); ≥40 mm = without limited opening. Normal opening about 40–55 mm (UNVERIFIED) | Schiffman 2014; Schiffman & Ohrbach 2016 |
| 5. Lateral and protrusive | Measure in mm; ask about pain | Familiar TMJ pain on any of these counts toward arthralgia | Schiffman & Ohrbach 2016 |
| 6. Joint noises | Patient reports; examiner listens and palpates on opening, closing, lateral, protrusive | Click on opening and closing (or one of them plus a lateral or protrusive click) = DDwR (0.34, 0.92). Crepitus = DJD (0.55, 0.61); fine vs coarse not separated | Schiffman 2014; Schiffman & Ohrbach 2016 |
| 7. Locking | Closed lock needing a manoeuvre to open; open lock needing one to close | Intermittent closed lock + click = DDwR with intermittent locking (0.38, 0.98). Open lock = subluxation (0.98, 1.00) | Schiffman & Ohrbach 2016 |
| 8. Muscle palpation | Temporalis and masseter, 1 kg for 2 s; hold 5 s to look for spreading or referred pain | Familiar pain = myalgia (0.90, 0.99). Pain beyond the muscle = myofascial pain with referral (0.86, 0.98) | Schiffman 2014 |
| 9. TMJ palpation | Lateral pole 0.5 kg; around the lateral pole 1 kg | Familiar pain = arthralgia (0.89, 0.98) | Forces: DC/TMD exam form and protocol, seen only in search snippets (PDFs not opened); lateral aspect only: Schiffman & Ohrbach 2016; accuracy: Schiffman & Ohrbach 2016 |
| 10. Headache | Familiar temple headache on temporalis palpation or jaw movement | Headache attributed to TMD (0.89, 0.87); needs a pain-related TMD as well | Schiffman & Ohrbach 2016 |
| 11. Axis II | GCPS, pain drawing, JFLS-8, PHQ-4, OBC | PHQ-4 over 6 = watch, over 9 = assess further or refer. GCPS intensity ≥50/100 = high; grade 3–4 = disability | Schiffman 2014 |
| 12. Imaging | Only for significant mechanical problems, suspected pathology, or failed TMD treatment | MRI for disc; CT for bone. MRI catches only 59 % of DJD seen on CT; panoramic 26 % | Schiffman & Ohrbach 2016 |

Accuracy figures are sensitivity, specificity. Familiar pain is the key word in every pain diagnosis.

## Spot-the-occlusion checklist

| Check | Look for | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|

## Grand case: how to reason aloud

1. **Diagnose with DC/TMD.** Name each Axis I diagnosis from history plus familiar pain, e.g. "right masseter myalgia and right disc displacement with reduction". Add Axis II: PHQ-4, GCPS, oral behaviours. Image only for significant mechanical problems, suspected pathology or failed TMD treatment: MRI for the disc, CT for bone (Schiffman & Ohrbach 2016).
2. **Screen and stratify with Jbara.** Give the tier (0–3, 4–6, ≥7) and any module (DJD, arthralgia, MAD trismus). Name bruxism and stress as modifiers that push toward the higher tier. Say once that the score is unvalidated, so it frames the decision but does not make it.
3. **Manage the TMD first, per BMJ 2023.** For pain of 3 months or more: education, self-care, jaw exercise and stretching, manual therapy, CBT if stress is prominent. A splint only as an adjunct (conditional against). No irreversible splint, no NSAID + opioid. No occlusal adjustment either: not assessed by BMJ 2023, but RCDSO 2019 (Busse Table 1) advises against routine irreversible occlusal change. Refer to orofacial pain if moderate or high risk, or not settling.
4. **Time the orthodontics.** Start once pain is controlled and stable (Michelotti 2025; Okeson 2018; Jbara high tier). Moderate tier: start, with review every 6 months. A flare mid-treatment: diagnose, pause elastics and activations, conservative care, resume when pain-free.
5. **Splint and mandibular position caveat.** A splint may change where the mandible closes, so re-check CR and MIP once things settle, and take records only on a stable, repeatable position (course topic; UNVERIFIED). A bite that keeps changing (new open bite, growing overjet) points to the joint: image before planning (Schiffman & Ohrbach 2016). Okeson: if the splint resolves symptoms, finish the occlusion to the stable joint position; if not, occlusion is probably not the cause (Okeson 2018).
6. **Consent and records.** Record the baseline. Tell the patient orthodontics treats the bite and appearance, not TMD, and that pauses may be needed.

## Lines not to say

| Myth | Say instead | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Braces will fix your jaw." | No orthodontic approach has been shown to cure TMD; the TMD is treated first, the bite for its own reasons | Michelotti 2025; Busse 2023 (no orthodontic intervention assessed) |
| "Extractions push the condyles back and cause TMD." | Extraction and non-extraction patients showed no difference in 62 TMD signs and symptoms about 14 years later | Beattie 1994; McNamara 1995 |
| "That click needs treating." | Disc displacement is found in about a third of people without symptoms; a painless click is recorded and watched | Busse 2023 (context); watch-and-record advice: UNVERIFIED |
| "Let's balance the bite to stop the pain." | Irreversible occlusal therapy is not supported; reversible, conservative care comes first | Busse 2023; RCDSO 2019 in Busse Table 1; Cochrane 2024 (57 RCTs) via Michelotti 2025 |
| "A splint is the treatment for TMD." | BMJ 2023 rates reversible splints conditional against; education, exercise, manual therapy and CBT come first | Busse 2023 |

## Questions you could ask the speaker

1. For an adolescent with a painless click and nothing else, do you record it and proceed, or do you want imaging before bonding?
2. How do you use the Jbara score next to a DC/TMD exam, and which opening cut-off do you trust: its 38 mm or the DC/TMD 40 mm assisted rule?
3. After a stabilisation splint, how long do you wait, and what do you check, before taking orthodontic records?
