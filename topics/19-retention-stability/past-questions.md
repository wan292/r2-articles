## Important aspects of long term stability

### Q1 · Promotion 2022 · Last year (Khalid's recall)

> Lower anterior moderate crowding and rotation; what aids stabilization after ortho?
>
> - fiberotomy
> - IPR
> - put anteriors out of canines

- **Recalled answer:** IPR (circled) · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** Put the anteriors out of (labial to) the canines (Zachrisson 1997, 'Placing 2-2 Outside 3-3' (PDF p.3, Figs 5–6); stripping Fig 2 (PDF p.14))
- Zachrisson's lower-anterior stabilizing step is to set the incisors as a block slightly labial to the canine contacts after fully correcting rotations. Fiberotomy is less effective in the mandible. IPR (stripping to make contact areas) appears in the paper as a supporting step, so the circled IPR is arguable but not the paper's main lower-arch measure; 2023 and 2026 recalls chose 'labial to canines'.

### Q2 · R2 exam 2019

> Supracrestal fibrotomy poor on the mandible (reason)

- **Recalled answer:** multifactorial causes of lower anterior crowding · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Mandibular incisors tend to move toward least resistance under pressure, so relapse there is driven by other (multifactorial) forces, not only the gingival fibres (Zachrisson 1997, 'Early Correction of Rotations' (PDF p.3: Edwards, Riedel 19%); Third Molars section (PDF p.6, multifactorial late crowding); Melrose 1998 p.510)
- Edwards found fiberotomy reduced relapse more in the maxillary than the mandibular anterior segment. Riedel found 19% of fiberotomized teeth rotated away anyway, which Zachrisson explains by the lower incisors' tendency to give way to pressure. He also calls late lower crowding multifactorial, and Melrose adds that factors such as muscle pattern cause relapse after CSF. So the recalled 'multifactorial' answer fits.
- *Source tag in the recall:* R2 exam 2019 recall (remaining questions)

### Q3 · R2 exam 2019

> Supracrestal fibrotomy better in

- **Recalled answer:** rotational relapse · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Rotational relapse (and more effective in the maxillary than the mandibular anterior segment) (Zachrisson 1997, 'Early Correction of Rotations' (PDF p.3); Melrose & Millett 1998 p.510 'Soft and hard tissue adjunctive procedures')
- Zachrisson cites Edwards as confirming that fiberotomy reduces rotational relapse, mainly in the maxillary anterior segment. Melrose gives the figure: CSF reduces rotational relapse by about 30%, works better in the upper labial segment, and helps labiolingual relapse indirectly.
- *Source tag in the recall:* R2 exam 2019 recall (remaining questions)

### Q4 · R2 exam 2019

> According to Björk and Ricketts (molar eruption)

- **Recalled answer:** molar erupt upward and forward · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** The mandibular first molar normally erupts upward and mesially (forward) (Zachrisson 1997, Third Molars > 'Three-Dimensional Effects on Erupting Teeth' (PDF p.6) and Fig 14 (A Björk & Skieller implant superimposition, B Ricketts arcial growth; PDF p.23))
- Zachrisson cites Ricketts (ref 32) and Björk & Skieller (ref 40) for the normal eruption path of the lower first molar: upward and mesial, continuing while third molars emerge. So the source is the Zachrisson stability paper, not the third-molar paper. The recalled 'upward and forward' matches.
- *Source tag in the recall:* R2 exam 2019 recall (remaining questions)

### Q5 · Promotion 2021

> What is the reason of vertical relapse?

- **Recalled answer:** Dental interference; p13 handwritten: increased interincisal angle, overretraction of lower anterior · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** Incisors left too upright (increased interincisal angle), e.g. after over-retraction of lower anteriors in lower first-premolar extraction cases, so they overerupt and the deep bite returns (Zachrisson 1997, Vertical Relapse > 'Relationship Between Vertical Relapse and Mandibular Anterior Crowding' and 'Mandibular First Bicuspid Extractions…' (PDF p.6, Table 1, Fig 12); Melrose p.508)
- The paper names a large interincisal angle as the cause: upright incisors overerupt after debonding. Over-retracting the lower canines and incisors in first-premolar extraction cases produces that angle. The handwritten note (increased interincisal angle, over-retraction) matches; the typed 'dental interference' is not supported. Melrose adds that stable overbite needs a favourable interincisal angle.
- *Source tag in the recall:* R2 Promotion Exam 2021

### Q6 · Promotion 2021

> Patient came with lower anterior crowding and rotations. What's the best retention plan for lower anteriors?
>

> - Put the anteriors labial to the canines

- **Recalled answer:** Put the anteriors labial to the canines (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Put the anteriors labial to the canines (Zachrisson 1997, 'Placing 2-2 Outside 3-3' (PDF p.3, Figs 5–6); fiberotomy less successful in mandible (PDF p.3))
- For the lower anterior segment, Zachrisson fully corrects rotations and places the lateral incisors' distal contacts slightly labial to the canines' mesial contacts, then retains them. Fiberotomy is less effective in the mandible than in the maxilla (Edwards), so it is the weaker option here.

### Q7 · Promotion 2022

> What is the best approach to prevent relapse of crowding of the lower incisors?
>
> - a. placing 2-2 outside 3-3
> - b. IPR
> - c. fiberotomies

- **Recalled answer:** b. IPR (circled); fiberotomies highlighted with note 'it's better in upper' · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** a. Placing 2-2 outside 3-3 (Zachrisson 1997, 'Placing 2-2 Outside 3-3' (PDF p.3, Figs 5–6); IPR = Tuverson contact areas (PDF p.2–3, Fig 2); fiberotomy (PDF p.3))
- Zachrisson's measure written specifically for lower-incisor crowding relapse is to align the incisors as a block just outside the canine contacts. The 'better in upper' note on fiberotomy is correct. IPR is defensible but secondary: Zachrisson and Boese (in Melrose) use it alongside other measures, and an RCT in Al-Moghrabi found IPR similar to fixed retainers at 5 years. The item is therefore ambiguous; other years' recalls chose 'labial to canines'.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Zachrisson. Important aspects of long term stability

### Q8 · Promotion 2023 · Promotion 2024

> Case scenario of a patient with moderate lower crowding and rotated incisors. What will help in preserving the results?
>
> - a) Place lower incisors inside canines
> - b) IPR
> - c) Fiberotomy
> - d) Early correction of rotations

- **Recalled answer:** a) Place lower incisors labial to canines (underlined); d) Early correction of rotations (b) IPR struck through) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** d) Early correction of rotations (Zachrisson 1997, 'Early Correction of Rotations' (PDF p.3), citing Kusters (transseptal fibres) and Dugoni (19 of 25 stable))
- Transseptal fibres form only after the CEJ passes the alveolar crest, so derotating teeth soon after emergence avoids fibre-driven relapse. Option a (lower incisors inside the canines) is the opposite of Zachrisson's advice to place them labial. Fiberotomy is less effective in the mandible. If the real option a said 'labial to canines', it would compete.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles

### Q9 · Promotion 2024

> - b. Early correction
> - c. Fibrotomy

## Stability and relapse of dental arch alignment. British journal of orthodontics

### Q10 · R2 exam 2019

> [Exam-content note, item 32] Little: (1) most stable → generalized spacing; (2) % of relapse in premolar-extraction cases (bottom of Fig 3-C)

- **Per the paper:** (1) Generalized spacing cases had the most ideal long-term results; (2) premolar-extraction cases: under 30% kept satisfactory alignment (about 70% unsatisfactory), nearly 20% markedly crowded (Little 1990, Results of premolar extraction items 4 (p.236) and Clinical Implications > Premolar extraction / Generalized spacing (p.240); Figs 1–5 panel C = post-retention)
- Little's premolar-extraction sample (Figs 1–5, panel C post-retention) kept satisfactory alignment in under 30%, with nearly 20% markedly crowded. Generalized spacing gave the best long-term stability. Do not confuse these with the mixed-dentition arch-enlargement group (89% unsatisfactory, about 10% acceptable). Bishara (t19_4) instead cites Little 1981 as 90% unacceptable, so the figure differs between sources.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Handwritten exam-content note by reading item (sheet items 28-49, photo)

### Q11 · Promotion 2021

> Orthodontists wanted to make a study on 100 cases for relapse. What is the related factor for relapse?
>
> - Extraction
> - Nonextraction
> - Anterior crowding and rotation
> - Unknown variable

- **Recalled answer:** Unknown variable (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Unknown variable – no pretreatment factor predicts relapse (Little 1990, Abstract item 5 (p.235); Results items 1–2 and 5 (p.236); Clinical Implications (p.239–240))
- In the University of Washington records, nothing predicted long-term relapse: not Angle class, retention length, age, sex, initial or final alignment, overbite, overjet, arch dimensions, cephalometric values, or combinations of these. Extraction and non-extraction cases both relapsed. So 'unknown variable' is the paper-supported answer.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

### Q12 · Promotion 2022

> Orthodontist studied 100 casts to check which malocclusion is related to relapse?
>
> - a. crowding and rotation
> - b. spacing
> - c. unknown variable

- **Recalled answer:** a. crowding and rotation · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** c. Unknown variable (Little 1990, Abstract item 5 (p.235); Results item 2 (p.236); Clinical Implications (p.239–240))

'Crowding and rotation' is therefore not a proven predictor. Spacing cases were the most stable group, but even they relapsed unpredictably. ## Third molars a dilemma! Or is it Am J Orthod Dentofacial Orthop

### Q18 · R2 exam 2019 · Promotion 2021

> Most influencing factor on 3rd molar impaction:

- **Recalled answer:** vertical direction of condylar growth; Vertical direction of condylar growth (followed by small mandibular length, then backward-directed eruption of mandibular dentition, then retarded maturation of third molar) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Vertical direction of condylar growth (then small mandibular length, then backward-directed eruption; retarded third-molar maturation also significant) (Bishara 1999, Morphologic factors section (p.630), citing Björk et al)
- Bishara ranks Björk's skeletal factors as condylar growth direction first, short mandibular length second and backward-directed eruption third, with retarded third-molar maturation an added variable. Together they explained 80% of bilateral impactions, and the space behind the second molar was reduced in 90% of impactions.
- *Source tag in the recall:* R2 exam 2019 recall (typed list); answer continues on page 4

### Q19 · R2 exam 2019

> [Exam-content note, item 46] Impacted(?) article page 4 → definition of Xi point; know the 6 points on page 5

- **Per the paper:** Ricketts: predicted Xi point to distal of lower second molar along the occlusal plane (from head films at 8–9 y): ≥30 mm = enough space, ≤20 mm = inadequate (SE 2.8 mm, ~90% accuracy with Turley curves). Page 5 = the six consensus-conference points (Bishara 1999, 'Prediction of Space Availability for Third Molar Eruption' (p.631 = PDF page 4); Consensus points 1–6 (p.632 = PDF page 5))
- The paper uses Xi point but never defines it; the 'centre of the ramus' definition comes from Ricketts. The six points: (1) lower crowding is multifactorial, so don't extract third molars only for it; (2) distalizing molars can impact third molars, so some may be removed before retraction; (3) third molars are not needed for basal bone growth; (4) removal is easier with roots about two-thirds formed; (5) no enucleation from 7–9-year predictions; (6) warn of risks (permanent >0.5%, transitory ≥5%).
- *Source tag in the recall:* Handwritten exam-content note by reading item (sheet items 28-49, photo)

### Q20 · Promotion 2021

## Al-moghrabi2021

### Q27 · Promotion 2021

> Patient came with severe upper crowding and impacted right canine, extracted upper 4s, and finished treatment. What is the retention plan for this case?
>
> - No retention for 1 year
> - Fibrotomy
> - Removable or fixed retainers

- **Recalled answer:** Removable or fixed retainers (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Removable or fixed retainers (Al-Moghrabi et al 2021, Key points and Abstract (p.770); 'For how long should retainers be worn?' (p.771); Conclusions (p.775–776))
- The review treats prolonged retention as routine, recommending either long-term fixed retention or long-term part-time removable wear. Fixed and removable retainers perform similarly in the short term and no single retainer is best. 'No retention' is not supported; fibrotomy is an adjunct for rotations, not a retention plan.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

### Q30 · Promotion 2024

> What is the relationship of periodontal health with a fixed retainer?
>
> - a. Plaque and calculus
> - b. It will not affect periodontal health if the patient has good oral hygiene
> - c. Results in periodontal inflammation

### Q31 · Promotion 2024

> One patient was given a lower fixed retainer and upper Hawley, while the other received upper and lower Essix. After 10 years, which of the following changes would be expected?
>
> - a. Occlusal outcome will be the same
> - b. Compliance is more important

### Q32 · Promotion 2022

> Patient with 21 mm distance from Xi to the 2nd molar; it is indicative of?
>
> - a. impacted third molar

'Impacted third molar' is the best-supported option, but the paper does not settle 21 mm exactly.
