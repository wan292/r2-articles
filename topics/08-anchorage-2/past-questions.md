## Unequal outer and inner bow configurations. comparing 2

### Q1 · R2 exam 2019

> Class II subdivision left (asymmetric headgear)

- **Recalled answer:** longer inner bow on the left · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Asymmetric inner-bow headgear, with the inner bow lengthened (stops added) on the left, Class II side (Abstract conclusions p68; Clinical implications p74; Conclusions p75)
- For a Class II subdivision, Brosh recommends the inner-bow system. Stops lengthen the inner bow on the Class II side, and that molar gets most or all of the distal force (7:3 with 3 stops, 10:0 with 4 stops, at 10 N). The Class I molar is held in place.
- *Source tag in the recall:* R2 exam 2019 recall (typed list)

### Q2 · Promotion 2021

> Patient with subdivision class II left and was treated with headgear. What is the expected outcome?
>
> - Crossbite on right
> - Scissorbite on right

- **Recalled answer:** Scissorbite on right (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Scissor bite on the right (Class I, no-stop side); crossbite tendency on the left (Class II, stop side) (Results, inner-bow characteristic 4, p72; Discussion p74; Introduction p68)
- With the inner bow lengthened on the left, up to about 30–40% of the extraoral force is lost to lateral forces and moments. The authors tie this to a scissor bite on the no-stop side and a crossbite on the stop side, so the right side goes toward scissor bite.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

### Q3 · Promotion 2022

> Patient with unilateral Class II treated with asymmetric inner bow (headgear); what is the side effect? (photo)

- **Recalled answer:** scissor crossbite in Class I side · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Scissor bite on the Class I (no-stop) side, with a crossbite tendency on the Class II (stop) side (Results, inner-bow characteristic 4, p72; Discussion p74)
- The inner-bow design loses much of the extraoral force to lateral forces. The authors name these as the likely cause of a scissor bite on the side without stops, which is the Class I side in a subdivision.

### Q4 · Promotion 2022

> Case with photo of crossbite on one side only; using an unequal inner bow would result in?

- **Per the paper:** Lateral forces would make the crossbite worse on the stop side (the longer inner bow, the distalised Class II side) and push the opposite no-stop side toward a scissor bite (Introduction p68; Results p72; Discussion p74)
- Brosh reports that asymmetric systems create a lateral force that increases crossbite on the distalised molar. With an unequal inner bow, a crossbite already present on the Class II side would get worse, and the other side drifts toward scissor bite. The paper also notes that moving one molar distally into a wider lower arch can add to the crossbite.

### Q5 · Promotion 2022

> What is the most practical configuration of asymmetrical headgear?
>
> - 1. shortening 1 inner bow
> - 2. elongation of 1 inner bow
> - 3. shortening 2 inner bows
> - 4. elongation of 2 inner bows

- **Recalled answer:** 2. elongation 1 inner bow · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** 2. Elongation of 1 inner bow (Introduction p68)
- Brosh calls shortening one outer bow or lengthening one inner bow the most practical asymmetric designs, because a standard facebow can be converted at any visit. Of the listed options, only lengthening one inner bow matches.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Headgear Qs.

### Q6 · Promotion 2022

> In unilateral Class II molar relationship, what is the treatment of choice?
>
> - 1. asymmetrical inner bow headgear
> - 2. asymmetrical outer bow headgear
> - 3. Twin-block
> - 4. bionator

- **Recalled answer:** 1. asymmetrical inner bow headgear · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** 1. Asymmetrical inner bow headgear (Abstract conclusions p68; Clinical implications p74; Conclusions p75)
- With one side Class II and the other Class I, the 4-stop inner bow puts 0.8–6.1 N on the Class II molar and none on the Class I molar across 1–10 N of extraoral force. The outer-bow design is meant for bilateral but unequal Class II.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Headgear Qs.

### Q7 · Promotion 2023

> Photo of a case with Class II subdivision and crossbite on the Class II side. Which is correct regarding using asymmetric headgear?

- **Recalled answer:** Scissor bite on the Class I side · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Scissor bite on the Class I (no-stop) side; the existing crossbite on the Class II (stop) side tends to get worse (Results, inner-bow characteristic 4, p72; Discussion p74)
- The lateral forces of the unequal inner bow push toward crossbite on the distalised Class II side and scissor bite on the Class I side. Asymmetric headgear therefore makes a Class II-side crossbite worse.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; annotated '8,2'

### Q8 · Promotion 2024

> Unequal headgear outer bow?
>
> - a) More force on long side

- **Recalled answer:** a) More force on long side · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a) More molar distal force on the long outer-bow side (about 7:3 at a 40-mm length difference) (Abstract p68; Results p71; Table I p72; Conclusions p75)
- At 10 N with a 40-mm difference, the long side got 7.46 N and the short side 3.43 N (69% vs 31%, 2.17-fold). The total distal force roughly equalled the applied force, so almost nothing was lost to lateral forces.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 8, 2 Unequal outer and inner bow configurations: Comparing 2 asymmetric headgear systems, Tamar Brosh

### Q9 · R2 end-of-year exam

> If you have a Class II on one side and you want to correct it with a headgear, then the outer arm will be:
>
> - A. Longer on Rt side
> - B. Shorter on Rt side
> - C. Longer on Lt
> - D. Shorter on Lt

- **Recalled answer:** A. Longer on Rt side · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** The outer bow is longer on the Class II side (A, if the right side is the Class II side) (Introduction p68; Results p71; Table I p72; Conclusions p75)
- The recalled stem leaves out which side is Class II. The rule is that the longer outer bow gets the greater distal force (7:3 at 30–40 mm), so the long arm goes on the Class II side. Brosh prefers the inner-bow design for a true subdivision and the outer-bow design for bilateral but unequal Class II.

### Q10 · Growth + biomechanics list

> In a Class II subdivision on the right side, the outer bow on the right side should be?

- **Recalled answer:** (slide image in source) The outer bow is cut short on the side that needs the smaller movement and is left long on the side requiring the greater movement · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Longer on the right (Class II) side; the outer bow is cut short on the Class I side (Introduction p68; Table I p72; Conclusions p75)
- Distal force is greater on the long outer-bow side (69% vs 31% of the total at a 40-mm difference), so the side needing more movement keeps the long arm. For a strict subdivision, the paper would choose an inner bow lengthened on the right instead.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Asymmetric Headgear

## Effectiveness of early orthopaedic treatment with headgear

### Q11 · Promotion 2021

> Headgear with 20 degree upward tilt results in?

- **Recalled answer:** handwritten: Cervical, maxilla backward, downward · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Cervical headgear (Zhang 2010: outer bow 20° upward, expanded inner bow, 450 g, 12 h/day). It gives the cervical effect: maxilla held back and rotated downward-backward (Table 2 p5 (Zhang 2010 row); Results p4; subgroup analysis p7)
- In the review's headgear table, the 20°-upward outer bow belongs to Zhang 2010, a cervical headgear. Effects are not reported trial by trial. The pooled result is a lower SNA with posterior palatal-plane rotation, and that rotation was greatest for cervical pull.
- *Source tag in the recall:* short list 1–16 (p25–26) – same as S2-22

### Q12 · Promotion 2022

> Patient with increased overjet ... (stem incomplete)

- **Recalled answer:** increased risk of trauma (annotation: NNT = 9 pts) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** A large overjet is a recognised trauma risk. Early headgear cut incisor trauma during phase 2 (RR 0.34, 95% CI 0.14–0.80), NNT 9 (Results p4–6; Table 3 p7; Table 5 p9; Discussion p10)
- The review lists overjet among reported trauma risk factors, although the two included trials found no overjet–trauma link. Early headgear prevented about one injury for every nine children treated (moderate-quality evidence). Using the overall RR 0.68 also gives NNT 9 for an average patient and 6 for a high-risk one.
- *Source tag in the recall:* annotation 'NNT = 9 pts'

### Q13 · Promotion 2022

> Headgear might seem like a viable and effective treatment option for:
>
> - 1. the management of Class II malocclusion with maxillary prognathism
> - 2. the management of Class I malocclusion with maxillary prognathism
> - 3. the management of Class II malocclusion with temporomandibular joint pain
> - 4. the Class II treatment of patients in moderate risk of dental trauma

- **Recalled answer:** 1. the management of Class II malocclusion with maxillary prognathism · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** 1. The management of Class II malocclusion with maxillary prognathism (Conclusions p10; Abstract conclusions p1)
- The SNA reduction grew with baseline SNA, so the authors conclude headgear suits Class II with maxillary prognathism. The trauma point is aimed at high-risk patients, not moderate-risk ones, and TMJ effects could not be assessed robustly.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Headgear Qs. - Effectiveness of early orthopaedic treatment with headgear: a systematic review and meta-analysis

### Q14 · Promotion 2022

> Choose the most suitable answer:
>
> - 1. headgear is a viable option to modify sagittal growth of the maxilla in the short term in Class II patients with maxillary prognathism
> - 2. ... sagittal and vertical growth of the maxilla in the short term ...
> - 3. ... sagittal growth of the maxilla in the long term ...
> - 4. ... sagittal and vertical growth of the maxilla in the long term ...

- **Recalled answer:** 1. sagittal growth of the maxilla in the short term in Class II patients with maxillary prognathism · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** 1. Headgear is a viable option to modify sagittal growth of the maxilla in the short term in Class II patients with maxillary prognathism (Abstract conclusions p1; Conclusions p10)
- Only the short-term sagittal effect is supported. Vertical (palatal-plane) effects were very low quality and inconsistent in direction. Long term, the SNA effect shrank to −0.14°/yr, with limited evidence.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Headgear Qs. - Effectiveness of early orthopaedic treatment with headgear: a systematic review and meta-analysis

### Q15 · Promotion 2023

> High-quality evidence for headgear?

- **Recalled answer:** Short-term reduction of SNA · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Short-term reduction of SNA (MD −1.63°/yr; 95% CI −2.20 to −1.06) (Abstract p1; Sensitivity analyses p8; Conclusions p10)
- SNA reduction is the only effect the authors call high-quality evidence. Note that the GRADE table (Table 4, p8) rates it very low, and it reaches 'high' only in the sensitivity analysis that removes bias. Worth knowing if a consultant asks about it.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles

### Q16 · Promotion 2024

> The most compelling reason to start early headgear treatment is?
>
> - a. Increased OJ
> - b. Reduce risk of trauma

- **Recalled answer:** b. Reduce risk of trauma · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b. Reduce risk of trauma (Table 5 p9; Discussion p10; Conclusions p10)
- The skeletal gain shrinks long term. The PAR advantage disappears after phase 2 (MD −0.69, P=.53). Trauma during phase 2 fell (RR 0.34, NNT 9, moderate quality), which leaves trauma prevention as the lasting benefit. Tulloch 2004 (t09_3) supports the lack of lasting skeletal or occlusal benefit but does not report trauma.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 8, 4 Effectiveness of early orthopaedic treatment with headgear: a systematic review and meta-analysis, Spyridon N. Papageorgiou; 10, 3 Outcomes in a 2-phase randomized clinical trial of early Class II treatment, J. F. Camilla Tulloch

## Long-term skeletal effects of high-pull headgear followed by fixed appliances for the treatment of Class II malocclusions

### Q17 · Promotion 2022

> Class II correction using high-pull headgear results in:
>
> - A- restriction of maxillary horizontal growth
> - B- stimulating mandibular horizontal growth
> - C- significant vertical changes in LAFH and SN-MP
> - D- none of the above

- **Recalled answer:** A- restriction of maxillary horizontal growth · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** A. Restriction of maxillary horizontal growth (Abstract; Table 4 p6; Conclusions p7)
- Compared with controls, the treated group's SNA was 1.93° lower and A point 3.86 mm further back long term. The mandible was not stimulated. SN-MP and FMA rose only at T3, and vertical measures did not differ long term.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Headgear Qs.

### Q18 · Promotion 2022

> ANB angle reduction while using high-pull headgear is achieved through:
>
> - A- forward growth restriction of A-point
> - B- continued normal forward growth of the mandible
> - C- accelerated growth of the mandible while using high-pull headgear
> - D- (A and B)

- **Recalled answer:** D- (A and B) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** D. A and B: A point's forward growth restricted while the mandible kept growing forward normally (Conclusions p7; Discussion p5)
- ANB fell by 1.77° long term because A point was held back while B point kept its normal forward growth. Mandibular growth did not exceed the controls' (SNB not different), so option C is wrong.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Headgear Qs.

### Q19 · Promotion 2023

> Effect of high-pull headgear?

- **Recalled answer:** Maxillary growth restriction and continued normal mandibular growth · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Forward maxillary growth restricted (SNA −1.93°, A point −3.86 mm vs controls) with normal mandibular growth continuing; no long-term vertical effect (Abstract; Discussion p5; Conclusions p7)
- The study's main finding is that high-pull headgear followed by fixed appliances held back the maxilla while the mandible grew normally, and the result was stable at about 4 years after retention.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles

### Q20 · Promotion 2024

> Two brothers, one underwent headgear while the other did not. What is the same between them long term?
>
> - a) Vertical skeletal changes
> - b) SNB
> - c) ANB
> - d) Incisal angulation

- **Recalled answer:** b) SNB · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b) SNB (vertical skeletal measures also did not differ long term) (Table 4 p6 (SNB T4–T1 P=.83); Results p4; Conclusions p7)
- Long term, ANB was 1.77° smaller in the headgear group, but SNB, B point and pogonion did not differ from controls. SN-MP, FMA (P=.06) and vertical landmarks did not differ either, so option (a) is also defensible unless it was worded as a change. Incisor angulation was not measured. The source tag names Papageorgiou, but these data are Bilbo's (t08_4).
- *Source tag in the recall:* 8, 4 Effectiveness of early orthopaedic treatment with headgear: a systematic review and meta-analysis, Spyridon N. Papageorgiou

## Topic-level questions (no single paper states the answer)

### Q21 · Promotion 2021

> Figure of patient with cervical headgear. What is the treatment outcome?

- **Recalled answer:** Treatment reflects more of the inherent growth pattern (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** After cervical headgear, maxillary growth goes back to its usual downward-forward course, like untreated controls, so the long-term result mostly follows the patient's own growth pattern; the molar correction is kept (not stated in the paper as a figure question) (Bilbo et al, Introduction p1–2 (Melsen implant studies; long-term cervical headgear studies))
- Bilbo's introduction cites Melsen. Cervical traction holds back and tips the maxilla only for a while; afterwards growth resumes like untreated controls, and SNB and SN-MP follow the untreated pattern. No topic 8 paper shows a patient. Sarver 2001 (topic 7, t07_4) Fig 7 does show a cervical-headgear patient: the outcome was clockwise palatal-plane rotation, more lower face height and more incisor show. If the exam figure came from Sarver, the answer may differ.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

### Q22 · Promotion 2021

> Patient came and was treated with Kloehn headgear with upward angulation of the outerbow. What is the expected outcome?

- **Recalled answer:** Backward and downward growth of the maxilla (highlighted); handwritten: Downward, Backward; 'Headgear for distalization' · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Forward maxillary growth is held back (A point moves back, SNA falls) and the palatal plane rotates down and back (posteriorly); this rotation is largest with cervical (Kloehn) pull (Papageorgiou Results p4, subgroup analysis p7, Discussion p9, Table 2 p5 (cervical trials with upward outer bows); Bilbo Introduction p2 (Melsen: cervical traction restricts and rotates the maxilla clockwise))
- Pooled trials show headgear moves A point back (SNA −1.63°/yr) and rotates the palatal plane posteriorly. Cervical headgear did this most (SMD 1.50 vs 0.11 for high-pull), but the rotation evidence is very low quality. The review could not test outer-bow inclination itself. The dental effect of an upward bow (distal root moment on the molar) is Proffit material.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)
