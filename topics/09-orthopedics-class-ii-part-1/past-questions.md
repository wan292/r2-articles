## 10.1043@0003-321920020720410@CDOSFA2.0.CO2

### Q1 · Promotion 2021

> What is the best facial type for functional appliances?
>
> - Short face
> - Long face
> - Normal face

- **Per the paper:** Short face (Patel 2002, Table 1 p413 (UAFH 51.7 vs 54.9 mm, LAFH 60.6 vs 65.7 mm, LPFH 28.7 vs 33.2 mm, all P<.05); Conclusions p416; Introduction p411 (open bite predicts failure))
- Patients with a good skeletal response had smaller anterior and posterior face heights before treatment, and the introduction notes that open bite predicts failure. Caveat: these are smaller absolute heights. The mandibular plane angle and %LAFH did not differ between groups, so the paper supports 'short face' only in terms of size, not a low-angle pattern.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)

### Q2 · Promotion 2021

> Patient came with her cephalometric x-ray and said that another orthodontist said he would treat her with a functional appliance. What's a factor that could be a determining factor for functional appliance success?
>
> - Ramal length
> - Overjet

- **Recalled answer:** Ramal length (highlighted); handwritten: short ramus in class II is good indicator · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Ramal length (a short ramus predicts a skeletal response) (Patel 2002, Abstract p410; Table 1 p413 (Cd-Go 53.2 vs 58.4 mm, P=.030; Cd-Gn 110.6 vs 118.3 mm, P=.005); Results p414; Conclusions p416)
- Patients with a good skeletal response to functional appliances started with a smaller mandible: ramus height was about 5 mm shorter and overall length about 7.7 mm shorter. Overjet was also larger in responders (Table 1: 11.6 vs 8.9 mm, P=.032; the text says 11.9). The authors read that as a sign of the skeletal discrepancy, and the abstract stresses mandibular size, so ramal length is the intended answer.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

### Q3 · Promotion 2022

> Class II patient: what predicts success of (functional appliance) treatment?
>
> - a. ramus length
> - b. overbite

- **Recalled answer:** a. ramus length · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a. Ramus length (Patel 2002, Table 1 p413 (Cd-Go 53.2 vs 58.4 mm, P=.030; OB 6.0 vs 6.3 mm, P=.734 NS); Results p414)

### Q5 · Promotion 2021

> Mother came and is worried about her child being class II. What to tell her?

- **Recalled answer:** Best to be done during growth spurt · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Treatment is most effective when it is done during (includes) the pubertal growth spurt (Baccetti 2005, p126-127 Treatment Timing for Class II and Table 3; p128 Final Remarks point 1)
- The CVM paper concludes that Class II correction works best when treatment covers the mandibular growth peak (CS3-CS4). Table 3 shows extra mandibular length over controls of 2.4-4.7 mm when treatment included the peak, against 0.4-1.8 mm when it ended before. Tulloch backs this up: waiting for adolescence costs nothing.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)

### Q6 · Promotion 2021

> 10-year-old patient that was treated with early expansion. What is the expected outcome?
>
> - Short-term lateronasal width increase and intermolar width increase
> - Long-term lateronasal width increase and intermolar width increase
> - Short-term skull width, nasal, intermolar and orbital width
> - Long-term skull width, nasal, intermolar and orbital width

- **Recalled answer:** Short-term lateronasal width increase and intermolar width increase (highlighted) · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** Long-term increases in maxillary skeletal ('skull') width, lateronasal, intermolar and latero-orbital widths (option D) (Baccetti 2005, p127-128 Treatment Timing for Transverse Maxillary Deficiency; p128 Final Remarks point 3 (also cited in Lagravère 2005, t14_2 p157-158))
- A 10-year-old is before the peak. In the paper, the pre-peak RME group's only short-term advantage was nasal cavity width. In the long term they gained maxillary skeletal, maxillary intermolar, lateronasal and latero-orbital widths over controls. Lateronasal plus intermolar gains alone (options A/B) describe the late-treated group, so the highlighted short-term option does not match.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

### Q7 · Growth + biomechanics list

> The first vertebra changed in the initiation stage of C.V. (Hassel & Farman)?
>
> - CS1
> - CS2
> - CS3
> - CS4

- **Per the paper:** CS2: the first vertebral change is the concavity at the lower border of C2 (Baccetti 2005, p120 (Hassel & Farman cited for using only C2-C4), p124-125 (CS1 all flat; CS2 C2 concavity), discriminant analysis p123 (C2 concavity explains 80% of T1-T2 difference))

## Tulloch2004

### Q8 · Promotion 2021

> Patient with class II. What's the difference between early and late treatment?
>
> - Early treatment results in increased SNB angle
> - One stage could be terminated prematurely
> - Early treatment has less PAR score

- **Recalled answer:** Early treatment results in increased SNB angle (highlighted) · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** One stage could be terminated prematurely (by elimination; the paper refutes the SNB and PAR options) (Tulloch 2004, Table III p662 (end-of-phase-2 SNB 78.06/77.84/77.60, P=.86); Table IV p663 (PAR 9.3/8.4/7.2, P=.35); Table I p659 (4 functional-group children needed no phase 2); O'Brien 2003 p237 and p242 (16% did not complete Twin-block))
- At the end of comprehensive treatment, Tulloch found no difference in SNB or PAR between early-treated children and controls. Any phase-1 skeletal gain had disappeared, so the highlighted SNB option is wrong. The remaining option is only indirectly supported. Early functional treatment often stops early: 16% did not complete Twin-block treatment in O'Brien, and a few Tulloch children needed no second phase.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

### Q9 · Promotion 2022

> Class II patient had 2-phase (early) treatment and a second patient had one-phase treatment: which statement is correct regarding early treatment?
>
> - a. early tx improves SNB more
> - b. there is difference

- **Recalled answer:** there is no difference (annotation; 'not reliable' beside a, 'mandibular length' beside b) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** There is no (meaningful) difference between 2-phase and 1-phase treatment (Tulloch 2004, Abstract p657; Results p660; Table III p662 (SNB P=.86, ANB P=.41); Conclusions p665)
- After both groups finished fixed appliances, all skeletal and dental measures were alike, including SNB. The skeletal advantage from phase 1 disappeared, so 'early treatment improves SNB more' is not supported at the end of treatment. The 'mandibular length' note probably refers to Siara-Olds (topic 10).
- *Source tag in the recall:* article: Outcomes in a 2-phase randomized clinical trial of early Class II treatment; article: Long-term dentoskeletal changes with the Bionator, Herbst, Twin Block, and MARA functional appliances

### Q10 · Promotion 2023

> Optimal timing for functional appliance?
>
> - a) Late mixed dentition
> - b) Early permanent dentition

- **Recalled answer:** b) Early permanent dentition (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b) Early permanent dentition (Tulloch 2004, Abstract p657 and Conclusions p665; Baccetti 2005 p126-128 (treatment including the pubertal peak works best))

### Q15 · Promotion 2021

> 9-year-old patient with increased overjet 2–3 mm in class I skeletal relationship and is a swimmer. When is the most appropriate treatment?
>
> - Early permanent dentition
> - Early mixed dentition
> - As soon as possible
> - Late mixed dentition

- **Per the paper:** Early permanent dentition (not stated in the paper for this exact scenario) (Tulloch 2004, abstract p657; Discussion p665 (special indications); Conclusions p665; O'Brien 2003 p236 (appliance removed for swimming))
- Tulloch found that early two-phase Class II treatment gave no better result than one phase started in adolescence in the early permanent dentition. It reserves an early start for special reasons such as psychosocial distress or high injury risk. This child has Class I bases, a mildly increased overjet and no such indication, and swimming is not a contact sport. Treating once in the early permanent dentition is therefore the best fit. The trial itself enrolled only overjets of 7 mm or more.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)

### Q16 · Promotion 2021

> Patient came in clinic for growth modification. What is the best way to assess growth?
>
> - Anthropometric and cephalometric analysis
> - Growth acceleration chart

- **Per the paper:** Growth acceleration chart, i.e. serial height velocity (not stated in the paper; uncertain) (Baccetti 2005, p119 (list of maturity indicators: height gain, hand-wrist, dental development, menarche/voice, CVM); Proffit ch2/ch6 growth charts and statural evaluation (resident's notes))
- The paper lists growth in body height and CVM among the maturity indicators, and it recommends CVM because it needs only the routine ceph. It never names a single 'best' method. Proffit relies on following height on growth charts to see whether the spurt has started (wording UNVERIFIED). That favours the acceleration chart, but 'anthropometric plus cephalometric' (height plus CVM) is defensible, so treat this item as uncertain.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)

### Q17 · Promotion 2021

> 13-year-old male patient with class II div. 1 with proclined upper incisors, slightly retroclined lower incisors and CVM 4. What's the most appropriate treatment?

- **Recalled answer:** Twin-block with progressive activations (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Twin-block with progressive activations (not stated in the paper) (Baccetti 2005, p124 (CS4: peak 1-2 years before), p126-127 and Table 3 (treatment including CS3-CS4 works best); O'Brien 2003 Table II p238 (Twin-block retroclines upper and advances lower incisors))
- At CS4 the mandibular peak has only just passed, so the patient is still near the window the CVM paper links to the best Class II response. Proclined upper and retroclined lower incisors suit a Twin-block, which tips the incisors the opposite way. The paper does not cover progressive activation or compare options at CS4, so it supports this answer without settling it.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)
