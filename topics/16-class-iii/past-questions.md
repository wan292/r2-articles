## Cephalometric variables to predict future success of

### Q1 · Promotion 2023

> Class III patient with ceph readings showing short ramus, long mandible and large gonial angle: prediction for face mask?

- **Recalled answer:** Unsuccessful treatment after growth spurt (underlined) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Unsuccessful treatment after the pubertal growth spurt (Ghiz 2005 Abstract, Conclusions (p301). Table II (p304). Conclusions p305)
- A short ramus, long mandible and obtuse gonial angle are three of Ghiz's four failure predictors, the fourth being a forward condyle (small Co-GD). In the study these patients relapsed into reverse overjet after puberty.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles

### Q2 · Promotion 2024

> 10-year-old boy presented with Class III malocclusion, prognathic mandible, deep bite, reverse overbite. (They had a table with Co-Gn and Co-Goi decreased, and Co-Pg and Ar-Goi-Me increased.)
>
> - a. Successful outcome with facemask treatment if done early
> - b. Ideal treatment will be provided if deferred to 11-13
> - c. Treatment will be successful 56%
> - d. Indicates better success would be provided with camouflage treatment

- **Recalled answer:** a, b and c crossed out (X), leaving d. Indicates better success would be provided with camouflage treatment · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** The profile predicts an UNSUCCESSFUL early facemask outcome (relapse after pubertal growth). None of the recalled options states this. The paper links predicted failure to waiting for growth to finish (surgery), not to camouflage, so d is only the elimination choice (not stated in the paper) (Ghiz 2005 Abstract (p301). Table II (p304). Discussion p304: the model helps choose between camouflage during growth and waiting for surgery. Limitations p305)
- A decreased Co-GD (probably what 'Co-Gn' means here) and Co-Goi with an increased Co-Pg and gonial angle is the failure pattern, so a is wrong. Neither the 11-13 deferral (b) nor a 56% success rate (c) appears in the paper; its figures are 95.5% and 70% prediction accuracy. In the paper's logic, camouflage during growth goes with patients predicted to succeed, so d does not follow from it, though it may be the intended key.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 12, 1 Cephalometric variables to predict future success of early orthopedic Class III treatment, Matthew A. Ghiz

### Q3 · Promotion 2022

> Patient 10 years old; ceph shows increased mandibular length, short ramus and increased gonial angle. What is the prediction of treatment?
>
> - a. early tx with face mask successful
> - b. unsuccessful tx after peak of growth
> - c. patient indicated for surgical tx only

- **Recalled answer:** b. Unsuccessful tx after peak of growth · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b. Unsuccessful treatment after the peak of growth (Ghiz 2005 Abstract, Conclusions (p301). Discussion p304. Conclusions p305)
- Ghiz found that a long mandible, short ramus and obtuse gonial angle (together with a forward condyle) go with relapse after pubertal growth in children treated early with facemask. The model is not designed to sort surgical from non-surgical patients, so option c goes too far.
- *Source tag in the recall:* article: Cephalometric variables to predict future success of early orthopedic Class III treatment

### Q4 · Promotion 2022

> Case of Class III patient with a table showing mandible to cranial base, ramal length decreased, gonial angle and mandibular length increased; answers were about success of facemask treatment or whether she will have favourable or unfavourable growth (not remembered)

- **Per the paper:** Unfavourable growth: early facemask treatment is likely to be unsuccessful (relapse after pubertal growth) (Ghiz 2005 Abstract (p301). Table II (p304). Discussion p304 (smaller mandibular length and gonial angle, larger Co-GD and ramus length favour success). Conclusions p305)
- The handwritten note gives the favourable profile correctly: small mandibular length and gonial angle, large ramus length and Co-GD. The case in the stem is the reverse (short ramus, long mandible, obtuse gonial angle, mandible forward), so it predicts failure.

### Q5 · Promotion 2021

> Table of different values for Co-GD, Co-Goi, Co-Pg, and Ar-Goi-Me. What's the expected outcome?
>
> - Successful treatment
> - Unsuccessful treatment/growth

- **Recalled answer:** Unsuccessful treatment/growth (highlighted); handwritten arrows under the variables and 'Unsuccessful' · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Unsuccessful treatment or growth when Co-GD and Co-Goi are small and Co-Pg and Ar-Goi-Me are large (Ghiz 2005 Abstract (p301). Table II odds ratios (p304): Co-GD 1.21, Co-Goi 1.17, Co-Pg 0.87, Ar-Goi-Me 0.81. Table I unsuccessful vs successful means: Co-Pg 112.35 vs 107.06 mm, gonial angle 133.36° vs 124.31°. Conclusions p305)
- These are exactly the four predictors in Ghiz's logistic model. Success becomes more likely as Co-GD (condyle further back) and ramus length increase, and less likely as mandibular length and gonial angle increase. The recalled 'unsuccessful' is correct as long as the table values pointed the unfavourable way, as the handwritten arrows suggest.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

## Properties of ANB angle and the Wits appraisal in the

### Q6 · Promotion 2022

> Class III patient whose ceph shows low mandibular plane angle; what to use (to assess the AP discrepancy)?
>
> - a. ANB
> - b. Wits

- **Recalled answer:** a. ANB (handwritten: 'pt CIII hypo -> ANB') · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a. ANB (Iwasaki 2002 Table 3 (p480): G2 had a significantly smaller SN-MP than G1 (34.80° vs 38.59°) and a flatter occlusal plane. Discussion p481-482. Conclusions p482)
- The subgroup where ANB showed a much worse discrepancy than Wits had low mandibular plane angles and flat occlusal planes. Wits under-reads the Class III in that low-angle, counterclockwise pattern, so ANB is the more valid measure.
- *Source tag in the recall:* excerpt beside it: in Class III with counter-clockwise mandibular rotation and flattened occlusal planes, ANB is more valid than Wits

### Q7 · Promotion 2021

> Case of class III patient with forward growth and counterclockwise mandibular rotation. What is the most appropriate cephalometric measurement to analyze the discrepancy for this case?
>
> - ANB
> - Wits

- **Recalled answer:** ANB (highlighted; pasted excerpt: ANB more valid than Wits with CCW rotation and flat occlusal plane) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** ANB (Iwasaki 2002 Summary and Conclusions (p477, p482). Table 3 (p480): in G2, SN-MP 34.80° vs 38.59° and PP-OP 5.51° vs 10.73° in G1. Discussion p481-482)
- In Class III children with a flattened occlusal plane from counterclockwise mandibular rotation, Wits underestimates the skeletal discrepancy, because the occlusal plane is its reference line. ANB is therefore the more valid measurement in these patients.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

## Effectiveness of maxillary protraction using facemask with or without maxillary expansion a systematic review and meta-analysis

### Q8 · Promotion 2023

> Effect of protraction face mask?

- **Recalled answer:** Anticlockwise rotation of maxilla · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Counterclockwise (anticlockwise) rotation of the maxilla/palatal plane, together with forward maxillary movement (Foersch 2015 Results (PDF p6): palatal plane -0.91° without and -0.71° with expansion, SNA +2.47°/+1.71°, A point +3.41/+2.81 mm, mandibular plane +1.85°/+1.61°. Discussion p8)
- In the meta-analysis, the facemask moved the maxilla forward and rotated the palatal plane counterclockwise. The mandible rotated clockwise and the upper incisors proclined. The recalled answer names one of these real effects.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles

## Effect of maxillary protraction with alternating rapid palatal expansion and constriction

### Q9 · Promotion 2023

> 10-year-old patient with Class III, posterior crossbite and deep bite: treatment?
>
> - a) Face mask with RPE
> - b) Face mask with RPE/C (alternating expansion and constriction)

- **Recalled answer:** a) Face mask with RPE (underlined) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a) Face mask with RPE (Liu 2015 Discussion p647-648: the facemask suits low-angle Class III because it rotates the mandible clockwise, while RPE/C may suit high-angle or open-bite cases. Conclusion 3 (p649): differences under 1 mm or 1° are not clinically relevant. Foersch Conclusion (PDF p10): expansion is indicated by a crossbite)
- A deep-bite (low-angle) Class III patient benefits from the facemask's clockwise rotation of the mandible. Liu reserves RPE/C for high-angle or open-bite patients, where its smaller backward rotation of the mandible helps. The posterior crossbite is treated by the RPE expansion either way.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles

### Q10 · Promotion 2024

> Effect of facemask?
>
> - a) 2 mm additional forward displacement and anticlockwise rotation of maxilla

- **Recalled answer:** a) 2 mm additional forward displacement and anticlock wise rotation of maxilla · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a) About 2 mm forward displacement of the maxilla with anticlockwise (counterclockwise) rotation of the palatal plane (Liu 2015 Abstract and Table II (p646): RPE plus facemask A-VRL +2.11 mm, SN/PP -0.83°. RPE/C +3.04 mm, -1.73°. Discussion p646 cites SNA +2.10° from the Kim 1999 meta-analysis. Foersch (PDF p6): A point +2.81 mm and palatal plane -0.71° with expansion)
- The conventional RPE plus facemask arm advanced point A by about 2 mm and rotated the palatal plane counterclockwise, matching the recalled answer. Liu had no untreated control, so 'additional' compared with no treatment rests on Foersch's meta-analysis, which gives about 2.8 mm.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 10, 2 The Cervical Vertebral Maturation (CVM) Method for the Assessment of Optimal Treatment Timing in Dentofacial Orthopedics, Tiziano Baccetti (supplementary maxillary growth about 2 mm); 12, 4 Effect of maxillary protraction with alternating rapid palatal expansion and constriction vs expansion alone in maxillary retrusive patients: A single-center randomized controlled trial, Weitao Liu, Yanheng Zhou

### Q11 · Promotion 2021

> Patient with retruded maxilla and anterior crossbite. What is the best treatment?
>
> - Facemask
> - Facemask with ALT-RAMEC expansion

- **Recalled answer:** Facemask (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Facemask (conventional). Alt-RAMEC adds less than 1 mm of maxillary advance, which the authors judge not clinically relevant (Liu 2015 Abstract and Table II (p646): A-VRL 3.04 mm RPE/C vs 2.11 mm RPE, difference 0.93 mm. Discussion p648 and Conclusion 3. Foersch 2015 Conclusion (PDF p10-11). Proffit Ch 13 says Alt-RAMEC cannot be recommended)
- In the RCT, alternating expansion and constriction moved point A statistically further forward, but by under 1 mm and 1 degree, and both groups ended clinically alike. The authors advise choosing RPE/C carefully. Proffit goes further and advises against Alt-RAMEC because of the small gain and the risk to the sutures. Foersch is more positive about Alt-RAMEC, but only on two studies.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

### Q12 · Promotion 2021

> Picture of young patient with multiple restorations (or caries?) with class III and posterior crossbite. What is the most appropriate treatment?
>
> - Refer for caries restorations
> - Delaire facemask and then Hyrax

- **Per the paper:** Delaire facemask with Hyrax expansion (the expander goes in first, then protraction) if the teeth are already restored. If active caries is visible, control it first (Proffit, not stated in the paper) (Foersch 2015, Conclusion (PDF p10): the transverse deficit (crossbite) decides whether to expand. Liu 2015, Methods p642-643: Hyrax RPE is placed before the facemask. Caries control comes from Proffit, not from any topic-16 paper)
- Topic 16 supports facemask plus RPE for a Class III child with a posterior crossbite, because the crossbite is what justifies expanding. No paper here covers caries, so the 'refer for restorations' choice depends on whether the photo showed active decay or finished restorations. Note the recalled option has the order backwards: expansion comes before protraction.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)

### Q13 · Promotion 2021

> Patient came and needs growth modification. There is the option of using facemask and using the technique in the picture (Figure 13.22 A). What is the difference between them?

- **Recalled answer:** There is more maxillary growth with the technique in photo · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** The pictured skeletal-anchorage method (miniplates, most likely bone-anchored Class III elastics or facemask to miniplates) gives more true maxillary (skeletal) advancement and less dental compensation than a tooth-borne facemask (Proffit Ch 13; not quantified in the topic-16 papers) (Proffit Ch 13, 'Facemask traction to skeletal anchorage' and 'Class III elastics to maxillary and mandibular miniplates' (resident's Proffit notes). Foersch 2015 Discussion (PDF p8) only names skeletal anchorage (bone anchors, miniscrews) as a newer option. Which figure 13.22A shows is UNVERIFIED)
- Foersch only mentions skeletal anchorage as a way to improve protraction and gives no numbers. Proffit says skeletal anchorage gives more skeletal change than dental anchorage (about 4-5 mm at most). The bone-anchored Class III elastics are light continuous forces (150-250 g) started around 11-14 years, compared with 8-10 years for a facemask. So 'more maxillary growth with the pictured technique' fits.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)
