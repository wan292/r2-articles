## Surgical and orthodontic management of impacted maxillary canines · [Kokich 2004](#read/2004-kokich-impacted-maxillary-canines/past-questions)

### Q1 · Promotion 2023

> Kokich canine article (Fig 5, photos A-C of an impacted maxillary canine): what is the appropriate exposure technique?

- **Recalled answer:** Apical positioning flap · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** Early open exposure of a palatal canine: raise a full-thickness palatal flap, remove all bone down to the CEJ, then replace the flap with a window (hole) over the crown so the canine erupts on its own. No apically positioned flap. (Kokich 2004 Fig 5 legend p 282 (A palatal impaction, B flap raised and crown under bone, C bone removed to the CEJ, D flap replaced with a hole); text p 283 (Kokich & Mathews early uncovering; eruption to the occlusal plane in 6-8 months))
- Fig 5 is the palatal case, and the technique shown is early uncovering with full bone removal and a fenestrated replaced flap, followed by free eruption. The paper describes the apically positioned flap only for labial canines (Figs 2-3). The recalled 'apical positioning flap' fits only if the pasted figure was really Fig 2 or 3.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles (Kokich canine article Fig 5 pasted)

### Q2 · Promotion 2024

> Patient with impacted canine (radiograph and photo); after opening space, what is the best way to align it?
>
> - a) NiTi overlay
> - b) Looped stainless steel

- **Recalled answer:** a) NiTi overlay · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** b) Looped wire: Kokich's ballista loop, giving a vertical force that erupts the canine into the centre of the ridge (the paper does not state the wire material) (Kokich 2004 p 279 (eruption mechanics should mimic normal eruption, avoid pulling the tooth labially, ballista loop); Fig 4 legend p 281 (space opened, closed eruption, ballista loop); Proffit Ch 15 (resident notes: NiTi overlay on a heavy steel base wire))
- After space was opened in Fig 4, Kokich used a ballista loop to erupt the canine vertically into the middle of the alveolus, warning that labial pulling causes dehiscence and recession. The paper never mentions a NiTi overlay. The recalled 'NiTi overlay' is Proffit's general method, so the key depends on which source the item was written from.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 5, 3 Surgical and orthodontic management of impacted maxillary canines, Vincent G. Kokich

## Congenitally missing mandibular second premolars · [Kokich & Kokich 2006](#read/2006-kokich-missing-mandibular-second-premolars/past-questions)

### Q3 · Promotion 2023

> Kokich missing premolar article (Fig 1: submerged-looking retained deciduous mandibular second molar, radiograph, occlusal view): management?

- **Recalled answer:** Reduce width and maintain · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Reduce the E's mesiodistal width, protect it with composite and maintain it, closing the residual space to keep the ridge for a later implant (Kokich & Kokich 2006 Patient 1 p 437 (12 y 4 mo girl; E 13 mm wide vs 7.5 mm for a premolar; flat bone levels mean not ankylosed); Fig 1 legend p 438; reduction technique pp 441-442 (about 2 mm off each side))
- In Fig 1 the deciduous molar was not ankylosed (flat interproximal bone), so it was narrowed, built up with composite and kept to preserve buccolingual bone until growth ended. This is exactly the recalled answer.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles (Kokich Fig 1 pasted)

### Q4 · Promotion 2024

> 14-year-old girl presented with congenitally missing lower 5 and retained primary molar (photo and radiograph). Doctor decided to extract it. What is the effect?
>
> - a) Large bony defect
> - b) Excessive residual space
> - c) Tipping of adjacent teeth
> - d) Supra-eruption of opposing

- **Recalled answer:** a) Large bony defect · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a) Large bony defect: the ridge resorbs, narrowing vertically and buccolingually (Kokich & Kokich 2006 Patient 5 p 440 (14 y 6 mo girl, ankylosed E extracted, marked vertical and buccolingual ridge narrowing; Fig 5B p 442); p 441 (Ostler & Kokich: ridge 25% narrower within 4 years, 30% by 7 years, more on the facial side))
- The stem matches Kokich's Patient 5 almost exactly: after the deciduous molar was extracted the ridge collapsed in height and width. Kokich avoided a bone graft by moving the first premolar distally to regenerate bone.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 5, 4 Congenitally missing mandibular second premolars: Clinical options, Vincent G. Kokich

## Serial extraction in orthodontics · [Yezdani 2020](#read/2020-yezdani-serial-extraction-review/past-questions)

### Q5 · Promotion 2021

> 9-year-old woman came with crowding of 10 mm (OPG with extracted Cs and Ds and erupting canines). What is the best treatment?
>
> - Serial extraction
> - Expansion

- **Per the paper:** Serial extraction (continue the sequence by removing the first premolars) (Yezdani 2020 p 6433 (indications: severe crowding with an arch deficiency of 8-10 mm or more), p 6435 (mild to moderate crowding is a contraindication), pp 6438-6439 (Dewel/Tweed/Nance sequences))
- The review limits serial extraction to severe crowding of about 8-10 mm or more in the mixed dentition. A 9-year-old with 10 mm of crowding and the primary canines and first molars already removed fits that, so the next step is to extract the first premolars, not to expand.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)

### Q6 · Promotion 2023

> Parent came concerned about their child's situation (shown in the photo: lower arch with arrow at the canine region).

- **Recalled answer:** Early loss of primary canine · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Early (unilateral) loss of the primary canine from crowding, causing a midline shift to that side and loss of space for the permanent canine (Yezdani 2020 p 6434, Fig 2c and the indications list (unilateral loss of a deciduous canine with a shift to the same side))
- The review lists early unilateral loss of a deciduous canine as a sign of severe arch-length deficiency and an indication for serial extraction. It causes the midline to shift and the space for the permanent canine to close.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles

### Q7 · Promotion 2023

> Parent lives far from the clinic and is confused about serial extraction versus late extraction. Which option is correct?

- **Recalled answer:** Serial extraction has a shorter active phase of orthodontic appliance (underlined) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Serial extraction shortens the active (multibanded) appliance phase, but the whole programme needs many visits over 2-3 years (Yezdani 2020 p 6435 (advantages: shorter multibanded treatment; disadvantages: prolonged treatment with multiple visits over 2-3 years, and repeated extraction visits))
- The review states both sides: the fixed-appliance phase is shorter, but the overall programme is long and needs many visits. A family living far away needs to hear both. Last year's tag (O'Shaughnessy, folder 6) is not in this year's list, and Yezdani is this year's source.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; annotated 'folder 6, file3'

### Q8 · Promotion 2024

> Patient lives far away from the clinic and is concerned about the number of visits, asking about the difference between late premolar extraction and early serial extraction?
>
> - a) Serial: longer treatment time
> - b) Late premolar extraction: longer treatment time

- **Recalled answer:** a) Serial longer treatment time · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a) Serial extraction: longer overall treatment time with more visits (the active fixed-appliance phase is the shorter one) (Yezdani 2020 p 6435 (disadvantage: prolonged treatment with multiple visits over 2-3 years; advantage: shorter multibanded treatment))
- For a patient worried about travelling to visits, the relevant fact is that serial extraction stretches treatment over 2-3 years of supervision and extraction visits. The resident's side note is also true: late premolar extraction has the longer active appliance phase.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 6, 3 Efficiency of serial extraction and late premolar extraction cases treated with fixed appliances, Kevin W. O'Shaughnessy (note beside b: 'longer active tx time')

### Q9 · Promotion 2024

> 8-year-old patient presented with the following (picture: lower permanent incisors erupting lingual to retained primary incisors). What is the appropriate treatment approach?
>
> - a. Extraction of primary incisors
> - b. Extraction of primary incisors then 2x4
> - c. Extraction of primary incisors then expansion

- **Recalled answer:** a. Extraction of primary incisors. · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a. Extraction of the primary incisors (Yezdani 2020 p 6434 Fig 2b (permanent incisors erupting lingual to retained primary incisors); p 6435 (reversible phase: extracting anterior deciduous teeth lets the permanent incisors align))
- The review's first, reversible step for this picture is to remove the retained primary incisors so the permanent incisors can align on their own. It does not call for an appliance or expansion at this stage.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 6, 2 Serial Extraction In Orthodontics - Review Article, Dr. Arif Yezdani

## Long-term profile changes in extraction and nonextraction patients · [Stephens 2005](#read/2005-stephens-long-term-profile-extraction-nonextraction/past-questions)

### Q10 · Promotion 2022

> 12 years old complains of upper teeth sticking out; facial convexity without nose:
>
> - a. increases with age
> - b. decreases with age

- **Recalled answer:** b. decrease with the age (marked '?') · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b. Decreases with age: the profile straightens (Stephens 2005 Table III p 453 (N'-Sn-Pg' rose from 163.6° to 167.6° in the extraction group, +4.0°, and by +2.9° in the nonextraction group, both significant); Discussion pp 455-456 (convexity decreased considerably); Table IV (males +5.5° vs females +2.6°))
- The facial convexity angle excluding the nose opened by 3-4° between ages 15 and 30, so the face became less convex. The Results text calls this an 'increase in facial convexity' because it describes the angle's value, which is an easy trap. Total convexity including the nose (N'-Pn-Pg') did not change significantly, so the link note's 'total convexity increased' is wrong.

### Q11 · Promotion 2023

> 25-year-old who did not have orthodontic treatment before came complaining of retrusion of the lips after 10 years. Why?

- **Recalled answer:** Because of nose growth (handwritten '?' beside it; Proffit Fig 4.9 and Michigan Growth Study excerpt on upper-lip lengthening/thinning pasted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Growth of the nose (and chin) makes the lips look more retrusive (Stephens 2005 Discussion pp 455-456 (lip retrusion relative to the E and S lines comes from relatively greater nose and chin growth, as in untreated subjects); conclusion 2 p 457 (H-line, which is independent of the nose, did not change); Table V p 454 (pronasale moved forward 6.7 mm in males and 3.1 mm in females))
- The lips moved back relative to the E and S lines while lip fullness on the H-line stayed the same, so the change came from the nose and chin growing forward, not from the lips. The paper says this pattern also occurs in untreated people. The Proffit lip-thinning excerpt is a secondary point.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; Proffit Ch 4 Fig 4.9 excerpt pasted

### Q12 · Promotion 2024

> 35-year-old patient had 4 premolars extracted 10 years ago; he came complaining that he is unhappy with his smile and his profile. Cause?
>
> - a) Growth of the nose
> - b) Excessive retraction of incisors
> - c) Thinning of upper lip

- **Recalled answer:** a) Growth of the nose · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a) Growth of the nose (Stephens 2005 abstract p 450 and conclusion 1 p 457 (extraction and nonextraction changed alike); Discussion pp 455-456 and conclusion 2 p 457 (nose and chin growth retrude the lips relative to the E line; H-line unchanged))
- Ten to fifteen years after treatment, extraction and nonextraction patients flattened in the same way, so the extractions are not the cause. The lips look more retrusive because the nose and chin keep growing, while lip fullness itself did not change.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 7, 1 Long-term profile changes in extraction and nonextraction patients, Corbett K. Stephens

### Q13 · Promotion 2021

> Mandibular tooth missing and implant will be placed in 4–5 years:

- **Recalled answer:** Fixed retainer (A-splint) (highlighted)
- **Per the paper:** Fixed (bonded) retainer to hold the space until the implant (not stated in the paper) (Kokich & Kokich 2006 p 441 (implant waits for the end of vertical growth: girls about 17, boys about 21, Fudalej); retention itself appears only in Proffit 6th Ch 18 / 7th Ch 19 (resident notes; already filed in the hub's conflicts.md for this article))
- Kokich explains why the implant has to wait years (vertical facial growth must stop) but says nothing on how to hold the space in the meantime. The fixed-retainer answer comes from Proffit, so the paper neither confirms nor contradicts the recalled key.
- *Source tag in the recall:* untitled Q–A list (p3–4)

### Q14 · Promotion 2021

> 12-year-old patient came to the clinic wants to do orthodontic treatment (OPG with highly positioned developed canines and mesiodens between central incisors). What is clinically most significant?
>
> - Mesiodens
> - Impacted canine
> - Impacted premolars

- **Per the paper:** Mesiodens (not stated in the paper) (Kokich 2004 p 278 (canines are the most commonly impacted teeth after third molars) and p 281 (buccal object rule); mesiodens is not mentioned anywhere in the paper. Mesiodens is the key on the resident's Proffit high-yield page (two 2020 recalls).)
- Kokich 2004 deals only with the canine half of this stem (localising and uncovering an impacted canine) and never compares it with a supernumerary, so the paper cannot settle the item. The hub's own recall-based key is mesiodens, which disagrees with the link note's suggestion of the canine. File it here only as a 'see also'.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)

### Q15 · Promotion 2021

> Patient came with primary molars and no succedaneous successors (Figure 12.11 A and B). What is the best treatment?

- **Recalled answer:** Decoronation · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** Per Kokich: keep a non-ankylosed E after reducing its width, and extract an ankylosed, submerged E while the face is still growing. The recalled key 'decoronation' comes from Proffit Ch 12 and is not stated in the paper. (Kokich & Kokich 2006 p 437 (Patient 1), pp 442-443 (ankylosis, with extraction advised during growth), p 443 (no space maintainer); decoronation is in Proffit Ch 12 (resident notes, figure on ankylosed primary molars without successors))
- The stem cites a Proffit figure, and Proffit offers decoronation for an ankylosed primary molar without a successor because it preserves bone. Kokich never mentions decoronation: he maintains a healthy E or extracts an ankylosed one during growth. Answer decoronation when the stem shows Proffit's figure.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)

### Q16 · Promotion 2021

> Patient with pleasant profile came to the clinic for orthodontic treatment and the case was borderline (extraction or non-extraction), what will you do?
>
> - Therapeutic diagnosis
> - Ask the patient for her opinion

- **Per the paper:** Therapeutic diagnosis: start reversible treatment, then re-evaluate (not stated in the paper) (Stephens 2005 abstract p 450 and conclusion 1 p 457 (when both groups are finished to the same incisor position, the long-term profile is the same); 'diagnostic therapy' for borderline cases is from Proffit (resident notes, Ch 7 page, 2020 recall))
- Stephens shows that a borderline case finished to the same incisor and lip position ages the same way with or without extraction, so the long-term profile need not decide the case. The paper says nothing about therapeutic diagnosis or asking the patient. The keyed answer is Proffit's: keep the decision open with reversible treatment.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)

### Q17 · Promotion 2024

> Two brothers came after 10 years; one was treated with extraction of U&L4s while the other was treated non-extraction. Which of the following would be different?
>
> - a. Lower incisor crowding
> - b. Overjet
> - c. Overbite

- **Recalled answer:** a. Lower incisor crowding crossed out (X); b. Overjet and c. Overbite carry unclear handwritten marks
- **Per the paper:** None of the listed features is shown to differ (not stated in the paper); lower incisor crowding in particular recurs after both protocols (Stephens 2005 Table III p 453 and conclusion 1 p 457 (no long-term Ex vs Nonex differences in profile, U1-SN or L1-NB at about 15 years); Melrose t19_3 (topic 19): lower incisor crowding after retention follows extraction, serial extraction and nonextraction treatment)
- Stephens finds the two groups alike 15 years later in profile and incisor inclination but never measures crowding, overjet or overbite, and Kouvelis covers skeletal vertical dimension, not overbite. The resident's crossing-out of (a) fits topic 19, but no paper settles (b) versus (c), and the recalled marks are unreadable.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 7, 1 Long-term profile changes in extraction and nonextraction patients, Corbett K. Stephens
