## Slide 3 — Key Clinical Decisions: Two Pathways
- Layout: table
- Content:
  | | Keep the space open | Close the space |
  |---|---|---|
  | Aim | Right width + ideal ridge → implant | No harm to occlusion or profile |
  | Favoured when | Lower incisors positioned lingually relative to the chin (Patient 2) | Opposing-arch crowding; protrusive profile |
  | Main risk | Ridge loss while growth finishes | Lips over-retracted |
  | How, when | Implant after vertical growth stops | Plain closure, anchorage, or early hemisection |
  - Why not a bridge? Pulp risk in young teeth; resin-bonded posterior bridges have questionable survival
  - Decide on growth stage, ridge, profile (lips vs chin), crowding, occlusion
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: Every case starts with one decision: keep the space open or close it. If we keep it open, there are two keys: create the right amount of space, and leave an ideal ridge for the implant. If we close it, we must not harm the occlusion or the facial profile. Why an implant and not a bridge? A full-coverage bridge in a young patient can damage the pulp, and posterior resin-bonded bridges have questionable survival. So today the first choice is a single-tooth implant.
- Time: 50 s

  - All deciduous molars extracted, no space maintainer: 4 and 6 drifted together
  - Lower incisors positioned lingually relative to chin → proclined, space reopened → implant

- Speaker notes: Patient 2 is a girl of 8 years 3 months, missing both lower second premolars. In panel B, the bone beside the E is oblique, so it is ankylosed. The cephalogram in panel E shows the lower incisors positioned lingually relative to the chin. So they were proclined labially, the space was reopened, F, and an implant was placed, G. ## Slide 6 — Patient 3: One Implant Anchors a Two-Tooth Space
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - Adult woman; lower right 5 and 6 missing; lower 7 Class II
  - Space too big for 1 tooth, too small for 2
  - Wax-up → second-premolar implant → bracket on provisional crown
  - Lower 7 protracted to Class I; incisors and profile unchanged
- Figure: kfig3.png
- Speaker notes: Patient 3 is an adult woman missing her lower right second premolar and first molar. Her lower second molar was Class II, and the space was too big for one tooth but too small for two, A and B. After alignment, a diagnostic wax-up set the implant position, D. A provisional crown with a bracket went on the implant, E and F. The implant was then the anchor to bring the second molar forward into Class I, G to I, without moving the front teeth. One implant meant fewer restorations. If asked: the Figure 3 caption says first premolar position, but the text says a second premolar implant.

## Slide 7 — Patient 4: Close All Spaces With a Chincup

  - Class II, 5-mm overjet, minor crowding; jaws well related
  - Closing all spaces would over-retract the lips → protract molars instead
  - Chincup + elastics: Class I, profile kept, no restorations, 4 y

- Speaker notes: Patient 4 is a girl of 13 years 8 months, missing three second premolars: the upper right and both lowers. She was Class II with a 5-mm overjet and only minor crowding, A. Her jaws and incisors were well related, C. Extracting and closing all the spaces would have retracted the lips too much, so the molars had to come forward. This was before mini-implants, so a chincup and elastics were used. She finished in Class I, D, with the lower incisors held, F. No restorations were needed, but treatment took 4 years.

## Slide 8 — Patient 5: Move the 4 Through the Narrow Ridge

  - 4 pushed distally into the 5 site: its root rebuilt the ridge

## Slide 9 — Preserve the Space: Reduce the E
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - Guide: E width at the CEJ on a bitewing
  - Bur: ~2 mm off each side → crown ~7-8 mm, pulp safe
  - Composite: covers dentin, builds occlusion, prevents supereruption
  - Divergent E roots usually resorb; bone replaces them
- Figure: panels fig1-C=Before: 13 mm wide | fig1-D=Mesial and distal wafers cut | fig1-F=Composite build-up | fig1-H=Spaces closed around E
- Speaker notes: Now the techniques. First, how to keep the E until the implant. The E is too wide, so it is narrowed to the size of a premolar. Measure its width at the CEJ on a bitewing. Then, with a carbide or diamond bur, remove about 2 mm mesially and distally, leaving a crown of 7 to 8 mm without reaching the pulp. Composite covers the exposed dentin and builds up the occlusal surface. The spaces are closed, and the E works like a premolar. Its roots usually resorb and are replaced by bone.
- Time: 55 s

## Slide 10 — Ridge Resorption and Implant Timing
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - After E extraction: ridge −25% by 4 y, −30% by 7 y (Ostler)
  - Still implantable, but facial loss > lingual → lingual implant
  - Keep the E until vertical growth stops: girls ~17, boys ~21 y
  - Confirmed on serial cephs (ramus growth stopped)
- Figure: panels fig5-B=Narrowed ridge | fig5-G=Implant at 17 y
- Speaker notes: Why keep the E? Because the ridge shrinks after extraction. Ostler and Kokich showed the ridge narrows 25% in the first 4 years and another 5% by 7 years, 30% in total. It is still wide enough for an implant, but the facial side resorbs more. So the implant sits more lingually, and the restorative dentist must adjust the cusp loading. Place the implant after vertical growth stops on serial cephs: about 17 in girls and 21 in boys.
- Time: 50 s

## Slide 11 — Diagnosing Ankylosis
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - Flat bone between E and the 6 and 4 → not ankylosed
  - Oblique bone, lower around the E → ankylosis
  - Ankylosed + significant growth left → extract early
  - Little growth left + slight infraocclusion → can keep it
- Figure: panels fig1-B=Flat bone: not ankylosed | fig2-B=Oblique bone: ankylosed
- Speaker notes: How do we diagnose ankylosis? Look at the bone level between the E and the first molar and first premolar. Flat bone, as in Patient 1, means the teeth are erupting together. Oblique bone, lower around the E, as in Patient 2, confirms ankylosis. Then growth decides. With a lot of growth left, extract the E to prevent a vertical defect that may need a graft. With little growth left and slight infraocclusion, the E can stay to keep the ridge width.
- Time: 45 s

## Slide 12 — Orthodontic Implant-Site Development
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - No space maintainer: let the 4 and 6 drift together
  - Push apart later: roots leave bone → robust ridge, longer treatment
  - Late extraction, narrow ridge: move the 4 into the 5 site
  - Implant goes in the old 4 site, no graft
- Figure: panels fig2-C=4 and 6 drifted together | fig2-F=Space reopened | fig5-D=4 pushed distally | fig5-F=Bone found at surgery
- Speaker notes: After extracting the E, the authors usually do not place a space maintainer. In Patient 2, the first premolar and first molar drifted together, then were pushed apart. This takes longer, but the moving roots leave bone behind and give a good ridge. This is orthodontic implant-site development. If the extraction is too late and the ridge is narrow, as in Patient 5, the first premolar can be moved into the second-premolar site, and the implant goes where the first premolar was.
- Time: 50 s

## Slide 13 — Implant as Anchor, and When to Load It
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - 5 and 6 missing: one implant, two jobs, fewer restorations
  - Place it from a diagnostic wax-up, before space closure
  - Bracket on crown: immobile anchor, no unwanted movement
  - Early loading: orthodontic force is continuous, one direction
- Figure: panels fig3-D=Implant from wax-up | fig3-F=Bracket on provisional crown | fig3-G=Lower 7 pulled forward | fig3-H=Final crown, normal width
- Speaker notes: When the second premolar and first molar are both missing, one implant can do two jobs. It is placed using a diagnostic wax-up, D, restored with a provisional crown, and a bracket is bonded, F. It then works as a fixed anchor to move the teeth, G, and the patient needs fewer restorations, H. On loading, the authors say implants can be loaded early in orthodontic patients, because orthodontic force is continuous and in one direction, and this stimulates bone.
- Time: 50 s

## Slide 14 — When to Close the Space Instead
- Layout: two-column
- Content:
  Left: Closure is the simple choice when
  - The opposing arch is crowded
  - The profile is protrusive
  Right: Normal profile, no crowding → close without retracting incisors
  - Miniscrews or mini-implants (intraoral)
  - Facemask or chincup (extraoral; Patient 4)
  - Or early hemisection of the E: the 6 drifts mesially
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: The other pathway is closing the space. It is the simple choice when the opposing arch is crowded or the profile is protrusive. With a normal profile and no crowding, closure could harm the profile. So we need extra anchorage to bring the molars forward instead of the incisors back: miniscrews inside the mouth, or a facemask or chincup outside, like Patient 4. Another way is early hemisection of the E, so the first molar drifts forward. This needs an early start and regular monitoring.
- Time: 45 s

## Slide 15 — Summary of Treatment Options
- Layout: table
- Content:
  | Clinical scenario | Approach |
  |---|---|
  | Healthy E, implant planned | Reduce E + composite; keep until growth stops |
  | Ankylosed E, growth remaining | Extract early (avoid vertical defect), no space maintainer; later push 4 and 6 apart |
  | No crowding, normal profile, closure wanted | Close only with anchorage (TADs, chincup) |
  | 5 and 6 missing: wide space | One implant as anchor; close the rest |
  | Narrow ridge after late extraction | Move 4 into 5 site; implant in 4 site |
  - Opposing-arch crowding or protrusive profile → simple closure
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: This table brings it together. A healthy E: narrow it and keep it until growth stops. An ankylosed E in a growing child: extract early, without a space maintainer, and build the site later. Normal profile and no crowding: close only with anchorage. Two teeth missing: one implant as the anchor. A narrow ridge: move the first premolar back. And with crowding or a protrusive profile, simple closure.
- Time: 40 s

  - ~2 mm — off each side of the E → crown ~7-8 mm
  - 30% — ridge width lost by 7 y (25% by 4 y)

- Speaker notes: The numbers to remember. The E in Patient 1 was 13 mm, and an average lower second premolar is 7.5 mm. We remove about 2 mm from each side, leaving 7 to 8 mm. After extraction, the ridge loses 25% in 4 years and 30% by 7 years, but it still takes an implant, placed more lingually. Vertical growth ends at about 17 in girls and 21 in boys.

## Slide 17 — Final Conclusions
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - Decide early: choices made in childhood last a lifetime
  - Implant only after vertical growth stops on serial cephs
  - Protect the ridge: keep a reduced E, or build bone orthodontically
  - Close the space only when profile and occlusion allow, or add anchorage
  - Teamwork: orthodontist, surgeon and restorative dentist decide together
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: To conclude. Decide early, because these decisions last a lifetime. Place the implant only after vertical growth stops on serial cephs. Protect the ridge, by keeping a narrowed E or by moving teeth to build bone. Close the space only when the profile and occlusion allow, or use anchorage. And plan as a team: the orthodontist, the surgeon and the restorative dentist.
- Time: 40 s
