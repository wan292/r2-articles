## Marchetti2009

### Q1 · Promotion 2021

> Patient came with V-shaped maxilla and was treated with SARPE. What could be the long-term stability for SARPE?

- **Recalled answer:** Dental relapse, lingual movement of posterior teeth (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** SARPE is not very stable transversely. At 2 years about 28% of the intercanine expansion (2.5 mm) and 36% of the intermolar expansion (3.0 mm) relapsed, which is more than after segmental Le Fort I. Relapse is greatest at the molars. (Marchetti 2009, Abstract (p 74), Table 5 (p 76), Discussion (p 77) and Conclusions (p 78); overexpansion of 25–30% and 6-month retention advised (p 78).)
- Marchetti measured cusp-to-cusp widths on casts and found the biggest loss across the molars, so the recalled 'posterior teeth move back in' fits the data. The paper does not say whether the relapse is lingual tipping or skeletal; that mechanism comes from wider SARPE teaching, not from this paper.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

### Q2 · Promotion 2021

> 22-year-old male patient came with class III and maxillary constriction, 2 mm crossbite on each side. What's the appropriate treatment?
>
> - SARPE
> - Le Fort 3-piece maxilla

- **Recalled answer:** Le Fort 3-piece maxilla (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Le Fort 3-piece maxilla (segmental Le Fort I) (Marchetti 2009, Introduction (p 74): bipartition is for modest deficits up to 6–7 mm or when sagittal/vertical problems need surgery anyway. Methods (p 75): Le Fort group had deficits under 6 mm plus sagittal/vertical anomalies. Table 5 (p 76): 20% intermolar relapse vs 36% for SARPE.)
- A 2 mm crossbite per side is a small transverse need, and this Class III adult needs a maxillary osteotomy anyway. Marchetti reserves SARPE for isolated deficits larger than 6 mm and found segmental Le Fort I more stable. The paper studied a two-piece split; the three-piece option itself is textbook (Proffit) material.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

### Q3 · Promotion 2022

> Case: patient with open bite and skeletal crossbite of 2 mm:
>
> - a. SARP[E]
> - b. 3-piece Le Fort I

- **Recalled answer:** b. 3-piece Le Fort I · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b. 3-piece Le Fort I (Marchetti 2009, Introduction (p 74) and Methods (p 75): segmental Le Fort I is chosen for deficits under about 6 mm, or when sagittal or vertical anomalies also need surgery. Table 5 (p 76) for stability.)
- An open bite is a vertical skeletal problem that already needs a Le Fort I. A 2 mm crossbite is a modest transverse deficit, which is Marchetti's indication for segmental Le Fort I over SARPE. The paper describes a two-piece split; using three pieces to correct an open bite is Proffit (UNVERIFIED against the text here).
- *Source tag in the recall:* Orthognathic section (Proffit)

### Q4 · Promotion 2022

> SARPE, the relapse (exact wording not remembered):
>
> - a. lingual tipping of molars
> - b. skeletal

- **Recalled answer:** a. lingual tipping molar · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a. lingual tipping of molars (not stated in the paper) (Marchetti 2009, Table 1 (p 75) and Table 5 (p 76): SARPE intermolar relapse 36% (3.0 mm) vs intercanine 28% (2.5 mm). Measured on plaster casts at the mesio-palatal molar cusps (p 76).)
- Marchetti only measured dental arch widths on casts. Those widths show the largest relapse at the molars, which supports a dental, molar-led relapse. The paper never separates dental from skeletal relapse, so option a is the best-supported answer rather than one the paper states.
- *Source tag in the recall:* article (SARPE vs segmental Le Fort I): relapse mainly due to lingual movement of posterior teeth

### Q5 · Promotion 2023

> Retention after SARPE?

- **Recalled answer:** 6 months (underlined) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** 6 months (Marchetti 2009, Discussion (p 77): studies with 6-month stabilisation relapsed less than those with 3 months. Closing discussion (p 78): extend SARPE retention to 6 months. Their own protocol (p 75) was a 3-month consolidation plus a night Hawley for 12 months.)
- The authors blame part of their high relapse on their 3-month stabilisation period. They recommend lengthening SARPE retention to 6 months, and for two-piece Le Fort I they advise removing the final splint after 8 weeks.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Orthognathic surgery

### Q6 · Promotion 2023 · Promotion 2024

> Activation of expansion in SARPE?
>
> - a) After latency period of 5 days
> - b) Immediately
> - c) Latency period of 10 days

- **Recalled answer:** After latency period of 5 days; a) after latency period of 5 day · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a) After a latency period. Marchetti actually used 2 mm at surgery, then a 7-day latency; 5 days is not stated in the paper. (Marchetti 2009, Methods, Group 1 SARPE protocol (p 75).)
- Marchetti waits a week after surgery before daily 0.5 mm turns. That rules out 'immediately' as the activation scheme (only an initial 2 mm turn is made at surgery) and rules out 10 days. Option a is the closest match, but the paper's number is 7 days; the 5-day figure belongs to the older Suri review.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Orthognathic surgery

## Wilmes2021

### Q7 · Promotion 2023

> Major disadvantage of MARPE (mini-implant-assisted RPE)?

- **Recalled answer:** The need for local anesthesia · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** The need for local anaesthesia and placement of the mini-implants. Pain and discomfort are not greater than with a conventional hyrax. (Wilmes 2021, Introduction (p 147); Discussion (p 151): the only disadvantage named is mini-implant insertion, with no extra pain.)
- Wilmes names local analgesia and mini-implant insertion as the drawback of bone-borne expanders. The paper also cites an RCT showing no extra pain, so 'more pain' would be a wrong distractor.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles

### Q8 · Promotion 2024

> 18-year-old patient will be treated with a miniscrew-assisted palatal expander followed by clear aligners. Which of the following is the consequence?
>
> - a. More pain and discomfort
> - b. More root resorption of the anchored teeth
> - c. Decrease in buccal bone thickness of anchored teeth
> - d. Long retention period

- **Recalled answer:** All four options (a-d) crossed out (X); no answer marked
- **Per the paper:** d. Long retention period, in the sense that the expander can stay as a skeletal retainer for a relatively long time while aligners proceed (not stated as a 'consequence'; options a–c are contradicted by the paper) (Wilmes 2021, Introduction (p 147): MARPE gives less root resorption, less buccal bone loss and no more pain. Discussion (p 151): aligners can start right after expansion with the expander kept for retention. p 152: a skeletal retainer can hold the width over a relatively long period.)
- Wilmes says MARPE causes no more pain, less root resorption and less buccal-bone thinning of anchor teeth, so a, b and c are wrong. Its advantage with aligners is that the expander stays as a long-term skeletal retainer, so no separate retention phase delays treatment. Option d is therefore the only defensible answer. The resident marked no answer.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 16, 3 Treatment of maxillary transversal deficiency by using a mini-implant-borne rapid maxillary expander and aligners in combination, Benedict Wilmes (excerpt: nearly 1:1 molar movement with screw turns; little relapse potential, no need for significant overcorrection; skeletal retainer can retain width)

## Garrett2008

### Q9 · Promotion 2023

> Alveolar bending with RME?

- **Recalled answer:** Increased from anterior to posterior (underlined) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Alveolar bending increases from anterior to posterior: 6% of total expansion at P1, 9% at P2, 13% at M1 (0.23, 0.36, 0.53 and 0.84 mm extra buccal-plate expansion at C1, P1, P2 and M1). (Garrett 2008, Abstract; Discussion 'Dentoalveolar expansion or tipping' (p 8.e9); Table XII (p 8.e8); Conclusion 4 (p 8.e10).)
- In Garrett's CBCT data, how far the buccal plate moved beyond the suture grew steadily toward the molars. Skeletal share fell from front to back while alveolar bending and dental tipping rose. The sample was adolescents treated with Hyrax.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; annotated '16, 4' with Garrett 2008 abstract pasted (alveolar bending 6% P1, 9% P2, 13% M1)

## Topic-level questions (no single paper states the answer)

### Q10 · Promotion 2021

> Picture of big diastema and RPE. What is the reason for the diastema?

- **Recalled answer:** Normal effect of opening suture via expansion · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** The midline diastema is the expected sign that the midpalatal suture has opened. The suture opens in a wedge that is widest at the front, so the central incisors move apart the most. (Garrett 2008, Table IV (p 8.e5): sutural expansion 3.87 mm at Inc1 falling to 2.55 mm at M1. Discussion 'Skeletal expansion' (p 8.e9) and Conclusion 2 (p 8.e10): triangular opening with the wide base anteriorly.)
- Garrett measured the largest sutural separation at the central incisors (3.87 mm) and the smallest at the molars. That anterior-wide wedge explains why a diastema appears after RPE, so the recalled answer is right. Garrett never uses the word diastema, though; the Topic 8 MARPE paper (t08_1) is the one that treats the diastema as the clinical sign of suture opening.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)

### Q11 · Promotion 2023

> Side effect of expansion in adults?

- **Recalled answer:** Alveolar bending · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Alveolar bending (buccal tipping of the alveolar process), together with dental tipping of the anchor teeth (adult-specific effect not stated in the paper) (Garrett 2008, Table XII (p 8.e8) and Conclusions 4–5 (p 8.e10): bending and dental tipping make up 45–62% of total expansion. Wilmes 2021, Introduction (p 147): tooth-borne expansion also brings tipping, root resorption, buccal bone loss/dehiscence and recession.)
- Garrett puts numbers on alveolar bending, but in adolescents aged 10–17, and found age unrelated to any outcome, so no topic-15 paper speaks about adults directly. Wilmes lists the dentoalveolar side effects of tooth-borne RME; these are worse when the suture resists, which fits the recalled answer.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; annotated '15, 3'

### Q12 · Promotion 2024

> Which of the following is unlikely to occur with SARPE?
>
> - a) Alveolar tipping
> - b) Tipping of the molars
> - c) Overall expansion
> - d) Equal pattern

- **Recalled answer:** d) Equal pattern · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** d) Equal pattern (Garrett 2008, Discussion 'Skeletal expansion' (p 8.e9): opening is triangular, wider anteriorly, because the pterygoid plates resist posteriorly; Conclusions 2–5 (p 8.e10). Marchetti 2009, Table 4 (p 76): SARPE median expansion 8.5 mm intercanine vs 7.0 mm intermolar (derived: wider anteriorly).)
- Garrett shows that expansion is never parallel: the suture opens wider at the front, and alveolar and dental tipping add to it. That data is for RME, not SARPE. Marchetti's SARPE casts also expanded more at the canines than the molars despite pterygomaxillary disjunction, so an equal pattern is the unlikely outcome.
