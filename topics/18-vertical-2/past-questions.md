## Diagnosis of the vertical dimension

### Q1 · R2 exam 2019

> Symphysis tipped lingually

- **Recalled answer:** forward grower · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Forward (counterclockwise) grower: a symphysis that slopes backward (lingually) is one of Björk's forward-rotation signs (t18_1 p124, Table 1 'Björk's Seven Structural Guidelines' and the text on Fig 4B ('symphysis has a backward cant'). The same table is in t18_4 Table 1 p34 (Pearson).)
- Björk's structural sign 4 is the inclination of the symphysis. The anterior aspect slopes backward in forward rotators and forward in backward rotators, so a lingually tipped symphysis points to a forward grower.
- *Source tag in the recall:* R2 exam 2019 recall (remaining questions)

### Q2 · R2 end-of-year exam

> The chin in a forward rotator is:
>
> - A. Forward
> - B. Backward
> - C. Vertical geometry
> - D. Narrow and long

- **Per the paper:** B. Backward (the symphysis slopes backward in a forward rotator) (t18_1 p124 Table 1 (Björk's seven structural guidelines) and the Fig 4B description; t18_4 Table 1 p34)
- The item tests Björk's symphysis sign: the anterior face of the symphysis, just below B point, slopes backward in forward rotators and forward in backward rotators. 'Narrow and long' describes the backward rotator's symphysis. If the stem meant chin position rather than inclination, the paper would support 'forward', so the wording matters.

### Q3 · Growth + biomechanics list

> Björk (prediction of mandibular growth rotation) method:

- **Recalled answer:** structure methods · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Structural method: seven structural signs read from a single cephalogram (t18_1 p123–124 (Björk's method and Table 1 'Björk's Seven Structural Guidelines'); t18_4 p33–34 (Skieller/Pearson criteria))
- Björk predicted the direction of mandibular growth rotation from seven structural features that remodel during rotation. Vaden & Pearson add that the method is rarely used because some signs are hard to see, it is slow, and it has never been validated.

## Orthodontic management of the short face patient

### Q5 · Promotion 2021

> Case 1 Fig 3 (from article: short face orthodontic management). Patient came with short face and retrusive mandible and the treatment was orthognathic surgery. Which is true regarding the curve of Spee:
>
> - Postsurgical leveling of curve of Spee by extrusion of posterior teeth
> - Postsurgical leveling of curve of Spee by intrusion of anterior teeth
> - Presurgical leveling of curve of Spee by extrusion of posterior teeth
> - Presurgical leveling of curve of Spee by intrusion of anterior teeth

- **Recalled answer:** Postsurgical leveling of curve of Spee by extrusion of posterior teeth (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Postsurgical leveling of the curve of Spee by extrusion of posterior teeth (t18_2 p147 'Surgical-Orthodontic Therapy'; Case 1 p149 (curve of Spee kept before surgery, posterior teeth extruded afterwards with short Class II elastics))
- In short-face patients the deep curve comes from under-erupted posterior teeth, not over-erupted incisors. Turley therefore keeps the curve until after the mandibular advancement and then extrudes the posterior teeth; intruding the lower incisors before surgery would lose face height.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

### Q7 · Promotion 2022

> Decreased LAFH (surgical patient): level curve of Spee?
>
> - a. level curve presurgical
> - b. postsurgical

- **Recalled answer:** b. post surgical · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b. Postsurgical (keep the curve before surgery, then level by extruding the posterior teeth) (t18_2 p147 'Surgical-Orthodontic Therapy'; Case 1 p149)
- Turley gets the most gain in lower face height by leaving the curve of Spee until after surgery and then extruding the posterior teeth. Proffit Ch 22 gives the same rule for short-face deep bite.

### Q8 · Promotion 2022

> Picture of a skeletal Class II patient with short face; asked which surgery for this patient

- **Recalled answer:** max LF I down, man advancement + genio · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** For a severely short face: mandibular advancement (sagittal split with clockwise rotation), Le Fort I with inferior repositioning, and interpositional genioplasty if chin height is under 40 mm. For milder cases, a sagittal split with clockwise rotation alone is usually enough. (t18_2 p147 'Surgical-Orthodontic Therapy' (Fig 6); Case 1 p148–149)
- The recalled plan matches Turley's Case 1 and his rule for Class II with a severely short lower face. Note that Turley treats most Class II short-face patients with the mandibular osteotomy alone, and Proffit Ch 22 warns that a maxilla moved down tends to relapse upward.

### Q9 · Promotion 2023 · Promotion 2024

> Case about Class II div 2, short face with prominent chin (ceph readings given). Best surgical approach?
>
> - a) Mandibular advancement with BSSO
> - b) Mandibular advancement with vertical osteotomy
> - c) Mandibular advancement with vertical genioplasty
> - d) Mandibular advancement with anterior genioplasty

- **Per the paper:** a) Mandibular advancement with BSSO, rotating the distal segment clockwise. Add a vertical (interpositional) genioplasty only if chin height is under 40 mm. (t18_2 p147 (sagittal split with clockwise rotation, Fig 6; interpositional genioplasty if chin height <40 mm); p144 and Fig 5 p142 (each mm of opening reduces chin prominence by about 1 mm); p143 (Class II short-face patients often have an adequate or excessive chin))
- Turley says most Class II short-face patients do well with a sagittal split advanced with clockwise rotation, which lengthens the face and makes the chin less prominent. An anterior genioplasty would worsen an already prominent chin, and Proffit's 'functional genioplasty' is for long faces. A vertical ramus osteotomy is not an advancement procedure.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Orthognathic surgery; Proffit Ch 20 'Chin augmentation or reduction' excerpt pasted beside it (functional genioplasty highlighted)

### Q10 · Promotion 2024

> Case about Class II div 2, short face with prominent chin (ceph readings given: Class II skeletal base based on both ANB and Wits, retrognathic mandible, decreased LAFH). Best surgical approach?
>
> - a) Mandibular advancement with vertical genioplasty
> - b) Mandibular advancement with BSSO
> - c) Mandibular advancement with vertical osteotomy
> - d) Mandibular advancement with anterior genioplasty

- **Recalled answer:** b) Mandibular advancement with BSSO [underlined, with handwritten '?'] · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b) Mandibular advancement with BSSO, with clockwise rotation of the distal segment. A vertical (interpositional) genioplasty is added only if chin height is under 40 mm. (t18_2 p147 'Surgical-Orthodontic Therapy' and Fig 6; p144 and Fig 5 (chin prominence falls about 1 mm per mm of opening); p143 (Class II short face with adequate or excessive chin))
- With a retrognathic mandible, short LAFH and a prominent chin, the sagittal split advanced with clockwise rotation corrects the Class II, lengthens the face and reduces chin prominence. The ceph data given include no chin height, so nothing justifies adding a genioplasty, and an anterior genioplasty would be contraindicated.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 17, 6 Orthodontic Management of the Short Face Patient, Patrick K. Turley (excerpt: clockwise rotation of the chin to a less prominent position; each mm of vertical opening reduces chin prominence by about 1 mm)

### Q11 · Promotion 2024

> 10-year-old patient presented with retrognathic maxilla, midface deficiency, and deep bite reverse overjet. What will be your treatment plan?
>
> - a) Facemask with expansion
> - b) 2x4 appliance
> - c) Chincap with expansion
> - d) Delay treatment

- **Per the paper:** a) Facemask with (maxillary) expansion (t18_2 p144 'Orthopedic Therapy' (Class III best treated with expansion plus facemask protraction; the maxilla drops 2.2 mm at PNS and 0.8 mm at ANS); Proffit Ch 13 on timing)
- Turley recommends expansion plus facemask for a growing short-face Class III with maxillary deficiency. As the maxilla moves down and forward, the mandible rotates clockwise, which also helps a deep bite. At age 10 it is still worth trying, though Proffit notes the skeletal response declines after about 8–10.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Proffit ch 13 for face mask (excerpt: chance of true skeletal change declines beyond age 8, clinical success declines at age 10 to 11); 17, 6 Orthodontic Management of the Short Face Patient, Patrick K. Turley (excerpt: Class III best treated with maxillary expansion and protraction using a facemask/reverse headgear); handwritten '?' after stem

### Q12 · Growth + biomechanics list

> Case: contraindications to extraction in Class II div 2 due to profile.

- **Per the paper:** Avoid extraction in a short-face (low-angle, forward-rotating) Class II div 2 with a retrusive profile, deep labiomental fold and prominent chin. Extraction retracts and uprights the incisors, flattens the profile and does not lengthen the lower face. (t18_2 p144 'Orthodontic Therapy' (nonextraction increases LAFH compared with extraction; avoid extractions if increasing LFH is the goal); t18_1 p127 'The Overclosed Forward Rotator' (uprighting lower incisors harms esthetics in low-angle patients))
- Turley favours nonextraction treatment for hypodivergent faces because it increases lower anterior face height. Vaden & Pearson add that uprighting the lower incisors in a low-angle patient worsens facial esthetics, so the profile argues against extraction.

## Skeletal Morphologic features of anterior open bite

### Q13 · Promotion 2021

> Patient with anterior open bite in OPG. What is the reason?
>
> - Increased anterior facial height
> - Decreased posterior facial height

- **Per the paper:** Decreased posterior facial height is the better-supported option. Cangialosi reports both a shorter PFH and a relatively greater AFH (PFH/AFH 0.602 vs 0.669), and the paper does not rank them. (t18_3 Abstract; Table I p31 (PFH 73.8 vs 87.8 mm; AFH 122.8 vs 130.6 mm; PFH/AFH 0.602 vs 0.669); Conclusions 1–2 p35)
- Cangialosi concludes that open-bite subjects have a shorter posterior face height and a greater anterior face height. In his absolute data, though, only PFH is clearly short: AFH is not larger, because half the open-bite sample were children. An OPG shows ramus (posterior) height, so the short PFH is the more defensible choice, but the paper states both.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)

### Q14 · Promotion 2021

> Patient with anterior open bite and with cephalometric readings increased Sn-MP & PP-MP, decreased PFH/AFH & UFH/LFH. What's the most likely cause?
>
> - Skeletal open bite
> - Dentoalveolar open bite

- **Recalled answer:** Skeletal open bite (highlighted); handwritten: High angle · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Skeletal open bite (t18_3 Abstract; Tables I–II p31; skeletal-subgroup results p32–33; Conclusions 1–4 p35)
- Cangialosi found that open-bite subjects had larger SN-GoGn (38.3° vs 29.8°) and PP-GoGn (31.4° vs 21.9°) angles and lower PFH/AFH and UFH/LFH ratios. The subgroup he classed as skeletal showed these changes even more strongly.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)

## Control of the Vertical Dimension

### Q15 · R2 exam 2019

> Intrusion force for 4 lower incisors

- **Recalled answer:** 40 grams · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** The paper says 80 g for the lower incisors (100 g for the upper), using a segmented arch with the posterior segments joined by a lingual or transpalatal arch. By Proffit's 10–20 g per tooth, four incisors take 40–80 g. (t18_4 p40, section 'Use of Appropriate Force Systems During Anterior Tooth Intrusion to Prevent Posterior Tooth Extrusion' (citing Weiland et al 1996). Proffit's force table (Ch 8): intrusion 10–20 g per tooth.)
- Kusnoto & Schneider give 80 g for the lower incisor segment, so the recalled 40 g does not match the paper. 40 g does fit Proffit's per-tooth figure (about 10 g × 4), so the right choice depends on whether the item was written from Proffit or from this paper.
- *Source tag in the recall:* R2 exam 2019 recall (typed list)

### Q16 · Promotion 2021

> Case of anterior open bite (case 1 from article: skeletal anchorage system for open-bite correction). What is the appropriate treatment?
>
> - Surgery
> - Upper molar intrusion using skeletal anchorage
> - Lower molar intrusion using skeletal anchorage

- **Recalled answer:** Lower molar intrusion using skeletal anchorage (highlighted); handwritten note: molar intrusion with minimal lower incisor extrusion and counterclockwise rotation of the occlusal plane · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Lower molar intrusion using skeletal anchorage (titanium miniplates) (t18_4 p40–41, section 'Use of Implant-Based Skeletal Anchorage System for Openbite Correction' and Fig 6 (reprinted from Umemori et al 1999, last year's article, which is not in this year's list))
- Miniplates fixed near the apices of the lower first and second molars intruded those molars 3–5 mm. This closed the open bite with almost no vertical movement of the anterior teeth, and a lingual arch was needed to control the buccal moment. The paper does not mention occlusal-plane rotation.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25); pasted case photos

### Q17 · Promotion 2022

> (Skeletal anchorage system for open bite) Fig 3, case 1 - the treatment:
>
> - a. implant miniplates

- **Recalled answer:** a. implant miniplates · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a. Implant (titanium) miniplates, the skeletal anchorage system, used to intrude the lower molars (t18_4 p40 SAS section and Fig 6 p41 (Umemori et al 1999; the original article's case 1 and Fig 3 are not in this year's list))

## Topic-level questions (no single paper states the answer)

### Q18 · Promotion 2021

> What is the reason for this technique (picture of MEAW technique [Fig. 9.15 A])?

- **Recalled answer:** Increased length of beam · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Loops increase the effective length of wire (the beam) between brackets, giving lower stiffness and more springiness and range (Proffit Ch 9, Fig 9.15; not stated in the paper) (t18_4 p37–38, 'Multiloop Edgewise Arch Wire' and Fig 4 (Kim's MEAW for open bite). The mechanical reason is in Proffit Ch 9 (beam length, Fig 9.15).)
- Kusnoto describes MEAW only clinically: it uprights and intrudes the upper molars, relies on vertical elastics, and its effect is mainly dentoalveolar. The 'longer beam' rationale is from Proffit, and the recalled answer agrees with Proffit; the paper itself does not discuss it.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–115, no header (p4–12)
