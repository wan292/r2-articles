### Q6 · Promotion 2022

> Patient has Class III with spacing and crossbite, with defects in the letters l, n, t, d and s; which malocclusion is related to the defect?
>
> - a. increased overbite
> - b. reverse overjet
> - c. anterior spacing

- **Recalled answer:** b. Reverse overjet · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b. reverse overjet (anterior spacing also linked, to /l/, /n/, /d/) (Johnson & Sandy 1999, p307-308 Class III relationships (Laine: mesial mandibular dentition and medioalveolar consonants; 4.5x and 3.7x risk); p308 spacing and /l/, /n/, /d/; p308 Increased overbite (conflicting, Laine found no relationship))
- Mesial occlusion and mandibular overjet raised the risk of alveolar consonants being made too far forward, and Class III tongue posture impairs /s/. Together these cover l, n, t, d and s. Spacing explains only /l/, /n/, /d/, and increased overbite has no consistent link.

## Duration of nutritive and nonnutritive sucking behaviors and their effects

### Q9 · Promotion 2024

> Mother presented with her 4-year-old son who is still using a pacifier. What is the expected clinical outcome?
>
> - a. Increased overjet
> - b. Increased overbite
> - c. Posterior crossbite
> - d. Decreased intercanine width

- **Recalled answer:** c. Posterior crossbite · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** c. Posterior crossbite (Warren & Bishara 2002, Table IV p353 (posterior crossbite 6.6% under 12 months, 16.3% at 24-36, 22.2% at 36-48, 41.7% at 48+ months; P=.001); Table III (mandibular canine width up, P=.041; maxillary canine width NS, P=.221; overjet NS))
- Pacifier use beyond 24 months raised posterior crossbite prevalence because the mandibular arch widened while the maxillary arch tended to narrow. Overjet did not change with pacifiers, overbite decreased, and the maxillary intercanine width drop was not significant.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 3,3 Duration of nutritive and nonnutritive sucking behaviors and their effects on the dental arches in the primary dentition, John J. Warren

### Q10 · Promotion 2024

> Child presented with shallow palatal depth, increased mandibular arch width, anterior open bite, and crossbite. What is the cause of his malocclusion?
>
> - a. Thumb sucking
> - b. Pacifier

- **Recalled answer:** b. Pacifier · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** b. Pacifier (Warren & Bishara 2002, Table III p353 (palatal depth 14.8 to 13.3 mm, P=.001; mandibular canine width 23.6 mm at 36-48 months, P=.041); Table IV (open bite 25.0%, crossbite 41.7% at 48+ months); Discussion p351)
- The authors summarise pacifier habits as producing posterior crossbite, wider mandibular arches and shallower palates, plus open bite. Digit habits instead give more overjet, narrower maxillary widths and deeper maxillary arches.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Duration of nutritive and nonnutritive sucking behaviors and their effects on the dental arches in the primary dentition, John J. Warren (excerpt: pacifier habits associated with posterior crossbite; digit habits with greater overjet, greater maxillary arch depth, smaller maxillary arch widths)

### Q11 · Promotion 2021

> What complicates anterior open bites in children?
>
> - Posterior crossbite
> - Thumb sucking

- **Recalled answer:** Thumb sucking (highlighted) · ✅ matches

## Myofunctional therapy for tongue-thrusting. background and recommendations

### Q12 · Promotion 2022

> Patient 12 years old with Class II div 1, lisping and tongue thrust; what is your treatment option to improve the speech?
>
> - a. speech therapy
> - b. myofunctional tx
> - c. ortho tx

- **Recalled answer:** a. speech therapy · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a. speech therapy (Proffit & Mason 1975, p409 'Tongue thrust with associated speech problems only'; p411 Summary recommendation 4)
- If lisping and tongue thrust or malocclusion coexist before puberty, a speech clinician should start speech (articulation) therapy. Classic myofunctional therapy adds little and is contraindicated if the faucial isthmus is narrow. Orthodontics does not correct the speech.
- *Source tag in the recall:* excerpt beside it: if lisping and tongue-thrusting coexist before puberty, speech clinician should start speech therapy; myofunctional therapy of little added help

### Q13 · R2 exam 2019

### Q16 · Promotion 2021

> Patient came with tongue thrust, malocclusion and speech difficulties. What is the most appropriate treatment?

- **Recalled answer:** Speech therapy (highlighted) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Speech therapy by a speech clinician, started without waiting for orthodontics (myofunctional exercises only as an adjunct in pubertal patients) (Proffit & Mason 1975, p410 'Tongue thrust with malocclusion and a speech problem'; p411 Summary recommendations 4 and 5)
- With tongue thrust, malocclusion and a speech problem together, the authors say a speech clinician should start articulation therapy and not delay it until orthodontics. Myofunctional therapy adds little before puberty and may help resting posture alongside ortho later.
- *Source tag in the recall:* numbered list 1–98, no header, article-based (p16–25)
