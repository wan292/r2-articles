## Slide 2 — Why superimposition matters
- Layout: two-column
- Content:
  - Left, the question: did the molar erupt, or did the maxilla descend around it?
  - Left: overall superimposition = total change; regional = tooth movement within the jaw; the difference = growth.
  - Right, what goes wrong: register on a structure that moved and its movement is subtracted from everything.
  - Right: palatal best fit hid 1.33–1.84 mm of 4-year eruption (Nielsen, Table IV).
  - Right: tracing error 0.5 mm (Doppel, Methods); head rotation shifts a zygomatic implant up to 3 mm (Julius 1971, literature via Doppel).
- Figure: schematic of two overlaid maxillary tracings (T1 grey, T2 black) with the same upper first molar; left panel registered on the palatal plane, right panel on three implant dots; the molar's displacement arrow is visibly shorter on the left.
- Speaker notes: Every number we quote for treatment change comes from a superimposition, so the whole question is: registered on what? If the reference structure remodels during the interval, its movement is subtracted from everything we measure. Nielsen put a figure on that. A palatal best fit hid between 1.3 and 1.8 millimetres of four-year eruption, about a third of molar eruption and half of incisor eruption. And before any biology there is a technical floor: Doppel's duplicate tracings disagreed by a quarter of a millimetre, so a total tracing error of about half a millimetre. Keep that half millimetre in mind for the whole talk.
- Time: 90 s

## Slide 3 — The gold standard: Björk's implants
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - Tantalum pins in bone, serial films: Björk 1955 (literature; reference 1 in all three papers).
  - Maxillary sites: below ANS, in the zygomatic process, palate/alveolar border medial to U6 (Doppel, Methods).
  - Implants showed remodelling: nasal floor resorbs, orbital floor apposes, ratio 3 : 2 (Björk & Skieller 1977, via Nielsen Fig 2).
  - Anterior zygomatic surface stable in 9 of 9 boys (Björk & Skieller 1977, via Doppel).
  - Implants are no longer placed (ALARA, IRB): Nguyen used bone plates as regional implants.
- Figure: Nielsen Fig 2 (Björk and Skieller's apposition-resorption diagram), or a redrawn lateral maxilla with an upward arrow at the orbital floor and a downward arrow at the nasal floor labelled 3 : 2.
- Speaker notes: Björk's implant method is the reference for all three papers. Tantalum pins in the jaw do not move relative to the bone around them, so a tracing registered on the pins shows true growth and true tooth movement. The implants revealed that the maxilla remodels far more than the old anatomic methods assumed: the nasal floor resorbs, the orbital floor and the alveolar process appose, and in Björk and Skieller's nine boys the orbital floor rose about three units for every two the nasal floor dropped. The anterior surface of the zygomatic process stayed put in all nine. We cannot place implants today, which is why Nguyen's bone plates matter.
- Time: 90 s

## Slide 4 — Nielsen 1989: aim and method
- Layout: two-column
- Content:
  - Aim: how far do two anatomic methods mis-read 4 years of growth versus implants?
  - 18 of 35 implanted UCSF subjects usable; 6 boys, 12 girls; excluded on film quality only.
  - T1 10 ± 0.37 y; interval 4 ± 0.56 y; treated and untreated mixed, mostly Class II.
  - Three superimpositions: best fit (palatal plane at ANS), implant, structural (anterior zygomatic surface).
  - Structural method set orbital apposition equal to nasal-floor resorption, 1 : 1 (Methods).
  - Downs occlusal-plane coordinates; double determinations; second observer for the structural method only.
- Figure: Nielsen Fig 1 (six historical maxillary registrations, a to f) beside Fig 3 (the structural method on the zygomatic process).
- Speaker notes: Nielsen asked a plain question: if we superimpose the same films by the palatal best fit, by Björk's structural method and by the implants, how different are the answers? Eighteen children from the UCSF implant series, ten years old at the first film, four years apart. He excluded films for quality only; the structural method needs both zygomatic processes visible without double contours, which is why seventeen of thirty-five were lost. One detail matters later: he held orbital-floor apposition equal to nasal-floor resorption, one to one, although his own Figure 2 quotes Björk's three to two. Every landmark was plotted in a Downs occlusal-plane grid and the methods were compared statistically.
- Time: 90 s

## Slide 6 — Doppel 1994: aim and method
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - Aim: which maxillary structures stay closest to a three-implant superimposition over ≥ 3 years?
  - 50 of ≈ 700 UW implant records (≈ 7 %, derived); 23 M, 27 F (Table I); films 1967–1975.
  - T1 11.9 ± 1.4 y, T2 16.0 ± 1.7 y; interval 4.2 ± 0.96 y (Table I).
  - Majority treated: edgewise, headgear, Class II and III elastics, extraction and non-extraction.
  - Metric: maximum distance each of 17 structures deviated, digital calipers to 0.01 mm (Table II).
  - Tracing error: duplicate tracings of 10 films ± 0.25 mm, total 0.5 mm; one tracer (Methods).
  - Figs 1–6: one case traced by six anatomic methods with the implants drawn in.
- Figure: Doppel Fig 1 (palatal plane registered at ANS: the implants appear to drift posteriorly and superiorly) as the example of a distorting method.
- Speaker notes: Doppel's group at Washington went the other way round: superimpose on the implants, then ask how far each anatomical structure drifted. Fifty cases out of roughly seven hundred implant records met the entry criteria: all three implants perfectly oriented, at least three years between films, the same cephalostat, the same head tilt. Most were treated, including headgear and elastics, so unlike Björk's this is a treated sample. The metric is the maximum deviation of each structure, averaged along curved contours, so it includes tracing error and head-position error. Figures 1 to 6 show one patient traced by six historical methods with the implants drawn in; that is the most useful teaching image in the paper.
- Time: 90 s

## Slide 8 — Nguyen 2018: aim and method
- Layout: two-column
- Content:
  - Aim 1: which Björk mandibular regions are stable in 3D, judged against bone plates?
  - Aim 1 sample: 20 Class III bone-anchor patients, 12.1 ± 1.3 → 13.3 ± 1.1 y; plates at the incisor apices.
  - Candidate regions: chin (pogonion to B point), symphysis inner cortex to the mental foramina, third-molar crypt.
  - Canal excluded a priori: it changes with growth (Björk; Krarup 2005, literature).
  - Voxel registration: Slicer CMF, ITK-SNAP; i-CAT or NewTom 3G; 0.3 mm voxels; absolute mean surface distance.
  - Aim 2: 25 growing patients, 12.7 ± 1.4 y, CVM 2–5; 10 Herbst, 10 Class II elastics, 5 Class III anchors.
  - Aim 2: interval 12.6 ± 0.9 months; two observers; ICC; n = 25 from the ICC power calculation (ρ0 .75, ρ1 .95, α .05, power .80).
- Figure: Nguyen Fig 3 (colour-coded registration error at the chin, symphysis and third-molar crypt on a bone-plate registration).
- Speaker notes: Nguyen moves to the mandible and to CBCT. The trick is the reference: Class III patients with bone plates near the incisor apices, so the plates act as implants without placing any. In twenty of those patients he registered the one-year scan to baseline on the plates, then measured how far three candidate regions had moved: the chin from pogonion to B point, the inner cortex of the symphysis, and the third-molar crypt. The mandibular canal was excluded before testing because it changes with growth. Then, in a separate sample of twenty-five growing patients, two observers registered the scans on the chosen region to test reproducibility. Twenty-five is the number the ICC power calculation asked for.
- Time: 90 s

  - Interobserver error, chin + symphysis combined: 0.12 ± 0.11 mm (abstract prints ± 1.1); voxel 0.3 mm.
  - Error < 0.25 mm over the whole mandible, < 0.12 mm over 75 % of its surface; ICC 0.998 (95 % CI 0.995–1.000).
  - Remodelling seen on that registration: pogonion stable; ≈ 2 mm deposition near menton (Fig 4D).
  - Anterior ramus resorption, posterior deposition (Fig 6): the Björk and Enlow pattern (literature).
  - Take-home: chin + symphysis is the 3D regional mandibular reference; crypt out, canal out.
- Figure: Nguyen Fig 4 A–D (symphysis colour maps; D shows the red band of deposition along the lower border) with Fig 6 (ramus remodelling) beside it.
- Speaker notes: Against the bone plates, the chin and the inner symphysis each moved less than half a millimetre in a year, close to the 0.3-millimetre voxel. The third-molar crypt moved almost two millimetres and was worst in vertical growers, so it fails as a reference at this age. Combining chin and symphysis, two observers agreed to 0.12 millimetres with an ICC of 0.998. That is the reproducibility claim; the stability numbers are the validity claim. Importantly, the registration showed the remodelling we expect from Björk: nothing at pogonion, about two millimetres of deposition near menton, resorption at the anterior ramus and deposition behind. If the plates had simply flattered the chin, that two-millimetre band would not appear.

## Slide 10 — What the three agree on
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - Implants or plates are the truth; every anatomic method is a proxy with a measurable bias.
  - ANS, PNS, PTM and A point are never registration points (Doppel Table II 2.1–2.4 mm; Nielsen under-read 1.1–2.2 mm).
  - The anterior zygomatic surface is the maxillary reference (Nielsen's method; Doppel 0.95 ± 0.57 mm).
  - Same ratio three ways: Björk & Skieller 3 : 2 (literature), Doppel 1.5 : 1, ABO 3/5 : 2/5 (literature).
  - Scatter comes from locating landmarks, not from registering (Nielsen: similar SDs across methods; Nguyen 0.12 mm with surfaces).
  - Björk's mandibular core survives 3D: chin 0.37 mm, inner symphysis 0.40 mm (Nguyen).
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: Put side by side, the three papers agree on more than they disagree. All treat implants as truth and all admit the truth has error. Both maxillary papers condemn the same landmarks: ANS, PNS, PTM and A point drift two millimetres or more in four years. Both end up on the anterior surface of the zygomatic process. The vertical ratio, orbital floor to nasal floor, is the same number whether you call it three to two or one and a half to one. And all three say the scatter in their tables comes from finding landmarks, not from the registration; that is exactly why Nguyen's surface-based method gets down to a tenth of a millimetre between observers.
- Time: 75 s

## Slide 11 — Where they differ
- Layout: table
- Content:

| Issue | Nielsen 1989 | Doppel 1994 | Nguyen 2018 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anterior zygomatic surface | No resorption seen (n = 18) | Direction variable: 27.5 % anterior, 35 % posterior, 37.5 % none | — |
| Vertical ratio | 1 : 1 executed, 3 : 2 cited | 1.5 : 1 recommended | — |
| Posterior zygomatic contour | Reject: appositional (Riedel) | Keep, apposition allowed for | — |
| Internal palate | Reject: hides 30–50 % of eruption | Best number 0.85 mm, worst fit, limited value | — |
| Third-molar crypt, canal | — | — | Crypt fails (1.94 mm); canal excluded; Björk 1983 listed both (literature) |
| Verdict | Structural method valid and reliable | No maxillary area completely reliable | Chin + symphysis stable and reliable |

  - Nielsen's horizontal offset: Discussion says posterior; Tables I–II put structural landmarks 0.5–0.75 mm anterior to implants.
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: Now the tensions. Nielsen found no resorption on the anterior zygomatic surface. Doppel, with fifty treated cases and an exact three-implant fit, found it moved forward in a quarter, backward in a third, and not at all in the rest. Nielsen ran the structural method with apposition equal to resorption and paid for it with a slight vertical over-read and a half-millimetre horizontal shift; Doppel's one and a half to one is the correction. They disagree on the posterior contour: Nielsen rejects it, Doppel keeps it with the apposition allowed for. Nguyen quietly drops two of Björk's four mandibular structures, the crypt and the canal. One internal wrinkle: Nielsen's text and tables disagree on which way his offset runs.

- Speaker notes: This is the slide to photograph. ## Slide 13 — Reading our own cephalograms and CBCTs
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - First question for any change value: registered on what?
  - Palatal or ANS registration: upper molar extrusion under-read, upper incisor intrusion over-read, same direction every time.
  - Size of the bias scales with growth per unit time: ≈ 0.3–0.6 mm/y skeletal at 10–14 y (derived from Nielsen Table III).
  - Over 6–13 weeks at 16–17 y the bias is ≤ 0.1 mm (derived, UNVERIFIED); over 2–4 years of treatment it is millimetres.
  - 2D resolution is 0.5 mm (Doppel): between-group differences below 0.5 mm are not readable on tracings.
  - Mandible: register on chin + inner symphysis; lower border and corpus axis absorb remodelling (Springate & Jones 1998, registry).
  - Example (registry): Khaleel 2026 used palatal-vault and corpus-axis registration over 44–94 days; bias small, but 0.2–0.35 mm arm differences sit below tracing error.
  - CBCT: voxel registration on chin + symphysis; name the reference region in every report.
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: What changes at the viewbox? First, always ask what the superimposition was registered on before trusting a number. A palatal registration has a fixed direction of bias: it makes maxillary teeth look less descended than they are. The size depends on how much the nasal floor remodelled in the interval, so it is a few tenths of a millimetre per year at puberty and almost nothing over a few weeks; our last article is an example of the latter. Second, the half-millimetre tracing floor: if two treatment groups differ by less than that, a tracing cannot separate them. Third, for the mandible, use the chin and the inner symphysis, not the lower border and not a corpus axis, because those move with remodelling.
- Time: 90 s

## Slide 14 — Limitations across the topic
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - All retrospective, best-film samples: 50 of ≈ 700; 18 of 35; database scans minus artefacts. Reported errors are lower bounds.
  - Mostly or wholly treated samples (Doppel, Nguyen); Nielsen mixed and could not separate them.
  - Pubertal intervals only: 4.2 y, 4 y, ≈ 1 y. No adults; no 3D stability data beyond one year.
  - Gold standards carry error: three closely spaced implants; not all bilateral; loaded plates checked clinically only.
  - Metrics are not interconvertible: maximum deviation vs landmark displacement vs surface distance.
  - Observers: one tracer (Doppel); second observer after brief instructions (Nielsen); two observers (Nguyen).
  - Reporting slips: Nielsen Table IV footnote; Nguyen ± 1.1 vs ± 0.11; Doppel 32 % vs n = 34 of 50.
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: Before the textbook, the weaknesses they share. Every sample was filtered for image quality or implant stability, so the errors reported are the best case, not what a routine film gives. Two of the three samples were treated, and treatment can decouple the internal and external rotations the ratios depend on. All three cover puberty only, so none tells us how stable these regions are in an adult or over five years of CBCT. The gold standards are imperfect: Doppel's implants were close together, so error grows with distance; Nguyen's plates were loaded by elastics and checked clinically. And the metrics cannot be compared across papers, which is why the internal palate ranks first in Doppel and last in Nielsen.
- Time: 60 s

## Slide 15 — What the textbook says
- Layout: two-column
- Content:
  - Proffit Ch 6: three superimpositions: cranial base; maxilla on the maximum contour of the maxilla (Fig 6.59); mandible on the symphysis (Fig 6.60) (Proffit, study pages; wording UNVERIFIED against the book).
  - Proffit Ch 4 [6th p88]: implants show internal rotation of both jaws, masked by surface remodelling; palatal plane changes little (Proffit).
  - Proffit Ch 2: implant radiography raised accuracy and showed remodelling had been underestimated; now largely superseded by 3D imaging (Proffit 5e, VERIFIED).
  - ABO maxillary method: both zygomatic processes, orbital floors, palatal floor; AP on the anterior zygomatic surface; 3/5 : 2/5 (ABO 2025 deck, VERIFIED) = Doppel's conclusion.
  - ABO mandibular method still lists the inferior alveolar canal and the third-molar bud before roots (ABO 2025, VERIFIED); Nguyen drops both in 3D.
  - Proffit's CBCT indications do not include growth monitoring; cephalogram 0.005 mSv vs CBCT 0.1–0.15 mSv (Proffit Ch 6, study pages).
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: Proffit presents the same three-level logic: overall superimposition on the cranial base, regional on the maxilla and on the symphysis. The growth chapter is where the implant biology lives: internal rotation of both jaws hidden by surface remodelling, which is the mechanism behind every bias in these papers. The ABO's required structural method is essentially Doppel's conclusion written as instructions, with the three-fifths to two-fifths rule, and the ABO cites Doppel and Nielsen as its validation. Where the textbook and the board diverge from the 3D paper is the mandible: the canal and the third-molar bud are still on the ABO list, and Nguyen removed both. I have marked the references I could not verify; please correct me if the wording differs.
- Time: 60 s

## Slide 16 — Open questions and the future
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - No 3D maxillary region has been validated against implants; current work is reproducibility (Ruellas 2016, literature) or mini-screws (Fan 2023, literature).
  - Graf 2022 systematic review: 27 studies, 25 at high risk of bias; no method proved accurate (literature, abstract VERIFIED).
  - How long do chin and symphysis stay stable? Chen 2020: < 1.0 mm over 4.6 y; foramina moved 3.0–3.4 mm (literature).
  - Automated 2D registration: AI RMSE 0.48 ± 0.35 mm vs best observer 0.38 ± 0.22 mm, NS (Vasileiou 2026, literature).
  - Fully automated 3D pipelines: regional differences between workflows −1.7 to 1.1 mm (Huang 2026, literature).
  - Dose: serial CBCT for growth is not a Proffit indication; 0.1–0.15 mSv per scan (Proffit, study pages).
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: Where does this leave us? The mandible has a 3D reference validated against something hard; the maxilla does not yet. The 3D maxillary papers test whether two observers agree, which is necessary but not the same as being right. The 2022 systematic review concluded that no cephalometric superimposition method has been proved accurate, which is a fair reading of the risk of bias in the whole field, these three included. Automation is arriving: AI registration now matches a good observer in 2D and is being tested in 3D, where regional registrations vary more between workflows than cranial-base ones. The constraint that does not go away is dose; nobody should take serial CBCTs to measure growth.
- Time: 45 s

## Slide 17 — Summary
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - Palatal best fit hides ≈ 30 % of molar and ≈ 50 % of incisor eruption over 4 years (Nielsen).
  - The maxillary reference is the zygomatic process; orbital floor rises 1.5× what the palate lowers (Doppel).
  - ANS, PNS, PTM, A point and the palatal plane are not registration points (both).
  - The 3D mandibular reference is chin + inner symphysis: 0.37–0.40 mm/y stability, 0.12 mm between observers (Nguyen).
  - 2D tracing resolves 0.5 mm; the first question about any number is: registered on what?
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: Five lines to take away. One: a palatal best fit hides about a third of molar eruption and half of incisor eruption over four years of growth. Two: the maxillary reference is the zygomatic process, with the orbital floor rising one and a half times what the palate lowers. Three: ANS, PNS, PTM, A point and the palatal plane itself are not stable, so never register on them. Four: in the mandible, in three dimensions, the chin and the inner symphysis are stable to under half a millimetre a year and two observers agree to a tenth. Five: a tracing resolves half a millimetre, and the first question about any number is what it was registered on.
