## Slide 2 — Why superimposition matters
- Layout: two-column
- Content:
  - The question: did the molar erupt, or did the maxilla descend around it?
  - Overall superimposition = total change; regional = movement within the jaw; difference = growth.
  - Register on a structure that moved and its movement is subtracted from everything.
  - Palatal best fit hid 1.33–1.84 mm of 4-year eruption (Nielsen, Table IV).
  - Tracing error 0.5 mm; head rotation shifts a zygomatic implant 3 mm (Doppel, Methods).
- Figure: schematic of two overlaid maxillary tracings (T1 grey, T2 black) with the same upper first molar; left panel registered on the palatal plane, right panel on three implant dots; the molar's displacement arrow is visibly shorter on the left.
- Speaker notes: Every number we quote for treatment change comes from a superimposition, so the whole question is: registered on what? If the reference structure remodels during the interval, its movement is subtracted from everything we measure. Nielsen put a figure on that. A palatal best fit hid between 1.3 and 1.8 millimetres of four-year eruption, about a third of molar eruption and half of incisor eruption. And before any biology there is a technical floor: Doppel's duplicate tracings disagreed by a quarter of a millimetre, so a total tracing error of about half a millimetre, and he quotes Julius that head rotation alone can move a zygomatic implant three millimetres. Keep that half millimetre in mind for the whole talk.
- Time: 90 s

## Slide 3 — The gold standard: Björk's implants
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - Tantalum pins in bone, serial films: Björk 1955 (reference 1 in Doppel and Nguyen).
  - Maxillary sites: below ANS, zygomatic process, palate–alveolar border medial to U6 (Doppel, Methods).
  - Nasal floor resorbs, orbital floor apposes, ratio 3 : 2 (Björk & Skieller, via Nielsen p. 423).
  - Anterior zygomatic surface stable in all 9 cases (Björk & Skieller 1977, via Doppel p. 162).
  - Implants no longer placed (ALARA, IRB; Nguyen): bone plates used as regional implants.
- Figure: Nielsen Fig 2 (Björk and Skieller's apposition-resorption diagram), or a redrawn lateral maxilla with an upward arrow at the orbital floor and a downward arrow at the nasal floor labelled 3 : 2.
- Speaker notes: Björk's implant method is the reference for all three papers. Tantalum pins in the jaw do not move relative to the bone around them, so a tracing registered on the pins shows true growth and true tooth movement. The implants revealed that the maxilla remodels far more than the old anatomic methods assumed: the nasal floor resorbs, the orbital floor and the alveolar process appose, and in Björk and Skieller's nine subjects, boys in Nielsen's Figure 2, the orbital floor rose about three units for every two the nasal floor dropped. The anterior surface of the zygomatic process stayed put sagittally in all nine. We cannot place implants today; Nguyen gives ALARA and ethics-board limits as the reason, which is why his bone plates matter.
- Time: 90 s

## Slide 4 — Nielsen 1989: aim and method
- Layout: two-column
- Content:
  - Aim: how far do two anatomic methods mis-read 4 years of growth versus implants?
  - 18 of 35 UCSF implant subjects (6 boys, 12 girls), selected on film and implant visibility.
  - T1 10 ± 0.37 y; interval 4 ± 0.56 y; treated and untreated mixed, mostly Class II.
  - Three methods: best fit (palatal plane at ANS), implant, structural (anterior zygomatic surface).
  - Structural method held apposition equal to resorption, 1 : 1 (Methods); Downs grid; second observer.
- Figure: Nielsen Fig 1 (six historical maxillary registrations, a to f) beside Fig 3 (the structural method on the zygomatic process).
- Speaker notes: Nielsen asked a plain question: if we superimpose the same films by the palatal best fit, by Björk's structural method and by the implants, how different are the answers? Eighteen children from the UCSF implant series, ten years old at the first film, four years apart. Films were selected for quality and for three or four stable zygomatic implants visible on both; the structural method needs both zygomatic processes clear without double contours, which is why seventeen of thirty-five were lost. One detail matters later: he held orbital-floor apposition equal to nasal-floor resorption, one to one, although his own Figure 2 quotes Björk's three to two. Every landmark was plotted in a Downs occlusal-plane grid, double determinations were made, and a second observer repeated the structural method only.
- Time: 90 s

## Slide 6 — Doppel 1994: aim and method
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - Aim: which maxillary structures stay closest to a three-implant superimposition over ≥ 3 years?
  - 50 of ≈ 700 UW implant records (≈ 7 %, derived); 23 M, 27 F; films 1967–1975.
  - T1 11.9 ± 1.4 y, T2 16.0 ± 1.7 y, interval 4.2 ± 0.96 y (Table I); majority treated.
  - Metric: maximum deviation of 17 structures, calipers to 0.01 mm; tracing error 0.5 mm.
  - Figs 1–6: one case traced by six anatomic methods with the implants drawn in.
- Figure: Doppel Fig 1 (palatal plane registered at ANS: the implants appear to drift posteriorly and superiorly) as the example of a distorting method.
- Speaker notes: Doppel's group at Washington went the other way round: superimpose on the implants, then ask how far each anatomical structure drifted. Fifty cases out of roughly seven hundred implant records met the entry criteria: all three implants perfectly oriented, at least three years between films, the same cephalostat, the same head tilt. Most were treated, with edgewise, headgear and Class II or III elastics, so unlike Björk and Skieller's twenty-one untreated subjects this is largely a treated sample. The metric is the maximum deviation of each structure, averaged along curved contours, measured by one tracer whose duplicate tracings of ten films agreed to a quarter of a millimetre; it includes tracing error and head-position error. Figures 1 to 6 show one patient traced by six historical methods with the implants drawn in; that is the most useful teaching image in the paper.
- Time: 90 s

## Slide 8 — Nguyen 2018: aim and method
- Layout: two-column
- Content:
  - Aim 1: which Björk mandibular regions stay stable in 3D against bone plates?
  - Aim 1: 20 Class III bone-anchor patients, 12.1 ± 1.3 → 13.3 ± 1.1 y; plates at incisor apices.
  - Regions: chin (pogonion to B point), symphysis inner cortex, third-molar crypt; canal excluded a priori.
  - Voxel registration (Slicer CMF, ITK-SNAP); i-CAT or NewTom 3G; 0.3 mm voxels; absolute mean surface error.
  - Aim 2: 25 patients, 12.7 ± 1.4 y, CVM 2–5, 12.6 ± 0.9 months; two observers; ICC.
- Figure: Nguyen Fig 3 (colour-coded registration error at the chin, symphysis and third-molar crypt on a bone-plate registration).
- Speaker notes: Nguyen moves to the mandible and to CBCT. The trick is the reference: Class III patients already carrying bone plates near the lower incisor apices, so the plates act as implants without placing any. In twenty of those patients he registered the one-year scan to baseline on the plates, then measured how far three candidate regions had moved: the chin from pogonion to B point, the inner cortex of the symphysis, and the third-molar crypt. The mandibular canal was excluded before testing because it changes with growth, citing Björk and Krarup. Then, in a separate sample of twenty-five growing patients, ten Herbst, ten Class II elastics and five Class III bone anchors, two observers registered the scans on the chosen region to test reproducibility. Twenty-five is the number the ICC power calculation asked for, with rho-nought 0.75 against 0.95.
- Time: 90 s

  - Interobserver error, chin + symphysis: 0.12 ± 0.11 mm (abstract misprints ± 1.1); voxel 0.3 mm.
  - ICC 0.998 (95 % CI 0.995–1.000); one subject: < 0.25 mm whole mandible, 75 % within 0.12 mm.
  - Remodelling seen: pogonion stable; ≈ 2 mm deposition near menton (Fig 4D, one subject).
  - Anterior ramus resorption, posterior deposition (Fig 6): Björk and Enlow pattern (cited by Nguyen).
  - Take-home: chin + symphysis is the 3D regional mandibular reference; crypt out, canal out.
- Figure: Nguyen Fig 4 A–D (symphysis colour maps; D shows the red band of deposition along the lower border) with Fig 6 (ramus remodelling) beside it.
- Speaker notes: Against the bone plates, the chin and the inner symphysis each moved about four tenths of a millimetre in a year, roughly one 0.3-millimetre voxel. The third-molar crypt moved almost two millimetres, and the authors say it was worst in vertical growers, though they give no data for that, so it fails as a reference at this age. Combining chin and symphysis, two observers agreed to 0.12 millimetres with an ICC of 0.998; the whole-mandible figures of a quarter of a millimetre and seventy-five percent within 0.12 come from one patient in Figures 7 and 8. That is the reproducibility claim; the stability numbers are the validity claim. The authors also point to the remodelling we expect from Björk: nothing at pogonion, about two millimetres of deposition near menton in one subject, resorption at the anterior ramus and deposition behind. They argue the menton band shows the plates did not simply flatter nearby bone; note that the Figure 4 caption says that map comes from a symphysis registration, so take the argument as theirs, not proven.

## Slide 10 — What the three agree on
- Layout: two-column
- Content:
  - Implants or plates are the truth; every anatomic method is a proxy with measurable bias.
  - ANS, PNS, PTM, A point never registration points (Doppel 1.3–2.4 mm; Nielsen under-read 1.1–2.2 mm).
  - Anterior zygomatic surface is the maxillary reference (Nielsen's method; Doppel 0.95 ± 0.57 mm).
  - Same ratio: Björk & Skieller 3 : 2, Doppel 1.5 : 1, ABO 3/5 : 2/5 (literature, UNVERIFIED).
  - Scatter comes from locating landmarks (Nielsen, Discussion); Björk's chin and symphysis survive 3D (Nguyen).
- Figure: figures/method-errors.svg
- Speaker notes: Put side by side, the three papers agree on more than they disagree. All treat implants as truth and all admit the truth has error. Both maxillary papers condemn the same landmarks: ANS, PNS, PTM and A point drift between one and two and a half millimetres in four years. Both end up on the anterior surface of the zygomatic process. The vertical ratio, orbital floor to nasal floor, is the same number whether you call it three to two or one and a half to one. Nielsen says the scatter in his tables comes from finding landmarks, not from the registration, and Nguyen's result fits that: a surface-based method with no landmarks gets down to a tenth of a millimetre between observers. And Björk's mandibular core, the chin and the inner symphysis, survives in three dimensions.
- Time: 75 s

## Slide 11 — Where they differ
- Layout: table
- Content:

| Issue | Nielsen 1989 | Doppel 1994 | Nguyen 2018 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anterior zygomatic surface | No resorptive remodelling seen (n = 18, Discussion) | Direction variable: 27.5 % anterior, 35 % posterior, 37.5 % none | — |
| Vertical ratio | 1 : 1 executed; 3 : 2 cited (Fig 2) | 1.5 : 1 recommended; cites Nielsen as agreeing | — |
| Posterior zygomatic contour | Reject: appositional (Riedel's method) | Keep as "most reliable" though posterior drift in 50 of 50 | — |
| Palatal registration | Best fit at ANS hides 30–50 % of eruption | Internal palate 0.85 mm, best number, "limited value" | — |
| Third-molar crypt, canal | — | — | Crypt fails (1.94 mm); canal excluded; Björk used both (cited) |
| Verdict | Structural method valid and reliable | No maxillary area completely reliable | Chin + symphysis stable and reliable |

  - Nielsen's offset: Discussion says posterior; Tables I–II show structural landmarks 0.13–0.75 mm anterior (derived).
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: Now the tensions. Nielsen found no sign of resorption on the anterior zygomatic surface. Doppel, with fifty mostly treated cases and an exact three-implant fit, found it moved forward in about a quarter, backward in about a third, and not at all in the rest. Nielsen ran the structural method with apposition equal to resorption and says himself that this explains his slight vertical over-read and the half-millimetre horizontal shift. Doppel used one and a half to one and, oddly, cites Nielsen as agreeing with that ratio. They disagree on the posterior contour: Nielsen rejects it because it is appositional, Doppel calls it most reliable while reporting that it drifted backward in every case. Nielsen's best fit was the palatal plane at ANS; Doppel's internal palate is a different method with the best number and the worst fit. Nguyen quietly drops two of Björk's four mandibular structures, the crypt and the canal. One internal wrinkle: Nielsen's text and tables disagree on which way his offset runs.
- Time: 90 s

## Slide 13 — Reading our own cephalograms and CBCTs
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - First question for any change value: registered on what?
  - Palatal registration under-reads upper eruption: ≈ 0.3–0.6 mm/y at 10–14 y (derived, Nielsen Table III).
  - 2D resolves 0.5 mm (Doppel): group differences below 0.5 mm are not readable on tracings.
  - Mandible: chin + inner symphysis; corpus axis errs ≥ 4 mm in ≈ 10 % (Springate & Jones, registry).
  - Khaleel 2026 (registry): palatal vault + corpus axis over 44–94 d; arm differences 0.2–0.35 mm, sub-resolution.
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: What changes at the viewbox? First, always ask what the superimposition was registered on before trusting a number. A palatal registration has a fixed direction of bias: it makes maxillary teeth look less descended than they are, so molar extrusion is under-read and incisor intrusion over-read. The size depends on how much the nasal floor remodelled in the interval: a few tenths of a millimetre per year at puberty in Nielsen's sample. Extrapolating to our last article, six to thirteen weeks at sixteen or seventeen, it would be a tenth of a millimetre or less, but that is my extrapolation, nobody has measured it. Second, the half-millimetre tracing floor: if two treatment groups differ by less than that, a tracing cannot separate them, and Khaleel's between-arm differences were two to three and a half tenths. Third, for the mandible, use the chin and the inner symphysis, not the lower border and not a corpus axis, which Springate and Jones found off by four millimetres or more in about one patient in ten. For CBCT, voxel registration on chin plus symphysis, and name the reference region in every report.
- Time: 90 s

## Slide 14 — Limitations across the topic
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - All retrospective, best-film samples: 50 of ≈ 700; 18 of 35; database scans minus artefacts.
  - Treated samples: Doppel majority, Nguyen all; Nielsen mixed and could not separate them.
  - Pubertal intervals only (4.2 y, 4 y, ≈ 1 y): no adults, no 3D data beyond one year.
  - Gold standards carry error: closely spaced implants, not all bilateral, loaded plates checked clinically only.
  - Reporting slips: Nielsen Table IV footnote; Nguyen ± 1.1 vs ± 0.11; Doppel 32 % vs 34/50.
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: Before the textbook, the weaknesses they share. Every sample was filtered for image quality or implant stability, so the errors reported are the best case, not what a routine film gives. Two of the three samples were treated, and Doppel says his rotational variability may be a result of treatment. All three cover puberty only, so none tells us how stable these regions are in an adult or over five years of CBCT. The gold standards are imperfect: Doppel's implants were close together, so error grows with distance; Nguyen's plates were loaded by elastics and checked clinically only. The metrics cannot be compared across papers either: Doppel measures maximum deviation of a contour, Nielsen landmark displacement under each method, Nguyen a surface distance, so Doppel's 0.85 millimetres for the internal palate and Nielsen's one-to-two-millimetre best-fit under-read are not the same quantity. Observers: one tracer in Doppel, a second observer after brief instructions in Nielsen, two in Nguyen. And each paper has a printed slip that an examiner can use.
- Time: 60 s

## Slide 15 — What the textbook says
- Layout: two-column
- Content:
  - Proffit Ch 6: cranial base; maxilla "maximum contour" (Fig 6.59); mandible on symphysis (Fig 6.60) (study pages, UNVERIFIED).
  - Proffit Ch 4: implants show internal rotation of both jaws, masked by surface remodelling (study pages, UNVERIFIED).
  - Proffit Ch 2: implant radiography "largely superseded by 3-D imaging" (study pages, UNVERIFIED).
  - ABO maxillary method: zygomatic processes, orbital and palatal floors, 3/5 : 2/5 (literature, UNVERIFIED) = Doppel.
  - ABO mandibular list keeps canal and third-molar bud (literature, UNVERIFIED); Nguyen drops both in 3D.
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: Proffit presents the same three-level logic: overall superimposition on the cranial base, regional on the maxilla and on the symphysis. The growth chapter is where the implant biology lives: internal rotation of both jaws hidden by surface remodelling, which is the mechanism behind every bias in these papers. The ABO's required structural method reads like Doppel's conclusion written as instructions, with the three-fifths to two-fifths rule. Where the textbook and the board diverge from the 3D paper is the mandible: the canal and the third-molar bud are still on the ABO list, and Nguyen removed both. I have not been able to check the exact wording of the textbook or the board document against the originals for this talk, so please correct me if it differs.
- Time: 60 s

## Slide 16 — Open questions and the future
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - No 3D maxillary region validated against implants; current work tests reproducibility only (literature, UNVERIFIED).
  - Graf 2022 review: 27 studies, 25 at high risk of bias; no method proved accurate (literature, UNVERIFIED).
  - Chen 2020: chin and symphysis < 1.0 mm over 4.6 y; foramina 3.0–3.4 mm (literature, UNVERIFIED).
  - Automated registration: AI RMSE 0.48 ± 0.35 vs observer 0.38 ± 0.22 mm, NS (Vasileiou 2026, literature, UNVERIFIED).
  - Dose: growth monitoring is not a Proffit CBCT indication; ≈ 0.1 vs 0.005 mSv (study pages, UNVERIFIED).
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: Where does this leave us? The mandible has a 3D reference validated against something hard; the maxilla does not yet. The 3D maxillary papers test whether two observers agree, which is necessary but not the same as being right. A 2022 systematic review concluded that no cephalometric superimposition method has been proved accurate, which is a fair reading of the risk of bias in the whole field, these three included. Automation is arriving: AI registration is reported to match a good observer in 2D and is being tested in 3D, where regional registrations are said to vary more between workflows than cranial-base ones. I have not verified those recent papers against the originals. The constraint that does not go away is dose; nobody should take serial CBCTs to measure growth.
- Time: 45 s

## Slide 17 — Summary
- Layout: bullets
- Content:
  - Palatal best fit hides ≈ 30 % of molar, ≈ 50 % of incisor eruption over 4 years (Nielsen).
  - Maxillary reference: zygomatic process; orbital floor rises 1.5× what the palate lowers (Doppel).
  - ANS, PNS, PTM, A point and the palatal plane are not registration points (both).
  - 3D mandibular reference: chin + inner symphysis, 0.37–0.40 mm over ≈ 1 y; 0.12 mm interobserver (Nguyen).
  - 2D tracing resolves 0.5 mm; first question about any number: registered on what?
- Figure: none
- Speaker notes: Five lines to take away. One: a palatal best fit hides about a third of molar eruption and half of incisor eruption over four years of growth. Two: the maxillary reference is the zygomatic process, with the orbital floor rising one and a half times what the palate lowers. Three: ANS, PNS, PTM, A point and the palatal plane itself are not stable, so never register on them. Four: in the mandible, in three dimensions, the chin and the inner symphysis moved less than half a millimetre in a year and two observers agree to a tenth. Five: a tracing resolves half a millimetre, and the first question about any number is what it was registered on.
