# Topic 1 — Superimposition Methods

## The question this topic answers

A cephalogram measures change only by superimposition, and a superimposition is only as true as the structures it is registered on. Björk's tantalum implants (1955) are the reference standard: pins in bone do not remodel, so after registering on them anything that moves is remodelling or tooth movement. Doppel 1994 ranks 17 maxillary structures by how far they drift from a three-implant fit over 4.2 y. Nielsen 1989 measures how far two anatomic maxillary methods (the palatal "best fit" and the Björk–Skieller "structural" method) misread 4 y of growth against implants. Nguyen 2018 asks which of Björk's mandibular structures survive in 3D voxel registration, with bone-anchor plates standing in for implants.

## What they agree on

- **Palatal registration hides the descent of the maxilla.** The nasal floor resorbs and the oral palate apposes, so a palate-registered tracing rides down with it. On implants ANS came down 1.62 mm and A point 2.33 mm in 4 y; best fit put ANS 0.28 mm up and A point 0.47 mm down (Nielsen Table I). Doppel's Figs 1 and 4 show the same direction.
- **The anterior surface of the zygomatic process is the best anatomic maxillary reference.** Nielsen builds the structural method on it; Doppel measures 0.95 ± 0.57 mm (Table II) and recommends it.
- **ANS, PNS, PTM and A point are not registration points.** Doppel: A point 2.13 ± 1.63, PNS 2.27 ± 1.75 (visible in 27 of 50), PTM vertical 2.39 ± 1.69 mm. Nielsen: PNS moved back 2.34 mm on implants but 1.18 mm on best fit.
- **Orbital-floor apposition exceeds nasal-floor resorption.** Björk & Skieller's 3 : 2 (Nielsen Fig 2; literature) and Doppel's 1.5 : 1 are the same ratio; the ABO writes it 3/5 : 2/5.
- **Implants are the truth, with a caveat each.** Doppel kept only films with a perfect three-implant fit (7 % of records, derived), which favours stable-looking anatomy; Nielsen excluded unstable implants; Nguyen's plates sit next to the chin they validate and were checked clinically only.
- **Landmark location, not the superimposition, drives the scatter.** Nielsen's SDs were similar across all three methods; Nguyen replaces landmarks with surface voxels and gets 0.12 ± 0.11 mm between observers.

## Where they disagree or differ

- **Is the anterior zygomatic surface stable?** Björk & Skieller: stable in 9 of 9 (as cited by Doppel). Nielsen's Introduction repeats Björk's finding that the anterior surface is unchanged except at the key ridge and orbital floor, against Enlow's resorptive view, but reports no observation of his own. Doppel (n = 40 traceable): anterior change 27.5 %, posterior 35 %, none 37.5 %.
- **What to do with the posterior zygomatic surface.** Both agree it apposes. Nielsen rejects any registration on it (Riedel's method). Doppel finds it the most traceable structure (100 %), 0.97 ± 0.43 mm, posterior in every case, and keeps it as a guide.
- **The vertical ratio.** Doppel recommends 1.5 : 1. Nielsen set apposition equal to resorption (1 : 1) and concedes a slight vertical over-read (implant minus structural −0.04 to −0.33 mm, NS) plus a horizontal offset of 0.53–0.66 mm (P < .01), whose direction his Discussion and Tables I–II state differently.
- **The internal palate.** Doppel's smallest number (0.85 ± 0.43 mm) is overruled by eye as "of limited value"; Nielsen rejects palatal methods outright. They reconcile: a maximum-contour metric cannot see a palate descending en bloc; landmark displacement can.
- **How strong a verdict.** Doppel: no maxillary area is completely reliable; Nielsen: "valid and reliable"; Nguyen: stable. Doppel's metric is the maximum deviation per case (averaged across cases), which includes a 0.5 mm tracing error; the others report mean displacements or mean surface errors.
- **Björk's 2D mandibular list versus 3D.** The ABO deck keeps the canal and the third-molar bud before root formation. Nguyen excludes the canal a priori (Krarup 2005, literature) and measures the crypt at 1.94 ± 0.06 mm, five times the chin error (derived).

## What Proffit says and how it maps

From the Proffit study pages (7th-edition numbering): three superimpositions (cranial base, Fig 6.58; maxilla "on the maximum contour of the maxilla", Fig 6.59; mandible on the symphysis, Fig 6.60); implant radiography (Ch 2) is "largely superseded by 3-D imaging"; Ch 4 [6th p88]: surface remodelling masks internal rotation; CBCT has four indications, growth monitoring not among them (cephalogram 0.005 mSv, both-jaw CBCT 0.1 mSv). AGREES on the three-level logic and the biology; NUANCE on the maxilla, since "maximum contour" is not the structural method Nielsen validated; whether Proffit lists the canal and crypt for the mandible is UNVERIFIED. Check Fig 6.59's printed legend before quoting it.

## How this affects reading the other articles this year

Khaleel & Al-Nimri 2026 (registry) superimposed the maxilla on "the palatal vault (Broadbent)" and the mandible on the Ricketts corpus axis at Pm. Broadbent's registration is the best fit Nielsen condemned, so upper posterior extrusion is under-read and upper-incisor intrusion over-read; over 44–94 d at 15.8–17.4 y the hidden descent would be 0.03–0.14 mm even at Nielsen's pubertal rate (0.27–0.56 mm per year, derived) and less in late adolescence (UNVERIFIED), negligible. The real problem is resolution: Doppel's 0.5 mm tracing error exceeds Khaleel's upper posterior values (0.07–0.42 mm) and between-arm differences (0.2–0.35 mm). In the mandible, Pm sits in Nguyen's stable chin region but Xi in remodelling ramus bone; Springate & Jones 1998 (registry) found errors ≥ 4 mm in ≈ 10 % with this method over ≈ 2 y of treatment, and over 6–13 weeks any such rotation is small (a 0.2° rotation moves a molar 40 mm from Xi by ≈ 0.14 mm, derived illustration). Khaleel's intra-examiner ICC 0.92–0.98 is repeatability of measurement, not validity of the superimposition.

## Exam radar for the topic

1. Björk 1955: tantalum implants, the reference standard for every anatomic method.
2. Structural maxillary method: AP on the anterior zygomatic surface; orbital-floor apposition : nasal-floor resorption 3 : 2 (ABO 3/5 : 2/5; Doppel 1.5 : 1).
3. Nielsen 1989: 18 of 35 subjects, T1 10 y, 4 y; best fit at ANS misses ≈ 30 % of molar and ≈ 50 % of incisor eruption.
4. Nielsen: structural vs implant, vertical NS; horizontal ≈ 0.5 mm (0.53–0.66 mm, P < .01).
5. Doppel 1994: 50 of ≈ 700 records, 4.2 y; key ridges 0.95–0.97 mm; A point 2.13, PNS 2.27, PTM vertical 2.39 mm; tracing error 0.5 mm.
6. Doppel Table III: palatal plane vs implant line +0.92 ± 2.10° (−5° to +8°); SN −0.56 ± 2.19°.
7. Björk's 2D mandibular structures: anterior chin, inner symphyseal cortex, canal, third-molar germ before root formation.
8. Nguyen 2018: chin 0.37 ± 0.16, symphysis 0.40 ± 0.14, crypt 1.94 ± 0.06 mm against bone plates; voxel 0.3 mm; canal excluded.
9. Nguyen reliability: 25 patients, 12.6 ± 0.9 months; 0.12 ± 0.11 mm; ICC 0.998 (0.995–1.000).
10. The ABO requires three structural superimpositions; its superimposition web page cites Doppel and Nielsen as validation; Graf 2022 (literature) rated 25 of 27 superimposition studies high risk of bias.

## One-paragraph verdict

The three papers say one thing from three directions: register on bone that does not remodel, and know how wrong you are when you cannot. In the maxilla that means the anterior zygomatic surface with the 3 : 2 vertical rule, never ANS, PNS, PTM, A point or a palatal best fit, which hides 30–50 % of eruption over four growing years (Nielsen); in the mandible the chin and inner symphysis, which hold to about one 0.3 mm voxel in 3D while the third-molar crypt errs 1.94 mm (Nguyen). None is strong evidence by design: all are retrospective, selected on image quality, and each rests on a reference standard with its own error. But implants cannot be repeated, so these are the data the ABO method stands on, and any growth paper that superimposes on the palate or the corpus axis must be read against them.
