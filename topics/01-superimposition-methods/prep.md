# Prep sheet — Topic 1

## 30-second opening

"Three papers, one question: which bone can we trust when we superimpose? Doppel and Nielsen tested maxillary methods against Björk's tantalum implants; Nguyen did the same for the mandible in 3D, with bone plates standing in for implants. Register on the anterior zygomatic surface above and the chin plus inner symphysis below. Register on the palate and you lose 30 to 50 % of eruption; register on the third-molar crypt and you are off by 2 mm. All three are retrospective and selected, so they give us the direction and size of the bias, not a correction factor."

## Bottom line

A palatal best fit under-reads maxillary descent (Nielsen: 1.1–2.2 mm skeletal, 1.3–1.8 mm dental over 4 y). The zygomatic surfaces drift least (Doppel: about 1 mm over 4.2 y, while ANS, A point, PNS and PTM drift 1.3–2.4 mm). In 3D the chin and inner symphysis sit within about a voxel of bone plates (0.37–0.40 mm), the crypt does not (1.94 mm), and two observers agree to 0.12 mm.

## The three papers in one breath

| Paper | One breath |
|---|---|
| Doppel 1994 | "Fifty implant cases, 4.2 years, seventeen maxillary structures ranked by drift: key ridges best at about 1 mm; ANS, A point, PNS and PTM worst; orbital floor rises 1.5 times what the palate lowers; no inferential statistics anywhere." |
| Nielsen 1989 | "Eighteen implant cases, 4 years, three superimpositions on the same films: best fit at ANS hides 30 to 50 % of eruption; Björk's structural method matches implants vertically and sits 0.5 mm off horizontally." |
| Nguyen 2018 | "Twenty Class III patients with bone plates as implants, CBCT a year apart: chin 0.37, symphysis 0.40, crypt 1.94 mm; then 25 patients, two observers, 0.12 mm, ICC 0.998." |

## Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

1. Implant or plate reference standards on the same images, which nobody can repeat today.
2. Within-subject method comparison (Nielsen) and a validity arm paired with a reliability arm (Nguyen).
3. The results match Björk's and Enlow's biology, so every bias has a mechanism, not just a number.

Weaknesses

1. Selection: 7 % of Doppel's records, 51 % of Nielsen's films, undeclared screening in Nguyen; all are the best-imaged cases.
2. Statistics: none in Doppel; an unnamed test, 64 uncorrected comparisons and implant rows that do not reproduce in Nielsen; an unnamed test and an implausible 0.06 mm SD in Nguyen.
3. Treatment and age mixed everywhere; a one-year interval in Nguyen; plates checked clinically and sitting next to the chin they validate.

## Three questions to ask

1. "Doppel ranks structures by how far they moved, not by whether they moved consistently; the posterior key ridge moved back in 50 of 50 and is still called most reliable. Should we read 'reliable' there as 'small consistent bias'?" Why it lands: it separates bias from noise, the methodological centre of the paper.
2. "Nielsen's 30 to 50 % is four years of pubertal growth. When we superimpose a three-month progress film on the palate in a 16-year-old, how much of that bias survives?" Why it lands: it moves the paper to the chair; the honest answer is a few hundredths of a millimetre, below tracing error.
3. "Nguyen drops the canal and the crypt that the ABO still lists. Should our 2D mandibular superimposition change, or is that a 3D-only finding?" Why it lands: it sets the board convention against the newest evidence and invites the seniors to defend it.

## Five questions you will be asked

1. Why is best fit wrong? The nasal floor resorbs and the oral palate apposes, so a palate-registered tracing rides down with the maxilla and hides its descent (Nielsen Table I: ANS down 1.62 mm on implants, up 0.28 mm on best fit).
2. What is the structural method? AP registration on the anterior surface of the zygomatic process, vertical placement with orbital-floor apposition to nasal-floor resorption 3 : 2 (Björk & Skieller; ABO 3/5 : 2/5; Doppel 1.5 : 1).
3. Which maxillary landmark drifted most in Doppel? PTM vertical at 2.39 ± 1.69 mm, then PNS 2.27 and A point 2.13; the cranial-base rows (2.5–2.6 mm) are maxillary displacement, not instability.
4. What is Nguyen's gold standard and what is wrong with it? Class III bone plates near the incisor apices, immobile clinically only, loaded by elastics, and a few millimetres from the chin they validate.
5. What does an ICC of 0.998 tell us? Near-perfect agreement between two observers' registrations; it is reproducibility, not validity, and the paper never names the variable correlated.

## Exam radar

Ten facts

1. Björk 1955: tantalum implants; the reference standard.
2. Nielsen: 18 of 35, T1 10 y, 4 y; best fit misses ≈ 30 % of molar and ≈ 50 % of incisor eruption.
3. Nielsen: structural vs implant vertical NS; horizontal ≈ 0.5 mm.
4. Doppel: 50 of ≈ 700, T1 11.9 y, 4.2 y; key ridges 0.95–0.97 mm; A point 2.13, PNS 2.27, PTM vertical 2.39 mm.
5. Doppel: palatal plane vs implant line +0.92 ± 2.10° (−5° to +8°); tracing error 0.5 mm.
6. Doppel: orbital-floor rise to palatal lowering 1.5 : 1 (= Björk & Skieller 3 : 2).
7. Björk's 2D mandibular structures: anterior chin, inner symphyseal cortex, canal, third-molar germ before roots.
8. Nguyen: chin 0.37, symphysis 0.40, crypt 1.94 mm; voxel 0.3 mm; canal excluded (Krarup 2005).
9. Nguyen: n = 25, 12.6 ± 0.9 months, 0.12 ± 0.11 mm, ICC 0.998 (0.995–1.000).
10. ABO: three structural superimpositions; cranial base on the anterior sella wall, planum sphenoidale, greater wings, cribriform plate, ethmoidal crests and orbital roofs.

MCQ stems

1. Superimposing on the palatal plane registered at ANS in a growing patient will most likely: (A) over-read molar extrusion; (B) under-read molar extrusion; (C) over-read forward growth at PNS; (D) have no systematic effect. Answer B (Nielsen: ≈ 30 % under-read).
2. In Doppel 1994 the maxillary structure traceable in 100 % of films with the smallest SD was: (A) ANS; (B) internal palate; (C) posterior zygomatic surface; (D) orbital floor. Answer C (0.97 ± 0.43 mm).
3. In Nguyen 2018 the region with the largest error against bone plates was: (A) anterior chin; (B) inner symphysis; (C) third-molar crypt; (D) mandibular canal. Answer C (1.94 ± 0.06 mm; the canal was excluded, not measured).

Oral question

"A paper reports 0.3 mm of upper molar extrusion over three months on a palatal superimposition. How do you read it?" Model answer: "Direction first: a palatal fit under-reads descent, so the true extrusion is if anything larger, but over three months in a late adolescent the hidden descent is a few hundredths of a millimetre (scaled from Nielsen's 0.3–0.6 mm per year at 10–14 y). The real problem is resolution: Doppel's tracing error is 0.5 mm, so 0.3 mm sits inside the noise. Direction plausible, magnitude unmeasured; ask for the method error."

## Verdict in one line

Register on bone that does not remodel: the anterior zygomatic surface with the 3 : 2 rule above, chin plus inner symphysis below; everything else carries a bias these three papers have measured.
