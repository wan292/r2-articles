## Orthodontic objectives in orthognathic surgery state of the art today

### Q1 · Last year (Khalid's recall)

> What aids in the stability after orthognathic surgery?

- **Recalled answer:** elastics and fixation plates? [Slide 'Postsurgical Orthodontic Maintenance and Detailing': intermittent elastics to retrain masticatory muscles, provide TMJ movement, increase comfort, confirm occlusal stability] · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Rigid fixation (plates and screws), with light elastics after surgery to guide jaw function (Sabri 2006 p178 (history: rigid fixation raised stability and precision) and p189 (surgical management: splint ligated, light elastics guide function); Bailey 2004 p275 (RIF needed for two-jaw cases))
- Sabri credits the routine use of rigid fixation since the 1990s with better stability. Bony segments are steadier right after surgery, and light elastics guide early function. Bailey adds that RIF is what makes two-jaw combinations stable, so 'elastics and fixation plates' fits.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Postsurgical Orthodontic Maintenance and Detailing (lecture slide)

### Q3 · Promotion 2023

- **Per the paper:** Two (intermediate and final) (Sabri 2006 p189 (double-jaw cast surgery: first splint after moving the maxillary cast, second thin final splint))

### Q5 · Promotion 2023

> What is the best extraction pattern for Class III with proclined upper incisors, retroclined lower incisors and 5 mm of crowding in the lower arch?
>
> - a) Upper 4's
> - b) Upper 4's, lower 5's
> - c) Upper 4's and lower central

- **Per the paper:** a) Upper 4's (no lower extraction; option b, upper 4s with lower 5s, is the alternative). The paper does not say how to choose between a and b. (Sabri 2006 p179 (surgical Class III: maxillary first premolars plus mandibular second premolars, or no lower extraction); p183-186 (decompensation, Class II elastics to procline lower incisors))
- For surgery, Sabri reverses the camouflage pattern: upper first premolars come out to retract proclined upper incisors, and the lower arch loses second premolars or nothing. Retroclined lower incisors need proclining anyway, and that proclination can absorb 5 mm of crowding, which favours no lower extraction. A lower incisor extraction would deepen the compensation.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Orthognathic surgery

### Q9 · Promotion 2023

### Q21 · R2 exam 2019

> [Which] surgical [procedure] needs mounting of casts on an articulator
>

> - b. Max impaction
> - c. Mand setback

- **Per the paper:** b. Max impaction (Sabri 2006 p188-189 (Planning for surgery: semi-adjustable articulator with facebow when Le Fort I is done without ramus surgery; arbitrary articulator for mandibular surgery alone))

## Considerations for orthognathic surgery during growth, Part 1 Mandibular deformities

### Q32 · Promotion 2024

> Patient will undergo TMJ surgery and orthognathic surgery. What is the appropriate treatment method?
>
> - a. Perform TMJ surgery before orthognathic surgery
> - b. Perform TMJ surgery and orthognathic surgery simultaneously
> - c. Perform orthognathic surgery prior to TMJ surgery

- **Recalled answer:** c. Perform orthognathic surgery prior to TMJ surgery · ⚠️ **differs from the paper**
- **Per the paper:** b. Perform TMJ surgery and orthognathic surgery simultaneously (Wolford). Proffit's key is c, defer TMJ surgery. (Wolford 2001 p95-96 (TMJs must be healthy before orthognathic surgery; untreated disc displacement led to pain and 30% condylar resorption) and p99 (concomitant high condylectomy plus SSRO, or condylectomy as stage 1))
- Wolford treats the joints first or at the same operation. He reports that operating on jaws over untreated displaced discs led to worse pain and condylar resorption. The recalled answer follows Proffit's advice to defer TMJ surgery, which conflicts with this paper. Expect the exam key to follow Proffit.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Proffit ch 20 (excerpt: if TMJ surgery is required along with jaw surgery, usually better to defer it until after the orthognathic surgery)

### Q33 · Promotion 2021

> 12 yrs old with high condylectomy

- **Per the paper:** High condylectomy (removing the top 3-5 mm of the condylar head, with disc repositioning) stops mandibular growth in active condylar hyperplasia. It is usually deferred to about 14 in girls and 16 in boys unless the deformity is severe. If done with jaw surgery, SSRO is the osteotomy of choice (SSRO from age 12). (Wolford 2001 p99 (Option 3 and 'High condylectomy'; Fig 4); p97 and p99 (SSRO after age 12))
- Wolford's preferred approach for accelerated Class III growth is to remove the active growth centre with a high condylectomy. This is done either together with the orthognathic correction (SSRO keeps the blood supply) or as stage 1. Appositional and dentoalveolar growth continue. Doing it at 12 is earlier than his usual age and is justified only by severity.
- *Source tag in the recall:* R2 Promotion Exam 2021

### Q34 · Promotion 2021

> 10 yrs and she has muscle pain and class II – what is the procedure?

- **Recalled answer:** Inverted L · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Inverted L osteotomy (VRO is the other option at this age) (Wolford 2001 p97 (SSRO best after age 12, once second molars erupt; ILO and VRO can be done at virtually any age; Fig 2))
- At 10, SSRO risks the unerupted second molars, so Wolford recommends waiting until 12 for it. The inverted L osteotomy avoids developing teeth and can advance the mandible and lengthen the ramus, with grafting and rigid fixation. VRO is limited by the coronoid and temporalis.
- *Source tag in the recall:* R2 Promotion Exam 2021
