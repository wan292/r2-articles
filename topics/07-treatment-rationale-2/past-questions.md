## Soft tissue changes following the extraction of premolars in nongrowing patients

### Q1 · Promotion 2023

> Patient with bimaxillary protrusion had extraction of upper and lower 4's. Expected upper lip retraction?

- **Recalled answer:** 2 - 3.2 mm (underlined; Leonardi et al. systematic review excerpt pasted: upper lip 2-3.2 mm, lower lip 2-4.5 mm) · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** About 2 to 3.2 mm upper lip retraction (lower lip 2 to 4.5 mm) (Leonardi et al 2010 Angle Orthod 80:211-216, Abstract and Conclusions (p211, p215); Table 4 (p214))
- The review of four retrospective studies of nongrowing bimaxillary-protrusion patients reports this range, and notes that a 'dished-in' profile is not expected. The paper is inconsistent with itself: the Discussion gives the upper-lip minimum as 2.4 mm (Table 4 range 2.4-3.23 mm), while the abstract says 2 mm.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles; Leonardi 'Soft tissue changes following extraction of premolars in nongrowing patients with bimaxillary protrusion' excerpt

### Q2 · Promotion 2024

> Amount of retraction of the lower lip following extraction is?
>
> - a. 2-4.5 mm

- **Recalled answer:** a. 2-4.5mm · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a. 2-4.5 mm (Leonardi et al 2010 Abstract (p211) and Conclusions (p215); Table 4 (p214))
- Across the four included studies of nongrowing bimaxillary-protrusion patients, the lower lip retracted 2 to 4.5 mm after four-premolar extraction. The paper notes wide individual variation, especially for the lower lip.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 7, 2 Soft Tissue Changes Following the Extraction of Premolars in Nongrowing Patients With Bimaxillary Protrusion: A Systematic Review, Rosalia Leonardi

## Lower incisor extraction in orthodontic treatment

### Q3 · Promotion 2023 · Promotion 2024

> Case of Class I malocclusion with lower 4.5 mm crowding, Bolton excess in lower anterior, less keratinized gingiva on lower left canine and normal profile. Extraction pattern?
>
> - a) Extraction of lower incisor

- **Recalled answer:** Extraction of lower incisor; a) Extraction of lower Incisor · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a) Extraction of a lower incisor (Kokich & Shapiro 1984 Case 1, p140-141)
- This is the same Case 1 profile: Class I, lower crowding, lower anterior Bolton excess, thin attached gingiva at the lower left canine, straight profile. Treatment was one lower incisor extraction with 4.2 mm of upper anterior IPR, planned on a waxed setup.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles

### Q6 · Promotion 2021

> Case; patient came with severe lower incisor irregularity with normal molar relationships and lower midline shifted 4 mm to the left with Bolton lower anterior excess of 0.4 mm. What is the treatment?

- **Recalled answer:** Extract lower incisor (highlighted); the Bolton phrase is struck through in the source · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Extract one lower incisor, and reduce the maxillary anterior teeth interproximally (4.2 mm) to offset the reversed tooth-size discrepancy (Kokich & Shapiro 1984 Angle Orthod 54:139-53, Case 1, p140-143 (scanned pages 2-5))
- This is Kokich & Shapiro's Case 1 almost word for word: 13-year-old girl, severe lower incisor irregularity, normal molars, lower midline 4 mm to the left, Bolton lower anterior excess of only 0.4 mm. A diagnostic setup supported one lower incisor extraction plus upper IPR. Nonextraction was rejected because of thin attached gingiva at the lower left canine, and four premolars because they would over-retract the uncrowded upper incisors. The paper is the Kokich report (t07_2), not Vilhjalmsson.

## Orthodontic treatment with removal of one mandibular incisor

### Q7 · Last year (Khalid's recall)

> Extraction of lower incisor: which incisor to extract?
>
> - the most labial tooth
> - tooth with the higher bone level

- **Recalled answer:** [Excerpt marked green: main factor in preventing black triangles is to extract the incisor with the greatest bone height around its cervical neck; if bone equal, choose the lingually inclined incisor; if crowding worse on one side, choose there] -> tooth with the higher bone level · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** Tooth with the higher (greatest) crestal bone level (Vilhjalmsson, Zermeno & Proffit 2019 AJODO 156:453-63, p455-456, section on selecting the incisor to remove)
- The paper names the greatest bone height around the cervical neck as the main factor in preventing black triangles. It warns against the labially displaced incisor because it usually has the worst bone and is hard to tip lingually. Tie-breakers: if bone is equal, take the lingually inclined incisor; if crowding is worse on one side, take a tooth from that side.

### Q8 · Promotion 2023

> For lower central incisor extraction, which tooth to choose?

- **Recalled answer:** Choose the tooth with optimum bone · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** The incisor with the best (greatest) crestal bone height (Vilhjalmsson et al 2019, p455-456)
- The paper adds that the lingually inclined incisor is often the same tooth with the best bone, which makes the choice easy. The labially displaced incisor usually has the least bone.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Literature review articles

### Q9 · Promotion 2024

> Patient came for extraction of a lower incisor. Which incisor / how?
>
> - a) Move it lingually then extract it
> - b) Extract incisor with least bone thickness
> - c) Extract tooth that is most buccally displaced

- **Recalled answer:** a) Move it lingually then extract it · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a) Move it lingually, then extract it (extraction site preparation) (Vilhjalmsson et al 2019 Material and Methods, p454-455; Fig 2 (p455))
- An elastic chain tips the chosen incisor lingually to about 45° while the space in front of it closes. The tooth is extracted once it sits behind its neighbours, which preserves crestal bone and the papilla. This takes about 2-6 months. Options b and c are the opposite of the paper's advice: choose the most bone, and avoid the labially displaced tooth.
- *Source tag in the recall:* 7, 4 Orthodontic treatment with removal of one mandibular incisor: Outcome data and the importance of extraction site preparation, Gisli Vilhjalmsson

### Q10 · Promotion 2022

> Extraction site preparation, extraction of lower incisor (which incisor to choose):
>
> - a. highest bone level

- **Recalled answer:** a. highest bone level · ✅ matches
- **Per the paper:** a. Highest bone level (Vilhjalmsson et al 2019, p455-456)
- Picking the incisor with the greatest crestal bone height keeps the alveolar crest that supports the papilla. Extraction site preparation then tips that tooth lingually before it is removed.

## 38 The importance of incisor positioning in the esthetic

### Q12 · Last year (Khalid's recall)

> Nonconsonant (inconsonant) smile picture - identify

- **Per the paper:** Nonconsonant (flat) smile arc: the curve of the maxillary incisal edges is flatter than the curve of the lower lip on posed smile (Sarver 2001 AJODO 120:98-111, p99 'Definition of the smile arc'; Fig 3B (p100))
- Sarver calls the arc consonant when the maxillary incisal-edge curve runs parallel to the lower lip, and nonconsonant (flat) when it is flatter than the lip. Fig 3B is the textbook picture of the nonconsonant arc.

### Q13 · Promotion 2022 · Last year (Khalid's recall)

> Young girl with anterior open bite and short face; how will you close the anterior open bite? (recaller not sure the question was exactly this)

- **Recalled answer:** [No answer stated; source adds 'For more info' with Sarver, The importance of incisor positioning in the esthetic smile: the smile arc, Fig 10: uprighting/retraction of maxillary incisors lengthens them and increases tooth display; mandibular incisor uprighting deepens the bite]
- **Per the paper:** Extrude and lengthen the upper incisors by uprighting and retracting them on round wire, plus cervical headgear for clockwise palatal-plane rotation. Avoid vertical-control or intrusion mechanics, which would shorten the face further. (Sarver 2001 Case 1, p101-105; Figs 7, 10, 11)
- This is the same case as 21last-30. Sarver notes that an open bite in a short-faced patient is rare and that the usual vertical-control approach would make the short face worse. The bite was therefore closed by incisor extrusion and palatal-plane rotation, with uprighting of the lower incisors also helping.
- *Source tag in the recall:* Sarver - smile arc (Fig 10)

### Q19 · Promotion 2021

### Q23 · Promotion 2024

> What's the best extraction pattern for Class III with proclined upper incisors and retroclined lower incisors and 5 mm of crowding in the lower arch?

> - b) upper 4's, lower 5's

### Q25 · Promotion 2021

> Pedo patient was referred from pedodontics with facial asymmetry and unilateral crossbite. What could be the reason?
>
> - Occlusal interference
> - Hemifacial microsomia
> - Condylar fracture

- **Per the paper:** Occlusal interference, meaning a functional mandibular shift (the paper does not say which cause is most likely) (Choi 2015 Arch Craniofac Surg 16:1-10, p1 Introduction (causes: congenital HFM, trauma, functional/occlusal interference); p6-7 'Occlusion and TMJ asymmetry' (CR-CO shift, unilateral crossbite, functional shift correctable in children))
